# The Gulf on a stopover: Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Doha and Jeddah, in pieces or all together

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 7 days if done back to back; each block is designed to work on its own in 24-48 hours
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, on our own, using the stopover programs of the Gulf airlines
**Categoria:** Medio Oriente · Emirati Arabi Uniti · Qatar · Arabia Saudita · stopover · architettura · deserto

## Apertura personale
This isn't an itinerary in the usual sense: it's a manual for not wasting your layovers. Anyone flying to Asia, Oceania or East Africa almost always connects through Dubai, Abu Dhabi or Doha, and almost always just passes over them: eight hours in a terminal, an expensive coffee, and off you go. The Gulf airlines, though, have built programs that make the stop almost free: Emirates gives you the hotel if the layover is long and unavoidable, Etihad gives it to you if you choose it, Qatar Airways sells it for fourteen dollars, and Saudia and flynas give away the visa for a country that otherwise costs a hundred and thirty euros. This document is built in independent blocks: each one works as a 24- or 48-hour stopover on its own, and lined up together they make a week. The first thing to understand is that these programs are not the same thing, and the difference is in the dedicated comparison in the Dubai page.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **November to March**, and not just for comfort. From May to September the whole Gulf goes past 45 degrees (113°F) with very high coastal humidity: a summer stopover comes down to shopping malls, museums and hotels, and what makes these cities interesting (the creek, the souqs, the Corniche, the desert) becomes impossible.
- **The programs, in brief** (the full comparison, with pros and cons of each, is in the Dubai destination page):
  - **Emirates Dubai Connect**: **free** hotel, meals, transfer and transit visa, for layovers in Dubai of roughly 8 to 26 hours. But it's a compensation, and only applies if that is **the shortest connection available**, on a single ticket issued by Emirates. The hotel is assigned and can be far away.
  - **Emirates Dubai Stopover**: the separate, paid commercial product, for people who stop by choice. Compare it with the price of an independent booking, because it isn't always the cheapest.
  - **Etihad Abu Dhabi Stopover**: one or two **free** hotel nights, but you have to opt in: you add it while booking a round-trip ticket, with a stop of at least 24 hours, and the hotel has to be booked a few days before the flight.
  - **Qatar Airways Stopover**: four-star hotel **from about $14 per person per night**, five-star from $20-30, from 1 to 4 nights, with a layover between 12 and 96 hours. Valid on Qatar Airways tickets, codeshares and oneworld. **You book it on a separate portal** (Discover Qatar) with the flight booking code: it's the step almost nobody knows about.
  - **Saudia and flynas, stopover visa**: no hotel, but **a free visa** for up to 4 days in the Kingdom, to apply for online after buying the ticket. It saves the roughly 130 euros of the ordinary tourist visa.
- **Visas, in two lines:** the Emirates and Qatar give Italian citizens **free entry on arrival**, with no advance paperwork (180 days with a 90-day stay in the Emirates, 30 days extendable in Qatar). Saudi Arabia requires a paid e-visa, **or** the free stopover one if you fly Saudia or flynas.
- **Real usable time:** a twelve-hour layover is not twelve hours in the city. Between landing, checks, transfer and getting back three hours early, you're left with five or six actual hours. The rule of thumb: **under ten hours of layover it's not worth going out**, over twelve it always is.
- **Alcohol:** in the Emirates and Qatar you can only drink in licensed venues and hotels, with zero tolerance for public drunkenness. **In Saudi Arabia it doesn't exist**, in any form and in any hotel.
- **Calls:** in the Emirates and Qatar **VoIP calls are restricted** on local networks: WhatsApp lets messages through but not calls. A foreign eSIM on roaming solves it. In Saudi Arabia there's no such block.
- **Outlets:** British type G in all three countries. The European adapter won't fit.
- **Clothing:** Dubai is the most permissive, Doha more conservative (shoulders and knees covered in public), Saudi Arabia requires covering, non-fitted clothes for everyone. Since 2019 foreign women are not required to wear the abaya, but arms and legs must be covered.
- **Ramadan:** it changes everything. Many restaurants are closed during the day, eating and drinking in public in daylight is discouraged (and in some contexts punishable), and schedules shift to the night. In Saudi Arabia the rules are applied more strictly. On the other hand, Ramadan evenings are the most interesting time of the year.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Dubai, the first 24 hours
The minimum block, the one that works with Dubai Connect or with any long layover.

From the airport we took the **metro**: in twenty minutes you're downtown, for a few dirhams. First stop **Al Fahidi**, the historic district with the wind towers and the coral-and-gypsum houses: it's the Dubai from before oil, and you can walk it in an hour.

Then the thing that saves the city from anyone ready to write it off: the **Dubai Creek by abra**. One dirham a crossing, wooden boats full of commuters and, from the water, the dhows moored at Deira loaded with household appliances bound for Iran and Somalia. Once across, we walked the **spice souq** and the **gold souq**, where you haggle.

For dinner we went to the **Karama or Bur Dubai** neighborhoods: Pakistani and Indian food in restaurants full of residents, for under ten euros. It's where you eat best in the city.

If you have time and energy left, there's the **Burj Khalifa** at sunset, with a ticket bought online days in advance, because the evening slots sell out.

### Day 2 — Dubai, the second day
With 48 hours you step outside the circuit.

In the morning there's **Alserkal Avenue**, the art gallery district carved out of industrial warehouses, the opposite of the image you have of this city. Or the **Museum of the Future**, with Arabic calligraphy carved into its torus-shaped facade.

The afternoon and evening we spent in the **desert**: an evening safari with dune bashing, dinner at a tented camp and back. Take it for what it is (organized, on modest dunes half an hour from the city, with ten other jeeps on the same dune), but it's still a pleasant evening. *The real desert is something else and it's at Liwa: see the extensions.*

### Day 3 — Abu Dhabi, the Etihad stopover
The free block: one or two hotel nights offered by Etihad on a round-trip ticket with a declared stop of at least 24 hours.

The stop that alone justifies the layover is the **Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque**, where we went in the late afternoon: a thousand white columns inlaid with mother-of-pearl, the largest hand-knotted carpet in the world, and marble that under the night lighting goes from white to pink and then blue. **Free entry but with a timed-slot reservation**, and a dress code that is seriously enforced: arms and legs covered for everyone, head covered for women. Arriving already dressed the right way saves you the queue for the loaner abayas.

In the evening there's the **Corniche** or the **Al Mina** fish market, where you pick at the counter and have it cooked next door.

### Day 4 — Abu Dhabi, the Louvre and the desert
Morning or evening at the **Louvre Abu Dhabi**, on Saadiyat Island. The building is worth as much as the collection: under the dome of almost eight thousand overlapping metal stars falls a light that moves all day, and Nouvel calls it a rain of light. The collection is arranged **by era and not by civilization**, so in the same room there are contemporary objects made by cultures that didn't know each other.

With one more day you go to **Liwa**, two hours away, on the edge of the Rub' al-Khali, the largest continuous sand desert in the world, with dunes over two hundred meters high. It's the real desert, the opposite of the evening safari. Or **Al Ain**, the UNESCO-listed oasis with a hundred and forty-seven thousand palms irrigated by channels dug two thousand years ago and still working.

### Day 5 — Doha, the fourteen-dollar stopover
The block with the best value for money in the whole Gulf. **You book it on the Discover Qatar portal with the flight booking code**, not together with the ticket: it's the step almost nobody knows about.

From the airport, **metro** again: twenty minutes to the center. The first stop was the **Museum of Islamic Art**, which Pei designed at ninety after traveling the Muslim world to understand what Islamic architecture was. From the outer steps, at sunset, you look at the West Bay skyline across the water.

In the evening at the **Souq Waqif**, restored but still a real market. The part that throws you is the falcon wing: rows of hooded raptors on perches waiting to be bought and, next to it, a **falcon veterinary hospital** you can visit, with the waiting room where owners sit with the bird on their glove. You walk in and look, no ticket.

We had dinner in the souq, with **karak chai** from the stalls: the spiced milk tea people drink here at all hours, costing a few cents.

### Day 6 — Doha, the inland sea
The day almost nobody does, and the reason to stretch the stopover by a day.

**Khor Al Adaid**, the inland sea, is an hour and a half from Doha, and the last half hour is on sand. The driver lets air out of the tires at the start of the track, then half an hour of ups and downs through the dunes, until you crest a ridge and the sea appears below: an arm of the Gulf that runs for kilometers into the desert, with the dunes dropping straight into the water and Saudi Arabia on the other shore. It's a nature reserve recognized by UNESCO, and there are very few places in the world with this shape.

**You need a 4x4 with a driver**: with a regular car you get stuck, it's a classic. It's best to stay until sunset, knowing that after dark the temperature drops even in winter.

*Alternatively, the north: the UNESCO-listed Al Zubarah fort, the abandoned fishing village of Al Jumail and Richard Serra's four steel slabs, fourteen meters high, planted in the Brouq desert, with no parking, no ticket and nobody around.*

### Day 7 — Jeddah, the Saudi stopover
A block unlike all the others: here you don't save on the hotel, you save on **the visa**. Flying Saudia or flynas you apply online for the **free stopover visa**, valid for up to 4 days, instead of the tourist visa at about 130 euros.

**Al-Balad**, the UNESCO-listed historic center, is the reason to get off: tower houses of coral stone taken from the Red Sea, with the **rawashin**, carved teak wood facades up to four stories high, which came from India on the pilgrim ships. See it **in the last hour of light**: by day it's a dusty neighborhood full of scaffolding, then the low light catches the wood and everything changes.

In the evening we went to the **Corniche**, which on Thursday is the city's social moment: families with rugs spread on the grass, barbecues lit, kids playing until midnight. That's where you best understand what kind of country this is.

*With four days of stopover visa you can also get to **AlUla**, on a domestic flight: two days in Jeddah and two at Hegra is the cheapest way in the world to see a Nabataean city with nobody in front of you.*

## What to see / what to do
- **Work out which program you hold before booking.** Dubai Connect is a compensation, the others are choices.
- **The creek by abra for one dirham**, the best thing in Dubai.
- **The Grand Mosque in the late afternoon**, already dressed the right way.
- **The Louvre Abu Dhabi for the building**, as much as for the collection.
- **Booking the Qatar Airways stopover on the separate portal.**
- **The falcon hospital at the Souq Waqif.**
- **Khor Al Adaid**, worth the extra day.
- **Al-Balad in the last hour of light.**
- **Eating in the neighborhoods where the workers live**, in all three cities: it's where you eat best and spend a tenth.

## Possible extensions
- **Liwa and the Rub' al-Khali (+2 days from Abu Dhabi):** the real desert, with dunes over two hundred meters high, instead of the evening safari on dunes half an hour from the city. With a night in a camp.
- **Al Ain (+1 day from Abu Dhabi):** the UNESCO-listed oasis with the falaj channels working for two thousand years, to understand what this country was like before oil.
- **Northern Qatar (+1 day from Doha):** Al Zubarah, the abandoned villages and Serra's slabs in the desert.
- **AlUla (+2 days from Jeddah):** Hegra, the second Nabataean city after Petra, reachable on a domestic flight and covered by the 4 days of the stopover visa.
- **Full Saudi Arabia (+12 days):** the site has a dedicated itinerary.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
- **Dubai:** with Dubai Connect the hotel is assigned and you don't choose. Outside the program, Deira and Bur Dubai for low prices and the metro, Downtown to be under the Burj Khalifa.
- **Abu Dhabi:** with the Etihad stopover you choose for free among the partner properties. Otherwise the most convenient area is the Corniche.
- **Doha:** with the stopover program you choose between four and five stars at token prices, and there's no reason to do otherwise.
- **Jeddah:** near Al-Balad to be inside the historic part, or on the Corniche for the sea and the restaurants.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
- **Dubai:** Pakistani biryani and curries in Karama and Bur Dubai, for under ten euros. Real Emirati food is rare: Emiratis are just over ten percent of the people who live here, and it shows first of all on the menus.
- **Abu Dhabi:** Al Mina fish market, where you buy at the counter and have it cooked next door for a few dirhams. Machboos and karak chai for local food.
- **Doha:** Souq Waqif for Levantine, Iranian and Qatari food; karak chai at the stalls; the neighborhoods of workers from the subcontinent for eating well on very little.
- **Jeddah:** kabsa, Yemeni mandi cooked in the underground oven, foul for breakfast, Red Sea fish on the Corniche. And Arabic coffee with dates, which here is a ritual of hospitality more than something you consume.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't confuse Dubai Connect with a tourist stopover.** If you chose a later flight instead of the shortest one available, the program lapses.
- **Book the Qatar Airways stopover on the Discover Qatar portal**, separately from the flight. Knowing it costs nothing, not knowing it costs a lot.
- **Add the Etihad stopover while booking the ticket**, not after, and book the hotel a few days before the flight.
- **Apply for the Saudi stopover visa right after buying the ticket**, not at the last minute.
- **Count the real usable time**, not the layover hours: under ten it isn't worth going out.
- **Don't go in summer.** This recommendation counts for more than all the others put together.
- **Bring the type G adapter.**
- **Don't count on WhatsApp for calls** in the Emirates and Qatar.
- **Dress the right way before heading to the mosque**, without counting on loaner abayas.
- **Remember that in Saudi Arabia alcohol doesn't exist**, and that bringing it in is a serious offense.

## Chiusura personale
What stays with you, after turning four layovers into four stops, is how little it costs to do it and how few people do. You spend your life treating a Gulf layover as a price to pay to get somewhere else, and meanwhile the airlines that profit from it have built programs that practically pay you to get off. But the reason it's worth it isn't financial. It's that in each of these cities, twenty minutes from the skyline everyone photographs, there's something that skyline doesn't tell you: a creek where ships are still loaded by hand, a hospital for falcons, a lagoon where the sea enters the desert, a neighborhood of coral houses held up by scaffolding. They're places nobody sells as a destination, and you can see them all within the dead hours of a ticket to another part of the world.
