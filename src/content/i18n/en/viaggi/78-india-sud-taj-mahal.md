# South India in 17 Days: Tamil Nadu Temples, Kerala, Hampi and a Jump to Agra for the Taj Mahal

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (itinerary and practical information complete and verified as of October 2026)
**Periodo del viaggio:** designed for December to March, the dry season across the South and the coolest in Agra
**Durata:** 17 days, 16 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independent, with a car and driver in Tamil Nadu and Kerala, a night on a houseboat, domestic flights, an overnight train to Hampi and the fast Delhi-Agra train
**Categoria:** Asia · India · India del Sud · Tamil Nadu · Kerala · Karnataka · Hampi · backwaters · templi · Taj Mahal

## Apertura personale
South India is a different country from the North. Different languages are spoken, you eat off banana leaves, the temples are stone cities with towers as tall as apartment blocks, and the landscape moves from rice paddies to tea hills to lagoons. The pace is slower, the traffic less ferocious, the people more reserved. For many travelers it's the gentlest way into India, or the second trip after Rajasthan.

This itinerary crosses three states. In **Tamil Nadu**, the Dravidian temples of Mamallapuram, Thanjavur and Madurai and the French interlude of Puducherry; in **Kerala**, the tea plantations of Munnar, a night on a houseboat on the backwaters and the colonial quarters of Fort Kochi; in **Karnataka**, the ruins of Hampi, the ancient capital of the Vijayanagara empire among granite boulders and rice paddies. At the end, a flight north for the Taj Mahal, because most people going to India for the first time don't want to come home without having seen it.

Hampi is the stop you can drop. It sits off the line of the trip, requires a flight and two nights on a train, but it's one of the most beautiful places in India: anyone with less time will find a 14-day version without it at the end.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** December to March. Kerala gets the southwest monsoon from June to August, with heavy rain and rough seas; the Tamil Nadu coast (Chennai, Mamallapuram, Puducherry) gets the northeast monsoon between October and December, with a risk of cyclones. Hampi is pleasant from October to February and scorching from April. In Agra, December and January bring fog at dawn.
- **Documents:** passport valid for at least 6 months from the date of the visa application and two blank pages, e-Tourist Visa on the official portal indianvisaonline.gov.in, to be requested at least 4 days ahead ($25 for 30 days, $40 for one year, plus a 2.5-3% bank fee; fees unchanged in 2026) and the free online e-Arrival Card within the 72 hours before arrival. Banned at entry: satellite phones, drones without a permit and e-cigarettes.
- **Currency and payments:** Indian rupee, roughly 95-105 to the euro. Cards in hotels and good restaurants; cash for the houseboat (often the balance too), tuk-tuks, temples, markets and tips.
- **Temples:** in 52 large Tamil Nadu temples, including the Meenakshi in Madurai, phones were banned from September 1, 2026; since mid-October 2026 you can bring them in as long as they're switched off, or leave them at the deposit for 5 rupees. Photos, videos and selfies remain banned. The inner sanctums of many temples are reserved for Hindus. In Kerala, at several temples men must take off their shirts, and some (like the Padmanabhaswamy in Thiruvananthapuram) don't admit non-Hindus.
- **Health:** bottled water, cooked food, mosquito repellent in the backwaters and at Hampi: malaria, dengue and chikungunya are more widespread in the South and after the monsoons. Hepatitis A and B and typhoid vaccines recommended, plus an up-to-date tetanus shot.
- **Clothing:** shoulders and knees covered in temples; dhotis and scarves can be bought for a few rupees on the spot. On village beaches, no bikinis outside the tourist areas.
- **Connectivity:** Airtel or Jio SIM on arrival in Chennai; good coverage, weak in some valleys around Munnar.
- **Electricity:** 230V, plug types C, D and M. A universal adapter is recommended.
- **Time zone:** UTC+5:30, 3.5 hours ahead of Italy during daylight saving time, 4.5 in winter.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Chennai and transfer to Mamallapuram
From Italy you reach **Chennai** with one stopover, almost always in the Gulf, often at night or early in the morning. The city is big and hot, and for a first day it's best to leave it: an hour and a half along the coast, on the East Coast Road, took us to **Mamallapuram**, a village of stonecutters by the sea. The rest of the day was for resting and watching the sunset over the **Shore Temple**, the eighth-century temple on the beach, battered by wind and salt.

**In practice:** transfer or prepaid taxi from the airport, about 1 hour 30 minutes · the driver for Tamil Nadu can be booked from here to Madurai · no swimming in the sea: the currents on the Coromandel coast are dangerous.

### Day 2 — Mamallapuram: the temple on the sea and the Five Rathas
Mamallapuram was the port of the Pallava kings, and here the sculptors of the seventh and eighth centuries invented the architecture of the temples of the South. We began early with the **Shore Temple** and continued with the **Five Rathas**, five chariot-shaped temples each carved from a single block of granite, with life-size stone elephants and lions. In the village are the **Descent of the Ganges** (also called Arjuna's Penance), a thirty-meter bas-relief with gods, animals and a river carved in the rock, and "Krishna's butter ball," a boulder balanced on a slope.

We spent the afternoon in the stonecutters' workshops, who still work the stone by hand, and on the fishermen's beach. If you have energy, make a detour to **Kanchipuram**, an hour away, the city of temples and silk.

**In practice:** a single ASI ticket for foreigners, about 600 rupees, covers the Shore Temple and Five Rathas · Arjuna's Penance and the village's rock-cut temples are free · the stone workshops sell at negotiable prices.

### Day 3 — Puducherry, the French city
Two hours farther south along the coast is **Puducherry**, a French colony until 1954. The White Town, between the seafront and the canal, has grid streets with French names, yellow villas with bougainvillea, policemen in red kepis and boulangeries; the Tamil quarter, across the canal, has houses with verandas and temples. We visited the **Sri Aurobindo Ashram** in silence, walked the **Promenade** at sunset, when they close it to cars, and had dinner in one of the Creole restaurants that mix France and Tamil Nadu.

If you like, make a trip to **Auroville**, the international community founded in the Sixties, half an hour away: the Matrimandir, the big golden sphere, can be seen from a viewpoint after a visit to the visitor center.

**In practice:** Mamallapuram-Puducherry about 2 hours · Auroville: to see the Matrimandir from the inside you need a reservation at the visitor center days ahead · Puducherry has lower alcohol taxes than Tamil Nadu, and it's easy to find a bar.

### Day 4 — Toward Thanjavur and the Brihadeeswarar temple
A day on the road inland, among rice paddies and coconut palms, with two stops worth the trip: the **Nataraja temple at Chidambaram**, dedicated to dancing Shiva, and the royal temple of **Gangaikonda Cholapuram**, almost always deserted. We reached **Thanjavur** in the afternoon for the **Brihadeeswarar**, the Great Temple of the Chola kings, a UNESCO site, built around the year 1000. The tower is about sixty meters tall, the cupola at the top weighs tens of tons, and the late-afternoon light turns all the granite orange.

**In practice:** Puducherry-Thanjavur about 4-5 hours with stops · Brihadeeswarar free entry, shoes at the deposit, phones switched off or at the deposit if the temple applies the state rule · the Maratha Palace and the Saraswathi Mahal library, in town, are paid.

### Day 5 — From Thanjavur to Madurai and the evening ceremony at the Meenakshi
A quiet morning in Thanjavur, with the **Maratha Palace** and the gallery of Chola bronzes, one of the finest collections of sculpture in India. Then three hours by road to **Madurai**, one of the oldest inhabited cities of the South, built around the **Meenakshi Amman temple**. In the evening we went back to the temple for the night ceremony, when the statue of Shiva is carried in procession to the goddess's chamber, among drums, flowers and lamps.

*The temple is alive at every hour, and the best thing to do is stand still in one spot and watch.* You enter without shoes and without large bags, with your phone switched off or left at the deposit. For photos from the outside you go up to one of the shop terraces around, where the owners show you the towers and then try to sell you fabrics.

**In practice:** Thanjavur-Madurai about 3 hours · Meenakshi open early in the morning and from late afternoon to evening, closed in the middle hours · free entry, phone switched off or at the deposit (5 rupees), no photos inside; the inner sanctum is reserved for Hindus.

### Day 6 — Madurai, the temple city
In the morning we visited the **Meenakshi temple** at leisure: the fourteen towers (gopurams) covered in thousands of painted deities, the hall of a thousand columns, the Golden Lotus tank, the musicians and the elephants that bless the faithful. Then the seventeenth-century **Thirumalai Nayak** palace, with a courtyard of enormous columns, and the **Mattuthavani** flower market, where tons of jasmine arrive at dawn.

The afternoon is for the heat, the evening for street food: Madurai is the city of **jigarthanda**, a cold drink of milk, gelatin, syrup and ice cream, and of parotta with the mutton curry of Chettinad cooking. *Madurai has no monuments to collect: it's a city to watch living.*

**In practice:** Thirumalai Nayak palace modest ticket for foreigners · the flower market is best before 8 · the tailors near the temple sew made-to-measure clothes in a day.

### Day 7 — From the plains to the hills of Munnar
From the heat of the plain we climbed toward the **Western Ghats**. The road from Madurai to **Munnar**, five to six hours, crosses the border between Tamil Nadu and Kerala and climbs in hairpins among cardamom, forests and the first tea plantations, up to 1,600 meters. The air changes, and in the evening you need a sweater. We arrived in the afternoon, with time for a first walk among the hills combed by rows of tea.

**In practice:** Madurai-Munnar about 5-6 hours by car with driver · sleep outside the town, which is congested, in a homestay or resort among the plantations · mountain road with curves: anyone who gets carsick should set off on a light stomach.

### Day 8 — Munnar: tea plantations and Eravikulam park
Munnar was the English summer retreat, and today it is a sea of tea plantations as far as the eye can see. Early in the morning we went to **Eravikulam National Park**, home of the Nilgiri tahr, a mountain goat found only here that comes close without fear. Then the Tata **tea museum** (KDHP), with the factory showing the stages of processing, and a walk or jeep ride among the plantations.

If you want something more, get up at 4 for the sunrise at **Kolukkumalai**, one of the highest plantations in the world, reachable only by jeep on a rough track. *The clouds filling the valleys below the ridges at sunrise are the reason you go up.*

**In practice:** Eravikulam closed from about February 1 to March 31 for the tahr births (in 2026 it reopened on April 1), open from 8 to 4, foreigner ticket around 500 rupees, better booked online · tea museum closed on Mondays · jeep to Kolukkumalai booked through the hotel.

### Day 9 — The backwaters: a night on a houseboat in Alleppey
In four to five hours we came down toward the coast, to **Alappuzha** (Alleppey), the gateway to the **backwaters**: a network of canals, lagoons and lakes running parallel to the sea, among rice paddies below water level and villages on strips of land. At noon we boarded a **kettuvallam**, a wood and bamboo houseboat with a cabin, bathroom, cook and crew. The afternoon was a slow glide among palms, churches, schools and women washing clothes on the banks. In the evening the boat moors: houseboats can't sail at night.

*The backwaters around Alleppey are also congested, with hundreds of boats in the same main canals.* To see the small canals, add a canoe ride the next morning around Kainakary, or choose a houseboat that departs from Kumarakom or less-trodden areas.

**In practice:** boarding around 12, disembarking at 9 the next morning, meals included · price for the boat with one room, full board: roughly 8,000-15,000 rupees in the standard range, 15,000-25,000 premium, much more in luxury, with steep rises at Christmas and New Year · book with registered operators and ask whether the boat has air conditioning only at night (often it does).

### Day 10 — From the backwaters to Fort Kochi
Breakfast on the boat, disembarking and an hour and a half by road to **Fort Kochi**, the most cosmopolitan city in Kerala: for centuries a spice port, passed through by the Portuguese, Dutch and English, with one of the oldest Jewish and Christian communities in Asia. In the early afternoon we walked along the sea to the **Chinese fishing nets**, big cantilevered structures that fishermen lower into the water by hand, and visited **St. Francis Church**, where Vasco da Gama was buried. In the evening, a **kathakali** show, the dance-theater of Kerala, arriving an hour early to see the actors put on their makeup.

**In practice:** Alleppey-Fort Kochi about 1 hour 30 minutes · kathakali at the Kerala Kathakali Centre, about 500-600 rupees, every evening · Fort Kochi is easy on foot and by bike.

### Day 11 — Fort Kochi and Mattancherry
**Mattancherry**, a couple of kilometers away, is the spice merchants' quarter. The **Dutch Palace**, actually built by the Portuguese, has Ramayana murals among the finest in Kerala. Next to it is **Jew Town**, the street of antique dealers leading to the sixteenth-century **Paradesi Synagogue**, with a floor of hand-painted Chinese tiles, each one different. Then the ginger and pepper warehouses, which perfume the whole street.

The afternoon is free: a Keralan cooking class, a coffee in one of the city's art galleries (Fort Kochi hosts the Biennale, between December and March in even-numbered years), a sunset over the sea. For dinner, fish chosen at the market near the nets and cooked by the restaurants across the way.

**In practice:** Dutch Palace and synagogue closed on Fridays, the synagogue also on Saturdays and Jewish holidays · modest entrance fees · *in the spice and antique shops you always haggle*.

### Day 12 — Flight to Bangalore and overnight train to Hampi
A transfer day toward Karnataka. Flight from Kochi to **Bangalore** (Bengaluru), about an hour, and a free afternoon in the city: **Lalbagh**, the botanical garden, Tipu Sultan's palace, the café district around Church Street. In the evening we took the **overnight train to Hosapete** (Hospet), the Hampi station, in a berth, in 2A or 3A, arriving at dawn.

**In practice:** Kochi-Bangalore flight 3,000-6,000 rupees if booked early · Hampi Express (16592), from Bangalore City around 9:50 p.m. arriving at Hosapete around 7 a.m., about 9 hours, daily, to be booked on IRCTC a few weeks ahead · alternatively fly to Vidyanagar (Jindal) airport, about an hour from Hampi, with few frequencies.

### Day 13 — Hampi: the bazaar, the Virupaksha temple and sunset on Matanga
At dawn, from Hosapete, it's twenty minutes by tuk-tuk to **Hampi**. The landscape is that of another planet: hills made of enormous rounded granite boulders, balanced one on another, among rice paddies, banana groves and the Tungabhadra river. Among these boulders stood **Vijayanagara**, the capital of one of India's greatest empires, destroyed in 1565; today it's a UNESCO site with over a thousand monuments.

We devoted the first day to the **Sacred Center**: the **Virupaksha temple**, still active, with the temple elephant blessing the faithful, the **Hampi bazaar** with its stone porticoes and the **Hemakuta** hill, dotted with temples. We watched the sunset from the top of **Matanga hill**, after a short but steep climb among the rocks, with the whole site below us.

**In practice:** Virupaksha small entrance ticket · in the Hampi bazaar no meat or alcohol · rent a scooter or bicycle to get around (international license), or a tuk-tuk by the day.

### Day 14 — Hampi: the Royal Center, the Vittala temple and the overnight train
The **Royal Center** preserves the traces of the court: the **queens' enclosure** with the Lotus Mahal, the domed **elephant stables**, the stepped tank, the Mahanavami platform. Then the **Vittala temple**, the most famous in Hampi, with the **stone chariot** that appears on the 50-rupee banknote and the "musical" columns that once resounded when struck. *Arrive early or in the late afternoon: at midday the granite gives off a heat that's hard to bear.*

In the afternoon, a ride in a **coracle**, the round wicker boats, on the Tungabhadra. In the evening we took the overnight train from Hosapete to **Bangalore**, the Hampi Express (16591) at about 9:10 p.m.

**In practice:** the Vittala ticket for foreigners (about 600 rupees) is also valid the same day for the queens' enclosure and the elephant stables · coracle 300-500 rupees per person · crossing the river toward Virupapur Gaddi depends on the water level.

### Day 15 — From Bangalore to Agra
Arrival in Bangalore in the morning and a flight north. IndiGo flies direct to **Agra** five to eight times a week, in about 2 hours 40 minutes; on other days you fly to **Delhi** (about 2 hours 30 minutes) and take a fast afternoon train to Agra, an hour and forty minutes. The change in climate is sharp: from December to February it's cold in the evening in Agra. If you arrive in time for sunset, from the garden of **Mehtab Bagh**, on the opposite bank of the Yamuna, you see the Taj Mahal straight ahead, in the evening light.

**In practice:** Bangalore-Delhi flight 4,000-8,000 rupees if booked early · Delhi-Agra train (Gatimaan Express or Vande Bharat) 800-1,600 rupees · sleep in Taj Ganj, near the east gate · check that the next day isn't Friday, the Taj's closing day.

### Day 16 — The Taj Mahal at dawn and Agra Fort
We were at the gates of the **Taj Mahal** half an hour before dawn, with the online ticket and no large bags, food, tripods or drones. The first hour is the best: the marble changes color, the pool reflects the dome and the platform is almost empty. After breakfast, **Agra Fort**, the red sandstone Mughal citadel from which Shah Jahan looked at the Taj in his last years, and the **Itimad-ud-Daulah**, the "baby Taj." In the afternoon we took the train to **Delhi**.

**In practice:** Taj Mahal about 1,100 rupees plus 200 for the mausoleum · Agra Fort about 650 rupees · Agra-Delhi train about 2 hours.

### Day 17 — Delhi and departure
The last day depends on the flight time. With a few hours to spare you see **Humayun's Tomb**, the mausoleum that inspired the Taj, and close the trip at the **Gurudwara Bangla Sahib**, the Sikh temple with a kitchen open to everyone. Many flights to Europe leave at night: a room at Aerocity, near the airport, allows a few hours of rest.

**In practice:** Humayun about 600 rupees · Airport Express metro from New Delhi to the airport in about 20 minutes · arrive at the airport 3 hours early for international flights.

## Where to stay
- **Mamallapuram:** guesthouses near the fishermen's beach in the budget range; resorts on the East Coast Road in the mid and high range.
- **Puducherry:** in the **White Town**, in colonial villas turned into heritage hotels, from 4,000 rupees up; guesthouses of the Ashram and the Tamil quarter are cheaper.
- **Thanjavur and Madurai:** city hotels in the mid-range near the temple; in Madurai those who want to spend more find heritage hotels in the hills around.
- **Munnar:** homestays and resorts **among the plantations**, outside the town, from 2,500 rupees for a homestay to big sums for resorts with a view.
- **Backwaters:** a night on a **houseboat** (see Day 9), or a homestay on the canals at Kainakary or Kumarakom for those who prefer to sleep on land.
- **Fort Kochi:** family-run guesthouses and homestays in the budget range; colonial bungalows and heritage hotels in the mid and high range.
- **Hampi:** the Hampi bazaar has simple guesthouses; across the river, at Virupapur Gaddi and Hanumanahalli, there are resorts among rice paddies and boulders. At the high end, resorts near Kamalapura or in Hosapete.
- **Agra and Delhi:** Taj Ganj in Agra; Aerocity in Delhi for the last night.

## Where to eat
- **Tamil Nadu:** the meal on a banana leaf (meals) with rice, sambar, rasam, vegetable curry and yogurt, served all-you-can-eat; dosa, idli and vada for breakfast with coconut chutney; filter coffee in a metal cup; the spicy cooking of Chettinad with chicken and mutton; in Madurai the jigarthanda and parotta with curry.
- **Puducherry:** Franco-Tamil Creole cooking, croissants and baguettes in the boulangeries of the White Town.
- **Kerala:** appam (fermented rice crêpe) with vegetable or chicken stew, grilled karimeen (pearl spot) fish in banana leaf, fish moilee in coconut milk, parotta with fried beef (beef is eaten in Kerala), the sadya, the big vegetarian feast-day lunch.
- **Karnataka:** the Bangalore breakfast with masala dosa and filter coffee; in Hampi simple thalis and coffee on guesthouse terraces, with no meat or alcohol in the bazaar.
- **Agra and Delhi:** Mughlai and Punjabi cooking to close in the North.
- **Golden rules:** bottled water, no raw salads in the first days, ask for reduced spice. You eat with your right hand.

## Getting around
- **Car with driver in Tamil Nadu and Kerala:** the most convenient choice for the Chennai-Madurai-Munnar-Alleppey-Kochi part, with many intermediate stops and few convenient trains. Roughly 3,000-4,500 rupees a day, with tolls, parking, interstate permits (crossing from Tamil Nadu to Kerala has a tax for tourist vehicles) and the driver's meals to be clarified in the contract.
- **Domestic flights:** Kochi-Bangalore (about 1 hour) and Bangalore-Delhi (about 2 hours 30 minutes) with IndiGo and Air India, or Bangalore-Agra direct with IndiGo (5-8 flights a week); 3,000-8,000 rupees if booked early.
- **Trains:** the overnight Bangalore-Hosapete and back, the Hampi Express (16592 outbound, 16591 return, about 9 hours, 2A or 3A, daily), and the fast Delhi-Agra train (Gatimaan Express or Vande Bharat). Book on irctc.co.in with a foreigner account.
- **At Hampi:** rented scooter or bicycle, or a tuk-tuk by the day (1,200-1,800 rupees). Distances between the monument groups are a few kilometers.
- **In town:** Uber and Ola in Chennai, Bangalore, Kochi and Delhi; tuk-tuks everywhere with the price fixed beforehand.

## When to go, month by month
- **October-November:** northeast monsoon on the Tamil Nadu coast, with heavy rain and a risk of cyclones; Kerala is green and generally dry from mid-October.
- **December:** the high season begins in the South. High prices between Christmas and New Year, houseboats to be booked early.
- **January:** the best month together with February. Pongal, the harvest festival in Tamil Nadu, around January 14-17. Fog at dawn in Agra.
- **February:** ideal weather, but in Munnar Eravikulam park closes for the tahr births (from February 1, reopening April 1).
- **March:** heat rising in Madurai and Hampi (35 °C and more), still good in Kerala and Munnar. End of the Kochi Biennale season in the years it's held.
- **April-May:** heavy heat on the plains (Hampi tops 40 °C), Munnar stays cool. Pre-monsoon in Kerala.
- **June-September:** southwest monsoon in Kerala, with intense rain and a closed sea; very green landscapes and low prices for those who accept the rain. Onam, Kerala's big festival, falls between August and September.

Many of the great temples of the South are free.

## Common scams and safety
- **Houseboats:** touts on the Alleppey piers offer boats at low prices that then turn out different from the photos, or add extras for air conditioning and meals. Book with registered operators and recent reviews, with a contract stating what's included.
- **The Meenakshi terraces:** the shopkeepers who invite you up "for the view" are friendly, then you have to listen to the fabric sales pitch. You can say thank you and go down.
- **Spices and antiques in Kochi:** prices to be haggled, "antiques" often new, saffron sometimes fake.
- **Tuk-tuks and drivers:** price agreed beforehand; "free" rides end up in shops paying commissions.
- **Northern scams in Agra and Delhi:** fake guides at the Taj gates, "closed" hotels, fake tourism offices near New Delhi station.
- **The sea:** the Tamil Nadu and Kerala coasts have strong currents and beaches without lifeguards; swim only where it's supervised.
- **Women traveling:** the South is calmer than the North, but the same rules apply: covered clothing, booked transfers for night arrivals, the upper berth on the train, firmness in answering anyone who insists.
- **The road:** the driver is the right choice; the road to Munnar has hairpins and trucks, better to travel it by day.

## Variations
- **Without Hampi, 14 days:** after Fort Kochi (Day 11) you fly directly from Kochi to Delhi or Agra and close with the Taj Mahal. You save two nights on a train and one flight.
- **With Periyar (+1 day):** between Madurai and Munnar, Periyar park at Thekkady offers forest trekking with guides, boat rides on the lake and spice plantations. Tigers are seldom seen; elephants and bison more often.
- **With the sea (+2-3 days):** the cliffs of **Varkala** or the beach of **Marari**, near Alleppey, to finish Kerala with a few days of rest. At the end of the trip, before the flight to Agra.
- **With Mysore and Coorg (+3 days):** from Bangalore, the Mysore palace and the coffee plantations of Coorg, instead of Hampi or in addition.
- **Without the Taj Mahal:** anyone who has already seen the North ends the trip in Bangalore or Kochi and flies straight home to Italy.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book the houseboat in advance** for December-January, and choose a boat with a single room for peace and quiet.
- **Keep your phone switched off or leave it at the deposit** in the great Tamil Nadu temples: photos and videos inside are banned and the rule is enforced.
- **Check closing days:** Taj Mahal on Fridays, Kochi's Dutch Palace and synagogue on Fridays, the synagogue also on Saturdays, Munnar tea museum on Mondays, Eravikulam closed from February 1 to March 31.
- **Visit Hampi at dawn and sunset**, not at midday.
- **Leave margin on flights:** no intercontinental flight on the day of a domestic flight without at least 4-5 hours of margin.
- **Pack clothes for three climates:** humid heat on the coast, cool in Munnar, cold in the morning in Agra in winter.
- **Don't swim where there are no lifeguards.**
- **Repellent in the backwaters**, especially at sunset.

## Chiusura personale
South India is remembered for its color. The green of the rice paddies and tea hills, the blue of the canals, the orange of the temples at sunset, the white of jasmine at the Madurai market. Hampi adds the granite and the silence, Agra the marble and the crowd. Anyone with seventeen days comes home with an image of India less chaotic than expected, and with the wish to see the North too.
