## Apertura personale
This itinerary is rooted in places Rick has personally visited, put together into one practical route for anyone who wants the Taj Mahal and South India in the same trip. For Varanasi and Rajasthan there is the other India trip: *India: Varanasi, Ranthambore and Rajasthan in 19 Days, by Overnight Train and with a Driver*.

You start in the North, because most first-timers in India don't want to go home without seeing the Taj Mahal. Two days in Delhi, between Old Delhi, Humayun's Tomb and the Qutub Minar. Then the fast train to Agra, the Fort, sunset from Mehtab Bagh and the Taj at dawn. From there a flight takes you to Chennai, and the other India begins.

South India is almost another country. People speak different languages, eat off banana leaves, and the temples are stone cities with towers as tall as apartment blocks. The landscape goes from rice paddies to tea hills to lagoons. The trip crosses three states: **Tamil Nadu**, with the Dravidian temples of Mamallapuram, Thanjavur and Madurai and the French interlude of Puducherry; **Kerala**, with the tea plantations of Munnar, the backwaters and Fort Kochi; **Karnataka**, with the ruins of Hampi. You fly home from the South, from Bangalore.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** December to March. Kerala gets the southwest monsoon from June to August, with heavy rain and rough seas; the Tamil Nadu coast (Chennai, Mamallapuram, Puducherry) gets the northeast monsoon between October and December, with a risk of cyclones. Hampi is pleasant from October to February and scorching from April. In Delhi and Agra, December and January bring fog at dawn and often polluted air.
- **Documents:** passport valid for at least 6 months from the date of the visa application and two blank pages, e-Tourist Visa on the official portal indianvisaonline.gov.in, to be requested at least 4 days ahead ($25 for 30 days, $40 for one year, plus a 2.5-3% bank fee; fees unchanged in 2026) and the free online e-Arrival Card within the 72 hours before arrival. Banned at entry: satellite phones, drones without a permit and e-cigarettes.
- **The Taj Mahal is closed on Fridays:** on Fridays the mosque inside the complex holds the midday prayer, reserved for Agra residents, and the monument is closed to visitors. On other days it's open from half an hour before sunrise to half an hour before sunset. At security, food, drones and tripods without a permit aren't allowed in, and there are lines: best to bring the bare minimum.
- **Intercontinental flights:** you fly into Delhi and out of Bangalore, on a "multi-city" ticket.
- **From the airport to the centre:** from Terminal 3 at **Delhi Indira Gandhi (DEL)** the **Airport Express** metro reaches New Delhi in about 20 minutes, about 64 rupees, roughly from 5am to 11:30pm · prepaid taxi from the official desk inside arrivals, about 450-700 rupees, 25% more at night · Uber and Ola from the marked pickup points. At **Chennai (MAA)** the blue metro line takes you to the centre for about 40 rupees, or there's the prepaid taxi. At **Bangalore (BLR)**, for the departure, the **Vayu Vajra** KIA-9 bus from Majestic, running 24 hours, costs about 300 rupees and takes 60-90 minutes; a taxi about 1,000-1,500 rupees. Don't believe anyone who says your hotel is closed or full.
- **Currency and payments:** Indian rupee, roughly 95-105 to the euro. Cards in hotels and good restaurants; cash for houseboats (often for the balance too), tuk-tuks, temples, markets and tips.
- **Temples:** in 52 major Tamil Nadu temples, including the Meenakshi in Madurai, phones were banned from September 1, 2026; since mid-October 2026 you can bring them inside as long as they're switched off, or leave them at the deposit for 5 rupees. Photos, videos and selfies are still banned. The inner sanctums of many temples are reserved for Hindus. In Kerala several temples require men to take off their shirts, and some don't admit non-Hindus.
- **Health:** bottled water, cooked food, mosquito repellent in the backwaters and in Hampi: malaria, dengue and chikungunya are more common in the South and after the monsoons. Hepatitis A and B and typhoid vaccines recommended, plus an up-to-date tetanus shot.
- **Clothing:** shoulders and knees covered in temples and mosques; dhotis and scarves cost a few rupees on the spot. On village beaches, no bikinis outside the tourist areas.
- **Connectivity:** Airtel or Jio SIM on arrival in Delhi; good coverage, weak in some of the Munnar valleys.
- **Electricity:** 230V, type C, D and M sockets. A universal adapter is a good idea.
- **Time zone:** UTC+5:30, 3.5 hours ahead of Italy during daylight saving time, 4.5 in winter.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Delhi and Old Delhi
You almost always land at Indira Gandhi airport at night or early in the morning. To get to the center there's the prepaid taxi from the official counter, Uber or the hotel transfer: *anyone at the exit telling you your hotel is closed or full is just after a commission on another one*. After a few hours of rest, the afternoon is for **Old Delhi**, by metro to Chandni Chowk: the **Jama Masjid**, India's largest mosque, the alleys by cycle rickshaw, the Khari Baoli spice market and the fried parathas of Paranthe Wali Gali. Head back before dark.

The Airport Express metro leaves from Terminal 3 and reaches New Delhi in about twenty minutes; from there the yellow line takes you to Chandni Chowk in a few minutes. At the **Jama Masjid** you can climb one of the minarets with a ticket of about 100 rupees, to see the rooftops of the old city and the Red Fort from above. *The Red Fort is closed on Mondays: if you want to add it, check your arrival day.* To eat safely, pick the busiest stalls, where food turns over quickly, and drink only sealed bottled water.

**In practice:** Airport Express metro to New Delhi in about 20 minutes · Jama Masjid closed to tourists during prayer · cycle rickshaw 300-500 rupees a ride, agree on the price first.

### Day 2 — Delhi: Humayun, Qutub Minar and New Delhi
Early in the morning, **Humayun's Tomb**, the Mughal mausoleum that inspired the Taj Mahal, with the gardens still almost empty. Then the **Qutub Minar**, the twelfth-century sandstone minaret south of the city, with the iron pillar that doesn't rust. In the afternoon, British New Delhi, with India Gate and the Kartavya Path avenue, and **Gurudwara Bangla Sahib**, the Sikh temple where anyone can go in with their head covered and eat for free at the community kitchen.

Humayun's Tomb opens at sunrise, the best moment for the light and the cool; between Humayun and the Qutub Minar it's forty minutes to an hour by road depending on traffic, so follow an order and don't double back. India Gate is lit up in the evening and full of families, and Bangla Sahib is open at all hours, even at night. *On Thursday evenings there's qawwali singing at the Nizamuddin shrine: you enter with your head covered and without shoes, and leave an offering.*

**In practice:** Humayun and Qutub Minar about 600 rupees each, tickets online on the ASI portal · Uber and Ola to get between the sites, which are far apart · the train to Agra leaves early the next day: pack the night before.

### Day 3 — By train to Agra: the Fort and sunset from Mehtab Bagh
The train is the best way to get to Agra: the **Gatimaan Express** or the Vande Bharat trains leave Delhi early in the morning and get there in just under two hours, against three or four by road. Once your bags are at the hotel, head to **Agra Fort**, the red sandstone Mughal citadel from which Shah Jahan, imprisoned by his son, looked out at the Taj in the last years of his life. In the afternoon, **Itimad-ud-Daulah**, the "baby Taj," a mausoleum inlaid with semi-precious stones that's almost always quiet.

At sunset you cross the Yamuna to the **Mehtab Bagh** garden, where you see the Taj Mahal head-on, from the opposite bank, in the pink evening light. *It's a good way to see it for the first time without crowds, and save the real entry for tomorrow's dawn.*

**In practice:** Delhi-Agra train (Gatimaan Express or Vande Bharat) 800-1,600 rupees depending on class · Agra Fort about 650 rupees · Mehtab Bagh is outside the Taj complex, with its own ticket (300 rupees for foreigners) · stay in Taj Ganj, near the east gate · **the Taj Mahal is closed on Fridays**: if Day 4 is a Friday, go into the Taj on the afternoon of Day 3 (it closes half an hour before sunset) and move the Fort to the morning of Day 4, before the train.

### Day 4 — The Taj Mahal at dawn and the flight to Chennai
Be at the **Taj Mahal** gates half an hour before dawn, with your online ticket and no big bags, food, tripods or drones. The first hour is the best: the marble changes color, the pool reflects the dome and the platform is almost empty. After breakfast you head back to Delhi by train, about two hours, and in the afternoon you fly to **Chennai** (check duration and times when you book). From the airport, an hour and a half along the coast on the East Coast Road takes you to **Mamallapuram**, a stone-carvers' town on the sea. If you land late, sleep in Chennai and leave the next morning.

**In practice:** Taj Mahal about 1,100 rupees plus 200 for the mausoleum · Agra-Delhi train about 2 hours · leave at least 3-4 hours between the train's arrival and the flight · transfer booked from Chennai airport · the driver for Tamil Nadu and Kerala can be booked from here to Fort Kochi.

### Day 5 — Mamallapuram: the temple on the sea and the Five Rathas
Mamallapuram was the port of the Pallava kings, and this is where seventh- and eighth-century sculptors invented the temple architecture of the South. Start early with the **Shore Temple**, the eighth-century temple on the beach, battered by wind and salt, and go on to the **Five Rathas**, five chariot-shaped temples each carved from a single block of granite, with life-size stone elephants and lions. In town there's **Arjuna's Penance**, a thirty-meter bas-relief with gods, animals and a river carved into the rock, and "Krishna's butter ball," a boulder balanced on a slope.

The afternoon is for the stone-carvers' workshops, where they still work the stone by hand, and for the fishermen's beach. At sunset, back to the Shore Temple, with the low light on the granite.

**In practice:** a single ASI ticket for foreigners, about 600 rupees, covers the Shore Temple and the Five Rathas · Arjuna's Penance and the town's cave temples are free · no swimming in the sea: the currents on the Coromandel coast are dangerous.

### Day 6 — Puducherry, the French city
Two hours farther south down the coast is **Puducherry**, a French colony until 1954. The White Town, between the seafront and the canal, has a grid of streets with French names, yellow villas with bougainvillea, police officers in red képis and boulangeries; the Tamil quarter, across the canal, has houses with verandas and temples. Visit the **Sri Aurobindo Ashram** in silence, walk the **Promenade** at sunset, when it's closed to cars, and have dinner at one of the Creole restaurants that mix France and Tamil Nadu.

If you like, make a quick trip to **Auroville**, the international community founded in the sixties, half an hour away: the Matrimandir, the big golden sphere, can be seen from a viewpoint after a stop at the visitor center.

**In practice:** Mamallapuram-Puducherry about 2 hours · Auroville: to see the Matrimandir from the inside you need to book at the visitor center days ahead · Puducherry has lower alcohol taxes than Tamil Nadu, and finding a bar is easy.

### Day 7 — Toward Thanjavur and the Brihadeeswarar temple
A road day heading inland, through rice paddies and coconut palms, with two stops that make the trip worth it: the **Nataraja temple in Chidambaram**, dedicated to the dancing Shiva, and the royal temple of **Gangaikonda Cholapuram**, almost always deserted. You reach **Thanjavur** in the afternoon for the **Brihadeeswarar**, the Great Temple of the Chola kings, a UNESCO site built around the year 1000. The tower is about sixty meters high, the dome on top weighs dozens of tons, and the late afternoon light turns all the granite orange.

The order matters because of the hours of Tamil Nadu temples, which usually close in the middle of the day and reopen in the late afternoon. Chidambaram should therefore be seen in the morning, before noon; at Gangaikonda Cholapuram the shrine follows similar hours, but the enclosure and the outer sculptures, including Shiva crowning his devotee, can be seen in the middle hours too. Lunch is on the road at a vegetarian restaurant, with thali served on a banana leaf. *At Brihadeeswarar it's best to arrive after 4 pm, when it reopens, and stay until sunset.*

**In practice:** Puducherry-Thanjavur about 4-5 hours with stops · Brihadeeswarar free entry, shoes at the deposit, phones off or at the deposit if the temple applies the state rule · the Maratha Palace and the Saraswathi Mahal library, in town, are paid.

### Day 8 — From Thanjavur to Madurai and the Meenakshi temple
An early morning in Thanjavur, with the **Maratha Palace** and its gallery of Chola bronzes, one of the finest sculpture collections in India. Then three hours on the road to **Madurai**, one of the oldest inhabited cities in the South, built around the **Meenakshi Amman temple**. In the late afternoon, when it reopens, you visit the temple: the fourteen towers (gopurams) covered in thousands of painted deities, the hall of a thousand pillars, the golden lotus tank. In the evening you stay for the night ceremony, when the statue of Shiva is carried in procession to the goddess's chamber, among drums, flowers and lamps.

*The temple is alive at every hour, and the best thing to do is stand still off to one side and watch.* You go in without shoes and without big bags, with your phone off or left at the deposit. For dinner, **jigarthanda**, a cold drink of milk, jelly, syrup and ice cream, and parottas with curry.

**In practice:** Thanjavur-Madurai about 3 hours · Meenakshi open early in the morning and from late afternoon into the evening, with a break in the middle of the day · free entry, phone off or at the deposit (5 rupees), no photos inside; the inner sanctum is reserved for Hindus · the seventeenth-century Thirumalai Nayak palace can be added if there's time.

### Day 9 — The flower market and the climb to Munnar
At dawn, the **Mattuthavani** flower market, where tons of jasmine arrive, then you set off toward the **Western Ghats**. The road from Madurai to **Munnar**, five to six hours, crosses the border between Tamil Nadu and Kerala and climbs in hairpin bends through cardamom, forests and the first tea plantations, up to 1,600 meters. The air changes, and in the evening you need a sweater. You arrive in the afternoon, with time for a first loop through the hills combed by rows of tea.

After the Kerala border the road climbs through the **Gap Road**, a gorge between green walls with views over the valleys below and several viewpoints to stop at. Munnar town is a busy crossroads of shops and stalls selling tea and spices, useful for buying cardamom and pepper but not for sleeping. In the evening you eat at the hotel or homestay, often home cooking: appam, coconut vegetable stews and tea from the surrounding hills. *In rain or fog the mountain road slows down a lot: better not to travel after dark.*

**In practice:** the flower market is at its best before 8 a.m. · Madurai-Munnar about 5-6 hours by car with driver · stay outside town, which is busy, in a homestay or resort among the plantations · winding mountain road: if you get carsick, set off on a light stomach.

### Day 10 — Munnar: tea plantations and Eravikulam park
Munnar was the British summer retreat, and today it's a sea of tea plantations as far as the eye can see. Early in the morning, **Eravikulam National Park**, home of the Nilgiri tahr, a mountain goat found only here that comes up close without fear. Then Tata's **tea museum** (KDHP), with the factory showing the stages of processing, and a walk or jeep ride through the plantations.

If you want more, get up at 4 for dawn at **Kolukkumalai**, one of the highest plantations in the world, reachable only by jeep on a rough track. *The clouds filling the valleys below the ridges at sunrise are the reason you go up.*

**In practice:** Eravikulam closed from roughly February 1 to March 31 for the tahr birthing season (in 2026 it reopened on April 1), open 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., foreigner ticket around 500 rupees, best booked online · tea museum closed on Mondays · jeep for Kolukkumalai booked through the hotel.

### Day 11 — The backwaters: a night on a houseboat in Alleppey
In four to five hours you come down toward the coast, to **Alappuzha** (Alleppey), the gateway to the **backwaters**: a network of canals, lagoons and lakes running parallel to the sea, among rice paddies below water level and villages on strips of land. At midday you board a **kettuvallam**, a wood and bamboo houseboat with a cabin, bathroom, cook and crew. The afternoon is a slow glide past palms, churches, schools and women washing clothes on the banks. In the evening the boat drops anchor: houseboats aren't allowed to sail at night.

*The backwaters around Alleppey are also congested, with hundreds of boats on the same main canals.* To see the small canals, add a canoe trip the next morning around Kainakary, or choose a houseboat leaving from Kumarakom or less-traveled areas.

**In practice:** boarding around noon, off at 9 the next morning, meals included · price per boat with one bedroom, full board: roughly 8,000-15,000 rupees in the standard range, 15,000-25,000 premium, much more for luxury, with steep increases at Christmas and New Year · book with registered operators and ask whether the boat has air conditioning only at night (it often does).

### Day 12 — From the backwaters to Fort Kochi
Breakfast on the boat, off you get, and an hour and a half on the road to **Fort Kochi**, Kerala's most cosmopolitan city: for centuries a spice port, passed through Portuguese, Dutch and British hands, with Jewish and Christian communities among the oldest in Asia. In the early afternoon you walk along the sea to the **Chinese fishing nets**, big cantilevered structures the fishermen lower into the water by hand, and visit **St. Francis Church**, where Vasco da Gama was buried. In the evening, a **kathakali** show, Kerala's dance-theater, with an hour beforehand to watch the actors put on their makeup.

Fort Kochi can be walked in half a day: besides St Francis there are the **Santa Cruz Basilica**, the old Dutch cemetery and the colonial houses of Princess Street, with cafés and galleries. The Chinese nets are at their best at sunset, when the fishermen work against the light and the fresh catch is sold on the seafront. For the kathakali arrive early: the actors' make-up starts before the show and can be watched up close, while the danced part lasts an hour or a little more. *Mosquitoes in Kochi are many, especially in the evening near the water: always carry repellent.*

**In practice:** Alleppey-Fort Kochi about 1 hour 30 minutes · kathakali at the Kerala Kathakali Centre, about 500-600 rupees, every evening · Fort Kochi is easy to get around on foot and by bike.

### Day 13 — Fort Kochi and Mattancherry
**Mattancherry**, a couple of kilometers away, is the spice merchants' quarter. The **Dutch Palace**, actually built by the Portuguese, has Ramayana murals among the finest in Kerala. Next door is **Jew Town**, the street of antique dealers leading to the sixteenth-century **Paradesi Synagogue**, with its floor of hand-painted Chinese tiles, each one different. Then the ginger and pepper warehouses that scent the whole street.

The afternoon is free: a Keralan cooking class, a coffee in one of the city's art galleries (Fort Kochi hosts the Biennale, between December and March of even-numbered years), a sunset over the sea. For dinner, fish picked at the market near the nets and cooked by the restaurants across the way.

**In practice:** Dutch Palace and synagogue closed on Fridays, the synagogue also on Saturdays and Jewish holidays · modest entrance fees · *in spice and antique shops you always haggle*.

### Day 14 — Flight to Bangalore and overnight train to Hampi
A transfer day toward Karnataka. Flight from Kochi to **Bangalore** (Bengaluru), about an hour, and a free afternoon in the city: **Lalbagh**, the botanical garden, Tipu Sultan's palace, the café district around Church Street. In the evening, the **overnight train to Hosapete** (Hospet), Hampi's station, in a sleeper berth, arriving at dawn.

Bangalore airport is to the north, far from the center, and the traffic is the heaviest of the trip: for the afternoon pick just one or two places close to each other, like Lalbagh and Tipu Sultan's summer palace, which are a few kilometers apart. The train leaves from KSR Bengaluru, Bangalore City station: arrive at least half an hour early to find the platform and the coach, shown on the boards and on the posted lists. *In a sleeper, lock your luggage or keep it under the seat, and keep valuables on you.*

**In practice:** Kochi-Bangalore flight 3,000-6,000 rupees if booked early · Hampi Express (16592), from Bangalore City around 9:50 p.m. arriving at Hosapete around 7 a.m., about 9 hours, every day, in 2A or 3A, to be booked on IRCTC as soon as booking opens · alternatively, a flight to Vidyanagar (Jindal) airport, about an hour from Hampi, with few departures.

### Day 15 — Hampi: the bazaar, the Virupaksha temple and sunset on Matanga
At dawn, from Hosapete, it's twenty minutes by tuk-tuk to **Hampi**. The landscape looks like another planet: hills made of huge rounded granite boulders, balanced one on top of another, among rice paddies, banana groves and the Tungabhadra river. Among these boulders stood **Vijayanagara**, the capital of one of India's greatest empires, destroyed in 1565; today it's a UNESCO site with more than a thousand monuments.

The first day is for the **Sacred Center**: the **Virupaksha temple**, still active, with the temple elephant blessing the faithful, the **Hampi bazaar** with its stone colonnades and **Hemakuta hill**, dotted with temples. Watch the sunset from the top of **Matanga hill**, after a short but steep climb through the rocks, with the whole site below.

**In practice:** Virupaksha free entry, about 50 rupees for a camera · no meat or alcohol in Hampi bazaar · rent a scooter or bicycle to get around (international license), or hire a tuk-tuk for the day.

### Day 16 — Hampi: the Royal Center, the Vittala temple and the overnight train
The **Royal Center** keeps the traces of the court: the **Zenana enclosure** with the Lotus Mahal, the domed **elephant stables**, the stepped tank, the Mahanavami platform. Then the **Vittala temple**, Hampi's most famous, with the **stone chariot** that appears on the 50-rupee note and the "musical" pillars that used to ring when struck. *Go early or late in the afternoon: at midday the granite gives off a heat that's hard to bear.*

In the afternoon, a ride in a **coracle**, the round wicker boats, on the Tungabhadra. In the evening, the overnight train from Hosapete to **Bangalore**, the Hampi Express (16591) at around 9:10 p.m.

**In practice:** the Vittala ticket for foreigners (about 600 rupees) is also valid the same day for the Zenana enclosure and the elephant stables · coracle 300-500 rupees per person · the river crossing to Virupapur Gaddi depends on the water level.

### Day 17 — Bangalore and departure
The train gets into Bangalore in the morning. With a few hours before the flight, have breakfast with a masala dosa and a filter coffee, and stop at Lalbagh if there wasn't time on Day 14. Kempegowda airport is north of the city, an hour or more by road depending on traffic. Many flights to Europe leave in the evening or at night, with one stopover.

Bangalore is the city of the South Indian breakfast: **masala dosa** with coconut chutney and sambar, and filter coffee served in a metal tumbler with its saucer. There are historic places like **MTR**, open since the 1920s near Lalbagh, where people queue in the morning. For the airport there are the Vayu Vajra buses, cheap and with luggage space, or taxis and Uber, which from the city at rush hour can take much more than an hour. *If you still have rupees, change or spend them before security: outside India they're not easy to exchange.*

**In practice:** Bangalore City to the airport one to one and a half hours by taxi or airport bus · get to the airport 3 hours before international flights · the overnight train is sometimes late: no flight in the early morning.

## Where to stay
- **Delhi:** Karol Bagh or Connaught Place in the mid-range, South Delhi for quiet. Near a metro station, to get to the train station easily on Day 3.
- **Agra:** Taj Ganj and Fatehabad Road, walking distance from the Taj's east gate.
- **Mamallapuram:** guesthouses near the fishermen's beach in the budget range; resorts on the East Coast Road in the mid and high range.
- **Puducherry:** in the **White Town**, in colonial villas turned into heritage hotels, from 4,000 rupees up; cheaper guesthouses run by the Ashram and in the Tamil quarter.
- **Thanjavur and Madurai:** mid-range city hotels near the temple; in Madurai, if you want to spend more, there are heritage hotels in the surrounding hills.
- **Munnar:** homestays and resorts **among the plantations**, outside town, from 2,500 rupees for a homestay to serious money for resorts with a view.
- **Backwaters:** one night on a **houseboat** (see Day 11), or a homestay on the canals in Kainakary or Kumarakom if you'd rather sleep on land.
- **Fort Kochi:** family-run guesthouses and homestays in the budget range; colonial bungalows and heritage hotels in the mid and high range.
- **Hampi:** Hampi bazaar has simple guesthouses; across the river, in Virupapur Gaddi and Hanumanahalli, there are resorts among rice paddies and boulders. At the high end, resorts near Kamalapura or Hosapete.

## Where to eat
- **Delhi and Agra:** parathas in Paranthe Wali Gali, kebabs and nihari around the Jama Masjid, chaat in Chandni Chowk, Punjabi and Mughlai cooking, the langar at Gurudwara Bangla Sahib, petha in Agra.
- **Tamil Nadu:** the banana-leaf meal ("meals") with rice, sambar, rasam, vegetable curries and yogurt, served unlimited; dosa, idli and vada for breakfast with coconut chutney; filter coffee in a metal cup; the spicy Chettinad cooking with chicken and mutton; in Madurai, jigarthanda and parottas with curry.
- **Puducherry:** French-Tamil Creole cooking, croissants and baguettes in the White Town's boulangeries.
- **Kerala:** appam (fermented rice crepes) with vegetable or chicken stew, karimeen (pearl spot) fish grilled in a banana leaf, fish moilee in coconut milk, parottas with fried beef (in Kerala, people eat beef), the sadya, the big vegetarian festive lunch.
- **Karnataka:** a Bangalore breakfast of masala dosa and filter coffee; in Hampi, simple thalis and coffee on guesthouse terraces, with no meat or alcohol in the bazaar.
- **Golden rules:** bottled water, no raw salads in the first few days, ask for less spice. You eat with your right hand.

## Getting around
- **Delhi-Agra train and back:** Gatimaan Express or Vande Bharat, just under two hours, 800-1,600 rupees depending on class. On the way back, on Day 4, take a morning train to keep margin before the flight.
- **Domestic flights:** Delhi-Chennai and Kochi-Bangalore with IndiGo and Air India: prices and times vary a lot, book early and compare.
- **Car with driver in Tamil Nadu and Kerala:** the most comfortable choice for the Chennai-Madurai-Munnar-Alleppey-Kochi stretch, with lots of stops in between and few convenient trains. Roughly 3,000-4,500 rupees a day; tolls, parking, interstate permits (crossing from Tamil Nadu into Kerala carries a tax for tourist vehicles) and the driver's meals to be clarified in the contract. Better a registered agency with recent reviews, a written contract and no mandatory shop stops.
- **Overnight trains for Hampi:** the Hampi Express (16592 outbound, 16591 on the way back, about 9 hours, 2A or 3A, every day). Book on irctc.co.in with a foreigner account; bookings open 60 days ahead. A wait-listed (WL) ticket doesn't get you a berth.
- **In Hampi:** rent a scooter or bicycle, or hire a tuk-tuk for the day (1,200-1,800 rupees). The distances between the groups of monuments are a few kilometers.
- **In the cities:** metro, Uber and Ola in Delhi, Chennai, Bangalore and Kochi; tuk-tuks everywhere, with the price agreed first.

## When to go, month by month
- **October-November:** northeast monsoon on the Tamil Nadu coast, with heavy rain and a risk of cyclones; Kerala is green and generally dry from mid-October. In Delhi the smog begins.
- **December:** high season starts in the South. High prices between Christmas and New Year, houseboats to be booked early. Fog at dawn in Delhi and Agra.
- **January:** the best month along with February. Pongal, the Tamil Nadu harvest festival, around January 14-17. Fog in Agra can hide the Taj at dawn and delay trains.
- **February:** ideal weather, but in Munnar Eravikulam park closes for the tahr birthing season (from February 1, reopening April 1).
- **March:** rising heat in Madurai and Hampi (35 °C and more), still good in Kerala and Munnar. Holi in the North (March 22 in 2027).
- **April-May:** heavy heat on the plains (Hampi tops 40 °C, and so do Delhi and Agra), Munnar stays cool. Pre-monsoon in Kerala.
- **June-September:** southwest monsoon in Kerala, with intense rain and the sea closed; very green landscapes and low prices if you're fine with rain. Onam, Kerala's big festival, falls between August and September.

## Common scams and safety
- **Delhi and Agra:** taxi and tuk-tuk drivers saying your hotel is closed, fake tourist offices near New Delhi station, people at the station saying your train is canceled, fake guides at the Taj gates. Call the hotel, check your ticket on the app, keep walking.
- **Houseboats:** touts on the Alleppey jetties offer boats at low prices that turn out to be different from the photos, or add extras for air conditioning and meals. Book with registered operators with recent reviews, with a contract stating what's included.
- **The Meenakshi terraces:** the shopkeepers who invite you up "for the view" are friendly, and then you have to sit through a textile pitch. You can say thank you and go back down.
- **Spices and antiques in Kochi:** prices to haggle over, "antiques" often new, saffron sometimes fake.
- **Tuk-tuks and drivers:** price agreed beforehand; "free" rides and courtesy stops end up in shops that pay commission.
- **The sea:** the Tamil Nadu and Kerala coasts have strong currents and beaches without lifeguards; only swim where it's supervised.
- **Women traveling:** the South is calmer than the North, but the same rules apply: covered clothing, booked transfers for night arrivals, the upper berth on the train, firmness with anyone who insists. In Delhi the metro has a women-only car.
- **The road:** a driver is the right choice; the road to Munnar has hairpin bends and trucks, better driven by day.

## Variations
- **Without Hampi, 14 days:** after Fort Kochi (Day 13) fly home straight from Kochi, which has international flights with a stopover in the Gulf. You save two nights on the train and a flight.
- **With a slower Madurai (+1 day):** a second night in Madurai for the Thirumalai Nayak palace, the tailors near the temple and the evening street food.
- **With Periyar (+1 day):** between Madurai and Munnar, Periyar park in Thekkady offers guided forest treks, boat rides on the lake and spice plantations. Tigers are rarely seen; elephants and gaur more often.
- **With the sea (+2-3 days):** the cliffs of **Varkala** or **Marari** beach, near Alleppey, to finish Kerala with a few days of rest.
- **With Mysore and Coorg (+3 days):** from Bangalore, Mysore palace and the coffee plantations of Coorg, instead of Hampi or on top of it.
- **Without the North:** if you've already seen Delhi and Agra, fly straight into Chennai and gain three days for the South.
- **With Rajasthan:** if you want the whole North, Varanasi and Rajasthan, the trip Rick actually took, is in *India: Varanasi, Ranthambore and Rajasthan in 19 Days, by Overnight Train and with a Driver*.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Check for Fridays:** the Taj Mahal is closed on Fridays (if Day 4 is a Friday, move the Taj to the afternoon of Day 3), and so are Kochi's Dutch Palace and synagogue; the synagogue on Saturdays too. The Munnar tea museum closes on Mondays, Eravikulam from February 1 to March 31.
- **Keep margin between train and flight on Day 4:** in December-January fog between Agra and Delhi delays trains.
- **Book the houseboat ahead** for December-January, and choose a boat with just one bedroom for peace and quiet.
- **Book the trains as soon as booking opens**, 60 days ahead, and check your ticket status right up to the end.
- **Keep your phone switched off or leave it at the deposit** in the big Tamil Nadu temples: photos and videos inside are banned and the rule is enforced.
- **Visit Hampi at dawn and sunset**, not at midday.
- **Pack for three climates:** cold mornings in Delhi and Agra in winter, humid heat on the coast, cool in Munnar.
- **Don't swim where there are no lifeguards.**
- **Repellent in the backwaters**, especially at sunset.

## Chiusura personale
This trip holds two Indias together. The first is the North: the noise of Delhi, the marble of the Taj at dawn, the crowds. The second is the South: the green of the rice paddies and the tea hills, the blue of the canals, the orange of the temples at sunset, the white of the jasmine at the Madurai market, and at the end the granite and silence of Hampi. If you have seventeen days and don't want to choose between the Taj and the South, this is a way to have both.
