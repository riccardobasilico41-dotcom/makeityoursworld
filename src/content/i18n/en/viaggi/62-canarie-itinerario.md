# Canary Islands in 9 Days: Tenerife, Gran Canaria and Lanzarote

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (trip taken by Rick; itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 9 days, 8 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, domestic flights between the islands, rental car on each island
**Categoria:** Europa · Spagna · Canarie · vulcani · natura · isole

---

## Apertura personale
The Canaries are Spanish by flag and African by geography: off the coast of Morocco, closer to Casablanca than to Madrid, with a subtropical climate that almost never changes (16-21°C even in January). Among the Spanish destinations on the site, it is the only one designed for the European cold season, when elsewhere people are running from the cold and here you are simply comfortable all year round.

Seven islands in all, of which this itinerary covers three (Tenerife, Gran Canaria and Lanzarote), chosen for a reason: three islands well covered are worth more than seven seen in a rush. Tenerife has the Teide, the highest point in Spain; Gran Canaria packs almost Saharan dunes and mountains into a small space; Lanzarote is the most volcanic island of all, and the one where a single artist, César Manrique, shaped the entire urban planning.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** year-round. The climate stays mild and stable, without the big seasonal swings of the rest of Europe: that is the practical reason the Canaries work so well in winter, when the rest of Spain (and of Italy) is cold.
- **Time zone:** note that the **Canaries are one hour behind** mainland Spain and Italy (UTC+0 versus UTC+1), all year. Keep this in mind for flights and connections.
- **Teide, summit permit:** the cable car (about €40 round trip) goes up to 3,555 meters, but the last stretch on foot to the true summit crater (3,715 m) requires a **limited-entry permit** (200 people a day; since 2026 paid for non-residents, about 15 euros), bookable up to **56 days ahead**. Request it well in advance, months ahead in peak periods. See the dedicated experience page.
- **Timanfaya:** the National Park can only be entered on the Ruta de los Volcanes by **guided bus**. You can't get off or walk freely on the lava, both for safety (the ground stays hot a few meters below the surface) and for environmental protection.
- **Domestic flights between the islands:** Binter Canarias and Canaryfly cover the routes between the islands in 30-45 minutes. The alternative is the ferry, slower (1h30-2h30) but useful if you travel with a rental car on a single island.
- **Rental car:** recommended on each island to reach national parks and rural areas; you return it and rent again at each island change, except for specific multi-island packages.
- **Electricity and currency:** euro, type C/F sockets identical to Italian ones.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Tenerife: Puerto de la Cruz and the north coast
We landed in Tenerife (South or North airport depending on the flight) and moved to the north coast. The car is picked up at the airport: from the North airport, near La Laguna, Puerto de la Cruz is about half an hour by motorway; from the South, where most flights from Italy arrive, it takes about an hour and a quarter. *Times must be read in Canary Islands time, an hour behind Italy, including for the car pick-up.* The north coast is greener and more often cloudy than the south, because the trade winds stop against the mountains: that is why gardens grow here.

We spent the afternoon in **Puerto de la Cruz**, with its botanical gardens and colonial old town, greener and less developed for tourism than the island's south coast. The **Jardín Botánico**, founded in the eighteenth century to acclimatize tropical plants before taking them to Spain, takes an hour to visit. Then the center: the Plaza del Charco, the small fishing harbor, the houses with wooden balconies. On the sea are the **Lago Martiánez** pools, designed by César Manrique, and the black sand beach of Playa Jardín. In the evening, papas arrugadas with mojo.

**In practice:** North airport-Puerto de la Cruz about 30 minutes, South airport about an hour and a quarter · Jardín Botánico about 3 euros · car rental to be booked ahead, especially in the winter high season · for tomorrow: cable car ticket and summit permit already booked.

### Day 2 — Teide National Park
The day was entirely for the **Teide**. The cable car climbs from the base station (2,356 m) to the upper station (3,555 m) in about 8 minutes; those who booked the permit can walk the last stretch to the true summit crater (3,715 m), as the dedicated experience page explains. From Puerto de la Cruz you drive up via La Orotava in about an hour; it pays to leave early, because the cable car car park fills up and the first rides are the quietest. At the top you walk slowly: the air is thin and you run out of breath quickly. *In strong wind the cable car stops even without warning, so it's better to keep a spare day free.*

Around it is the lunar landscape of **Las Cañadas**, the big caldera surrounding the volcano. Back down from the cable car, the finest walk is the **Roques de García** loop, about an hour and a half among the lava spires next to the Parador, with the Teide always in front of you. Along the road there are viewpoints and lay-bys; lava flows of different colors tell of eruptions from different ages. In the evening, weather permitting, it's time for the stars: Tenerife is one of the best places in the world for stargazing. Above two thousand meters it is cold at night even in summer.

**In practice:** Puerto de la Cruz-cable car about an hour by car · cable car about 42 euros return for non-residents, timed ticket on the official website, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. in winter · summit permit on Tenerife ON, since 2026 about 15 euros for non-residents, separate from the cable car ticket · layers, sunscreen and water.

### Day 3 — Anaga Rural Park
We moved to the far north of the island, to **Anaga Rural Park**, a UNESCO biosphere reserve since 2015: a laurel forest that is a relic of the Tertiary era, covered almost all year by the trade-wind mist. From La Laguna you drive up the winding TF-12 to the **Cruz del Carmen** visitor center, free, where staff give information on trail conditions. The **Sendero de los Sentidos** also starts there, a set of short, easy routes through the forest. *The mist soaks you even when it doesn't rain: you need a jacket and shoes with a sole that grips.* The El Pijaral forest can only be visited with a free permit requested in advance.

We walked the trails (for example from Cruz del Carmen to Taganana) and visited **La Laguna**, the island's former capital, a UNESCO site for its Renaissance town plan. A one-way trail means organizing the return, by taxi or with a car left at the end. La Laguna is a university town, with pedestrian streets on a grid, colonial houses with patios and the tower of the Concepción church. Its layout served as a model for many towns later founded in the Americas. It can be explored on foot in an afternoon, and the evenings are lively thanks to the students.

**In practice:** La Laguna-Cruz del Carmen about 20 minutes by car · visitor center free · El Pijaral only with a free permit booked in advance · tram between Santa Cruz and La Laguna in about 40 minutes · anyone flying early tomorrow can sleep in La Laguna, near the North airport.

### Day 4 — From Tenerife to Gran Canaria
We took the domestic flight to **Gran Canaria** (about 30-40 minutes). It's best to return the car in Tenerife and pick up another on arrival, because rental is done island by island. From Gran Canaria airport to central Las Palmas it's about 25 minutes by car. *Inter-island flights are frequent, but services drop in low season: book them together with the rest.* Hand luggage on inter-island flights has its own rules, to be checked on the airline's website before packing.

The afternoon in **Las Palmas de Gran Canaria**, the capital, between the old town of Vegueta and the urban beach of Las Canteras. **Vegueta** is the founding district, from the late fifteenth century: Santa Ana cathedral, with its panoramic terrace, the **Casa de Colón**, linked to Columbus's stop before the 1492 crossing, and the covered market. Next to it is Triana, the pedestrian shopping area. **Las Canteras** is on the other side of the city, three kilometers of sand protected by a natural rock reef that keeps the water calm. At sunset you walk along the promenade as far as the Alfredo Kraus auditorium.

**In practice:** Tenerife-Gran Canaria flight about 30-40 minutes with Binter or Canaryfly · airport-Las Palmas about 25 minutes · Vegueta and Triana on foot, Las Canteras by city bus or taxi · Las Palmas is often covered by low cloud even when the south is sunny.

### Day 5 — The dunes of Maspalomas
We went down to the south coast, to the **Maspalomas Dunes**, a nature reserve of almost Saharan shifting sand. From Las Palmas it's about fifty minutes by motorway. The reserve covers about 400 hectares between Playa del Inglés and the Maspalomas lighthouse, with the Charca lagoon at one end. You walk only on the marked trails: the dunes are fragile, leaving the paths is forbidden and can mean a fine, and there are patrols at sunset. The morning is the time for the beach, the lighthouse and the promenade; *at midday the sand is scorching and the glare is strong, so you need a hat and water.*

Among the dunes it's best to walk at sunset, when the heat drops. Then a stop at **Puerto de Mogán**, the harbor village with navigable canals. On long days you can manage Mogán in the early afternoon and return to the dunes for sunset; in winter it's better to swap, with Mogán in the morning. Puerto de Mogán is half an hour to the west, with low white houses trimmed in color, bougainvillea on the little bridges and the fishing harbor. On Fridays there is a very busy market, and parking becomes difficult.

**In practice:** Las Palmas-Maspalomas about 50 minutes, Maspalomas-Puerto de Mogán about 30 · dunes freely accessible, only on the marked trails · Mogán market on Fridays · for sleeping, Las Palmas or the Maspalomas area, as in the dedicated section.

### Day 6 — The mountainous center of Gran Canaria: Roque Nublo
We climbed toward the center of the island, to **Roque Nublo**, the volcanic rock formation that is the symbol of Gran Canaria: on clear days the view reaches as far as the Teide on Tenerife. The mountain roads are narrow and slow, and driving up from Las Palmas or the south takes at least an hour. Since 2026 access to the trail has been regulated: between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m. a free online booking is required, and the old car park at the Degollada de la Goleta is closed, so you arrive by shuttle from the car parks indicated by the Cabildo. *The rules are new and keep changing: check them again a few days before.* It pays to go up early, before the clouds arrive.

Nearby is **Tejeda**, the white village below the rocks, good for a stop and for almond sweets. We also stopped at the **Barranco de Guayadeque**, the canyon inhabited since pre-Hispanic times, with cave houses still lived in. The barranco is on the east side, between Agüimes and Ingenio, about an hour's drive from the center of the island. At the entrance there is an interpretation center on the ancient inhabitants; at the end of the road there are restaurants carved into the rock, serving grilled meat and island cheeses. The drive back to Las Palmas takes about forty minutes.

**In practice:** Roque Nublo with free online booking between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m. and shuttle access, rules introduced in 2026 to be checked beforehand · hiking shoes: the last stretch is steep · Guayadeque about an hour from Tejeda · on mountain roads allow more time than the satnav says.

### Day 7 — From Gran Canaria to Lanzarote
For **Lanzarote** there is the domestic flight or the ferry. The flight from Gran Canaria takes about three quarters of an hour; the ferry from Las Palmas to Arrecife takes about six and a half hours, so it eats the whole day and only makes sense for those traveling with their own car. Lanzarote airport is a few minutes from Arrecife, and the car is picked up there. From the window you see the difference at once: no forests, few plants, low white buildings, as Manrique wanted. *Here too the rental car must be booked ahead, because in high season they run out.*

We spent the afternoon in Arrecife, with a first stop in the wine area of **La Geria**, where the vines grow in hollows dug into the volcanic ash. In Arrecife you walk around the **Charco de San Ginés**, the lagoon with the fishing boats, and out to the San Gabriel castle on the sea; the San José castle houses the contemporary art museum that Manrique wanted. La Geria is about half an hour away: each vine sits in a hollow protected by a low wall of lava stone, and the wineries along the road sell malvasía volcánica.

**In practice:** Gran Canaria-Lanzarote flight about 40-50 minutes, Las Palmas-Arrecife ferry about six and a half hours · airport-Arrecife a few minutes · Arrecife-La Geria about 30 minutes · for sleeping, Arrecife or Puerto del Carmen and Playa Blanca, as in the dedicated section.

### Day 8 — Timanfaya and La Geria
In the morning we went to **Timanfaya National Park**: the Ruta de los Volcanes by guided bus among the lava flows of 1730-1736, with the geothermal demonstrations at the Islote del Hilario (see the dedicated experience page). The Montañas del Fuego entrance is near Yaiza, about half an hour from Arrecife. It pays to be at the gate at opening time, around 9 a.m.: later a long queue of cars builds up on the access road. At the Islote del Hilario you see water shooting back up as steam and straw catching fire in the ground; the El Diablo restaurant, designed by Manrique, cooks meat over the volcano's heat. *The bus tour lasts about three quarters of an hour, and you don't get off.*

In the afternoon we did a wine tasting in La Geria, among the vines protected by semicircular walls. The best-known winery is El Grifo, one of the oldest in the Canaries, with a small wine museum; along the road there are others, small and large. Whoever drives doesn't taste, as always on wine days. On the way back you can pass by **El Golfo**, with the green lagoon at the foot of the crater, and the Janubio salt pans, good for sunset on the west coast.

**In practice:** Montañas del Fuego about 30 euros after recent price rises, online ticket recommended in high season · Ruta de los Volcanes bus included in the ticket · Mancha Blanca visitor center free · El Golfo about 20 minutes from Timanfaya.

### Day 9 — The works of César Manrique and end of the trip
The last day was for the works of the artist-architect who gave the island its visual identity: the **Jameos del Agua**, a lava tube turned into an arts center, the **Cueva de los Verdes** and the **Mirador del Río**, with a view of the islet of La Graciosa. All three are in the north, about 30-40 minutes from Arrecife. It's best to start with the **Cueva de los Verdes**, which can only be visited with a guide, in groups and at set times, for about a kilometer of tunnel; then the Jameos, a few minutes away, with the underground lake of tiny blind white crabs and the auditorium carved into the rock. *In the cave there is a final surprise the guides ask you not to reveal: it's worth getting there without knowing it.*

The **Mirador del Río** sits on top of the Famara cliff, at about 475 meters, with the bar behind its large windows and La Graciosa below, sheer above the sea. Anyone with time left before the flight can add the Jardín de Cactus in Guatiza or the Fundación César Manrique in Tahíche, the house built inside five lava bubbles. Then the flight home from Lanzarote airport. *Allow at least half an hour of margin to return the car.*

**In practice:** Jameos del Agua and Cueva de los Verdes about 17 euros each, Mirador del Río about 9 · the combined ticket for the island's Tourist Centers pays off from four centers upward · Cueva de los Verdes only with a guided tour · Arrecife-airport about 10 minutes.

## What to see / what to do
- **The Teide**, the highest point in Spain, by cable car and (with a permit) all the way to the true summit crater.
- **Anaga Rural Park**, the laurel forest that is a relic of the Tertiary era.
- **The Maspalomas Dunes**, almost Saharan, on the south coast of Gran Canaria.
- **Roque Nublo**, the rock formation that is the symbol of Gran Canaria.
- **Timanfaya National Park**, the most intact volcanic landscape in the Canaries.
- **The works of César Manrique**, a single statement on the relationship between architecture and volcanic landscape.

## Possible extensions
- **Fuerteventura (+3-4 days):** the island of the longest and whitest beaches in the archipelago, for those who want to add pure sea to the tour of volcanoes and mountains.
- **La Palma (+3-4 days):** the "isla bonita", with the Caldera de Taburiente National Park and one of the best-protected skies in the world for astronomical observation.
- **La Graciosa (+1 day):** the car-free islet north of Lanzarote, reachable by ferry in a day.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
In Tenerife, **Puerto de la Cruz** for the greener, more authentic north coast or **Costa Adeje/Los Cristianos** for the sunnier south coast. In Gran Canaria, **Las Palmas** for city life or **Maspalomas** for those focused mainly on dunes and sea. In Lanzarote, **Arrecife** for the connections or **Playa Blanca/Puerto del Carmen** for the sea, all with low buildings thanks to the urban planning rules Manrique pushed for.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
**Papas arrugadas** with mojo rojo and mojo verde are the signature dish of all three islands; **gofio**, toasted cereal flour of Guanche origin, shows up in a thousand variations. In Lanzarote the wines of La Geria, in particular the malvasía volcánico, are an experience in themselves, grown in a landscape like no other.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book the Teide summit permit well ahead**: 200 people a day, up to 56 days ahead, best to request it as soon as you set the date of your trip.
- **Don't get off the bus at Timanfaya**: it is forbidden, for safety and environmental protection.
- **Dress in layers for the Teide**: it is much colder at the top than in the valley, in every season.
- **Remember the Canaries' time zone**, one hour behind the rest of Spain, so you don't miss domestic flights or connections.
- **Don't stop at the tourist coast**: in both Tenerife (Anaga) and Gran Canaria (the mountainous center) the inland areas are something else entirely compared with the southern beaches.
- **Check domestic flight schedules**: frequencies drop in the low-season months.

## Chiusura personale
What stays with me is how well the three chosen islands complete each other: Tenerife for Spain's highest volcano and the primeval forest of Anaga a few km away, Gran Canaria for the contrast between almost Saharan dunes and mountains, Lanzarote for a lunar landscape that a single artist managed to turn into a coherent statement about living on a volcano.
