# Côte d'Azur: 8 Days from Antibes, with the Mercantour

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 8 days, 7 nights, fixed base in Antibes
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, without a car for the coast, with a car for the Mercantour
**Categoria:** Europa · Francia · Costa Azzurra · mare · arte · montagna

---

## Apertura personale
Two years lived in Antibes teach one thing that no guidebook writes clearly enough: the Côte d'Azur has two seasons, and they are the reverse of what you imagine. From June to September it's a difficult place: lines on the coast road, impossible parking, packed beaches. From October to May it's one of the most pleasant places in Europe: sun, empty trails, markets that go back to being markets. This itinerary uses **Antibes as a fixed base** (the best compromise between price, location and livability) and lines up museums, trails, villages and, for those with time, two days of high mountains an hour and a half from the sea.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **May-June and mid-September-October.** July and August are the worst time: constant traffic, peak prices, saturated beaches. **Winter is the best-kept secret**: mild, empty, with mimosa in February and skiing an hour and a half away.
- **Base:** **Antibes.** Old town inside the ramparts, a real market, sandy beaches and a train to everything else in a quarter of an hour.
- **Transport:** almost always the right answer is **the regional train along the coast**: frequent, cheap, skips the traffic. The car is only needed for the hinterland and the Mercantour, and everywhere else it's a burden: **parking is expensive, scarce and in summer a battle**.
- **Crit'Air sticker:** if you drive toward Marseille or other big cities, it must be ordered online **weeks ahead**.
- **Beaches:** most of the coast, Nice included, is **pebbles**. With rubber water shoes the day changes.
- **Food:** socca, pissaladière, pan bagnat, salade niçoise without potatoes or green beans. The midday menu costs half as much as the evening one.
- **From the airport to the centre:** **Nice Côte d'Azur (NCE)**. · **Tram line 2** to central Nice and the port: about €1.70 a ride plus €2 for the rechargeable card (there is also an airport return ticket for about €10), at the machines or in the Lignes d'Azur app, about 30 minutes, until late evening · for the **Antibes** base: from **Nice Aéroport** station, reachable by tram, the **regional train** takes about 15 minutes and costs about €5 · **taxi** at the official 2026 flat rates: €32 to central Nice, €72 to Cap d'Antibes and Juan-les-Pins · take only taxis from the rank, with roof sign and number; Uber works.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Antibes: the old town and the port
We arrived and settled in Antibes. From Nice airport you get here in about twenty minutes by regional train or bus, with no need for a car. The first walk was in the old town, inside the ramparts: the **Marché Provençal** on the Cours Masséna if it's still morning, otherwise the walk along the ramparts toward **Port Vauban**, the largest marina in the Mediterranean by tonnage, with the pier of enormous yachts. The covered market closes around lunchtime and, out of season, on Mondays; across the port you see the **Fort Carré**, the sixteenth-century star fort on the point.

In the afternoon we went to the **Musée Picasso**, in the Château Grimaldi: the works Picasso made there in the autumn of 1946 hang in the same building where they were created, and there is the terrace over the sea. Among them is *La Joie de vivre*, painted in those very weeks; on the terrace there are outdoor sculptures with the sea behind them. *Out of season the museum closes for lunch: better to arrive calmly after 2 p.m.* Before dinner there is time for the small Gravette beach, inside the walls. We spent the evening in the old town, with the first socca of the week, the chickpea-flour pancake baked in a wood oven.

**In practice:** Nice airport-Antibes about 20 minutes by regional train or bus · Marché Provençal in the morning, closed on Mondays out of season · Musée Picasso closed on Mondays, about 12 euros, open without a lunch break only in summer · Antibes can be explored entirely on foot.

### Day 2 — The Cap d'Antibes on foot
We gave the day to the Cap. In the morning we were on the **coastal path**, which goes around the point over the rocks, between the sea and the walls of private estates: it's free, it takes an hour and a half and it's the best thing on the coast. You start from the **plage de la Garoupe**, reached by Envibus city bus or taxi from the center, and follow the coast south-west over steps, walkways and smooth rocks, as far as the Villa Eilenroc area. Early in the morning the light is low and there are few people. *With rough seas or after heavy rain some stretches become dangerous and may be closed: on those days, give it up.*

The afternoon can be spent on the Garoupe beach or climbing up to the Garoupe lighthouse, for the view from above over the cape and the coast. On the lighthouse plateau there is also the chapel of **Notre-Dame-de-la-Garoupe**, with the sailors' ex-votos. On clear winter days you can see the snowy Alps behind the bay from up there. The climb is short but steep, on foot from the beach side. You return to Antibes by bus or on foot along the east side of the cape, toward the old town, with the sunset over the ramparts.

**In practice:** free path, about 1h30, shoes with a sole that grips, no shade and no water along the way · plage de la Garoupe is sandy, partly with private beach clubs · Envibus city buses from central Antibes, with few services on Sundays: check the timetable.

### Day 3 — Nice
We took the train to Nice (a quarter of an hour). We spent the morning in **Vieux Nice** and at the **Cours Saleya**, then climbed the **Colline du Château** for the view over the Baie des Anges. In the morning the Cours Saleya hosts the flower and produce market, except on Mondays, when there are antiques; around it are Sainte-Réparate cathedral and the narrow lanes of the baroque town. You climb the Colline by the stairways or with the free lift, and from the top you see the port on one side and the bay on the other. *Socca in Vieux Nice is best in mid-morning, straight out of the oven.*

In the afternoon we went up to **Cimiez**, to the **Matisse** and **Chagall** museums, and at sunset we walked along the **Promenade des Anglais**. Cimiez is the residential hill above the city, reached by city bus from the center. The Musée Matisse is in a red Genoese villa among the olive trees, next to the Roman remains and the Franciscan monastery where Matisse is buried; the Musée Chagall, lower down, holds the Biblical Message cycle. The Promenade runs for seven kilometers along the sea. In the evening we went back by train.

**In practice:** Antibes-Nice Ville by train about 15-25 minutes, frequent services · Matisse and Chagall museums closed on Tuesdays, about 10-12 euros each · Cours Saleya: flowers and produce in the morning, antiques on Mondays · free lift up the Colline du Château.

### Day 4 — Saint-Paul-de-Vence and Vence
A day in the nearby hinterland. You reach **Saint-Paul-de-Vence** by bus or by train plus bus; we spent the morning at the **Fondation Maeght**, with Giacometti's courtyard and Miró's labyrinth. The simplest way is the train to Cagnes-sur-Mer and then bus 655, which climbs to Saint-Paul and carries on to Vence: the old direct line from Nice no longer exists, so timetables need checking beforehand. The Fondation, opened in 1964 in a building by Josep Lluís Sert among the pines, is about ten minutes' uphill walk from the village; besides Giacometti and Miró there are works by Braque, Chagall and Calder.

After lunch we went down into the village, which at midday is saturated with coaches: it's better to explore it late in the morning or mid-afternoon. Saint-Paul is a fortified village, with intact walls and art galleries in every lane; in the small cemetery at the end of the village is Chagall's grave. In the early afternoon we went to nearby **Vence**, for Matisse's **Chapelle du Rosaire**. **Opening hours are very limited: check them beforehand.** Matisse designed all of it, from the yellow, green and blue windows to the vestments, between 1949 and 1951. Then a walk in the old town of Vence, before the bus back.

**In practice:** train Antibes-Cagnes-sur-Mer a few minutes, then bus 655 with a separate ticket · Fondation Maeght with tickets also online · Chapelle du Rosaire about 7 euros, closed on Sundays, reduced and variable hours to be checked on the chapel's website · check the last bus to Cagnes before going up to Vence.

### Day 5 — Èze, La Turbie and Monaco
We got off the train at **Èze-sur-Mer** and walked up to the village along the **Nietzsche Path** (an hour to an hour and a half). From Antibes the train takes about forty minutes. The path is named after the philosopher, who walked it in the years he was writing *Zarathustra*: about four hundred meters of ascent, all uphill, with very little shade. *In summer do it at dawn or skip it; anyone who doesn't feel up to it takes bus 83 from the station and walks down instead.* At the top we visited the **Jardin Exotique**, with the view over the coast: cacti and succulents on the ruins of the castle, more than four hundred meters above the sea.

By bus we reached **La Turbie**, home to the Roman Trophy of Augustus and the view from above over Monaco. The trophy was raised in 6 BC to celebrate the subjugation of the Alpine peoples, and was partly rebuilt in the twentieth century. Then we went down to **Monaco**: the casino district, the port, the prince's palace. On the Rocher, besides the palace, there is the cathedral with Grace Kelly's grave; the changing of the guard is every day at 11:55, so only those arriving in the morning will see it. In the evening we went back by train.

**In practice:** train Antibes-Èze-sur-Mer about 40 minutes · Nietzsche Path about 400 meters of ascent, hiking shoes and water · Jardin Exotique about 10 euros · Trophy of Augustus closed on Mondays, about 7 euros · buses between Èze, La Turbie and Monaco have few services, check timetables first · Monaco-Antibes by train about an hour.

### Day 6 — Cannes and the Lérins Islands
Train to Cannes. In the morning we were on the Croisette and at **Le Suquet**, the hill of the old town, with the Forville market. From Antibes the regional train takes ten to fifteen minutes. The covered **Forville market** is open in the morning from Tuesday to Sunday; on Mondays it makes way for the flea market. Le Suquet is a few minutes' climb on foot, up to the church and the old castle that houses a museum, with the tower from which you see the whole bay. *The Croisette is nicer in the morning, before it fills up: the afternoon belongs to the islands.*

The ferry (fifteen minutes) takes you to the **Lérins Islands**: **Sainte-Marguerite**, with the Fort Royal and trails through the pine forest, or **Saint-Honorat**, with the working monastery and the monks' vineyard. The two islands are served by different companies, departing from the old port, and you usually can't go from one to the other on the same ticket. In the Fort Royal you can visit the cell where the Man in the Iron Mask was held. There are hardly any shops on the islands: bring water and a packed lunch. You return in the late afternoon, with the last boat to be checked before leaving.

**In practice:** train Antibes-Cannes about 10-15 minutes · boats from Cannes old port, around 19 euros return · Saint-Honorat served by the monks' boat, Sainte-Marguerite by other companies · Forville market in the morning, flea market on Mondays.

### Day 7 — Grasse and the perfume hills
A day in the perfume hinterland: we went to **Grasse**, to the Musée International de la Parfumerie, then into the **Gorges du Loup** and to **Tourrettes-sur-Loup**, the village of the violet. For Grasse there is the train via Cannes, half an hour from Cannes, but for the gorges you need a car: for this day it's worth renting one. The museum tells the story of perfume from antiquity to distillation techniques, and takes an hour and a half. Grasse's big perfume houses offer free tours of their workshops. *Those who come in May find centifolia roses in the fields, in August jasmine.*

After lunch you head down toward the **Gorges du Loup**, a narrow gorge between rock walls, with the Courmes waterfall along the road and the village of Gourdon perched above, one of the finest balconies over the hinterland. **Tourrettes-sur-Loup** is a stone village on a spur where violets are grown: you find them candied in the shops, and in early March there is the violet festival. The roads are narrow and winding, so drive calmly. In the evening we went back to Antibes for the last dinner in the old town.

**In practice:** train Cannes-Grasse about 30 minutes, then shuttle or a fifteen-minute uphill walk to the center · Musée International de la Parfumerie about 6 euros · for the Gorges du Loup and Tourrettes you need a car · Grasse-Antibes by car about 40 minutes.

### Day 8 — Departure, or extension into the Mercantour
The last morning in Antibes is free. It's the moment to go back to the Marché Provençal, at its best in the morning, or take a last walk on the ramparts, with the sea on the left and the mountains in the background on clear days. Anyone who hasn't swum yet can go down to the Gravette or the Salis beach, both sandy and a few minutes from the old town. *If the Musée Picasso was skipped on the first day, now is the time to catch up, as long as it isn't a Monday.* For Nice airport, half an hour and the regional train are enough.

Those with time can extend the trip from here toward the **Mercantour** (see below) instead of leaving. In that case you pick up the car in Antibes or at the airport and drive up toward the Vésubie or Roya valleys, about an hour and a half from the coast. The Vallée des Merveilles can be visited only in summer and only with an authorized guide in the main areas, so the guide needs booking ahead. After Storm Alex in 2020 some roads were rebuilt in stages: check their condition before setting off.

**In practice:** Antibes-Nice airport about 20-30 minutes by train or bus · for the Mercantour you need a car and at least two days · Vallée des Merveilles only in summer and with an authorized guide in the main areas · check the condition of mountain roads before going up.

## Extension: two days in the Mercantour
With two more days (and the car, which is necessary) you go up toward the **Roya and Vésubie valleys**, with the **Vallée des Merveilles** under Mont Bégo: thousands of Bronze Age rock engravings, visitable **only in summer and only with an authorized guide** in the main areas. **The roads were rebuilt in stages after Storm Alex in 2020: check their state before leaving.** You sleep in one of the valleys, with the Alpine fauna (marmots, chamois, ibex) an hour and a half from the sea.

## What to see / what to do
- **The Cap d'Antibes coastal path**, free, not to be skipped.
- **The Musée Picasso**, in the castle where Picasso worked.
- **The Fondation Maeght**, with the Giacometti courtyard under the pines.
- **The Nietzsche Path** up to Èze, with the garden at the top.
- **The Lérins Islands**, a quarter of an hour from Cannes, but in another world.
- **The Antibes market in the morning**, which is still a real market.

## Possible extensions
- **Mercantour (+2 days):** high mountains and rock art an hour and a half from the sea.
- **Marseille and the Calanques (+2-3 days):** toward the west, it links to Provence.
- **Skiing in the Southern Alps (+1-2 days):** Isola 2000, Auron or Valberg; skiing in the morning and sea in the afternoon.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
In Antibes, in the old town. It's the best compromise on the coast between price, location and livability, with sandy beaches and the train close by for everything else.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
Hot socca at the market, pan bagnat on the beach, salade niçoise without potatoes, daube in neighborhood restaurants away from the seafront, Bellet wines.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't come by car in July or August** without having booked parking.
- **Use the coastal train**, which almost no tourist takes advantage of.
- **Staying on the coastal strip is a mistake**: ten kilometers inland, the Côte d'Azur is still itself.
- **Check the hours of the Chapelle du Rosaire**, which are very limited.

## Chiusura personale
I lived in Antibes for two years, and that's why I use it as a base. What I learned there is that the seasons work the other way round: in summer it's a hard place, from October to May it's one of the most pleasant in Europe, with sunshine, empty trails and markets that go back to being markets.
