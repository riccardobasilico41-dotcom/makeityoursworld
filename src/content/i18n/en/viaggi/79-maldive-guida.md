## Apertura personale
The Maldives aren't just the overwater bungalows from the photos. Since 2009 Maldivians have been allowed to open guesthouses on inhabited islands, and today you can sleep in a fishing village, eat tuna curry with families and go out on a boat with fishermen at a fraction of the price of a resort, and then close the trip with a few nights in a resort. That's how this trip and this guide are built.

The reason to come is still the sea: 26 atolls, about 1,200 islands, coral reefs that drop into the blue and channels where the ocean flows in and out with the tide, carrying plankton and big animals. Mantas, whale sharks, hammerhead sharks, tiger sharks, turtles on almost every dive. The guide explains when to go month by month, which atoll to pick and why, how to behave on the local islands, how to organize excursions and transfers, where to see the biggest fish and when, how to stay safe in the water and how a dive cruise, the liveaboard, works. At the end there is a ten-day itinerary, to adapt.

It's a guide for people who love the sea more than luxury, for people who snorkel or dive and for people who want to see what life is like on the islands beyond the resort.

## Scheda pratica
- **Documents:** electronic passport (with chip), mandatory since 2016; the authorities ask for 30 days of validity beyond your return, Viaggiare Sicuri recommends 6 months for layovers. Free 30-day tourist visa on arrival, extendable up to 90, with a return or onward ticket, confirmed accommodation booking and sufficient funds. In the 96 hours before arrival you must fill in the **IMUGA** declaration (Traveller Declaration) online, free, at imuga.immigration.gov.mv; for departure it has no longer been required since August 15, 2024.
- **Customs bans:** it's forbidden to import alcohol, pork, drugs, pornographic material and non-Islamic religious objects meant for display. Since November 12, 2024, e-cigarettes are also banned, import and use included; tobacco is allowed up to 200 cigarettes, 25 cigars or 250 grams. Alcohol found in your luggage is seized at the airport. Penalties for drugs go up to life imprisonment even for small amounts.
- **Currency:** Maldivian rufiyaa (MVR), about 15.4 to the dollar. The US dollar is accepted everywhere in the tourist circuit, but only bills in good condition and printed from 2013 onward; on the local islands it's worth having some rufiyaa too for ferries, cafés and shops.
- **Payments:** cards in resorts, in the hotels of Malé and Hulhumalé and in many guesthouses (often with a 3-5% fee). Cash for ferries, excursions with small operators, tips and island shops. ATMs in Malé, Hulhumalé, Maafushi and on the bigger islands; on the small ones often none.
- **Stay taxes:** for every night you pay the green tax ($6 per person in guesthouses on inhabited islands with at most 50 rooms, $12 in resorts, big hotels and safari boats; children under 2 exempt), the 17% TGST (in force since July 1, 2025) and usually 10% service. Since December 2024 the departure tax for foreigners in economy is $50, to which another $50 airport development fee is added: $100 in all, almost always already in the airfare.
- **Connectivity:** Dhiraagu and Ooredoo, tourist SIMs at the airport counter. Good 4G signal on almost all inhabited islands and in resorts.
- **Health:** no mandatory vaccinations, except yellow fever for those arriving from an at-risk country or who had a layover of over 12 hours in one. Dengue is present: repellent on the local islands. The most frequent danger is the sun, with the water reflecting and fooling you: mineral sunscreen, lycra and a hat. Health insurance that covers diving (hyperbaric chambers are few and emergency transfers expensive).
- **Language:** Dhivehi; English is spoken everywhere in tourism.
- **Electricity:** 230V, 50Hz, type G (British) and sometimes D sockets. You need an adapter.
- **Time zone:** UTC+5, 3 hours ahead of Italy during daylight saving time, 4 in winter. Some resorts use an "island time" shifted by one hour: ask on arrival.
- **Religion and rules:** the Maldives is a Sunni Muslim country. On inhabited islands local rules apply (see below); resorts are islands unto themselves with different rules.
- **From the airport to the centre:** **Velana (MLE)** is on the island of Hulhulé: for **Malé** there's the public ferry, about 10-15 minutes (about $1 in rufiyaa, roughly every 15 minutes from 5am to midnight), or the bus over the bridge (about 10-15 MVR) or a taxi (about 70 MVR); for **Hulhumalé** bus about 10-20 MVR or taxi about 80 MVR, ten minutes or so · for the other islands the transfer is arranged by your accommodation: resort desk in arrivals for speedboats and seaplanes, which only fly by day · if you land in the evening and are heading to a distant atoll, plan on a night in Hulhumalé · book the transfer beforehand rather than relying on people offering boats at the jetty.

## When to go, month by month
The Maldives has two monsoons. The **Iruvai**, the northeast monsoon (December to April), brings dry weather, calm seas and clear water. The **Hulhangu**, the southwest monsoon (May to November), brings more wind, waves and heavy showers, alternating with sunny days. There's no "wrong" month for everything: visibility, the side of the atolls where mantas concentrate and prices all change.

- **January-March:** dry season, sun almost every day, calm sea, excellent visibility. Currents enter the atolls from the east: the channels on the eastern side are the best for sharks, mantas move to the western side. High season with top prices, especially at Christmas, New Year and Easter.
- **April:** the hottest month, sea often flat, visibility still excellent. High season ends.
- **May:** transition toward the southwest monsoon, first storms, prices falling. At the end of the month the first mantas may arrive at Hanifaru.
- **June-July:** the rainiest and windiest months, with a rough sea on speedboat transfers and some gray days. Surf season in the central Maldives. Low prices, mantas on the eastern side of the atolls, start of the Hanifaru season.
- **August-October:** the best period for **Hanifaru Bay** in Baa atoll, with the peak of manta aggregations in the weeks around the new and full moon. Variable weather, long sunny days alternating with storms.
- **November:** transition toward the dry season, calm sea, end of the Hanifaru season, prices still mid-range. Excellent value for money.
- **December:** start of the dry season and the high season, with peak prices from December 20.

**Whale sharks:** they're in South Ari atoll all year. According to operators the most cited window runs from December to April, for sea and visibility, and a second good period falls between August and November. **Hammerhead sharks:** Rasdhoo, at dawn, most frequent from December to May. **Tiger sharks:** Fuvahmulah, all year.

**Ramadan:** in 2027 roughly from February 8 to March 8, with the end-of-fast feast on March 9; the exact date is set by the Maldivian moon-sighting committee and can shift by a day. On the local islands many cafés and restaurants stay closed during the day and life moves to the evening; guesthouses still serve meals to guests. Eating, drinking or smoking in public during the day is rude. In resorts almost nothing changes.

## Which atoll to choose and why
The atoll matters more than the individual island: from one atoll to another the distance from Malé, the ways of getting there, the marine life and the type of islands change a lot.

- **North Malé and South Malé (Kaafu):** the closest to the airport, 20-90 minutes by speedboat. They have lots of resorts, the most developed local islands (**Maafushi**, the most touristy, then **Guraidhoo**, **Gulhi**, **Huraa**, **Himmafushi**, **Thulusdhoo** for surf) and dive sites with sharks and turtles. They suit anyone with few days or who wants to spend little on transfers. Maafushi is convenient and full of services, but crowded and built up: for a real village you need smaller islands.
- **Vaavu:** south of South Malé, little touristed, with **Fulidhoo** and Thinadhoo as local islands. It has channels with gray reef sharks and the **Alimatha** night dive, known for dozens of nurse sharks. You get there by speedboat or ferry.
- **North Ari (Alifu Alifu):** **Rasdhoo** with hammerhead sharks at dawn, **Ukulhas** with its beach and its commitment on waste, **Thoddoo** with its fruit plantations. From Malé it takes 1 to 1.5 hours by speedboat.
- **South Ari (Alifu Dhaalu):** the **whale shark** area, along the outer reef between Maamigili and Dhigurah, a marine protected area. **Dhigurah** and **Dhangethi** are the most suitable local islands, with long beaches and bikini beaches. Mantas frequent the cleaning stations of Rangali and around. From Malé, 1.5 to 2 hours by speedboat.
- **Baa:** UNESCO biosphere reserve, with **Hanifaru Bay** and its manta aggregations between June and November. You arrive by seaplane (about 35-45 minutes) or by domestic flight to Dharavandhoo and then by boat; among the local islands, Dharavandhoo and Thulhaadhoo. It's the atoll for those traveling in manta season.
- **Lhaviyani and Raa:** resorts and a few local islands, channels with sharks and mantas, fewer crowds. You arrive by seaplane or on long speedboat rides.
- **Fuvahmulah:** an atoll made up of a single island in the south, on the equator, with no lagoon, the deep ocean a few meters from the shore. People go for the **tiger sharks**, present all year, and for thresher sharks and mola mola in some seasons. From Malé it's about 1 hour 20 minutes by domestic flight. Suited to experienced divers.
- **Southern atolls (Laamu, Huvadhoo, Addu):** far away, reachable by domestic flight, with less tourism, surf at Laamu, mantas and the British Loyalty wreck at Addu. For a second trip or for a liveaboard.

**In short:** first time with a modest budget and snorkeling, South Ari; first time with few days, South Malé or Vaavu; mantas in August-October, Baa; sharks and demanding dives, Rasdhoo, Vaavu and Fuvahmulah; honeymoon in a resort, any atoll, choosing the resort for its house reef.

## Resorts and local islands: two different trips
- **Resort:** one island, one hotel. You arrive with the resort's transfer, eat at its restaurants, go on excursions with its dive center. Alcohol, bikinis and couples holding hands are normal. A simple resort costs $300-400 a night and from there prices climb to very high figures; the transfer (speedboat or seaplane) and meals are paid separately, often expensive if not included. Before booking, check the **house reef**, the reef you can reach by swimming from the beach.
- **Local islands:** islands inhabited by Maldivians, with a mosque, school, harbor, soccer field and guesthouses. A double with breakfast costs $60-150 a night, you eat at local cafés or in the guesthouse, you go on excursions with small operators at much lower prices. The rules are those of a conservative Muslim village.

**The rules of the local islands:**
- **No alcohol.** It isn't sold, brought or drunk. Some islands, like Maafushi, have bar-boats anchored outside the lagoon and organize excursions to nearby resorts for a day with alcohol.
- **Bikinis only on the bikini beach**, a marked and somewhat hidden beach. Elsewhere, men and women with shoulders and knees covered; for swimming outside the bikini beach, T-shirt and shorts. Nudism and topless are banned across the country, resorts included.
- **No public displays of affection.**
- **Friday** is the day off: many shops and cafés close from late morning to early afternoon for prayer, public ferries on the long routes don't run and excursions may be reduced.
- **Food:** no pork; cooking based on tuna, rice, coconut and curry, with the afternoon "short eats" (savory and sweet snacks) and mas huni (tuna, coconut, onion and chili) at breakfast.
- **Silence during prayer** near mosques, photos only with permission, shoes off when entering homes.

**The best solution for a first trip** is often mixed: a few nights on a local island for the excursions and village life, then two or three nights in a resort for the house reef and rest. It's the scheme of the itinerary.

## How to get around and get organized
- **Arrival:** Velana international airport is on an island facing Malé. Speedboats, seaplanes and domestic flights leave from here; Malé is reached by ferry (about 10 minutes, $1-2) or by bus over the bridge; Hulhumalé, the artificial island next door, by bus or taxi.
- **Shared speedboats:** the most used means for the nearby local islands. Roughly $25-40 per person each way for South Malé, $40-70 for Ari and Rasdhoo. They leave at fixed times, once or more a day; you book through the guesthouse, which often also arranges the pickup on arrival.
- **MTCC public ferries:** slow and cheap ($2-6 each way), leaving mostly from the Villingili terminal in Malé, once or a few times a week for the long routes, and they don't run on Friday. Tickets are bought in cash at the terminal on the same day. Schedules on the MTCC site (rtl.mv). Suited to those with time and light luggage.
- **Seaplanes:** used by the far-off resorts, with Trans Maldivian Airways. They fly only in daylight: those who land in the evening sleep in Malé or Hulhumalé. They cost roughly $500-1,200 per person round trip, included in the resort price or to be added, with tight luggage limits (often 20-25 kg in all). The flight over the atolls already counts as an excursion.
- **Domestic flights:** Maldivian and Manta Air connect Malé with Dharavandhoo (Baa), Maamigili (South Ari), Kooddoo, Kadhdhoo (Laamu), Fuvahmulah and Gan (Addu), in 30 minutes to 1.5 hours, followed by a boat for many islands.
- **Excursions from the local islands:** organized by guesthouses and dive centers: snorkeling with the whale shark or mantas ($60-120), sandbank with picnic ($40-80), sunset fishing on a dhoni ($30-50), a day at a nearby resort ($100-200 with lunch and sometimes alcohol), snorkeling with turtles and nurse sharks. Indicative prices per person.
- **Practical tip:** if the return flight leaves early, it's best to spend the last night in Hulhumalé or on an island near the airport, because with a rough sea a boat can be canceled.

## Snorkeling and diving
The Maldives is among the best places in the world to see big animals in the water even with just a mask and fins. On the resorts' **house reefs** and on the reefs near the local islands live turtles, blacktip sharks, rays, Napoleon wrasse and thousands of reef fish. On excursions you swim with whale sharks, mantas and nurse sharks.

Dives divide into **thila** (submerged pinnacles inside the atolls or in the channels), **giri** (pinnacles that come close to the surface), **kandu** (the channels between an atoll and the ocean) and outer **walls**. In the channels the current brings gray reef sharks, eagle rays and schools of jacks, but experience and skill are needed. Maldivian rules: maximum depth 30 meters for recreational dives, dive computer mandatory, surface marker buoy recommended; spearfishing is banned. Indicative 2026 prices: $50-80 per dive from the local islands' centers, $100-150 in resorts; Open Water course $500-800.

**Sites and seasons for the big animals:**
- **Mantas:** **Hanifaru Bay** (Baa) from June to November, peak August-October, snorkeling only (diving banned), regulated access with a $20 per person per day ticket for the atoll's conservation fund, usually already included in the excursion; cleaning stations at **Lankan** and others in North Malé, **Rangali Madivaru** and around in South Ari in the dry season, **Fesdu** and Moofushi in Ari, **Addu** all year.
- **Whale sharks:** outer reef of **South Ari** between Dhigurah and Maamigili (SAMPA, marine protected area), all year; snorkeling.
- **Hammerhead sharks:** **Rasdhoo**, dawn on the outer side of the atoll, from December to May; deep blue-water dive for experienced divers.
- **Tiger sharks:** **Fuvahmulah**, all year, with thresher sharks in some seasons; dives for certified divers with experience.
- **Gray and nurse sharks:** **Vaavu** (Fotteyo, Miyaru Kandu, the Alimatha night dive), channels of North Malé and South Malé.
- **Turtles:** almost everywhere, particularly around Maafushi, Dhigurah and on the house reefs.

## Safety in the water
- **The currents of the kandu are the main danger.** In the channels between atoll and ocean the current can exceed the speed of a good swimmer and change with the tide. Never swim in a channel alone and never go beyond the edge of the reef without a support boat.
- **Snorkeling on the house reef:** ask the resort or guesthouse which are the safe entries and exits, enter and exit only at the marked points, swim in pairs, with a buoy or a vest if you're not a strong swimmer.
- **Boats in the lagoon:** dhonis and speedboats pass fast near the islands. Outside the indicated swimming areas you need a colored buoy.
- **Sun:** long-sleeved lycra for snorkeling, because your back burns in half an hour.
- **Seabed:** stonefish and sea urchins live in the shallow lagoons: reef shoes and no walking on the coral.
- **Sharks and big animals:** Maldivian sharks are used to divers and aren't aggressive if respected; the guides' rules must always be followed. With the whale shark: at least 3 meters distance, no flash, no touching, no swimming in front of the mouth.
- **Diving:** insurance with diving coverage, last dive at least 24 hours before the flight, seaplane included.

## How to respect corals and local culture
- **Don't touch, don't step on, don't take anything.** It's forbidden to take away corals, shells and sand, even from the beach: at the airport they're seized and you risk a fine.
- **Mineral sunscreen** (zinc oxide, no oxybenzone) or, better, lycra and a hat.
- **Fins under control** near the reef, neutral buoyancy for divers.
- **No food for the fish** and no chasing turtles and mantas.
- **Choose operators who follow the rules** of the protected areas, especially with whale sharks: few boats at a time, small groups, distance.
- **Plastic:** many islands have waste management problems. Water bottle and filtered water in the guesthouses that offer it, no single-use plastic brought onto the islands.
- **Culture:** covered clothing on inhabited islands, respect for prayer and for Friday, no imported alcohol, photos of people only with permission.
- **The Maldives has been a shark sanctuary since 2010:** shark fishing and trade are banned. Turtles are protected too.

## The liveaboard: the Maldives from a boat
A liveaboard, or "safari boat", is a dive cruise usually lasting a week: you sleep on a boat with cabins, eat on board and do three dives a day from a support dhoni, which carries the equipment and the compressor. It's the best way to see many atolls and the farthest sites without paying for a resort in each area.

**Main routes:**
- **Central** (Malé, Ari, Vaavu, sometimes Rasdhoo): the classic route for a first time, with mantas, whale sharks, gray reef sharks and the night dive with nurse sharks. All year, best in the dry season.
- **North** (Baa, Raa, Lhaviyani, North Malé): quieter, with Hanifaru in manta season (August-October).
- **Deep South** (Laamu, Huvadhoo, Fuvahmulah, Addu): huge channels, tiger, hammerhead and gray sharks in great numbers, very few other boats. For experienced divers; often with domestic flights at the start or end.

**Indicative 2026 costs:** from $1,500 to $3,500 per person for a week in a double cabin, depending on boat and route, to which add the green tax ($12 a day), marine park fees, nitrox, equipment rental, tips for the crew and domestic flights for the Southern routes.

**Who it's for:** for certified divers who want to dive a lot. For the channels and the Southern routes you need an advanced certification and a good number of dives (often at least 50). There are also snorkeling cruises for those who don't dive, a good solution for seeing many atolls when you travel with a diver. It isn't suited to people who get seasick: transfers between atolls can be rough, especially from May to November. To check when choosing: age and maintenance of the boat, separate support dhoni, oxygen and emergency equipment on board, maximum number of divers per guide.

## The itinerary in brief
A ten-day example designed for a first time: four nights on a local island in South Ari for the whale sharks, the mantas and village life, three nights in a resort for the house reef, one spare night near the airport. The islands and the resort are to be chosen according to budget and season: the scheme also works with South Malé or Vaavu in place of Ari, or with Baa in August-October.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Malé and speedboat to Dhigurah
Flights from Italy reach Velana with one stopover, often in the morning. After the checks I looked for the speedboat operator booked by the guesthouse at the arrivals desk. **Dhigurah**, in South Ari Atoll, is an hour and a half to two hours away across open sea. *In rough seas the crossing is felt: if you get seasick, sit towards the stern and take something before leaving.* It's also worth changing some cash or carrying small US dollar notes, handy on local islands for tips and excursions.

We arrived for lunch, with time to settle in, have a first swim on the **bikini beach** (a long tongue of white sand at the tip of the island) and watch the sunset on the west side. The first afternoon is the right moment to agree the following days' excursions with the guesthouse, starting with the whale shark, which depends on the weather and should be set for the first suitable day. Dhigurah is long and narrow, covered on foot or by bike: no cars, village rhythms.

**In practice:** shared Malé-Dhigurah speedboat roughly $50-70 per person each way, generally two departures from the airport (around 10:45-11 and 4 p.m.) and different times on Friday · those landing in the evening sleep in Hulhumalé and leave the next morning · IMUGA filled in within the 96 hours before the flight.

### Day 2 — Dhigurah: the whale shark on the South Ari reef
The whale shark is the reason to come to South Ari. We went out by boat in the morning along the outer reef between Dhigurah and Maamigili, a marine protected area where young whale sharks come close to the surface looking for plankton. The guides search for them from the boat and the group gets into the water only when one is close: you swim a few meters away and follow it as long as it allows. *A sighting is likely but not guaranteed: many operators offer a second outing at a discount or free if the first day comes up empty.*

When a shark is close there can be quite a few boats, and the best time is precisely the morning, with the sun high and the sea calmer. You enter the water quietly, without jumping, and stay to the side of the animal, never in front of its mouth or above its tail. Outings often include a snorkeling stop on the reef, where turtles are common. We kept the afternoon free, between the beach and the reef in front of the island.

**In practice:** excursion roughly $60-120 per person · minimum distance 3 meters, no flash and no touching · lycra and mineral sunscreen, because you stay at the surface a lot.

### Day 3 — Snorkeling with mantas and a sandbank
In the dry season mantas visit the atoll's cleaning stations, where small fish clean their skin and gills. The boat took us to one of these spots, like those around Rangali, to watch from above as the mantas circled slowly. Mantas are watched from the surface, without diving down over the station and without chasing them: if you move slowly they pass by again and again. *Visibility changes a lot with the currents, which is why the guides choose the spot the same morning.*

We spent the second part of the day on a **sandbank**, a strip of sand emerging in the middle of the lagoon, with a packed lunch and snorkeling on the nearby reef. There is no shade on the sandbank: umbrella or sarong, plenty of water and repeated sunscreen are essential. Back in the late afternoon, a last swim on the bikini beach is worth it, when the light is softer and the beach empties.

**In practice:** combined manta and sandbank excursion about $70-130 per person · from May to November the mantas move to the eastern side of the atolls and the excursion changes site · divers book with the island's dive center.

### Day 4 — Village life and sunset fishing
A slower day. In the morning we walked around the island, which you cross in half an hour: sandy streets, low houses with gardens, the mosque, the harbor where tuna is unloaded, the café where fishermen have tea with short eats. Short eats are Maldivian snacks, fried and filled with tuna and coconut, to have mid-morning with sweet tea. You can snorkel on the reef near the beach or take a kayak.

At sunset we went out on a **dhoni**, the traditional boat, for handline fishing the way Maldivians do it; the catch was cooked for us in the evening at the guesthouse. The line is wound around a spool, with no rod, and the guides show you how to feel the bite. *In the middle of the day the sun is very strong: keep walks for the morning and late afternoon, and spend the rest in the shade.*

**In practice:** sunset fishing about $30-50 per person · *if the day falls on a Friday, shops and cafés close around noon for prayer and boat outings may be reduced* · covered clothing outside the bikini beach.

### Day 5 — Last morning in Ari and a dive
Certified divers do a dive on one of the atoll's thilas, with whitetip sharks, turtles and schools of fusiliers; non-divers do a last round of snorkeling or a try dive with the dive center. Thilas are coral pinnacles rising from the bottom almost to the surface, swept by currents: that's why dives usually go in the morning, with the guide first checking the direction of the current. *If you haven't dived for a while, ask for a refresher dive before the sites with strong current.*

In the afternoon we packed and stayed on the beach: the next day we were changing island. It's also the moment to settle the guesthouse bill, often paid in cash or by card with a small fee, and to confirm the time of the transfer to the resort. If the transfer involves a seaplane, the last dive has to be done early this morning, to respect the interval before flying.

**In practice:** single dive $50-80 from the local islands' centers · beginner try dive $100-150 · leave 24 hours between the last dive and a seaplane flight.

### Day 6 — From the local island to the resort
On the sixth day we moved to the **resort**. Many South Ari resorts send a speedboat to pick up guests on nearby islands; for distant ones you go back to Malé and take the seaplane, which only flies in daylight. Arriving by seaplane means allowing for a wait at the Velana terminal, because flights leave in groups and the time is often confirmed only the day before. *Heavy hard-shell suitcases are the most common problem: soft bags are better.*

At the resort the rules change: alcohol, bikinis everywhere, dinners on the beach. In the afternoon, after asking for the safe entry and exit points, we explored the **house reef**. The first round of the house reef is best done calmly, checking the currents and the exit channels shown by the dive center. The evening is the time to book the next day's excursions and ask for low-tide times, which change the reef conditions a lot.

**In practice:** speedboat transfer from the resort to be arranged · seaplane $500-1,200 per person round trip if not included · check the seaplane's luggage limits.

### Day 7 — Resort: the house reef and an excursion
A day in the water. I swam the house reef early in the morning, when the light is low and the fish are more active, and in the late afternoon, when the turtles come up to breathe. Between the two swims the middle hours are spent in the shade: the Maldivian sun burns even in the water, and a long-sleeved rash guard protects better than any sunscreen.

The resort's dive center organizes snorkeling boat trips or dives in the nearby channels, with grey reef sharks, eagle rays and, in the right season, mantas. In the channels the current can be strong, which is why outings go with a guide and a support boat. *Book the day before, because boat places are few and the sites change with sea conditions.*

**In practice:** excursions from resorts cost more than from local islands (snorkeling by boat $80-150, dives $100-150) · colored buoy for the house reef if the resort provides it.

### Day 8 — Resort: a day of rest
A day with no plans, between beach, reading, kayaking in the lagoon and a sunset dinner. Kayaks and SUP boards, often included, are best used in the morning, when the lagoon is flat and the wind hasn't picked up yet. It's also the right day for a spa treatment or a walk around the island at low tide, when the exposed sand reveals rays and small sharks in a few centimeters of water.

If you still want the water, try guided night snorkeling on the house reef: nurse sharks, lobsters and, on moonless nights, sometimes the bioluminescent plankton lighting up the shoreline. You go in with a torch and stay close to the guide. *Bioluminescent plankton can't be booked: it shows best on dark beaches, away from the bungalow lights, and only on some nights.*

**In practice:** night snorkeling or diving only with a guide · resort spas are expensive: ask for the packages on arrival.

### Day 9 — Resort and transfer to Hulhumalé
A last morning in the water, then we moved to **Hulhumalé**, the artificial island next to the airport, with time to see the city beach and have dinner at one of the restaurants on the seafront. Resort check-out and transfer time should be set the day before, bearing in mind that seaplanes don't fly after sunset. *If the transfer is by seaplane, skip the last dive: surface snorkeling is fine.*

Sleeping here the last night, on the morning of the flight you don't depend on a boat or a seaplane. Hulhumalé is a modern town, with wide avenues, the public beach and places where Maldivians gather in the evening, when the heat drops. On the beach covered swimwear applies, as on the other inhabited islands.

**In practice:** seaplanes only in daylight, speedboats depend on the sea · Hulhumalé hotels 10-15 minutes from the airport · in Hulhumalé, an inhabited island, local rules apply: no alcohol.

### Day 10 — Malé and departure
If your flight is in the afternoon, take a walk around **Malé**, the densest capital in the world: the fish market, where tuna arrives in the morning, the fruit market, the 17th-century coral-stone Friday Mosque (open to visitors outside prayer times, with covered clothing). From Hulhumalé to Malé you go by bus or taxi across the bridge, and the center can be walked in a couple of hours. *Entering the Friday Mosque may require a permit: ask locally; in any case the courtyard and the tombs around it can be seen from outside.*

The fish market is liveliest in the late morning, when the dhonis come in; next to it is the fruit and vegetable market, with bananas, coconuts and chillies from the islands. On Fridays Malé stops for midday prayer, worth knowing before planning the morning. Then take the ferry or the bus to the airport.

**In practice:** Malé-airport ferry 10 minutes, $1-2 · Friday Mosque open to visitors with covered clothing, not during prayer · departure tax usually already included in the ticket.

## Where to sleep
- **Local islands, budget (a double with breakfast $60-100 a night):** simple guesthouses, often family-run, in Maafushi, Dhigurah, Fulidhoo, Ukulhas, Thoddoo, Rasdhoo.
- **Local islands, mid-range ($100-200):** boutique guesthouses and small hotels with a pool or beachfront rooms, increasingly common in Dhigurah, Maafushi and Thulusdhoo.
- **Resorts, mid-range ($300-600 a night):** simple resorts by speedboat from Malé, often with good-value full board or all inclusive.
- **Resorts, high-end (from $700 up):** far-off resorts by seaplane, overwater villas, multiple restaurants. Choose for the quality of the house reef, which matters more than the villas.
- **Hulhumalé:** hotels and guesthouses for the first or last night, $60-150.
- **Taxes:** on top of the prices add the 17% TGST, the 10% service and the green tax ($6 per person a night in guesthouses up to 50 rooms, $12 in resorts).

## Where to eat
- **On the local islands:** **mas huni** at breakfast (tuna, grated coconut, onion and chili with roshi, a flatbread), **garudhiya** (tuna broth with rice, lime and chili), tuna and fish curry, grilled fish caught the same day, the afternoon **short eats** (gulha, bajiya, kavaabu), sweet tea. No pork and no alcohol.
- **In resorts:** international buffets, seafood restaurants, beach dinners. À la carte prices are high: full board or all inclusive often pays off.
- **In Malé and Hulhumalé:** local cafés for short eats, restaurants on the Hulhumalé waterfront.
- **Water:** bottled or desalinated and filtered in the guesthouses that offer it; better to bring a water bottle.

## Variations
- **One week:** four nights on a nearby local island (South Malé or Vaavu) and two in a resort by speedboat, one night in Hulhumalé.
- **Two weeks for divers:** a week of liveaboard on the central route, then five nights between Rasdhoo for the hammerhead sharks and a resort in Ari.
- **Mantas in August-October:** four nights in Dharavandhoo or Thulhaadhoo in Baa atoll for Hanifaru, then a resort in Baa or Raa.
- **Tiger sharks:** three or four nights in **Fuvahmulah**, reachable by domestic flight, added to an itinerary of local islands.
- **Local islands only:** two different islands (South Ari and Rasdhoo, or South Malé and Vaavu), five nights each, with a day at a nearby resort.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Fill in IMUGA** within the 96 hours before arrival (not earlier, or it has to be redone) and keep the QR code. It's free: sites that ask for money aren't official.
- **Don't bring alcohol or e-cigarettes in your luggage:** they're seized on arrival.
- **Bring recent dollars in good condition**, along with some rufiyaa for ferries and the islands.
- **Choose the atoll based on season and animals**, not just the bungalow photo.
- **Ask about the house reef** before booking a resort: is there one, can you reach it by swimming, what is the coral like?
- **Keep a spare day** before the return flight, sleeping near the airport.
- **Don't swim in the channels** and don't go beyond the edge of the reef alone.
- **Respect the rules of the local islands**: bikinis only on the bikini beach, no public displays of affection, keep Friday and Ramadan in mind.
- **Don't touch and don't take anything away** from the sea and the beach.
- **Book ahead** for Christmas, New Year and Easter, and for Hanifaru between August and October.

## Chiusura personale
You come to the Maldives for the sea. I swam the house reef early in the morning and again in the late afternoon, when the turtles come up to breathe, and spent the middle of the day in the shade. I built the trip this way, a local island first and then a few nights at a resort: that's what I'd recommend to anyone who loves the sea more than luxury.
