# Egypt in 11 Days: Cairo, the Pyramids, Luxor and the Red Sea

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (itinerary, prices and rules verified in October 2026 against official sources and recent guides)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 11 days, 10 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independent, with a Cairo-Luxor domestic flight, a private driver for the Luxor-to-sea transfer and a few local guides
**Categoria:** Africa · Egitto · archeologia · piramidi · Nilo · Mar Rosso · snorkeling

---

## Apertura personale
This is the Egypt trip that makes the most sense for anyone with two weeks of vacation at most who wants to combine three things: the pyramids, the temples of Luxor and a few days of real sea. The order matters. You start in Cairo, which is the most tiring part, fly down to Luxor for the two densest days of archaeology, and end on the Red Sea, which acts as decompression before the flight home. On the way back you don't return to Cairo: from Hurghada and Marsa Alam there are direct flights to Italy.

The choice of sea is the first question that comes up, and it's explained at length in the Scheda pratica. In short: this itinerary is written around **Marsa Alam**, which has the most beautiful coral reef reachable from shore and a direct road from Luxor; **Hurghada** is the more convenient and cheaper alternative; **Sharm el-Sheikh** only works for those willing to take one more flight, because from the Sinai you can't get to Luxor by car in a reasonable time.

It suits first-time visitors to Egypt, couples and families with kids old enough to handle early wake-ups and days at sites. It's less suited to anyone who wants Aswan, Abu Simbel and the Nile cruise: for that there's the guide to complete Egypt.

## Scheda pratica

### Which sea: Marsa Alam, Hurghada or Sharm
- **Marsa Alam (this itinerary's choice).** Resorts scattered along 100 km of coast, almost all with a reef out front and a jetty for getting in the water. Abu Dabbab for turtles and dugong, Dolphin House for dolphins, Elphinstone for sharks. From Luxor about 4-4.5 hours by road via Edfu. Downside: it's isolated, there's little outside the resort, and direct flights from Italy are mostly seasonal charters.
- **Hurghada.** The most practical choice: airport with many flights from Italy, a road of about 4 hours from Luxor, lower prices, nightlife in El Gouna and in town. Downside: the shore reef is less beautiful and for serious snorkeling you almost always go by boat, to the Giftun Islands.
- **Sharm el-Sheikh.** Ras Mohammed and Tiran remain among the most famous dives in the Red Sea, and Dahab is an hour away. Downside: it's in the Sinai, and for Luxor you need a flight, usually with a stop in Cairo. It only makes sense by reversing the circuit (Cairo, Luxor by plane, back to Cairo, flight to Sharm) and budgeting an extra day of transfers.

### When to go, month by month
- **October-November:** the most balanced period. Luxor at 28-33 °C (82-91 °F) by day, sea at 26-28 °C (79-82 °F). High rates start in late October.
- **December-February:** high season for the sites, clear skies, 20-24 °C (68-75 °F) in Cairo and Luxor, cold nights. The sea drops to 22-23 °C (72-73 °F) and a strong wind blows: snorkeling with a shorty wetsuit, boat days sometimes canceled. Christmas and New Year are the priciest and most crowded days of the year.
- **March-April:** climate still good, possible days of sandy wind (khamsin). In 2027 Ramadan falls roughly between February 8 and March 9: sites open, reduced hours, local restaurants closed during the day.
- **May-September:** very warm sea and discounted resorts, but Luxor often exceeds 40 °C (104 °F) and in summer reaches 45 (113 °F). It can be done, with visits between 6 and 10 and afternoons in the hotel; it's not the ideal period for a first time.

### Documents, currency and payments
- **Documents:** passport with at least 6 months of remaining validity on arrival, or, for tourism only, an electronic ID card valid for travel abroad with more than 6 months remaining and two passport photos (without a photo the visa isn't issued). An ID card extended with the paper slip is not accepted. The passport avoids arguments at check-in.
- **Visa:** $30 on arrival in Cairo (increased as of March 1, 2026, Viaggiare Sicuri still reports $25), at the counter before the checks, or e-visa on the official portal visa2egypt.gov.eg. Beware of sites that imitate the official one and ask for double.
- **Currency:** Egyptian pound (EGP), roughly 58-62 EGP to the euro in October 2026. Many tourist prices are in dollars.
- **Payments:** card at resorts, hotels and the ticket offices of state sites, which accept cards only; tickets can also be bought online at egymonuments.com. Cash in small bills for taxis, tips, restrooms, water and markets.
- **Tips (baksheesh):** part of the system. Roughly 10-20 EGP for restrooms and small services, 50-100 EGP a day for the driver, 100-200 EGP a day for a guide, per person. They should be given for a real service, not to anyone who holds out a hand.

### Site tickets, approximate prices October 2026
Prices for foreign tourists have gone up several times since 2024 and change without much notice. The figures below are those of the official online ticket office egymonuments.com as of October 30, 2026 (foreign adult; students half price); they should be rechecked before leaving. At 60 EGP to the euro, 600 EGP is about €10.
- **Grand Egyptian Museum:** 1,830 EGP (about $35) from November 1, 2026, previously 1,570 EGP. Entry by time slot, to be booked online.
- **Giza plateau:** 700 EGP; inside the Great Pyramid about 1,500 EGP separately.
- **Saqqara:** 600 EGP; **Dahshur:** 200 EGP.
- **Karnak:** 600 EGP; **Luxor Temple:** 500 EGP.
- **Valley of the Kings:** 750 EGP with three tombs; **Tutankhamun** about 700 EGP; **Seti I** about 2,000 EGP, separate tickets sold at the valley entrance.
- **Temple of Hatshepsut:** 440 EGP; **Medinet Habu** 220 EGP; **temple of Edfu** 550 EGP.

### Getting around
- **From the airport to the centre:** from **Cairo (CAI)** Uber or Careem from the departures parking lot, 30-60 minutes (up to 90 at rush hour) and roughly 200-400 EGP to the center, 300-500 EGP to Giza; the metro does not reach the terminals · shuttles and public buses to the center, slow (60-90 minutes) and awkward with luggage · taxis only at a price agreed before getting in or booked through the hotel; ignore anyone offering rides inside the terminal · at **Luxor (LXR)** the center is 15-20 minutes away by taxi, price to agree first · **Marsa Alam (RMF)** is about 60 km from the town and **Hurghada (HRG)** 15-30 minutes from the hotels: almost everyone uses the hotel transfer.
- **Cairo-Luxor:** one-hour EgyptAir flight, roughly €60-120 if booked in time. Alternatively the overnight sleeper train by Abela Trains (abelatrains.com), from Cairo Upper Egypt (Bashtil) or Giza stations: about 10-11 hours, $110-125 per person in a shared double cabin, $150-170 alone, dinner and breakfast included.
- **Luxor-Marsa Alam:** car with a private driver, 4-4.5 hours, roughly $100-150 per vehicle; for Hurghada 4 hours and $70-120. There are also Go Bus buses to Hurghada, cheaper but less flexible.
- **Within Luxor:** on foot and by Uber on the east bank, car with a driver for the day on the west bank ($25-40).
- **Return:** direct flight from Marsa Alam or Hurghada to Italy, or a domestic flight to Cairo for those with a round-trip ticket.

### Safety and common scams
The real risk isn't crime but commercial pushiness, which at Giza and Luxor is the strongest in the country.
- **Camels and horses at Giza:** the classic is the low price to get on and a request three times higher to get off. Agree beforehand on the total price, duration and route, or use the internal electric bus.
- **"It's closed, I'll take you":** whoever stops the taxi before the entrance saying the site is closed wants to take you to his camel stable. The official entrance is always open on schedule.
- **Fake guides:** they approach as friends or "guardians," tell you a couple of things and then ask for money. Real guides have the Ministry ID card; better to book them in advance.
- **Papyrus, perfumes and alabaster:** "free" visits to shops where the guide or driver takes the commission. You can say no at the start of the day, clearly.
- **Little gifts:** the bracelet or scarab put in your hand "as a gift" should be returned immediately.
- **Guards in the tombs:** they offer to let you take a forbidden photo or show a closed corner, then ask for a tip, sometimes threatening a fine.
- **Taxis without meters and horse carriages in Luxor:** agree on the price beforehand, in Egyptian pounds, for the whole group. Uber solves the problem in the city.
- **Drones:** banned without authorization, confiscated at customs on arrival.
- **Roads at night:** Viaggiare Sicuri recommends not traveling by car or coach at night and using only authorized drivers and operators; it also asks not to photograph military, police and security installations and not to approach demonstrations.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Cairo
The first day was only for arriving. We landed in Cairo: anyone who doesn't already have the e-visa buys the visa at the banks' counter before the checks, then looks for an Uber or the hotel transfer. For such a short itinerary it's best to sleep in **Giza**, ten minutes from the pyramids and the Grand Egyptian Museum: you save two trips through traffic, which in Cairo is the variable that decides your days.

If you arrive in the afternoon, many Giza hotels have a terrace overlooking the plateau, and in the evening there's the sound and light show at the Sphinx. Nothing else is needed: tomorrow the wake-up is early.

**In practice**
- Visa on arrival: $30, cash or card
- Uber from the airport to Giza: 45-75 minutes depending on traffic
- First SIM or eSIM: Vodafone and Orange desks in arrivals

### Day 2 — The pyramids of Giza and the Grand Egyptian Museum
The plateau opens at 7, and it's the right time: the light is low, the coaches arrive after 9 and the air is still cool. We entered through the main entrance and took the internal **electric bus** that links the pyramids, the viewpoint and the Sphinx, which avoids the camel and horse middlemen. Those who want to enter the **Great Pyramid** buy the ticket separately: the narrow corridor and the Grand Gallery are the experience, the chambers are bare.

After lunch we went on to the **Grand Egyptian Museum**, two kilometers away. The museum is huge: the monumental staircase with the statues of the pharaohs leads to the main galleries, and the **Tutankhamun** halls display the whole tomb assemblage together for the first time. You need at least three or four hours. The time slot must be booked online before leaving.

**In practice**
- Giza plateau 700 EGP, inside of Khufu about 1,500 EGP
- Grand Egyptian Museum 1,830 EGP (about $35), online booking only
- Comfortable shoes and a hat: there's no shade on the plateau

### Day 3 — Saqqara, Dahshur and the evening at Khan el-Khalili
This is the day many skip and the one that best explains the pyramids. At **Saqqara** there's the step pyramid of **Djoser**, the first built in stone, and around it the mastabas of officials with reliefs of daily life still crystal clear. At **Dahshur**, half an hour further south, the **Bent Pyramid** shows the miscalculation of the slope and the **Red Pyramid** is the first "true" pyramid: you go down inside with very few people around.

You need a car with a driver for the day, booked through the hotel. Back in the city, in the evening we went to **Khan el-Khalili**, the bazaar of medieval Cairo, with a tea at El Fishawy café and koshari for dinner.

**In practice**
- Driver for Saqqara and Dahshur: roughly $30-50 for the car
- Saqqara 600 EGP, Dahshur 200 EGP
- Khan el-Khalili: always haggle, starting from a third of the asking price

### Day 4 — Islamic and Coptic Cairo, flight to Luxor
We gave the morning to a different Cairo. The **Citadel** of Saladin with the Muhammad Ali Mosque gives the widest view of the city; below, the **Sultan Hassan Mosque** and **al-Muizz** street are the heart of Mamluk Cairo. Those who prefer Coptic Cairo go to Mar Girgis by metro, for the Hanging Church and the Ben Ezra Synagogue. Women keep a scarf in their bag for the mosques, and everyone keeps shoulders and knees covered.

In the afternoon we took the flight to **Luxor**, one hour. We landed in time for Luxor Temple at sunset, a few minutes' walk from the east bank hotels, when the lighting comes on.

**In practice**
- Citadel 550 EGP
- Cairo-Luxor flight: roughly €60-120
- Luxor Temple 500 EGP, open until evening

### Day 5 — Luxor, west bank: Valley of the Kings and Hatshepsut's temple
The west bank is done at dawn, for the heat and the crowds. We began with the **Valley of the Kings**: the basic ticket includes three tombs of your choice among those open that day, the tomb of **Tutankhamun** is small but paid separately, the one of **Seti I** is the largest and most decorated and costs much more. A licensed local guide helps pick the tombs that are open and less crowded.

Then we went to the **temple of Hatshepsut** at Deir el-Bahari, on terraces at the foot of the mountain, and to the **Colossi of Memnon** on the way back. Those with energy left add **Medinet Habu**, the temple of Ramesses III with the best-preserved colors and few visitors. The afternoon is for resting; at sunset we took the public ferry back to the east bank.

**In practice**
- Car with driver for the west bank: $25-40
- Valley of the Kings 750 EGP (3 tombs), Tutankhamun about 700, Seti I about 2,000
- Hatshepsut 440 EGP, Medinet Habu 220 EGP

### Day 6 — Balloon at dawn, Karnak and Luxor Temple
We woke up at 4 for the **hot-air balloon**: the flight takes off from the west bank at sunrise and lasts 40-60 minutes above temples, sugarcane fields and villages. Book with a reputable company and not on the lowest price; with strong wind flights are canceled and refunded.

Back at the hotel, we went to **Karnak**, the largest religious complex of antiquity, with the 134-column hypostyle hall. You need two or three hours and early morning is better. The afternoon is free: a **felucca** ride on the Nile, or the **Luxor Museum**, small and well laid out.

**In practice**
- Balloon: roughly $50-100 per person, transfer included
- Karnak 600 EGP
- Sunset felucca: price per boat, to be agreed beforehand

### Day 7 — From Luxor to the Red Sea
We left in the morning with the driver. The road to **Marsa Alam** passes through **Edfu**, home to the best-preserved temple of Horus in Egypt: an hour's stop that nicely breaks up the four and a half hours of travel. Then the road crosses the Eastern Desert and reaches the sea in the early afternoon. Those who chose **Hurghada** take the Qena road, about four hours with no notable stops.

We spent the rest of the day on the beach. After six days of sites and early wake-ups, the first swim on the reef is the change of pace this itinerary is looking for.

**In practice**
- Private car Luxor-Marsa Alam: roughly $100-150 per vehicle
- Temple of Edfu: 550 EGP
- There are checkpoints along the road: keep your passport at hand

### Day 8 — Sea: the coral reef from the shore
First full day at the sea. At Marsa Alam almost all resorts have a jetty or an opening in the reef, and a mask and fins are enough to see corals and fish a few meters from shore. The best-known spot is the bay of **Abu Dabbab**, with turtles grazing on the seagrass meadows and, with luck, the dugong; it's best to go early in the morning, before the boats.

For those who have never dived, here you can try a **discovery dive** with a dive center, with no certification.

**In practice**
- Entrance to Abu Dabbab for those not staying there: roughly $10-20
- Discovery dive: roughly $60-90
- Reef-safe sunscreen and a lycra shirt: the sun on the reef burns in an hour

### Day 9 — By boat to the islands or diving
A boat day. From Marsa Alam the classic excursion is **Dolphin House (Samadai)**, the horseshoe reef where spinner dolphins live: you watch them in the water, without chasing them, and access is limited in numbers. From Hurghada you instead go to the **Giftun Islands** and Orange Bay.

Certified divers do two dives: **Elphinstone** for oceanic whitetip sharks (October to December), or the calmer reefs around Marsa Alam for those with little experience. The full program for divers is in the guide to complete Egypt.

**In practice**
- Dolphin House excursion with lunch: roughly $40-60
- Two boat dives: roughly $70-110, equipment separate
- Excursions are canceled in rough seas, more frequent in winter

### Day 10 — Wadi el Gemal or a free day
The last full day is chosen based on the energy left. The **Wadi el Gemal** park, south of Marsa Alam, combines desert, mangroves and the bay of Sharm el Luli, with white sand and some of the clearest water on the coast. Alternatively a desert excursion at sunset, with a Bedouin dinner under the stars, or simply a day on the beach.

For Wadi el Gemal you leave early: **Sharm el Luli** is some sixty kilometres south of Marsa Alam, about an hour's drive, and in the first hours the beach is still almost empty and the water calm. Many excursions add a stop at the **Qulan mangroves**, near Hamata, with shallow channels and white sand, and a stretch of track in the wadi where gazelles are sometimes seen. *The park is a protected area: you enter the water from the sand, without walking on the coral, and you take nothing away.* Those who prefer to stay at the hotel keep the evening for a last snorkel from the shore, in the low sunset light.

**In practice**
- Excursion to Wadi el Gemal and Sharm el Luli: roughly $35-60
- Bedouin dinner in the desert: roughly $30-50
- Last swim, then bags: the next day the airport transfer is early

### Day 11 — Return
Transfer to **Marsa Alam** airport (or Hurghada) and direct flight to Italy. At the outgoing security check, shells, corals and sand are confiscated: it's forbidden to take them away, even in small quantities.

**Marsa Alam airport** is about 60 km north of the town, near Port Ghalib: from the southernmost hotels allow an hour or more of transfer, to be arranged with the hotel the evening before. Those flying from Hurghada should allow about three and a half hours of driving along the coast. Direct flight times to Italy change with the season and the airline, and rooms usually have to be vacated by noon. *With an evening flight it's worth asking for a late check-out or a day room for a shower after the last swim.*

**In practice**
- Get to the airport 3 hours early: checks are double, at the entrance and at the gate
- Spend your last Egyptian pounds before the gate: outside the country they can't be exchanged

## Where to stay
- **Giza:** budget: guesthouses with a terrace on the pyramids in Nazlet el-Semman, €30-50 a night · mid-range: boutique hotels near the GEM, €70-120 · luxury: Marriott Mena House, beneath Khufu.
- **Luxor:** budget: guesthouses on the west bank, €25-40 · mid-range: Nile hotels on the east bank, €60-110 · luxury: Sofitel Winter Palace, Al Moudira on the west bank.
- **Marsa Alam:** mid-range: all-inclusive resorts with a reef out front, €70-130 a room · luxury: more isolated resorts with a private beach, over €180 · for divers: eco-lodges like Wadi Lahami, with on-site diving.
- **Hurghada:** budget: hotels in town, €40-60 · mid-range: Sahl Hasheesh and Makadi, €70-130 · luxury: El Gouna.

## Where to eat
- **Cairo:** **koshari** (rice, lentils, pasta, chickpeas and fried onion) at Abou Tarek; **ful medames** and **taameya** (fava bean patties) for breakfast at the stands; traditional cooking at Zooba or Abou El Sid; **molokhia** and **fattah** for anyone who wants the home-style dish.
- **Luxor:** pigeon stuffed with rice (**hamam mahshi**), meat and vegetable tagine and hot baladi bread in the west bank eateries; fresh sugarcane juice at the stands.
- **Red Sea:** grilled fish in the harbor restaurants of Hurghada; in all-inclusive resorts it's worth at least one dinner out for the fish of the day.
- **Sweets and drinks:** **om ali**, **basbousa**, karkadè (hibiscus infusion) hot or cold, mint tea everywhere.

## Variations by length
- **8 days:** two days in Cairo (Giza and GEM the first, Saqqara the second), flight to Luxor, two days in Luxor, three days in Hurghada, return from Hurghada.
- **14 days:** after Luxor, a 3-4 night cruise to Aswan with Edfu and Kom Ombo, a trip to Abu Simbel, flight to Hurghada or Marsa Alam for four days of sea.
- **In reverse:** anyone who finds a convenient direct flight to Marsa Alam or Hurghada can start from the sea, go up to Luxor and finish in Cairo. It works, but you end the trip with the most tiring part.
- **With Sharm el-Sheikh:** Cairo, flight to Luxor, Luxor-Cairo-Sharm flight, four days in the Sinai with Ras Mohammed and Dahab. One extra day needed.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book the Grand Egyptian Museum online before leaving:** entries are by time slot.
- **Do the sites at opening**, even in winter: at noon the groups arrive and the light is flat.
- **Buy the e-visa only on the official portal** visa2egypt.gov.eg, or pay the $30 at the counter on arrival.
- **Say no right away to shop visits** when booking a driver or guide.
- **Agree on every price beforehand** (camels, horse carriages, feluccas, taxis), clarifying currency, duration and whether it's per person or per boat.
- **Keep small bills** for tips and restrooms: change often "isn't available."
- **Dress with shoulders and knees covered** in Cairo, in mosques and in villages; swimwear only at the resorts.
- **Don't leave the shorty wetsuit at home** if you're going to the sea between December and March.
- **Don't buy or collect coral and shells:** they're confiscated at the airport and you risk a fine.
- **Leave the drone at home:** without a permit it's confiscated on arrival.

## Chiusura personale
My advice is to do the sites at opening time, even in winter: at midday the groups arrive and the light goes flat. And agree every price beforehand, for camels, carriages, feluccas and taxis, making clear the currency, the duration and whether it's per person.
