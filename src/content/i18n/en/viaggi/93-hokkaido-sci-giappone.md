# Hokkaido and Skiing in Japan: Sapporo, Niseko, Rusutsu, Kiroro, Furano and How Hakuba Compares

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (a guide with a sample itinerary, built from research on official sources, with 2026-27 season prices checked in November 2026; no personal memories in the text)
**Periodo del viaggio:** mid-December to late February for powder snow; January is the safest month, early February coincides with the Sapporo Yuki Matsuri
**Durata:** 11 days, 10 nights, 7 of them on skis; one-week variant and a variant with Hakuba
**Compagni di viaggio:** independently, with seasonal buses and JR trains from New Chitose, daily or multi-day lift passes, rental on site

## Apertura personale
You go skiing in Japan for the snow. In winter, cold winds from Siberia cross the Sea of Japan, pick up moisture and drop it on the mountains of Hokkaido as light, dry snow, the kind skiers call "Japow." It snows often, sometimes for days on end, and you ski through the trees in snow that is rarely found elsewhere.

This guide explains where to go, when to leave and when to book, how much it costs, how to get from the airport, and the rules that matter on the mountain and in the onsen. There's an eleven-day sample itinerary: two days in Sapporo, a day at Kiroro, five nights in Niseko with a day at Rusutsu, two in Furano. At the end, a comparison with Hakuba, in the Japanese Alps, the alternative you can reach from Tokyo.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** Hokkaido resorts usually open between late November and early December and close between late March and early May. Niseko United in 2026-27 opens on November 28; Grand Hirafu and Annupuri stay open until May 5, Niseko Village and Hanazono close in April. The most consistent powder is between the second half of December and February. March has sunny days and heavier snow, and lower prices.
- **Yuki Matsuri:** the Sapporo snow festival, with large snow and ice sculptures in Odori, Susukino and Tsudome, runs February 4-11 in 2027. On those days Sapporo hotels fill up and cost more.
- **When to book:** lodging in Niseko for Christmas, New Year and Chinese New Year is the first thing to book, many months ahead, and the same goes, a little less, for January and February. It's worth buying flights to Tokyo or Osaka at the same time. Airport buses are booked as soon as sales open, and lift passes are bought online when it's cheaper.
- **Getting there:** the airport is Sapporo's New Chitose (CTS). From Europe you almost always arrive with a stopover in Tokyo, Osaka or an Asian hub. The JR Rapid Airport train goes to Sapporo.
- **From the airport to the slopes:** seasonal buses that need booking. The Bigruns to Rusutsu takes about two hours and in 2026-27 costs 5,000 yen one way booked online, at least seven days before, and 5,500 yen booked other ways. For Niseko there's the Hokkaido Resort Liner, with 2026-27 prices not yet published as of November 2026. Alternatively, train to Kutchan with a change at Otaru, and taxi or shuttle.
- **The Shinkansen:** the bullet train only reaches Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto, in the south of the island. The extension to Sapporo has been postponed, now expected around the end of fiscal year 2038.
- **Cold:** in Hokkaido in January, it is often well below freezing at altitude. You need goggles, a balaclava, warm gloves and an extra layer for the chairlifts, many of which are open.
- **Insurance:** one that covers skiing, off-piste through the gates, and rescue. In Niseko an off-piste rescue costs at least 100,000 yen.

## Where to ski: the Hokkaido resorts
- **Niseko United.** Four connected resorts on Mount Annupuri: Grand Hirafu, Hanazono, Niseko Village and Annupuri, with a single lift pass. It's the best-known destination, with plenty of lodging, restaurants and schools in English. It also has the most international feel and the highest prices. Night skiing in 2026-27 from December 12 to March 22.
- **Rusutsu.** Three mountains near Niseko, a large resort with lots of tree runs and fewer crowds. You sleep almost only in the resort.
- **Kiroro.** Closer to Sapporo and Otaru, with lots of snow and hotels inside the resort. Handy for a day from Sapporo with the Ski Bus or the Kiroro Powder Express, which in 2026-27 runs from December 19 to February 28.
- **Furano.** In the center of the island, with drier cold and very light snow. A real town around the slopes, less touristy than Niseko. You get there from Sapporo by train or bus, or from Asahikawa airport.
- **The others:** Sapporo Teine and Kokusai, close to the city, for a day without changing hotels. Tomamu, in the center of the island. Asahidake, on the volcano, for guided off-piste skiing.

## How much it costs: 2026-27 prices
- **Niseko United lift pass (All Mountain), adults:** 1 day 13,500 yen in high season (December 24, 2026-February 28, 2027), 12,600 in regular season (December 12-23 and March 1-22), 8,800 at the start and end of the season. 7 days 91,500 yen in high season. Season pass 184,400 yen.
- **Rusutsu lift pass, adults:** 1 day 16,700 yen at the ticket office in regular season (December 19, 2026-March 14, 2027), 13,200 online; 10,500 in mid-December and in spring; 7,900 at the start of the season.
- **Hakuba Valley lift pass, adults:** 11,100 yen for 1 day, 74,500 for 7 days (to be used within 12 days).
- **Kiroro and Furano:** the 2026-27 prices are on each resort's website; we don't list them because the pages couldn't be accessed at the time of checking.
- **Lodging and rental:** in Niseko prices are among the highest in Japan in the Christmas and New Year weeks, and many places require a minimum number of nights. Furano, Sapporo and Rusutsu cost less outside the holidays. Book rental online in advance, with the discount and the right size.
- **Airport buses:** 5,000 yen one way to Rusutsu (2026-27). For the others, prices on the operators' websites.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival at New Chitose and Sapporo
You land at New Chitose, often after a domestic flight from Tokyo or Osaka. The Rapid Airport train gets you to Sapporo in just over half an hour. The first evening in **Susukino**, the nightlife district, with Sapporo miso ramen or jingisukan, grilled lamb.

### Day 2 — Sapporo and the Yuki Matsuri
In the morning **Nijo market** for a breakfast of crab, sea urchin and salmon. If the trip falls in early February, the day is for the **Yuki Matsuri**: the large sculptures along **Odori Park**, the ice ones in Susukino, the snow slides in Tsudome. Outside the festival, the TV tower, the Sapporo beer museum and Mount Moiwa with the ropeway at sunset.

### Day 3 — Otaru and Kiroro
First day on skis, without changing hotels. The **Ski Bus** or the **Powder Express** take you from Sapporo to **Kiroro**, with packages that include lift pass and rental. If you prefer the train, get off at Otaru and continue by taxi, about forty minutes. In the evening, if there's time, **Otaru** with its canal and lit-up stone warehouses.

### Day 4 — Transfer to Niseko
From Sapporo to Niseko you go by bus, or by train to Kutchan with a change at Otaru. You arrive by lunch, check in, pick up the rental gear, and take your first afternoon runs at **Grand Hirafu**. In the evening there's night skiing.

### Day 5 — Niseko: Hanazono and Grand Hirafu
A day between **Hanazono** and Grand Hirafu, with the tree runs. If it snowed overnight, be at the first chairlift: the best parts get tracked out in the first two hours. In the evening an onsen.

### Day 6 — Niseko: Niseko Village and Annupuri
The other side of the mountain: **Niseko Village** and **Annupuri**, usually less crowded. With good visibility, from the top you can see Mount Yotei, the cone-shaped volcano facing the slopes.

### Day 7 — Niseko: off-piste with a guide
Off-piste in Niseko is done only through the open gates, according to the Niseko Rules. If you don't know the mountain, go with a certified guide, with transceiver, shovel and probe. It's also the natural rest day: if your legs are tired, go snowshoeing or have a long onsen.

### Day 8 — Rusutsu
By bus or shuttle to **Rusutsu**. Three mountains and lots of tree runs. There are often fewer people than in Niseko even in peak weeks. Back to Niseko in the evening.

### Day 9 — From Niseko to Furano
A transfer day, almost entirely: bus or train to Sapporo, then train or bus to **Furano**. You arrive in the afternoon. The evening in town, with Furano curry or an izakaya in the center.

### Day 10 — Furano
A full day on the slopes of **Furano**, which with the dry cold of the island's center often have the lightest snow of the trip. If you want a different experience, take a day trip to **Asahidake**, with a guide.

### Day 11 — Return home
From Furano you reach Asahikawa airport, which is closer, or go back to New Chitose via Sapporo. During the day you fly to Tokyo or Osaka and leave from there.

## Hakuba and the Japanese Alps: how they compare
**Hakuba Valley**, in Nagano prefecture, brings together ten resorts under a single lift pass. From Tokyo you get there in at least 2 hours and 50 minutes with the Hokuriku Shinkansen and an express bus, from Kanazawa in at least 2 hours 40 with the Shinkansen and the JR Oito line, from Osaka in at least 4 hours 50 by train. Compared to Hokkaido it has higher, steeper mountains, alpine views and more vertical. The snow is plentiful but on average less dry, and at low altitudes it gets heavy late in the season. The advantage is logistics: it combines easily with Tokyo, Kyoto or Kanazawa on the same trip, and you avoid the domestic flight. The other well-known areas in the Alps are **Nozawa Onsen**, a hot-spring village with slopes above it, and **Shiga Kogen**, considered one of the largest resorts in the country. In the same area there's the Jigokudani monkey park, with macaques in the hot pools.

In short: Hokkaido if you're after powder snow and accept a domestic flight. Hakuba if you want high mountains and a trip that brings skiing and cities together.

## Rules and tips
- **Niseko Rules:** off-piste you enter only through the open gates. Don't duck under the ropes, don't go when the gates are closed, don't enter prohibited areas. Off-piste, helmet and transceiver are mandatory; shovel and probe very highly recommended. Elementary school children only with an adult. If you break the rules you can lose your lift pass. Conditions are in the Niseko avalanche report.
- **Onsen:** wash thoroughly before entering the shared bath, naked, no swimsuit, towel out of the water. Long hair tied up.
- **Tattoos:** many onsen still ban them. In Niseko, with so many foreign tourists, several places accept them, others ask you to cover them with a patch. Better to ask first, or choose a private onsen.
- **On the slopes:** groomed runs are wide and orderly, and chairlift lines are respected. Wild off-piste through the trees, outside the gates, is forbidden at many resorts.
- **Cash and reservations:** in the resorts cards work everywhere. Restaurants in Niseko in peak weeks need booking days ahead.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book lodging early**, especially for the holidays and Chinese New Year. It's the item that sells out first.
- **Buy lift passes online** when it pays off: at Rusutsu the difference between ticket office and online in regular season is 3,500 yen a day.
- **Book the airport buses**, which have limited seats and close sales before departure.
- **Don't go off-piste without a guide and without avalanche gear.**
- **Leave a free day**: with heavy snowfall roads and trains slow down, and flights from New Chitose can be canceled.

## Chiusura personale
Skiing in Japan is above all a matter of snow: light, frequent, and around it a country with onsen, ramen and an organization that takes away many worries. Hokkaido is the choice for those looking for powder. Hakuba is the choice for those who want the Alps and the rest of Japan on the same trip. Either way, what makes the difference is booking early.
