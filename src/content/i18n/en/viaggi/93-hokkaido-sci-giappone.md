# Hokkaido and Skiing in Japan: Sapporo, Niseko, Rusutsu, Kiroro, Furano and How Hakuba Compares

**Durata:** 11 days, 10 nights, 7 of them on skis; one-week variant and a variant with Hakuba

## Apertura personale
You go skiing in Japan for the snow. In winter, cold winds from Siberia cross the Sea of Japan, pick up moisture and drop it on the mountains of Hokkaido as light, dry snow, the kind skiers call "Japow." It snows often, sometimes for days on end, and you ski through the trees in snow that is rarely found elsewhere.

This guide explains where to go, when to leave and when to book, how much it costs, how to get from the airport, and the rules that matter on the mountain and in the onsen. There's an eleven-day itinerary: two days in Sapporo, a day at Kiroro, five nights in Niseko with a day at Rusutsu, two in Furano. At the end, a comparison with Hakuba, in the Japanese Alps, the alternative you can reach from Tokyo.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** Hokkaido resorts usually open between late November and early December and close between late March and early May. Niseko United in 2026-27 opens on November 28; Grand Hirafu and Annupuri stay open until May 5, Niseko Village and Hanazono close in April. The most consistent powder is between the second half of December and February. March has sunny days and heavier snow, and lower prices.
- **Yuki Matsuri:** the Sapporo snow festival, with large snow and ice sculptures in Odori, Susukino and Tsudome, runs February 4-11 in 2027. On those days Sapporo hotels fill up and cost more.
- **When to book:** lodging in Niseko for Christmas, New Year and Chinese New Year is the first thing to book, many months ahead, and the same goes, a little less, for January and February. It's worth buying flights to Tokyo or Osaka at the same time. Airport buses are booked as soon as sales open, and lift passes are bought online when it's cheaper.
- **Getting there:** the airport is Sapporo's New Chitose (CTS). From Europe you almost always arrive with a stopover in Tokyo, Osaka or an Asian hub. The JR Rapid Airport train goes to Sapporo.
- **From the airport to the centre:** from the JR station under the terminal at **New Chitose (CTS)** the **Rapid Airport** reaches Sapporo in 33-37 minutes, about 1,230 yen (a reserved seat costs about 840 yen extra), roughly from 6:40am to 11:20pm, tickets from the machines or with an IC card · buses to Sapporo take 70-80 minutes, about 1,300 yen, handy for hotels far from the station · a taxi costs around 15,000-20,000 yen, and in snow journey times stretch a lot. In blizzards the trains stop: leave some margin and follow JR Hokkaido's alerts.
- **From the airport to the slopes:** seasonal buses that need booking. The Bigruns to Rusutsu takes about two hours and in 2026-27 costs 5,000 yen one way booked online, at least seven days before, and 5,500 yen booked other ways. For Niseko there's the Hokkaido Resort Liner, with 2026-27 prices not yet published as of November 2026. Alternatively, train to Kutchan with a change at Otaru, and taxi or shuttle.
- **The Shinkansen:** the bullet train only reaches Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto, in the south of the island. The extension to Sapporo has been postponed, now expected around the end of fiscal year 2038.
- **Cold:** in Hokkaido in January, it is often well below freezing at altitude. You need goggles, a balaclava, warm gloves and an extra layer for the chairlifts, many of which are open.
- **Insurance:** one that covers skiing, off-piste through the gates, and rescue. In Niseko an off-piste rescue costs at least 100,000 yen.

## Where to ski: the Hokkaido resorts
- **Niseko United.** Four connected resorts on Mount Annupuri: Grand Hirafu, Hanazono, Niseko Village and Annupuri, with a single lift pass. It's the best-known destination, with plenty of lodging, restaurants and schools in English. It also has the most international feel and the highest prices. Night skiing in 2026-27 from December 12 to March 22.
- **Rusutsu.** Three mountains near Niseko, a large resort with lots of tree runs and fewer crowds. You sleep almost only in the resort.
- **Kiroro.** Closer to Sapporo and Otaru, with lots of snow and hotels inside the resort. Handy for a day from Sapporo with the Ski Bus or the Kiroro Powder Express, which in 2026-27 runs from December 19 to February 28.
- **Furano.** In the center of the island, with drier cold and very light snow. A real town around the slopes, less touristy than Niseko. You get there from Sapporo by train or bus, or from Asahikawa airport.
- **The others:** Sapporo Teine and Kokusai, close to the city, for a day without changing hotels. Tomamu, in the center of the island. Asahidake, on the volcano, for guided off-piste skiing.

## How much it costs: 2026-27 prices
- **Niseko United lift pass (All Mountain), adults:** 1 day 13,500 yen in high season (December 24, 2026-February 28, 2027), 12,600 in regular season (December 12-23 and March 1-22), 8,800 at the start and end of the season. 7 days 91,500 yen in high season. Season pass 184,400 yen.
- **Rusutsu lift pass, adults:** 1 day 16,700 yen at the ticket office in regular season (December 19, 2026-March 14, 2027), 13,200 online; 10,500 in mid-December and in spring; 7,900 at the start of the season.
- **Hakuba Valley lift pass, adults:** 11,100 yen for 1 day, 74,500 for 7 days (to be used within 12 days).
- **Kiroro and Furano:** the 2026-27 prices are on each resort's website; we don't list them because the pages couldn't be accessed at the time of checking.
- **Lodging and rental:** in Niseko prices are among the highest in Japan in the Christmas and New Year weeks, and many places require a minimum number of nights. Furano, Sapporo and Rusutsu cost less outside the holidays. Book rental online in advance, with the discount and the right size.
- **Airport buses:** 5,000 yen one way to Rusutsu (2026-27). For the others, prices on the operators' websites.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival at New Chitose and Sapporo
You land at New Chitose, often after a domestic flight from Tokyo or Osaka. The Rapid Airport train gets you to Sapporo in just over half an hour. The JR station is beneath the terminal: follow the train signs, pay with an IC card or at the ticket machine, and if you are carrying your own skis or board you will find more room outside rush hour. *If you land in heavy snow, expect delays: in winter even the airport trains slow down.* In Sapporo it is best to stay near the station or Odori, because the buses to the slopes on day 3 leave from the centre.

The first evening in **Susukino**, the nightlife district, with Sapporo miso ramen or jingisukan, grilled lamb. Ramen is eaten at the counter, often paying first at the ticket machine by the door; jingisukan you cook yourself on a domed grill, and your clothes take on the smell. Keep the first evening simple: jet lag kicks in and the market opens early tomorrow. Between Susukino and Odori you can also walk underground, through the pedestrian passages linking the subway stations, handy when it is very cold outside.

**In practice:** Rapid Airport New Chitose-Sapporo, fastest in 33 minutes, with some reserved-seat cars at a supplement · in Sapporo, subway and underground passages between Sapporo, Odori and Susukino · izakaya in Susukino are worth booking on Friday and Saturday nights.

### Day 2 — Sapporo and the Yuki Matsuri
In the morning **Nijo market** for a breakfast of crab, sea urchin and salmon. Stalls open early and many close in the early afternoon, so go by nine: choose a bowl of rice topped with raw fish, kaisendon, or buy crab at the counter. Prices are displayed, but crab is sold by weight and can be very expensive: ask first. From the market to **Odori Park** is a few minutes on foot.

If the trip falls in early February, the day is for the **Yuki Matsuri**: the large sculptures along **Odori Park**, the ice ones in Susukino, the snow slides in Tsudome. The Odori sculptures are worth seeing by day and again in the evening, when they are lit up; Tsudome is far from the centre and is reached by subway and shuttle. Outside the festival, the TV tower, the Sapporo beer museum and Mount Moiwa with the ropeway at sunset. *Sapporo's icy pavements are slippery: wear shoes with a good grip, or the slip-on spikes sold in convenience stores.*

**In practice:** Yuki Matsuri 2027 from 4 to 11 February, hotels to book well ahead · Sapporo beer museum free to enter, paid tastings, closed on Mondays · Mount Moiwa ropeway plus the small cable car to the top about 2,100 yen return, suspended in strong wind.

### Day 3 — Otaru and Kiroro
First day on the slopes, without changing hotel. The **Ski Bus** or the **Powder Express** take you from Sapporo to **Kiroro**, with packages that include lift pass and rental. You leave early in the morning from central Sapporo and return in the late afternoon: the package saves you looking for rental on the spot and queuing at the ticket office. If you prefer the train, get off at Otaru and continue by taxi, about forty minutes. Kiroro gets a lot of snow and its runs are spread over two mountains, with tree sections where powder lasts for hours after a snowfall.

In the evening, if there is time, **Otaru** with its canal and lit-up stone warehouses. It is an old port town, with blown-glass workshops and streets of sweet shops; after dinner the canal takes half an hour to walk, and the local train gets you back to Sapporo quickly. *If your legs are tired after the first day, save Otaru for later: you can also stop there on the way on day 4, changing trains there.*

**In practice:** Kiroro Ski Bus from 10 December 2026 to 11 April 2027, Powder Express from 19 December to 28 February, seats to be booked · taxi Otaru-Kiroro about 40 minutes, from about 12,000 yen · Kiroro 2026-27 lift pass prices on the resort's website.

### Day 4 — Transfer to Niseko
From Sapporo to Niseko you go by bus, or by train to Kutchan changing at Otaru. The train takes a good couple of hours: from Otaru onward the line is single-track through the mountains, trains are local and infrequent, and from Kutchan you need a taxi or bus to the ski villages. The bus leaves from central Sapporo and drops you directly in the villages, which matters with skis and suitcases.

You arrive for lunch, check in, pick up your rental gear, and take your first afternoon runs at **Grand Hirafu**. For the first afternoon the groomed runs below the village are enough to get a feel for the snow and the boots. In the evening there is night skiing: Hirafu's lit runs stay open after sunset and, with snow falling under the lamps, they are one of the classic images of Niseko. *If you rented online, you collect on arrival: go early, as the village shops are packed on Saturday afternoons.*

**In practice:** train Sapporo-Otaru-Kutchan on the JR Hakodate line, then taxi or bus to Hirafu · Niseko United night skiing from 12 December 2026 to 22 March 2027 · Niseko United All Mountain pass 2026-27 from 12,600 yen a day in regular season to 13,500 in peak season.

### Day 5 — Niseko: Hanazono and Grand Hirafu
A day between **Hanazono** and Grand Hirafu, with the tree runs. Hanazono is the easternmost side, with gentler runs lower down and birch woods on either side: the right place for anyone starting out in deep snow. Grand Hirafu is the largest of the four resorts and the one with the most lifts. With the All Mountain pass you move from one to the other up top, without taking your skis off, when the upper lifts are open.

If it snowed overnight, be at the first chairlift: the best parts get tracked out in the first two hours. After that, the snow in the woods near the lifts is already skied, and you look for powder further away or on the following days. At midday you eat at one of the mountain restaurants or go down to the village. In the evening, an onsen. *In strong wind the highest lifts stop, sometimes for the whole day: the morning report on the Niseko United website tells you what is open.*

**In practice:** day or multi-day pass best bought online in advance, 7 days 91,500 yen in peak season · in onsen you wash before getting in, naked, towel out of the water · tattoos: ask first, many places still ban them.

### Day 6 — Niseko: Niseko Village and Annupuri
The other side of the mountain: **Niseko Village** and **Annupuri**, usually less crowded. You reach them up top by lift or, if the summit connections are closed because of wind, with the shuttles that run between the bases. Niseko Village has a few large hotels around it and feels more compact; Annupuri, at the western end, has wide, long runs, suited to anyone who wants to ski calmly without the Hirafu queues.

With good visibility, from the top you can see Mount Yotei, the cone-shaped volcano facing the slopes. Clear days in Niseko are not many in midwinter, and when they come it pays to head up high in the morning, before the clouds return. At the end of the day you go back towards Hirafu by lift or shuttle. *With the All Mountain pass you pay nothing extra: if your pass covers a single resort, check first what it includes.*

**In practice:** Niseko Village closes in April, Annupuri and Grand Hirafu stay open until 5 May 2027 · shuttles between the four bases, timetable on the Niseko United website · Niseko restaurants to book days ahead in peak weeks.

### Day 7 — Niseko: off-piste with a guide
Off-piste in Niseko is only done through open gates, according to the Niseko Rules. Anyone who does not know the mountain goes with a certified guide, with transceiver, shovel and probe. Guides are booked in advance, for half a day or a full day, and usually rent avalanche gear if you lack it. The guide chooses the gate based on the day's avalanche bulletin and the snow, and knows where the powder is still untouched.

It is also the natural rest day: anyone with tired legs goes snowshoeing or has a long onsen. Snowshoe outings leave from the villages and loop through the lower woods around the mountain, with no experience needed. *Going through closed gates or under the ropes costs you your lift pass, and an off-piste rescue costs at least 100,000 yen: your insurance must cover it.* Anyone staying in the village can spend the afternoon waxing skis and resting ahead of the Rusutsu day.

**In practice:** off-piste, helmet and transceiver mandatory, shovel and probe strongly recommended · read the Niseko avalanche bulletin in the morning · book guides and snowshoe outings at least a few days ahead.

### Day 8 — Rusutsu
By bus or shuttle to **Rusutsu**. Three mountains and lots of tree runs. Check timetables and bookings for connections from Niseko the day before, and take the first morning departure to have the whole day. Rusutsu is one large resort, with lifts linking the mountains and hotels at the base; lunch is in the resort restaurants.

There are often fewer people than in Niseko, even in peak weeks. Many of the runs are wide, with open woods on the sides that can be skied even by those with little deep-snow experience. The lift pass is cheaper bought online before you arrive: the difference from the ticket office in regular season is several thousand yen. Back to Niseko in the evening. *If you would rather stay a night in Rusutsu you can, but accommodation is almost only in the resort, at resort prices.*

**In practice:** Rusutsu lift pass 2026-27, adults, regular season 13,200 yen online and 16,700 at the ticket office · connections from Niseko on the Rusutsu and operators' websites, otherwise taxi · Bigruns bus from New Chitose for those arriving straight from the airport, about 2 hours, 5,000 yen online.

### Day 9 — From Niseko to Furano
A transfer day, almost all of it: bus or train to Sapporo, then train or bus to **Furano**. By train you change at Takikawa: limited express from Sapporo, then the local train on the Nemuro line, which runs only a few times a day. Check connections the evening before, and send suitcases ahead with takkyubin to travel light. Allow most of the day.

You arrive in the afternoon. Furano is a real small town, with low houses, everyday shops and the slopes a few minutes from the centre. After check-in, walk around the centre and buy what you need for the next day. In the evening in town, with Furano curry or an izakaya in the centre. *In heavy snow trains and buses slow down: that is why the transfer day should not be filled with anything else.*

**In practice:** Sapporo-Takikawa by limited express and Takikawa-Furano by local train, about 2.5-3 hours in total depending on connections · in summer there is a seasonal direct train, not in winter · book takkyubin at the hotel the day before.

### Day 10 — Furano
A full day on the slopes of **Furano**, which, with the dry cold of the island's interior, often have the lightest snow of the trip. The resort has two linked zones, with a ropeway rising from the base near town, and runs overlooking the valley and the Tokachi mountains. There are fewer foreigners than in Niseko, and even on the best days queues are short.

For something different, spend a day at **Asahidake**, with a guide. Mount Asahi is the highest volcano in Hokkaido, in Daisetsuzan National Park, and in winter the ropeway takes expert skiers up for ungroomed off-piste descents, with no marked runs. *It is not a place to go alone: the snow is deep, the weather changes fast, and off-piste there requires avalanche gear and a guide who knows the mountain.*

**In practice:** Furano 2026-27 lift pass on the resort's website, prices could not be checked at the time of verification · Asahidake ropeway closed for maintenance from 16 November to 10 December 2026, then running roughly from 9:00 to 15:40, one way about 1,700 yen · Asahidake is reached by car or on an organised outing.

### Day 11 — Return home
From Furano you reach Asahikawa airport, which is closer, or go back to New Chitose via Sapporo. Asahikawa has a direct bus from Furano in about an hour and flights to Tokyo, and it is the most convenient choice; New Chitose has more flights and times, but from Furano it takes half a day of trains. Before leaving, return your rental gear the evening before and pack up your skis.

During the day you fly to Tokyo or Osaka and leave from there. *With an intercontinental flight the same day, the risk is that a snowfall cancels the domestic flight: if you can, spend the last night in Tokyo or Osaka.* Skis and boards are checked in as special baggage: check the airline's rules and book them in when you buy the ticket.

**In practice:** bus Furano-Asahikawa airport about an hour, timetable on the operator's website · from Furano to New Chitose by train via Takikawa and Sapporo · flights Hokkaido-Tokyo about an hour and a half.

## Hakuba and the Japanese Alps: how they compare
**Hakuba Valley**, in Nagano prefecture, brings together ten resorts under a single lift pass. From Tokyo you get there in at least 2 hours and 50 minutes with the Hokuriku Shinkansen and an express bus, from Kanazawa in at least 2 hours 40 with the Shinkansen and the JR Oito line, from Osaka in at least 4 hours 50 by train. Compared to Hokkaido it has higher, steeper mountains, alpine views and more vertical. The snow is plentiful but on average less dry, and at low altitudes it gets heavy late in the season. The advantage is logistics: it combines easily with Tokyo, Kyoto or Kanazawa on the same trip, and you avoid the domestic flight. The other well-known areas in the Alps are **Nozawa Onsen**, a hot-spring village with slopes above it, and **Shiga Kogen**, considered one of the largest resorts in the country. In the same area there's the Jigokudani monkey park, with macaques in the hot pools.

In short: Hokkaido if you're after powder snow and accept a domestic flight. Hakuba if you want high mountains and a trip that brings skiing and cities together.

## Rules and tips
- **Niseko Rules:** off-piste you enter only through the open gates. Don't duck under the ropes, don't go when the gates are closed, don't enter prohibited areas. Off-piste, helmet and transceiver are mandatory; shovel and probe very highly recommended. Elementary school children only with an adult. If you break the rules you can lose your lift pass. Conditions are in the Niseko avalanche report.
- **Onsen:** wash thoroughly before entering the shared bath, naked, no swimsuit, towel out of the water. Long hair tied up.
- **Tattoos:** many onsen still ban them. In Niseko, with so many foreign tourists, several places accept them, others ask you to cover them with a patch. Better to ask first, or choose a private onsen.
- **On the slopes:** groomed runs are wide and orderly, and chairlift lines are respected. Wild off-piste through the trees, outside the gates, is forbidden at many resorts.
- **Cash and reservations:** in the resorts cards work everywhere. Restaurants in Niseko in peak weeks need booking days ahead.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book lodging early**, especially for the holidays and Chinese New Year. It's the item that sells out first.
- **Buy lift passes online** when it pays off: at Rusutsu the difference between ticket office and online in regular season is 3,500 yen a day.
- **Book the airport buses**, which have limited seats and close sales before departure.
- **Don't go off-piste without a guide and without avalanche gear.**
- **Leave a free day**: with heavy snowfall roads and trains slow down, and flights from New Chitose can be canceled.

## Chiusura personale
Skiing in Japan is above all a matter of snow: light, frequent, and around it a country with onsen, ramen and an organization that takes away many worries. Hokkaido is the choice for those looking for powder. Hakuba is the choice for those who want the Alps and the rest of Japan on the same trip. Either way, what makes the difference is booking early.
