# Okinawa in Summer in Two Weeks: Naha, Churaumi, the Kerama Islands, Miyako and the Yaeyama Islands

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (places Rick visited, put together into one practical route; practical information checked in October-November 2026, to recheck before departure; first-person memories only those given by Rick, none invented)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 13 days, 12 nights, with 10- and 14-day variants
**Compagni di viaggio:** independently, with a rental car on the main island, ferries to the Kerama and Yaeyama islands, domestic flights Naha-Miyako-Ishigaki
**Categoria:** Asia · Giappone · Okinawa · mare · snorkeling · isole · cultura ryukyu

## Apertura personale
Okinawa is the Japan you don't expect. Tropical sea, coral reef, mangroves, and a culture that for centuries belonged to a kingdom of its own, the Ryukyu Kingdom, with its own language, food and music.

This trip is built for summer, when the sea is at its best. It starts on the main island, between Naha, the Churaumi aquarium and the Yanbaru forest. Then two nights in the Kerama Islands, an hour by boat from Naha. Then Miyako, with the whitest beaches in the archipelago, and finally Ishigaki with the Yaeyama Islands: the jungle of Iriomote and the village of Taketomi.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** the rainy season in Okinawa starts on average around May 10 and ends around June 21 (JMA 1991-2020 average). In 2026 it ran from about May 4 to June 29. The weeks right after, late June to mid-July, are usually the most stable. Then the typhoons arrive: on average 1.5 approach Okinawa and Amami in July, 2.3 in August, 2.0 in September, 1.1 in October. In August and September the trip works, but with spare days.
- **Typhoons:** when one arrives, ferries and flights can stop, even for a few days. Spend the last night near the airport you leave from, get insurance that covers delays, make bookings you can change. The official forecasts are on the JMA website.
- **Getting there:** usually with a flight to Tokyo or Osaka and a domestic flight to Naha. There are also direct flights from Tokyo to Miyako and Ishigaki, useful for getting back without passing through Naha again.
- **Getting around:** on the main island you need a car; buses are slow and infrequent outside Naha. In Naha there's the Yui Rail monorail. Between Naha, Miyako and Ishigaki you fly. For the Kerama Islands you leave from Tomari port, in Naha; for Iriomote and Taketomi from Ishigaki port.
- **The sea:** in summer there's the habu jellyfish, which is dangerous. Many lifeguarded beaches have protective nets: swim inside the nets, and wear a wetsuit or a lycra suit. In many areas the currents are strong beyond the reef.
- **Sun:** strong even when the sky is overcast. Reef-safe sunscreen, a long-sleeved shirt in the water, plenty of water to drink.
- **Currency:** yen. Cards almost everywhere in Naha and Ishigaki, cash for small places and the smaller islands.
- **From the airport to the centre:** from **Naha (OKA)** the Yui Rail monorail leaves from the station linked to the terminal and reaches Kencho-mae, on Kokusai-dori, in 12-15 minutes, about 290 yen (320 to Makishi), ticket from the machine or an IC card such as Suica or PASMO, running from about 6:00 to 23:30; the JR Pass is not valid · taxi to the centre in 15-20 minutes, about 1,500-2,000 yen · at **Ishigaki (ISG)** bus to the port terminal in about 30-45 minutes, about 550 yen · at **Miyako (MMY)** bus or taxi, central Hirara is 10-15 minutes away · the typical mistake is picking up the car at the airport on the first day in Naha, where you don't need it and parking costs money.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Naha
You usually arrive in Naha in the afternoon, after the domestic flight. Monorail to the centre, and the first evening on **Kokusai-dori**, the main street. The Yui Rail leaves from the station linked to the terminal and in about a quarter of an hour reaches Kencho-mae and Makishi, at the two ends of Kokusai-dori: pay with an IC card or a ticket from the machine. You don't need the rental car for the first days, because tomorrow is for Naha and the day after you leave by ferry. *If you feel like walking, get off at Makishi and walk Kokusai-dori to Kencho-mae: a kilometre and a half of shops, souvenir stalls and signs.*

For dinner, an izakaya with live sanshin music, the island's three-stringed lute, and the first Okinawan dishes: goya champuru with bitter melon, soki soba, rafute, braised pork. Places with live music usually do several shows a night, and on summer weekends it is worth booking. To drink, the island's Orion beer or awamori, the local spirit, diluted with water and ice. The covered streets leading off Kokusai-dori, such as Heiwa-dori, are quieter in the evening and worth a wander.

**In practice:** Yui Rail from the airport to the centre about 15 minutes, about 290 yen, running roughly from 6:00 to 23:30 · the area between Kencho-mae and Makishi is the most convenient place to stay · rental car to pick up on day 5, booked from home for July and August.

### Day 2 — Naha: Shuri, the market and history
In the morning **Shuri Castle**, seat of the Ryukyu Kingdom, a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with the island's other gusuku sites. The main hall, the Seiden, burned down in 2019 and is being rebuilt, with work due to finish in autumn 2026: the public opening is announced for 23 November 2026, with timed entry to be booked online, and what is open and how the work is progressing can be checked on the park's website. You get there on the Yui Rail to Shuri station and then walk uphill for about fifteen minutes. Go at opening time, before the heat and the groups.

For lunch, **Makishi Public Market**, where you pick fish on the ground floor and have it cooked upstairs. The cooking is charged separately, per person. The afternoon is for more recent history: the **Peace Park** in Itoman, in the south, on the site of the final phase of the 1945 Battle of Okinawa. Get there by taxi, with a car for half a day, or by bus, which is slow. The park has the prefectural peace museum and the stones bearing the names of all the dead, civilian and military, of every nationality. 23 June, Irei no Hi, is the day of remembrance for the fallen. *Nearby is also the Himeyuri memorial, dedicated to the student nurses who died in the battle: it is visited in silence.*

**In practice:** Shuri paid area about 400 yen until 22 November 2026, then about 1,000 yen · prefectural peace museum about 300 yen, closed at the end of the year · at Makishi market you pay for the fish at the stall, the cooking at the restaurant.

### Day 3 — Ferry to Zamami, Kerama Islands
From Tomari port leave the Ferry Zamami (about two hours, 2,900 yen one way) and the fast Queen Zamami (about one hour, 3,950 yen), plus a 100 yen visitor tax (2025-2026 prices). In summer the fast boat sells out: book it. Tomari port is a few minutes by taxi from central Naha; arrive at least half an hour early to collect tickets. The slow ferry has the advantage of taking more luggage and handling rough seas better.

The **Kerama** islands are a national park, famous for a sea of a blue they call Kerama blue here. Zamami has a single village around the port, where almost all the accommodation is: drop your bags and head straight to the sea. The afternoon at **Furuzamami** beach, with snorkeling straight from the shore. From the village you walk there in about twenty minutes or take the village bus, and on the beach you can rent mask, fins and umbrellas. *The coral starts a few steps from the shore: at low tide swim slowly and don't put your feet down.*

**In practice:** book the Queen Zamami on the Zamami village website, well ahead for July and August · Zamami has few places to stay and few restaurants: book dinner · cash for the island's small places.

### Day 4 — Zamami: turtles and deserted islands
A day in the water. At **Ama beach** sea turtles often come to graze on the seagrass close to shore: watch them from a distance, without touching or chasing them. The beach is a few minutes' walk from the village, and early in the morning, before the day-trippers from Naha arrive, the water is calmer and the turtles easier to see. Swim above the seagrass without kicking too close to the bottom so you don't stir up the sand.

Local boats take you to the uninhabited islets nearby, for snorkeling and beach time. Outings are booked the day before at the port or through your accommodation, and usually leave in the morning and return in the early afternoon. In the evening, climb to one of the island's viewpoints for sunset: Kaminohama faces west over the other Kerama islands. *If you are short of time you can do the Kerama islands as a day trip from Naha on the fast boat, but it is a rush.* Outside summer, from these viewpoints you look for the humpback whales that pass between the islands in winter.

**In practice:** wetsuit or rash guard against sun and jellyfish, reef-safe sunscreen · bikes or scooters for hire at the port to reach the viewpoints · check tomorrow's return ferry in the evening, in rough seas the fast sailings can be cancelled.

### Day 5 — Back to Naha and north: Churaumi
Morning ferry to Naha, pick up the car, and a couple of hours' drive to the **Motobu Peninsula**. Rental agencies often have offices outside the city, with a shuttle from the centre or the airport: choose one close to the road north. The fastest route is the Okinawa Expressway to its end, then the coastal highway: the expressway is tolled, and with the rental car's ETC card you drive through without stopping.

The **Okinawa Churaumi Aquarium**, inside Ocean Expo Park, has one of the largest tanks in the world, with whale sharks and manta rays. Adult admission is 2,180 yen. The best time is late afternoon, when the tour groups have left and the Kuroshio tank can be watched through the great window with fewer people in front. The park around it also has Emerald Beach and free dolphin shows. Spend the night in the Motobu or Nakijin area. *In summer the aquarium extends its evening hours during certain periods: the dates change every year and are on the website.*

**In practice:** Italian licence with an international driving permit · Churaumi normally open from 8:30 to 18:30, later in some summer weeks · accommodation in Motobu and Nakijin to book for July and August, restaurants close early.

### Day 6 — Motobu: Kouri, Bise and Nakijin
In the morning the bridge and the island of **Kouri**, with shallow, clear water beneath the road. The bridge is almost two kilometres long, free, and early in the morning has the best light and very few cars: park on the islet of Yagaji or on Kouri and look at the water from the shore. On the island there are small beaches and stands selling sea urchin and ice cream.

Then the village of **Bise**, with its avenues of fukugi trees planted to protect the houses from typhoon winds. You walk or cycle around on a rented bike, in the shade, and it is a good place for the hottest hours. In the afternoon the walls of **Nakijin Castle**, another of the UNESCO gusuku sites, above the sea. The curving stone walls take half an hour to follow, and from the top you see the coast as far as Kouri. On moonless nights the sky over the beaches here is very dark. *In Bise the houses are lived in: walk quietly and don't go into the yards.*

**In practice:** Nakijin Castle has a paid entry, opening hours on the official website · Bise and Churaumi are close, you can do them on the same day if you move Kouri · water and a hat: on summer afternoons the heat is strong.

### Day 7 — Yanbaru, the northern forest
Head up to **Yanbaru**, the northern part of the island, a national park and since 2021 a UNESCO Natural World Heritage Site together with Amami, Tokunoshima and Iriomote. Subtropical forest, waterfalls, species found only here, such as the Okinawa rail. Leave early, because the road along the west coast is slow and villages are few. To walk in the forest, choose a maintained trail or an outing with a local guide, who knows where to look for the animals.

Drive to **Cape Hedo**, the northern tip, with its sheer cliffs. From the cape, on clear days, you can see the island of Yoron to the north. In the afternoon head back south. Return the car at Naha airport and take the evening flight to Miyako. *Protected animals cross these roads at night: drive slowly.* The drive back from the cape to the airport takes several hours: better book a flight that is not too early and allow for incoming traffic into Naha.

**In practice:** few petrol stations and restaurants north of Nago, fill up before · return the car at the rental office with a shuttle to the airport, allow an extra hour · book the Naha-Miyako flight in advance in the summer months.

### Day 8 — Miyako: the Irabu Bridge and Shimoji
In Miyako you need a car again. The **Irabu Bridge**, 3,540 metres long, links the main island with Irabu and Shimoji, and crossing it is already part of the trip. It is free and has a lay-by at the start from which you can photograph the span with the water beneath. Drive it in the morning, with the sun behind you, when the sea's colours are strongest.

On Shimoji island is the stretch of sea next to the airport runway, known as 17END, where the white seabed makes the water look almost unreal. At low tide sandbars appear: check the tide times before going. The afternoon at **Yonaha Maehama**, a long white sand beach. The beach has showers and gear rental, and towards evening the light turns warm over the Kurima Bridge, not far away. *At 17END keep away from the runway and don't fly drones: it is an airport zone.*

**In practice:** rental car to book in advance, buses are few on Miyako · Yonaha Maehama is a lifeguarded beach in summer · to stay, the Hirara area, the centre, or near the southern beaches.

### Day 9 — Miyako: snorkeling and the eastern tip
Snorkeling at **Yoshino beach** or **Shinjo beach**, where the coral starts a few metres from the shore. They are on the south-east coast, close to each other, and early in the morning there are fewer people and the water is stiller. Enter the water where the lifeguards indicate and stay inside the protected zone, because the currents beyond the reef are strong.

Then **Higashi-Hennazaki**, the tongue of land stretching east with a lighthouse at the end. From the car park you walk to the tip with the sea on both sides; the lighthouse can be climbed, with a ticket of about 300 yen, to see the whole peninsula from above. Evening flight to Ishigaki. *The Miyako-Ishigaki flight is short, but flights between the islands are few each day: with a ferry or weather delay there is not always an alternative that evening.*

**In practice:** mask and fins for hire on the beach or in shops in Hirara · wetsuit or rash guard, reef-safe sunscreen, and no feet on the coral · return the car at Miyako airport before the flight.

### Day 10 — Ishigaki: Kabira Bay
**Kabira**, Ishigaki's most photographed bay, is seen from above or from glass-bottom boats. You get there from Ishigaki town by car or bus, and it is best in the morning, when the sun is high and the water shows every shade of green and blue. The glass-bottom boats do short trips over the coral and fish, suitable even for non-swimmers.

Black pearls are farmed in the bay and boats pass through: for swimming, ask locally which nearby beaches are open and lifeguarded. In the afternoon, drive along the north coast towards the viewpoints and less crowded beaches, or stay in town to rest. In the evening at the port and in the centre, with Ishigaki beef and awamori, the archipelago's rice spirit. *Ishigaki beef restaurants fill up early: on summer evenings, book.*

**In practice:** glass-bottom boats about 1,300-1,700 yen depending on the company · to stay, central Ishigaki near the port, handy for the ferries of the next two days · a car for one day, or buses and taxis.

### Day 11 — Iriomote, the jungle
By ferry from Ishigaki to Iriomote, to Uehara port on the north-east coast or Ohara on the south-east: timetables and prices on the companies' websites. The crossing takes under an hour. *In rough seas the Uehara sailings are the first to be cancelled, and you may be landed at Ohara with a shuttle to the north: ask the evening before.* Take the first ferry, for more hours in the jungle.

The island is almost all jungle and mangroves, and home to the Iriomote cat, a wild cat found only here. The right way to see it is with a guide: kayaking on the Urauchi or Nakama river through the mangroves, and a hike to a waterfall. Since 2025 some areas, such as the Pinaisara falls, have limited numbers with an entry permit: local guides take care of it. You can sleep on the island or return to Ishigaki in the evening. Kayak outings last half a day or a full day and usually include lunch and pickup at the port.

**In practice:** ferries run by Yaeyama Kanko and Anei, tickets at Ishigaki port · kayak outings to book in advance for July and August · water shoes, repellent and a dry change of clothes.

### Day 12 — Taketomi, the village
A short ferry ride from Ishigaki and you reach **Taketomi**, a flat island with a village of low houses roofed in red tiles, coral walls and white sand streets. The ferry takes about a quarter of an hour, and sailings are frequent. At the port the bike rental shops run a shuttle to the village: arrive on the early sailings, before the groups.

You get around by bike, or on the cart pulled by a water buffalo. The cart ride lasts about half an hour, with the driver sometimes playing the sanshin. **Kondoi** beach has shallow, calm water; at **Kaiji** you look for star-shaped sand. At midday the heat on the white roads is strong, so stop in the shade or at the beach. Back to Ishigaki for the last evening. *The village is lived in and protected: stay on the streets, don't go into the yards, and don't take the star sand away.*

**In practice:** Ishigaki-Taketomi ferry about 15 minutes · buffalo cart about half an hour, a few thousand yen · little shade at Kondoi, bring an umbrella and water.

### Day 13 — Return home
Flight from Ishigaki to Tokyo, Osaka or Naha, and the connection to Europe. With a typhoon approaching, this is the day you need the margin: better not to have the intercontinental flight on the same day. The airport is outside town, about half an hour by bus or taxi from the port: arrive with plenty of time, because on busy summer days the bag-drop queues are long.

If your flight is in the afternoon, you can spend the morning at the market in central Ishigaki, for a last lunch and to buy salt, awamori and island products. *With an international connection, bottled awamori goes in checked luggage: liquid limits apply at security for the flight to Europe.* Those leaving Tokyo on an evening flight to Europe, with a margin of several hours, can make it the same day, but at the risk of having to change everything if the Ishigaki flight is cancelled.

**In practice:** direct flights from Ishigaki to Tokyo and Osaka, as well as Naha · typhoon forecasts on the JMA website in the days before · insurance covering delays and changeable bookings.

## What to see / what to do
- **Snorkeling in the Kerama Islands,** with the turtles of Ama beach.
- **Churaumi,** the whale sharks in the big tank.
- **Yanbaru and Cape Hedo,** the northern forest.
- **The Irabu Bridge and Yonaha Maehama on Miyako.**
- **Kayaking among the mangroves of Iriomote** with a guide.
- **Taketomi by bicycle,** early in the morning or after the last group ferry.
- **Shuri and the Peace Memorial Park,** to understand the island's history, from the Ryukyu Kingdom to the war.
- **An evening of music and awamori** in a Naha izakaya.

## Variants
**10 days.** Keep the main island with one night in the Kerama Islands, then choose between Miyako and Ishigaki. Ishigaki is the more varied choice, for Iriomote and Taketomi; Miyako is simpler and all about the sea.

**14 days or more.** A night on Iriomote, for the jungle early in the morning. Hateruma, Japan's southernmost inhabited island, by ferry from Ishigaki. A second night in the north of the main island.

**Outside summer.** In winter and early spring you can see humpback whales in the Kerama Islands, with whale-watching trips from Zamami. The sea is cooler, but there are no typhoons and prices are lower.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Leave a buffer for typhoons:** two free days in a two-week trip, the last night near the departure airport.
- **Book cars and fast ferries ahead** for July and August, especially in the Japanese holiday weeks around mid-August.
- **Swim only where you can stand or inside the nets,** and ask about the currents. Many beaches have no lifeguards.
- **Don't touch the coral or walk on it,** don't collect shells or star sand in protected areas.
- **Drive slowly in Yanbaru and on Iriomote,** where protected animals cross the road.
- **Driving license:** to rent a car you need an international driving permit together with your Italian license.

## Chiusura personale
Okinawa is the Japan you don't expect: tropical sea, coral reef, mangroves and the culture of a kingdom that for centuries stood on its own. It's a trip for the summer, when the sea is at its best.
