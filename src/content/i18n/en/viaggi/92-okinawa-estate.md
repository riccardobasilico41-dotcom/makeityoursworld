# Okinawa in Summer in 13 Days: Naha, Churaumi, the Kerama Islands, Miyako and the Yaeyama Islands

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (a guide itinerary built from research on official sources, checked in November 2026; no personal memories in the text)
**Periodo del viaggio:** designed for late June to mid-July, after the rainy season and before the peak of the typhoons; it also works in late September to October
**Durata:** 13 days, 12 nights, with 10- and 14-day variants
**Compagni di viaggio:** independently, with a rental car on the main island, ferries to the Kerama and Yaeyama islands, domestic flights Naha-Miyako-Ishigaki
**Categoria:** Asia · Giappone · Okinawa · mare · snorkeling · isole · cultura ryukyu

## Apertura personale
Okinawa is the Japan you don't expect. Tropical sea, coral reef, mangroves, and a culture that for centuries belonged to a kingdom of its own, the Ryukyu Kingdom, with its own language, food and music. The islands are closer to Taiwan than to Tokyo: from Ishigaki to Tokyo it's almost two thousand kilometers.

This trip is built for summer, when the sea is at its best. It starts on the main island, between Naha, the Churaumi aquarium and the Yanbaru forest. Then two nights in the Kerama Islands, an hour by boat from Naha. Then Miyako, with the whitest beaches in the archipelago, and finally Ishigaki with the Yaeyama Islands: the jungle of Iriomote and the village of Taketomi.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** the rainy season in Okinawa starts on average around May 10 and ends around June 21 (JMA 1991-2020 average). In 2026 it ran from about May 4 to June 29. The weeks right after, late June to mid-July, are usually the most stable. Then the typhoons arrive: on average 1.5 approach Okinawa and Amami in July, 2.3 in August, 2.0 in September, 1.1 in October. In August and September the trip works, but with spare days.
- **Typhoons:** when one arrives, ferries and flights stop, even for two or three days. Spend the last night near the airport you leave from, get insurance that covers delays, make bookings you can change. The official forecasts are on the JMA website.
- **Getting there:** usually with a flight to Tokyo or Osaka and a domestic flight to Naha. There are also direct flights from Tokyo to Miyako and Ishigaki, useful for getting back without passing through Naha again.
- **Getting around:** on the main island you need a car; buses are slow and infrequent outside Naha. In Naha there's the Yui Rail monorail. Between Naha, Miyako and Ishigaki you fly. For the Kerama Islands you leave from Tomari port, in Naha; for Iriomote and Taketomi from Ishigaki port.
- **The sea:** in summer there's the habu jellyfish, which is dangerous. Many lifeguarded beaches have protective nets: swim inside the nets, and wear a wetsuit or a lycra suit. In many areas the currents are strong beyond the reef.
- **Sun:** strong even when the sky is overcast. Reef-safe sunscreen, a long-sleeved shirt in the water, plenty of water to drink.
- **Currency:** yen. Cards almost everywhere in Naha and Ishigaki, cash for small places and the smaller islands.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Naha
You usually reach Naha in the afternoon, after the domestic flight. Monorail to the center, and the first evening on **Kokusai-dori**, the main street. For dinner, an izakaya with live sanshin music, the island's three-stringed lute, and your first Okinawan dishes: goya champuru with bitter melon, soki soba, rafute, the stewed pork.

### Day 2 — Naha: Shuri, the market and history
In the morning **Shuri Castle**, the seat of the Ryukyu Kingdom, a UNESCO World Heritage site together with the island's other gusuku sites. The main hall, the Seiden, burned down in 2019 and is being rebuilt: you visit the rest of the complex and the construction site, and should check the status of the works on the park's website. For lunch, **Makishi Public Market**, where you choose fish on the ground floor and have it cooked upstairs. The afternoon is for more recent history: the **Peace Memorial Park** in Itoman, in the south, on the site of the last phase of the 1945 Battle of Okinawa. June 23, Irei no Hi, is the day of remembrance for the fallen.

### Day 3 — Ferry to Zamami, Kerama Islands
From Tomari port leave the Ferry Zamami (about two hours, 2,900 yen one way) and the fast Queen Zamami (about an hour, 3,950 yen), plus a 100 yen visitor tax (2025-2026 prices). In summer the fast boat sells out: book it. The **Kerama Islands** are a national park, famous for a sea of a blue they call Kerama blue here. The afternoon at **Furuzamami** beach, with snorkeling straight from the shore.

### Day 4 — Zamami: turtles and deserted islands
A day in the water. At **Ama beach** sea turtles often come to eat the seagrass near the shore: watch them from a distance, without touching or chasing them. Local boats take you to the uninhabited islets around, for snorkeling and beach. In the evening you climb to one of the island's viewpoints for the sunset. *If you're short on time, you can do the Kerama Islands as a day trip from Naha on the fast boat, but it's a rush.*

### Day 5 — Back to Naha and north: Churaumi
Morning ferry to Naha, pick up the car, and two hours of driving to the **Motobu Peninsula**. The **Okinawa Churaumi Aquarium**, inside Ocean Expo Park, has one of the largest tanks in the world, with whale sharks and manta rays. Adult admission costs 2,180 yen. In the evening you sleep in the Motobu or Nakijin area.

### Day 6 — Motobu: Kouri, Bise and Nakijin
In the morning the bridge and the island of **Kouri**, with shallow, clear water under the road. Then the village of **Bise**, with its avenues of fukugi trees planted to protect the houses from typhoon winds. In the afternoon the walls of **Nakijin Castle**, another of the UNESCO gusuku sites, above the sea. On moonless nights the sky over the beaches in the area is very dark.

### Day 7 — Yanbaru, the northern forest
You head up to **Yanbaru**, the northern part of the island, a national park and since 2021 a UNESCO Natural World Heritage site together with Amami and Iriomote. Subtropical forest, waterfalls, species that live only here, like the Okinawa rail. You drive to **Cape Hedo**, the northern tip, with its sheer cliffs. In the afternoon you head back south. You return the car at Naha airport and take the evening flight to Miyako, about fifty minutes. *On these roads protected animals cross at night: drive slowly.*

### Day 8 — Miyako: the Irabu Bridge and Shimoji
On Miyako you need a car again. The **Irabu Bridge**, 3,540 meters long, connects the main island to Irabu and Shimoji, and crossing it is already part of the trip. On Shimoji island there's the stretch of sea next to the airport runway, known as 17END, where the white seabed makes the water look almost unreal. The afternoon at **Yonaha Maehama**, one of the longest and lightest beaches in Japan.

### Day 9 — Miyako: snorkeling and the eastern tip
Snorkeling at **Yoshino beach** or **Shinjo beach**, where the coral starts a few meters from the shore. Then **Higashi-Hennazaki**, the strip of land stretching east with the lighthouse at the end. Evening flight to Ishigaki, about thirty minutes.

### Day 10 — Ishigaki: Kabira Bay
**Kabira**, Ishigaki's most photographed bay, is seen from above or from glass-bottom boats: you don't swim here, because of the currents and the pearl farms. The swimming beaches are elsewhere, like **Sukuji**, not far away. In the evening the port and the center, with Ishigaki beef and awamori, the archipelago's rice spirit.

### Day 11 — Iriomote, the jungle
By ferry from Ishigaki to Iriomote: about 40-50 minutes to Uehara port (3,290 yen one way on the 2026 timetable), a little less to Ohara (2,520 yen). The island is almost all jungle and mangroves, and it's home to the Iriomote cat, a feline that lives only here. The right way to see it is with a guide: kayaking on the Urauchi or the Nakama river among the mangroves, and a walk to a waterfall. Since 2025 some areas, like the Pinaisara falls, have limited numbers, with registration and an entrance fee: local guides take care of it. You can sleep on the island or go back to Ishigaki in the evening.

### Day 12 — Taketomi, the village
Ten to fifteen minutes by ferry from Ishigaki and you reach **Taketomi**, a flat island with a village of low houses covered in red tiles, coral walls and white sand streets. You get around by bicycle, or on the cart pulled by a water buffalo. **Kondoi** beach has shallow, calm water; at **Kaiji** you look for star-shaped sand. Back to Ishigaki for the last evening.

### Day 13 — Return home
Flight from Ishigaki to Tokyo, Osaka or Naha, and the connection to Europe. With a typhoon on the way, this is the day you need the buffer: better not to have the intercontinental flight on the same day.

## What to see / what to do
- **Snorkeling in the Kerama Islands,** with the turtles of Ama beach.
- **Churaumi,** the whale sharks in the big tank.
- **Yanbaru and Cape Hedo,** the northern forest.
- **The Irabu Bridge and Yonaha Maehama on Miyako.**
- **Kayaking among the mangroves of Iriomote** with a guide.
- **Taketomi by bicycle,** early in the morning or after the last group ferry.
- **Shuri and the Peace Memorial Park,** to understand the island's history, from the Ryukyu Kingdom to the war.
- **An evening of music and awamori** in a Naha izakaya.

## Variants
**10 days.** Keep the main island with one night in the Kerama Islands, then choose between Miyako and Ishigaki. Ishigaki is the more varied choice, for Iriomote and Taketomi; Miyako is simpler and all about the sea.

**14 days or more.** A night on Iriomote, for the jungle early in the morning. Hateruma, Japan's southernmost inhabited island, by ferry from Ishigaki. A second night in the north of the main island.

**Outside summer.** From January to March you can see humpback whales in the Kerama Islands, with whale-watching trips from Zamami. The sea is cooler, but there are no typhoons and prices are lower.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Leave a buffer for typhoons:** two free days in a two-week trip, the last night near the departure airport.
- **Book cars and fast ferries ahead** for July and August, especially in the Japanese holiday weeks around mid-August.
- **Swim only where you can stand or inside the nets,** and ask about the currents. Many beaches have no lifeguards.
- **Don't touch the coral or walk on it,** don't collect shells or star sand in protected areas.
- **Drive slowly in Yanbaru and on Iriomote,** where protected animals cross the road.
- **Driving license:** to rent a car you need an international driving permit together with your Italian license.

## Chiusura personale
Okinawa in summer asks for just one thing: flexibility. The best sea comes right in the typhoon months, and a trip among the islands lives on ferries and small flights. With a few spare days and a plan that shifts without drama, you come home with the most unexpected Japan: turtles, mangroves, coral villages, and a kingdom few people in Europe know about.
