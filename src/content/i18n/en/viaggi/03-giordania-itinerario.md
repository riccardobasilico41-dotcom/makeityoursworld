# Jordan: New Year's Between Petra, Wadi Rum and the Dead Sea

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (structure, practical info and anecdotes ready; Petra and Wadi Rum enriched with real details of the places)
**Periodo del viaggio:** December 27 - January 3 *(New Year's; a trip made during the sharp drop in tourism after the Israel-Palestine conflict broke out in October 2023, when Jordan emptied of tourists even though it remained a safe and fully accessible country)*
**Durata:** 8 days, 7 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, organized through a local DMC, with an on-site coordinator for transfers, guides and activities (useful to contact if you want to replicate the same itinerary)
**Categoria:** Medio Oriente · Giordania · Capodanno · deserto · storia

---

## Apertura personale
Petra is one of the most visited sites in the world, but that New Year's I walked through it almost alone. The Siq was silent, and the Treasury opened up in front of me with just a handful of people instead of the crowds I had expected. It was a direct result of the conflict that had broken out in the Middle East a few months earlier, which had emptied Jordan of tourists even though the country stayed safe and fully accessible. It was a privilege I never expected, and it made this trip different from any other: a country full of wonders, experienced almost in solitude.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** December-January. Winter days are mild, but nights are cold, especially in the Wadi Rum desert and at higher elevations (Mount Nebo, Petra): you need heavy layers for the evenings in the tent.
- **Documents/visa:** Jordan Pass, highly recommended. It includes the entry visa and admission to the main sites (Petra, Jerash, Karak, Wadi Rum), as long as you stay in Jordan at least 2 consecutive nights.
- **Unusual timing:** I made this trip right after the conflict between Israel and Palestine began (October 2023). Tourism to Jordan had collapsed out of fear of its geographical proximity, even though the country was not involved in the conflict and remained entirely safe for visitors. So sites that are usually overrun, from Petra to Wadi Rum, I got to experience in a very rare quiet.
- **How I got around:** transfers organized along the whole route (minivan/car), with some long drives (up to 4h30 between Aqaba and Amman).
- **Budget:** activities and extras were handled through the DMC that organized the trip. Alcohol has to be bought at specialized shops (Soho, Hangover in Madaba) before reaching the more conservative areas or the desert.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 — Arrival in Amman
My first taste of Jordan was at the table. The welcome dinner was at **Zajal**, on Prince Mohammed Street, for about 15 JOD. If you arrive with some time to spare, you can ask locally about a food tour of the city.

Queen Alia airport is about thirty kilometers south of the city, a three-quarter-hour taxi or transfer. The visa is issued on arrival, but with the **Jordan Pass**, bought online before leaving, the visa fee is included for those staying at least three nights, along with entry to Petra, Jerash, Karak, Wadi Rum and dozens of other sites. If you arrive with a few hours of daylight you can climb up to the **Citadel**, on the highest hill in the center, with the Temple of Hercules and the view of the Roman Theater, or take a stroll on **Rainbow Street**, the street of cafés and bars. *In Jordan you drive on the right but Amman traffic is chaotic: to get around the city, use taxis or ride-hailing apps.*

**In practice:** airport to center about 45 minutes, fixed-price taxi or ride-hailing app · Jordan Pass from about 70 JOD (Petra one day) to 80 JOD (three days), to buy online before arriving · the Jordanian dinar is worth more than the euro, cash useful for tips and small purchases

### Day 2 — Jerash and the Dead Sea
We left Amman early for **Jerash**, about an hour away, with a stop along the way to buy alcohol. On a guided visit of the archaeological site we saw one of the best-preserved Roman cities outside Italy: the oval forum, the colonnaded cardo, the two theaters.

Around noon we headed down to the **Dead Sea**, an hour and a half away, and checked in at the **Holiday Inn Dead Sea**. Lunch, a rest, and then a swim at sunset: floating on the surface while the sun sets behind the hills of the West Bank is an experience of its own. You can also watch the sunset from Mount Nebo, where, according to tradition, Moses saw the Promised Land.

For dinner we were in Madaba, with two options between Adel Jumean Street and Khaled Ben Al-Walid Street: **Haret Jdoudna** or **Hikayet Sitti**. For an evening drink there was the bar at the Grand Hotel Madaba and the **Saint John Sky Bar**, with the city seen from above.

**In practice:** Jerash included in the Jordan Pass, best to arrive early in the morning before the groups · at the Dead Sea don't splash your face and eyes and don't go in with cuts or after shaving · alcohol easier to find in Madaba, a city with a strong Christian community

### Day 3 — Karak, Mount Nebo and Little Petra
Before leaving Madaba we stocked up on alcohol at Soho or Hangover. An hour and a half of transfer took us to **Karak Castle**, the great Crusader fortress that dominates the plateau south of the Dead Sea (admission is included in the Jordan Pass). Then **Mount Nebo** and the road to **Little Petra**, Siq al-Barid, with lunch along the way.

Of the **Little Petra Trek**, a single word survives in my travel notes: "tough." It's the antechamber of Petra, a small canyon with miniature Nabataean facades and fewer people, and it gave us our first rush of excitement on the eve of the big day. In the evening, a typical dinner organized by the local DMC.

Karak castle takes an hour and a half: vaulted corridors, underground halls and the walkways on the walls, from which the view reaches the Dead Sea depression. Mount Nebo, with the basilica of mosaics and the bronze serpent recalling Moses' staff, is half an hour from Madaba. It's best to reach Little Petra in the afternoon, when the low light warms the sandstone and you can better see the frescoes of the **Painted House**, one of the few Nabataean interiors with paintings still visible. *The trek from Little Petra to Petra's Monastery enters the site through the back door: it's best done with a guide, and the Petra ticket must be bought beforehand.*

**In practice:** Karak included in the Jordan Pass, open during the day · Mount Nebo with a ticket of a few dinars · Little Petra free to enter, about a quarter of an hour's drive from Wadi Musa

### Day 4 — Petra, Full Day
This is the day you come to Jordan for, and we gave it entirely to **Petra**, a UNESCO site and one of the New Seven Wonders of the World. With tourism down across the region, the site was almost deserted: a rare privilege.

We entered through the **Siq**, the gorge carved by water into pink sandstone. It's about a mile and a half long, barely ten feet wide in places, closed in between towering walls. After half an hour or forty minutes of walking, the **Treasury (Al-Khazneh)** appeared through a narrow crack: 39-40 meters of facade carved into the rock, the symbol of the country, made even more famous by *Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade*. Seeing it at the end of the Siq, with no crowd in front, is worth the whole trip on its own.

From there the site opens out over more than 8 km and 800 monuments, the Royal Tombs included. For those with legs and time, the real challenge is the **Monastery (Ad-Deir)**: over 800 steps carved into the rock, a steep 40-45 minute climb. At the top is Petra's second great monument, bulkier than the Treasury (45 meters high by 50 wide), and the effort pays off. Before heading down, a mint tea at the kiosk right in front.

In the evening, the hammam at **Al Yhakor** (Al Yakhor Turkish Bath, in Wadi Musa) loosened up our legs after a whole day on foot.

**In practice:** Petra opens at 6 and closes early in winter, around 4-5 pm: go in at opening · one-day ticket 50 JOD without the Jordan Pass · Petra by Night only on some evenings a week, separate ticket · hiking shoes, water and hat, even in winter

### Day 5 — Wadi Rum: Camels, Jeep Safari and New Year's in the Desert
The other star of the trip is **Wadi Rum**, the "Valley of the Moon": red sand and sandstone towers, the set of *Lawrence of Arabia* and of more than one film set on Mars. We got there in an hour and a half, with a surprise stop at a local pastry shop to buy a packed lunch to take into the desert (10 JOD).

We entered the desert on camelback, 45 minutes for 20 JOD, following in the footsteps of the caravans. Then the **jeep safari**, four to six hours on Bedouin pickups: Lawrence's Spring, the Khazali canyon with its Nabataean rock carvings, the red dunes, rock bridges like Umm Fruth, Lawrence's House. We went **sandboarding** on the dunes; anyone who felt up to it could drive the jeep in the desert, and there was even room for an improvised game of beach volleyball.

We reached the tent camp in time for sunset over the dunes. Dinner was Bedouin, cooked the traditional way: often it's zarb, cooked under the sand. And then **New Year's in the desert**, under one of the most beautiful starry skies in the world, far from any light. It's hard to imagine a wilder way to say goodbye to the old year.

**In practice:** entry to the Wadi Rum protected area included in the Jordan Pass, shown at the visitor center · the tented camp must be booked well ahead for New Year's Eve · in the desert at night, in winter, temperatures drop close to zero: you need a jacket and layers

### Day 6 — Desert Sunrise and Sea in Aqaba
We woke before the sun for the **sunrise over the dunes** (the time changes with the season), and the desert went from the blue of night to pink and then gold. An hour later we were on the Red Sea, in **Aqaba**: from desert to sea in under an hour, one of the best contrasts in Jordan.

After dropping our bags at the hotel, we were on the beach within fifteen minutes. There you could choose: beach clubs, jet skis and banana boats, snorkeling on the coral reefs, or the boat trip with **Deep Blue**, included in the package. For dinner in Aqaba, the names were the seafood at **Sheikh El Harah**, **Suzana**, the American food at **Buffalo Wings & Ring** or **Khubza & Seneya** on Al Nahda Street.

**In practice:** Wadi Rum to Aqaba about an hour's drive · Aqaba is a special economic zone, alcohol and some goods cost less than in the rest of the country · mask and fins can be rented on the beach, the best reef is in the south toward the Saudi border

### Day 7 — Back to Amman: Two Options
We left Aqaba early: four and a half hours to Amman. From there the program offers two versions of the day.

**Option 1, culture.** Lunch at the Bazaar, then the Citadel and the Roman Theater in the early afternoon. In the late afternoon there's time for a Turkish bath; in the evening, a cooking class and Arabic calligraphy lesson organized by the DMC. You wrap up at **Talet Al Jabal** or **C-Ling**, on Rainbow Street.

**Option 2, adventure.** Two hours of desert buggy near Amman (40 JOD), street lunch, then the Bazaar for souvenirs and shopping. In the evening the same cooking class with calligraphy from the DMC, and after dinner at **C-Ling**, on Rainbow Street.

The Desert Highway is straight and busy with trucks: a stop halfway, around Ma'an or Qatraneh, is a good idea. Once in Amman, the Citadel and the Roman Theater are close to each other and take a couple of hours. If you choose the buggy you leave the city and return in the afternoon, with time for the center and the souks. *In Amman on Fridays many shops in the Bazaar area open only after the midday prayer.* In the evening, Rainbow Street is the liveliest place for the last dinner, with terraces looking over the old town.

**In practice:** Aqaba to Amman about 330 km, four and a half hours · Citadel and Roman Theater included in the Jordan Pass · for souvenirs, in the souks prices are haggled

### Day 8 — Departure
Check-out, goodbyes, and then an Uber or taxi to the airport. If your flight allows, the day stays free for something extra.

For the airport leave with plenty of margin, especially from the center: at rush hour traffic heading south can stretch the time. If your flight is in the afternoon you can spend the morning at the **Jordan Museum**, in the Ras Al-Ain district, which holds fragments of the Dead Sea Scrolls, or at the small folklore museum beside the Roman Theater. Alternatively, a last breakfast of hummus and falafel in the downtown eateries. *Security checks at Amman airport begin right at the terminal entrance: better to arrive three hours early.*

**In practice:** center to Queen Alia airport about 45 minutes without traffic · Uber and Careem work in Amman · spend your last dinars at the airport duty free, abroad the exchange rate is poor

## What to see / what to do
- **Petra**: the heart of the trip. The Siq, the Treasury at the end of the gorge and, for the most determined, the climb to the Monastery. Experienced almost empty, it's a rare thing to tell about.
- **Wadi Rum**: jeep safari through canyons and red dunes, a camel ride, a night in a desert tent camp and an unmatched starry sky.
- **Jerash**: one of the best-preserved Roman cities in the world.
- **Dead Sea**: you float in the hypersaline water, even better at sunset.
- **Karak Castle**: Crusader fortress on the plateau.
- **Little Petra**: a miniature preview of Petra, off the beaten path.
- **Aqaba and the Red Sea**: snorkeling and water sports after days of desert and archaeological sites.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
- **Amman (Day 1 and 7)**: I stayed in a generic hotel (the name isn't in my travel documents). A comfortable downtown area works perfectly for getting around on foot between the Citadel, the Roman Theater and Rainbow Street.
  - **Budget:** Farah Hotel & Hostel, Cliff Hostel, both a short walk from the Roman Theater, with private rooms or dorms.
  - **Mid-range:** Toledo Hotel (near Rainbow Street), Kaya Hotel Amman (Abdali district, spa on site).
  - **Luxury:** Fairmont Amman, W Amman.

- **Dead Sea (Day 2)**: I stayed at the **Holiday Inn Resort Dead Sea by IHG**. Online reviews (Tripadvisor, about 5,600, average rating 4/5) describe an upper-mid-range resort with very attentive staff, three pools, a private beach on the Dead Sea and an abundant buffet at breakfast and dinner. The most common complaints are the high prices of food and drinks on site, some maintenance problems in the rooms and the ban on bringing in outside food or drinks. Overall it sits in the upper-mid range of Dead Sea resorts, not among the most luxurious names in the area.
  - **Budget:** Ramada Resort by Wyndham Dead Sea, O Beach Hotel & Resort (the latter more basic, but with direct access to the sea at a fraction of the price of the big resorts).
  - **Mid-range:** Dead Sea Spa Hotel, Crowne Plaza Jordan Dead Sea Resort & Spa (the same tier as the Holiday Inn).
  - **Luxury:** Hilton Dead Sea Resort & Spa, Kempinski Hotel Ishtar Dead Sea.

- **Petra/Wadi Musa area (Day 3-4)**: here too I stayed in a generic hotel (name not noted). Wadi Musa is convenient because it's a few minutes on foot or by shuttle from the Petra entrance.
  - **Budget:** Petra Cabin Hostel, Rocky Mountain Hotel (small family-run hotel, panoramic terrace).
  - **Mid-range:** Petra Panorama Hotel, La Maison Hotel Petra.
  - **Luxury:** Petra Moon Luxury Hotel (boutique, very close to the entrance), Petra Marriott Hotel.

- **Wadi Rum (Day 5)**: I stayed in a tent camp in the desert (name not noted), in a classic Bedouin tent with dinner and breakfast included and an unmatched starry sky. This kind of lodging is everywhere in the area, from simple tents to the newer "martian dome"/bubble tents.
  - **Basic camp, shared bathroom:** Wadi Rum Bedouin Camp, Bedouin Lifestyle Camp.
  - **Private tent with bathroom:** Wadi Rum Serenity Camp, Zeina Desert Lodge.
  - **Luxury desert camp:** Sun City Camp (famous for its "martian domes"), Palmera Camp (panoramic tents/bubble tents with private bathroom).

- **Aqaba (Day 6)**: I stayed in a generic hotel (name not noted), convenient because it was close to the beach and the waterfront.
  - **Budget:** Days Inn Hotel & Suites Aqaba, Extra Traveller (hostel).
  - **Mid-range:** Golden Tulip Aqaba, Bratus Hotel Aqaba.
  - **Luxury:** Kempinski Hotel Aqaba, Mövenpick Resort & Spa Aqaba.

## Practical tips / what to pack
- **Jordan Pass**: buy it in advance. It covers the visa and admission to the main sites, but it only pays off if you stay at least 2 consecutive nights in Jordan.
- **Heavy layers for the evening**: nights in the Wadi Rum desert and at elevation (Mount Nebo, Petra) are cold even though the days are mild.
- **Alcohol**: buy it at specialized shops (Soho, Hangover in Madaba) before heading to more conservative areas or into the desert, where it's hard to find.
- **Hiking shoes**: essential for Petra (especially the climb to the Monastery, over 800 steps) and for walking the Siq.
- **Respect for the local context**: dress appropriately at religious sites and in more traditional villages.

## Chiusura personale
The takeaway from this off-the-beaten-path New Year's comes down to one contrast: the beauty of Petra and Wadi Rum experienced almost alone, and the awareness that the quiet was the product of a difficult moment for the region. I felt safe at every moment of the trip, much more than the fears spread elsewhere would have led me to imagine. If I had to pick a single memory, it would be the starry sky over the tent camp in the desert on New Year's Eve, far from any light, in a place that seemed made for saying goodbye to the old year.
