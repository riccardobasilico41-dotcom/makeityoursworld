# Transylvania Express in 5 Days: Castles, Underground Salt Mines and the Most Beautiful Road in the World

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (itinerary, practical info and anecdotes ready thanks to the travel diary, the day-by-day sheet and the official Vade Mecums — opening and closing written from the author's memories)
**Periodo del viaggio:** Halloween long weekend, October 31 - November 4, 2024
**Durata:** 5 days, 4 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, on the road with a self-drive rental car for the whole route
**Categoria:** Europa · Romania · Transilvania · on the road · castelli

---

## Apertura personale
The strongest memory of this trip is the Transfăgărășan. The fall foliage lined the whole road, and bears showed up at the edge of the roadway. Incredible. It was the Halloween long weekend of 2024: five days in a rental car starting from Bucharest, between Wallachia and Transylvania, with the castles of Peleș and Bran, the Saxon villages, the underground salt mine of Turda and days with up to five hours of driving. Below is the tour stop by stop, with the places where we slept and ate.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** cold weather, often rainy or snowy, "perfect for getting into the Transylvania mood," as the official Vade Mecum puts it. The weather can change the itinerary on the fly, especially for the Transfăgărășan (see below).
- **Documents/visa:** Italian citizens only need a valid ID card or passport, no visa. Romania has been in the Schengen area since 2024, but it is still outside the euro.
- **Driving license:** the Italian one is valid, with no conversion or international permit.
- **Approximate budget:** a recommended starting budget of €80-90, to keep aside in cash (in lei) from the first days for the organized activities, plus an average personal spend of €120-150 (more if you add the half day at the spa on the last day).
- **How I got around:** self-drive rental car for the whole trip (pickup and return in Bucharest at Avis, Gran Hotel Bucuresti), Uber/Bolt/taxi for getting around in cities.
- **Currency:** Romanian leu (RON), approximate exchange rate €1 = 4.98 RON. Cards are accepted in tourist centers, but you need cash in lei for the whole trip: it is better to withdraw 500-700 LEI from a single ATM at the start of the tour.
- **Insurance/SIM/wifi:** medical and baggage insurance included in the package. Romania is covered by EU roaming, so a local SIM isn't needed.
- **Luggage:** a compact 40/50/60 liter backpack or duffel bag, no wheeled suitcase: you can leave everything in the hotel or in the car. Comfortable sneakers are a must (you walk a lot, but no real hiking), technical and warm clothing for the temperature swings, sun protection, a rain jacket or umbrella.

### A trip made (mostly) of road
Here, more than on other trips, time in the car is part of the experience: **5 hours of driving in a single day** (Day 3, Brașov-Sighișoara-Salina Turda-Sibiu) and another stretch of **3h45 non-stop** in the afternoon of Day 4, to get back to Bucharest after the Transfăgărășan. The mountain roads are narrow, full of hairpin bends and, on the highest passes, often closed or only partly passable, depending on the season. The Transfăgărășan itself is normally open only from June 20 to October 31; the rest of the year you go up to Lake Bâlea by cable car.

**One thing to keep firmly in mind before getting behind the wheel:** for several years in a row Romania has been the European Union country with the highest road mortality rate. In 2024, according to Eurostat/European Commission data, there were 78 deaths per million inhabitants, against an EU average of 44 (the safest roads are in Sweden, with just 20). It's not a perception, the numbers say so. That is why the itinerary itself repeatedly urges you to **stick to the schedule, not drive in a hurry and always check the weather**, especially on the mountain passes, where conditions change quickly and visibility in the hairpin bends is often poor. Beyond basic caution, a few practical tips:
- On the Transfăgărășan and on the road back toward Bucharest you often see **bears** at the edge of the roadway: you must not feed them or get out of the car. You photograph from the window and that's it.
- Mountain roads can close suddenly because of the weather: before leaving you need to check the official website, and you need a plan B (in this itinerary, the detour to one of the Saxon citadels if the pass is closed).
- Long driving stretches should be broken up with regular stops, especially on the days with the most hours at the wheel. Better to leave early and have a margin than to chase the clock.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Bucharest
We slept at the Hotel Duke Romana, and the first encounter with Bucharest was the most imposing one. In the late afternoon we visited the **Palace of the Parliament**, the second-largest building in the world after the Pentagon, built on the orders of dictator Nicolae Ceaușescu. The visit is guided, lasts an hour and costs about €12.50/pax, paid at the entrance. There is no luggage storage, so during the visit you have to carry everything with you.

If there is time, it's also worth seeing the **Romanian Athenaeum**, a neoclassical concert hall from 1888, home of the George Enescu Philharmonic Orchestra. For a photo stop 30 minutes is enough; entry costs €2/pax and it's a few steps from the Piața Romană district.

In the evening there was the welcome meeting at the hotel, then the **walking tour** with a local guide. Alternatively there are the free walking tours by GuruWalk or Freetour.com, or a personalized guide like Catalina or Stefania, about €100 for a 2.5-3 hour tour.

The **welcome dinner** (included) was at **Caru' cu Bere**, in the historic Lipscani district, a traditional restaurant where you taste a bit of all Romanian cuisine: excellent soups and pork knuckle. For me it was nice, tasty and atmospheric. During the evening, at regular intervals, there are folk shows with dancers in traditional costumes. The budget is €25-30/pax and covers a main dish (sarmale, mici or chicken, always with polenta, sauerkraut and potatoes), an alcoholic drink and a dessert: papanași, fritters with cream and berries, to be shared between two or three people, given the enormous portions. After dinner Lipscani is full of bars and clubs with music until late.

*Practical note: it's best to withdraw cash as soon as you land and book taxis from the touch screens in the arrivals terminal (the airport-center fare should not exceed 60 LEI). Reliable companies: Cobălcescu and Cris Taxi.*

### Day 2 — Bucharest, Peleș Castle, Bran Castle, Brașov
Early in the morning we picked up the car from Avis (Gran Hotel Bucuresti): it's best to arrive a little early. Then the road to **Sinaia** (2h), straddling Wallachia and Transylvania, for **Peleș Castle**. It was the summer residence of Carol I, Romania's first king; it is considered one of the most beautiful castles in Europe, built between 1873 and 1914 in Neo-Renaissance style, with over 160 rooms. I recommend the first and second floor ticket (about €20/pax). The visit lasts 1-1.5 hours and you get in on a first-come basis, with no mandatory booking (in high season it is better to book online). If there is time left, the **Sinaia Monastery** (1846) can be reached on foot in about 25 minutes.

For lunch, in the center of Sinaia, there is **Restaurant Bucegi** (with a terrace); alternatively a packed lunch or street food, to save time in the afternoon.

After lunch we set off again toward **Bran** (about 1h), with a possible photo stop at the **Cantacuzino Castle** (known from the Netflix series *Wednesday*). **Bran Castle** is the 13th-century medieval fortress known as "Dracula's Castle", even though there is no historical evidence that Bram Stoker was inspired by this particular castle or by Vlad III of Wallachia for his 1897 novel. The link is largely a tourist construction, and the castle is still fascinating in its own right. Tickets are bought online in advance; the official guide is booked (30-40 minutes, paid on site, in cash), and the self-guided visit lasts about an hour. The stalls and restaurants around the castle are expensive: real tourist traps.

In half an hour we got to **Brașov**: check-in, shower and out on foot to the **Râșnov Fortress**, on top of a rocky hill near the Hollywood-style town sign. You get there on foot or by cable car, and the atmosphere is more *Lord of the Rings* than vampires. With a bit of time you continue toward the old town: **Piața Sfatului**, with the Council House (14th century) and the Clock Tower, and the **Black Church (Biserica Neagră)**, the largest Gothic church in Romania, open until late afternoon.

Dinner is at **Sergiana**: steaming soups served in hollowed-out bread loaves, paprika meat stews, polenta with sour cream and melted cheese. Watch out for the prices of wine and palinka, often above average; book ahead by email. Alternatively there is **Casa Românească**, a rustic tavern with pan-fried chicken, smoked mutton and sarmale. For after dinner, the **Deane's Irish Pub & Grill** or the **Musik Cafe**.

### Day 3 — Saxon Villages, Sighișoara and Salina Turda
It's the longest day at the wheel, five hours of driving in all: you leave early and you have to stick to the schedule.

In the morning you can stop by the **Bear Sanctuary** (guided tour, tickets online within 14 days before). The bears are active only in the morning, so it has to be the first thing of the day. When the Transfăgărășan is open, the advice is to skip it, because you see bears along the road on Day 4 anyway.

An hour and twenty minutes later you reach **Viscri** (an optional stop, if time allows), one of the two main Saxon villages of Transylvania, with the 12th-century **Fortified Church**, a UNESCO World Heritage site (12 LEI/pax). I did all the optional stops: the Bear Sanctuary, Viscri and also the Făgăraș Citadel.

Then **Sighișoara**, a UNESCO medieval town with cobbled streets and colorful houses. The must-sees are the Clock Tower and the birthplace of Count Dracula (5 LEI to go up to the upper floor, at the **Vlad Dracul Restaurant**). For lunch you choose between **Gasthaus Altepost** and the Vlad Dracul Restaurant itself. Don't miss the climb to the upper part of town along **The Covered Stairway**, the covered wooden staircase.

In about 2h by car we reached the underground salt mine of **Salina Turda** (tickets included in the tour package, €10 on weekdays and €12 on weekends), one of the most spectacular attractions of the trip. The visit lasts 2-3 hours; at the bottom of the salt mine there are foosball, ping pong and rowboats for rent for a ride on the underground lake. The guide is booked (100 LEI, paid on site, in cash).

In the late afternoon we set off again for **Sibiu** (1h30 by car), with check-in and a dinner reservation. The options: **Crama Sibiul Vechi**, the most characteristic restaurant in the city, in a rustic cellar (minced meat meatballs, farmer's stew with polenta; few seats, reservation mandatory), or **La Dobrun**, very close to the hotel, or **Crama Ileana**. After dinner at the **Oldie's Pub** (good for large groups) or at the **Geea Caffe** (more intimate).

### Day 4 — Sibiu, Transfăgărășan and Back to Bucharest
It's the last day before heading home and there are still a lot of kilometers: early alarm.

In the morning we did the **Sibiu city tour** in English (2h, local guide by reservation): the crowded Piața Mare, the Brukenthal Museum with its art collection, the **Bridge of Lies (Podul Minciunilor)**, the medieval walls and towers of this UNESCO World Heritage city. A quick lunch or takeaway, then on the move again.

In the early afternoon, 1h30 by car to reach the **Transfăgărășan**, often described as one of the most beautiful roads in the world: spectacular hairpin bends up to 2,000 meters of altitude, with a recommended stop at the **Balea Lac Restaurant** on Lake Bâlea. **The stretch is open only from June 20 to October 31** (the dates should always be checked on the official website). In winter you only go up to the lake by cable car, but you can stop at the Balea Ice Hotel (open from December to mid-April) for a drink in the Ice Bar.

When the road is open you continue south: **another 3h45 by car to Bucharest**, leaving Lake Bâlea in the early afternoon to keep to the schedule. It's the stretch where you are most likely to see **bears** along the roadway: photos and videos yes, food and getting out of the car no. I did the Transfăgărășan both ways, by car along the famous road and by cable car. You can make a photo stop at the **Vidraru Dam**, a large concrete reservoir among the mountains.

In the evening we returned the car to Avis (Gran Hotel Bucuresti). For the **farewell dinner** (not included) there is **Hanu' lui Manuc**, in the Lipscani district: the idea is similar to Caru' cu Bere, prices are higher but the food is excellent, and you need to book. The other option is **Excalibur**, a medieval-style place: nice, but a bit overrated. For those who still have energy there is an organized **pub crawl** (€16/pax, 4 shots in 4 different pubs plus club entry), or the venues of the district: **Freddo**, **St. Patrick** and the lovely open-air garden of **Grădina Dorobanți**.

### Day 5 — Bucharest: Check-out, Spa and Goodbyes
The last day depends on the flight time: half a day (or the whole day) at **Therme București**. It's a huge spa park divided into three areas (water games, relaxation, restaurants/bars); you pay for everything with a wristband and settle up on the way out, €35 for the whole day. The spa is right next to the airport, so you can leave calmly even just 2 hours before the flight.

## What to see / what to do
- **Peleș Castle**, maybe the most beautiful architecturally of the whole trip.
- The **underground salt mine of Salina Turda**, with the lake you can row on and the games at the bottom.
- The **Transfăgărășan**, Romania's scenic road par excellence.
- The UNESCO old town of **Sighișoara**, with the birthplace of Count Dracula.
- **Less touristy gem:** the Râșnov Fortress in Brașov, much less crowded than Bran Castle and just as beautiful, with a more "epic" than "vampiric" atmosphere.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
**Bucharest, arrival — Hotel Duke Romana** *(Piața Romană area)*
I slept here the first night, a few steps from the Piața Romană metro stop and 20-30 minutes' walk from the old town. On Tripadvisor it's a 3-star with mixed but overall positive reviews. Those who booked it through Booking report a good breakfast, comfortable beds and great value for money; some complain about the attic rooms with low ceilings and impractical skylight windows, and occasional air conditioning problems.
*Alternatives in the area:* budget: Hello Hotels Bucharest, Trianon Hotel · mid-range: Casa Mendeleev (Duke Romana falls in this range anyway) · luxury (affordable): Epoque Hotel, Grand Hotel Continental

**Brașov — Curtea Brașoveana**
In Brașov I chose a small place about ten minutes' walk from the old town. It's among the most appreciated in the city, with a very high score (9.4 on Booking) from almost 600 reviews. The comments always come back to the same points: very helpful staff, clean and well-kept rooms, a lovely garden and the atmosphere of a hidden corner in the old center.
*Alternatives in the area:* budget: Drachenhaus, Pensiunea Casa Cranta · mid-range: Vila Katharina (Curtea Brașoveana is already at this level, even above) · luxury (affordable): Casa Wagner, Radisson Blu Aurum Hotel Brașov

**Sibiu — MyContinental Sibiu**
In Sibiu I slept in a modern place near the old town, with good ratings (8.5 on Booking, "Very Good" on Expedia). The reviews mention the upper-floor rooms with city views and the generous breakfast, along with a few isolated complaints about slow wifi and elevators sometimes out of order.
*Alternatives in the area:* budget: Casa Timpuri Vechi, Pensiunea Central Sibiu · mid-range: Casa Luxemburg (MyContinental is in this range anyway) · luxury (affordable): Noblesse Boutique Hotel, Mina Luxury Boutique Hotel

**Bucharest, return — Hotel Continental Forum** *(Palace of the Parliament area)*
For the last night I slept near the Palace of the Parliament, in a 4-star completely renovated in 2021. The reviews are very positive: praise goes to the generous breakfast (with vegan options too), the friendly and helpful staff, the clean and modern rooms.
*Alternatives in the area:* budget: Hotel Parliament, Volo Hotel Bucharest · mid-range: Filitti Boutique Hotel (Continental Forum is already in this range) · luxury (affordable): Radisson Blu Hotel Bucharest, JW Marriott Bucharest Grand Hotel

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Caution at the wheel, always**: Romania has the highest road mortality rate in the European Union (78 deaths/million inhabitants in 2024, against an EU average of 44). The mountain roads are beautiful but demanding, with tight hairpin bends and weather that changes quickly. Better to always leave with a margin and not drive in a hurry to chase the clock.
- Before leaving, check the opening of the Transfăgărășan on the official website: it is normally open only from June 20 to October 31.
- You need enough lei in cash: many traditional restaurants and local guides often take only that.
- The smaller traditional restaurants should be booked in good time (Crama Sibiul Vechi has only 5 tables).
- You don't feed the bears, and if you see one along the road you don't get out of the car.

## Chiusura personale
Mistakes not to repeat, this time, I have none. One piece of advice, yes, and it's about the road. In Romania you have to drive very carefully, especially at night: you still often run into horse-drawn carts with no lights. And the country has one of the highest road accident rates in Europe. Leave with a margin, don't chase the clock and, when you can, avoid driving in the dark.
