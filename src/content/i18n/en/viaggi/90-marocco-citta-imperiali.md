# Morocco: the Imperial Cities and the Blue City in 11 Days

## Apertura personale
Rick didn't take this itinerary. It's a proposal built on research, for anyone who wants northern Morocco: the medinas, mosques and palaces of the dynasties that ruled it. The trip Rick took is a different one, in the south: *Morocco: from Marrakech to the Sahara*, with the night in the Erg Chebbi desert.

There are four imperial cities: Fez, Meknes, Rabat and Marrakech. Each was the capital in a different era, and each has its own character. Three of them are here, plus **Chefchaouen**, the blue city in the Rif mountains, and the Roman ruins of **Volubilis**. Marrakech stays out because it's the starting point of the other itinerary. The two trips fit together well, and further down there's the version that joins them.

You fly into **Tangier** and out of **Casablanca**. The reason is geography. Chefchaouen is the stop closest to Tangier, about 110 km away, and it has no railway: it makes sense to do it first, by bus, and then head down to Fez. From there on you travel by train along the ONCF line, heading west, all the way to the Atlantic. Casablanca has more intercontinental flights than Tangier, and the last stretch from Rabat is on **Al Boraq**, the high-speed train. It also works the other way around, flying into Casablanca and out of Tangier.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** spring and fall. In summer Fez and Meknes are very hot. During Ramadan many restaurants are closed during the day and monument hours change.
- **Documents:** for Italian citizens, a passport valid for the whole stay, no visa for tourism up to 3 months. At the airport they may ask for your return ticket.
- **Currency and payments:** Moroccan dirham (MAD), a currency you can't take out of the country. Cards work in hotels and many city restaurants, but in the medinas, at food stalls and for taxis you need cash.
- **Trains:** the ONCF network links Fez, Meknes, Rabat and Casablanca with frequent trains. Al Boraq, the high-speed train, runs between Tangier, Rabat and Casablanca: Tangier to Casablanca takes about 2 hours 10 minutes. Tickets are sold on the official site oncf-voyages.ma or at the station. Schedules and travel times should be rechecked there before you go.
- **Buses:** CTM has stops in Tangier, Chefchaouen and Fez, with tickets on booking.ctm.ma. Chefchaouen has no train station.
- **Medinas:** cars can't enter the Fez medina. With luggage, ask your riad to send someone to the nearest gate.
- **Safety:** in the medinas and tourist areas there are pickpockets and fake guides. Official guides carry a badge: ask to see it before setting off with anyone. Moroccan law has zero tolerance for drugs of any kind, cannabis included, the Rif included.
- **Mosques:** in Morocco non-Muslims generally can't enter mosques in use. The Hassan II Mosque in Casablanca can be visited on a guided tour.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Tangier
You land in Tangier, the city on the Strait of Gibraltar, with Spain across the water. The first day is only for arriving. If the afternoon is free, walk up through the **medina** to the **kasbah**, the upper quarter above the port, and find a terrace with a view of the Strait. Early dinner: tomorrow you leave for the mountains.

### Day 2 — From Tangier to Chefchaouen, the blue city
About 110 km southeast, into the **Rif** mountains. You go by CTM bus or with a car and driver, in a good couple of hours. **Chefchaouen** was founded in 1471 as a fortress against the Portuguese, and it grew with the Andalusians, Moriscos and Sephardic Jews who came from Spain. The houses painted blue are the ones you see in every photo. There are several stories about why they're blue, and none of them is certain.

Leave the afternoon to the medina, with no destination. In the evening head down to **Outa el-Hammam** square, in front of the kasbah and the Great Mosque, when the cafés fill up.

### Day 3 — Chefchaouen: the medina, Ras el-Ma and the Spanish Mosque
Early in the morning the medina is almost empty, and it's the time to photograph the alleys. Go into the **kasbah**, the old fortress, now a museum. Then climb toward **Ras el-Ma**, the spring at the foot of the mountain, and continue on the trail to the **Spanish Mosque**, built in the 1920s on the hill opposite. From up there you see the whole blue city below the mountains, and it's the right place for sunset.

If you'd rather have nature, spend the day in **Akchour**, about 30 km away, among the river's gorges and waterfalls, with a car and driver.

### Day 4 — From Chefchaouen to Fez
A transfer day: about 200 km south, 4-5 hours on the CTM bus. The landscape shifts from the Rif mountains to the farmed hills around Fez. You arrive in the afternoon. Pick a riad inside the medina or just outside it, near a gate taxis can reach. The first evening, dinner on a rooftop is enough, watching the medina light up.

### Day 5 — Fez el-Bali: madrasas, tanneries and souks
**Fez** was founded in 789 by Idris I and remained the capital until 1912. The old city, **Fez el-Bali**, has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1981 and is one of the largest car-free urban areas in the world. For the first day, an official guide is worth it: the medina is a maze, and a morning with someone who knows it saves you hours lost and pushy sellers.

You enter through **Bab Bou Jeloud**, the blue gate, and walk down the Talaa Kebira. The first stop is the **Bou Inania Madrasa**, built between 1350 and 1355: it's the only madrasa in Morocco that also served as a Friday mosque. Further down is the **Al-Attarine Madrasa**, from 1323-1325, small and full of decoration. Next to it is the **Qarawiyyin**, founded as a mosque in 857-859 by Fatima al-Fihri: non-Muslims don't go in, but through the open doors you can see part of the courtyard. The afternoon is for the **Chouara tanneries**: you look down at the vats from the terraces of the leather shops around them. The smell is strong, so be ready for it.

### Day 6 — Fez: Fez Jdid, the Mellah and the medina from above
Today you leave the old city. **Fez Jdid**, the Marinids' new city, has the golden gates of the **Royal Palace**, which you can only see from outside, and the **Mellah**, the old Jewish quarter. Nearby are the **Jnan Sbil** gardens, for a break in the shade.

The afternoon is free to go back to the souks at an easy pace, or for a hammam. Before sunset, head up to the hills north of the medina, toward the **Marinid Tombs**, where you can see all of Fez el-Bali. Go by taxi; better not on foot alone once it's dark.

### Day 7 — Volubilis and Moulay Idriss, night in Meknes
In the morning take the train to **Meknes**, about 40 minutes. Drop your bags and continue with a taxi for the day to **Volubilis**, about 30 km away. It was already a town in the 3rd century BC and later the capital of the kingdom of Mauretania. Rome annexed it in 44 AD, and by the late 2nd century it had about 20,000 inhabitants, who lived mostly on olive oil. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1997. You walk along the **Decumanus Maximus** to the **Arch of Caracalla**, from 217, past the basilica and the Capitol, and above all you look at the **mosaics** left in the houses, like the Labours of Hercules and Orpheus. The site is open and has almost no shade: bring a hat and water.

A few kilometers away is **Moulay Idriss Zerhoun**, the holy town built around the mausoleum of Idris I. He founded the Idrisid dynasty at Volubilis in 787-788, and was killed there in 791. Walk around the town and climb to a viewpoint to see it from above. The mausoleum is a pilgrimage site, and like the mosques it's generally not open to non-Muslims. In the evening you return to Meknes.

### Day 8 — Meknes, the city of Moulay Ismail, then the train to Rabat
**Meknes** is the city of Moulay Ismail, the sultan who made it his capital and reigned from 1672 to 1727. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1996. Start from **Bab Mansour**, the monumental gate finished in 1732 by his son, and the **el-Hedim** square in front of it, whose name means "rubble": it comes from the demolitions carried out to open it. Then the **Mausoleum of Moulay Ismail**, a religious site also open to visitors, and the **Heri es-Souani**, the huge granary with its rows of arches, next to the Agdal basin. In the medina is Meknes's own **Bou Inania Madrasa**, older than the one in Fez.

Meknes is calmer than Fez, and easier to explore on your own. In the afternoon take the train to **Rabat**, about 2 and a half hours.

### Day 9 — Rabat: the Kasbah of the Udayas, the Hassan Tower and the Chellah
**Rabat** has been Morocco's capital since independence in 1956, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2012, for both its old and modern city. Compared with Fez it's tidier and you get around without pressure. The morning is for the **Kasbah of the Udayas**, at the mouth of the Bou Regreg river: you enter through the Almohad gate of **Bab Oudaya**, walk among the white and blue houses to the viewpoint over the Atlantic and Salé, and go down into the **Andalusian Gardens**. Entry to the kasbah is free.

In the afternoon, the **Hassan Tower**, the never-finished minaret of a mosque commissioned by the Almohad sultan Yaqub al-Mansur in the 12th century, with the **Mausoleum of Mohammed V** in front of it, completed in 1971, where King Hassan II and Prince Abdallah are also buried. Then the **Chellah**, where the ruins of the Roman city of Sala and the Marinid necropolis sit together, with storks nesting on the old minaret. The tram is the way to get around.

### Day 10 — By train to Casablanca and the Hassan II Mosque
From **Rabat Agdal** station take **Al Boraq** to **Casa Voyageurs**, in under an hour. In Casablanca there's one visit not to miss: the **Hassan II Mosque**, on the sea, which non-Muslims can only see on the guided tour. The tour covers the prayer hall, the minaret hall and the ablution halls. Tickets are 140 dirhams for foreign adults (2026). From mid-September to mid-March the tours run at 9, 10, 11, 12 and 3 p.m. Saturday to Thursday, and at 9, 10 and 3 p.m. on Friday. From mid-March to mid-September there's also a 4 p.m. tour. Shoulders and knees must be covered, and shoes come off at the entrance.

In the evening, walk along the seafront for the last dinner of the trip.

### Day 11 — Departure from Casablanca
Transfer to Mohammed V airport, by train or taxi, and flight home. Before leaving, change back your leftover dirhams: you can't exchange them outside Morocco.

## How it combines with Marrakech and the desert
The two itineraries split at **Fez**. This one continues west, by train, to the Atlantic. The other, the one Rick took, is all in the south: Marrakech, Aït Benhaddou, the Todra Gorge, the Erg Chebbi desert and the coast at Essaouira.

To join them, drop Rabat and Casablanca and head south from Fez toward the Sahara. You fly into Tangier and out of Marrakech, in about 13 days:

- **Days 1-6:** as above, from Tangier to Chefchaouen and then Fez.
- **Day 7:** Volubilis, Moulay Idriss and Meknes as a day trip from Fez, with a car and driver. Night in Fez.
- **Day 8:** from Fez to **Merzouga**, about 460 km across the Middle Atlas. It's a full day on the road, best done with a van and driver. Night in a tented camp at the foot of the Erg Chebbi dunes.
- **Days 9-10:** Rick's itinerary in reverse. The Todra Gorge and a night in Aït Benhaddou, then the Telouet Kasbah and the Atlas pass to Marrakech.
- **Days 11-13:** Marrakech, with the medina, Jemaa el-Fna and the Jardin Majorelle, and the flight home.

With 3-4 extra days, add the Draa valley, Agadir and Essaouira, as in Rick's trip. Routes, times, tented camps and desert tips are in the itinerary [Morocco: from Marrakech to the Sahara](/viaggi/marocco-itinerario).

## What to see / what to do
- **Chefchaouen:** the blue medina at dawn, Outa el-Hammam in the evening, sunset from the Spanish Mosque. With an extra day, go to Akchour.
- **Fez:** the Bou Inania and Al-Attarine madrasas, the Chouara tanneries from the terraces, Bab Bou Jeloud, the medina seen from the Marinid Tombs.
- **Volubilis:** the Decumanus Maximus, the Arch of Caracalla and the mosaics in the Roman houses.
- **Meknes:** Bab Mansour, el-Hedim square and the Heri es-Souani.
- **Rabat:** the Kasbah of the Udayas and the Andalusian Gardens, the Hassan Tower, the Mausoleum of Mohammed V, the Chellah.
- **Casablanca:** the Hassan II Mosque on the guided tour.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **In Fez, an official guide on the first day.** Ask to see the badge before setting off. Whoever offers on the street usually ends up taking you to partner shops.
- **Don't believe anyone who says a street is closed.** It's the most common way to steer tourists into a shop.
- **Book a reachable riad.** Cars can't enter the Fez medina: ask the riad where the taxi drops you and whether they send someone for the luggage.
- **Check train times on oncf-voyages.ma.** They change with the seasons and during Ramadan.
- **Covering clothes for mosques and religious sites.** At the Hassan II Mosque you can't enter in shorts or sleeveless.
- **Volubilis early in the morning.** The site is almost entirely in the sun.
- **No drugs, not even in the Rif.** Around Chefchaouen some people offer them, but Moroccan law punishes it with prison.
- **Change back your dirhams before the flight.** You can't exchange them outside Morocco.

## Chiusura personale
This is Morocco of the cities, the side usually seen less on a first trip, when everyone heads for Marrakech and the desert. Eleven days are enough for Chefchaouen, Fez, Meknes with Volubilis, Rabat and Casablanca, almost all by train. With two weeks you can join it to the southern itinerary, and head down from Fez to the dunes of Erg Chebbi.
