# Morocco: the Imperial Cities and the Blue City in 11 Days

## Apertura personale
This is my loop through northern Morocco: the medinas, mosques and palaces of the dynasties that ruled it. The south, from Marrakech to the Erg Chebbi desert, is in the other itinerary, *Morocco: from Marrakech to the Sahara*.

There are four imperial cities: Fez, Meknes, Rabat and Marrakech. They were capitals in different eras, and each has its own character. Three of them are here, plus **Chefchaouen**, the blue city in the Rif mountains, and the Roman ruins of **Volubilis**. Marrakech is left out because it's the starting point of the other itinerary. The two trips fit together well, and further down there's the version that joins them.

You fly into **Tangier** and out of **Casablanca**. The reason is geography. Chefchaouen is the stop closest to Tangier: it's best done first, before heading down to Fez. From there you go west, through Volubilis and Meknes, to Rabat and the Atlantic. All by road: that's how I prefer it, because you stop where you want, and Volubilis and Moulay Idriss lie along the way. The longest legs are Chefchaouen-Fez and Meknes-Rabat. It also works in reverse, flying into Casablanca and out of Tangier.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** spring and fall. In summer Fez and Meknes are very hot. During Ramadan the opening hours of shops and restaurants change, and eating, drinking or smoking in public during the day can cause offence.
- **Documents:** Italian citizens need a passport valid at least for the whole stay, and no visa for tourism up to 3 months. At the airport you may be asked for a return ticket and proof of sufficient funds (Viaggiare Sicuri, updated April 2026).
- **Currency and payments:** Moroccan dirham (MAD). Taking dirhams out of Morocco is forbidden, and at the end of the trip you can only change back those you bought, showing the receipts: keep them. Cards work in hotels and many restaurants in the cities, but in the medinas, at food stalls and for taxis you need cash.
- **Getting around:** everything by road, in daylight only: Viaggiare Sicuri advises avoiding night driving outside towns. You go with a car and driver or a rental car. The distances and driving times given for each day are calculated on the road route (OSRM, without stops or traffic): in reality it takes longer.
- **Buses:** CTM buses also run between Tangier, Chefchaouen and Fez, useful for the first two legs. Check services, times and tickets on ctm.ma.
- **Train, as an alternative:** Fez, Meknes, Rabat and Casablanca are also on the ONCF line (timetables on oncf-voyages.ma). Chefchaouen has no railway.
- **Medinas:** cars can't enter the Fez medina. With luggage, ask the riad to send someone to the nearest gate.
- **Safety:** pickpocketing and fake guides are common in the medinas and tourist areas. Official guides carry a badge: ask to see it before setting off with anyone. Morocco has zero tolerance for drugs, cannabis included, even in the Rif: you risk prison and heavy fines.
- **Mosques:** in Morocco non-Muslims generally don't enter mosques in use. The Hassan II Mosque in Casablanca can be visited on a guided tour.
- **From the airport to the centre:** **Tangier Ibn Battuta (TNG)**, about 15 km from the centre: **grand taxi** from the rank outside arrivals, about 100-150 MAD by day and more in the evening, 20-30 minutes, price to be agreed before getting in; bus to the centre and Tanger Ville railway station about 40 MAD, cash on board, roughly one an hour until the evening. The trip ends at **Casablanca Mohammed V (CMN)**, about 30 km out: **ONCF Al Bidaoui train** to Casa-Voyageurs, about 50 MAD in second class, about 40 minutes, roughly one train an hour from 6am to 10pm, ticket at the station or on oncf-voyages.ma; **grand taxi** about 250-350 MAD, more at night · the classic mistake is getting in without fixing the price, or accepting unrequested help with the bags.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Tangier
You land in Tangier, the city on the Strait of Gibraltar, with Spain on the other side of the sea. The first day is just for arriving: from Ibn Battuta airport to the center it's about twenty minutes by taxi, and the fare should be agreed before getting in. It's best to sleep in the medina or just outside it, near the **Grand Socco**, the big tree-lined square where you enter the old town, which the car and driver can reach without getting lost in the alleys.

If the afternoon is free, walk up through the **medina** to the **kasbah**, the upper quarter above the port, and look for a terrace with a view over the Strait. On the way you pass the **Petit Socco**, the little café square where writers and travelers once met, and if you still have energy you can go down to **Café Hafa**, opened in the 1920s on terraces dropping to the sea: you drink mint tea looking at the lights of Spain. *People offering themselves as guides on the street aren't needed: the Tangier medina is small and easy to explore alone.* Early dinner: tomorrow you leave for the mountains.

**In practice:** taxi from the airport about 20 minutes, fare to be agreed first · dirhams available from ATMs at the airport and in town · for the first night, a riad in the kasbah or a hotel near the Grand Socco.

### Day 2 — From Tangier to Chefchaouen, the blue city
About 110 km southeast, in the **Rif** mountains: about 2 hours of driving, with the car and driver or with the CTM bus. The road passes Tétouan and then climbs into the hills, with some bends and a few checkpoints. **Chefchaouen** was founded in 1471 as a fortress against the Portuguese, and grew with the Andalusians, Moriscos and Jews who arrived from the Iberian Peninsula. The houses painted blue are the ones you see in every photo. There are several versions of why they're blue, and none is certain.

Cars can't enter the medina: you leave the car in one of the guarded car parks outside the walls and reach the riad on foot, often with a porter and his cart. The afternoon should be left to the medina, without a destination, among stairways, painted doors and wool and textile workshops. *In the Rif people often offer hashish on the street: a firm no is enough, and it's better not to stop and argue.* In the evening you go down to **Outa el-Hammam** square, in front of the kasbah and the Great Mosque, when the cafés fill up.

**In practice:** Tangier-Chefchaouen about 2.5 hours with stops · guarded car parks outside the medina, a few dirhams a night · porters for luggage, tip to be agreed · riads are small and in demand: book ahead, especially in spring.

### Day 3 — Chefchaouen: the medina, Ras el-Ma and the Spanish Mosque
Early in the morning the medina is almost empty, and that's the time to photograph the alleys. You go into the **kasbah**, the old fortress, now a museum: inside there's an Andalusian garden, and from the tower you see the rooftops and the square from above. Then you climb towards **Ras el-Ma**, the spring at the foot of the mountain, where the water runs into channels and wash basins still in use, and carry on along the path to the **Spanish Mosque**, built in the 1920s on the hill opposite. From up there you see the whole blue city beneath the mountains, and it's the right place for sunset.

If you prefer nature, you can spend the day at **Akchour**, about 30 km away (40 minutes by road), among the gorges and waterfalls of the river, with a car and driver. From there two trails follow the river, one towards the waterfalls and one towards the **Pont de Dieu**, a natural rock arch over the gorge; each takes a few hours there and back. *You need shoes with a good sole: there are fords and stretches of wet rock.*

**In practice:** kasbah with an inexpensive ticket, best in the morning · from Ras el-Ma to the Spanish Mosque about half an hour uphill, little shade · Akchour by car with driver for the day, price to be fixed the evening before · if you come back after dark, use your phone light on the path.

### Day 4 — From Chefchaouen to Fez
A transfer day: about 190 km south, almost 4 hours of driving not counting stops, with the car and driver or with the CTM bus. The landscape goes from the Rif mountains to the cultivated hills around Fez. Halfway you pass through **Ouezzane**, one of Morocco's holy towns, a good place for a coffee break. It's best to leave after breakfast, to arrive in daylight and not drive the last stretch in the dark.

You arrive in the afternoon. The riad should be inside the medina or just outside it, near a gate reachable by taxi. Cars stop at the gates, and from there you go on foot; the riad usually sends someone to meet guests. *In front of the gates of Fez, fake guides often approach, offering to take you to your hotel and then asking for money or steering you into shops: better to be met by the riad.* The first evening, a rooftop dinner is enough, watching the medina light up.

**In practice:** Chefchaouen-Fez about 4.5 hours with one stop · the handiest gates for cars are Bab Bou Jeloud and Bab Guissa · book an official guide for tomorrow through the riad.

### Day 5 — Fez el-Bali: madrasas, tanneries and souks
**Fez** was founded in 789 by Idris I and remained a capital until 1912. The old city, **Fez el-Bali**, has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1981 and is one of the largest and oldest urban pedestrian zones in the world. For the first day an official guide is worth it: the medina is a maze, and a morning with someone who knows it saves hours of getting lost and pushy sellers.

Enter through **Bab Bou Jeloud**, the blue gate, and walk down the Talaa Kebira. The first stop is the **Bou Inania Madrasa**, built between 1350 and 1355: it's the only madrasa in Morocco that also served as a Friday mosque. Further down is the **al-Attarine Madrasa**, from 1323-1325, small and full of decoration. Next to it is the **Qarawiyyin**, founded as a mosque in 857-859 by Fatima al-Fihri: non-Muslims don't enter, but through the open doors you can see part of the courtyard. The afternoon is for the **Chouara tanneries**: you look at the vats from above, from the terraces of the leather shops around them. The smell is strong, be ready for it.

**In practice:** official guide for half a day, booked through the riad, with a clear price and no compulsory shop stops · Bou Inania about 20 dirhams, Attarine with a similar ticket · you reach the tanneries through the leather shops, with no ticket: you're expected to look at the goods or leave a tip.

### Day 6 — Fez: Fez Jdid, the Mellah and the medina from above
Today you leave the old city. **Fez Jdid**, the new city of the Marinids, has the golden doors of the **Royal Palace**, which can only be seen from outside, and the **Mellah**, the old Jewish quarter. In the Mellah you can visit the restored **Ibn Danan synagogue** and the **Jewish cemetery**, with white tombs in rows on the hillside; the houses have wooden balconies over the street, unlike those in the medina. Not far away are the **Jnan Sbil** gardens, for a break in the shade.

The afternoon is free to go back to the souks at leisure, or for a hammam. Neighborhood hammams are cheap but basic, and you bring your own soap and towel; those in riads and spas cost more and are easier for first-timers. Before sunset you go up the hills north of the medina to the **Marinid Tombs**, from where you can see all of Fez el-Bali. You get there by taxi. *At sunset you're never alone up there: come down before dark, by taxi, rather than walking back along the paths.*

**In practice:** Royal Palace from the outside only · synagogue and Jewish cemetery with a donation or modest ticket · Fez petit taxis are red and use the meter: ask for it to be switched on · for the hammam, ask the riad which one to choose.

### Day 7 — Volubilis and Moulay Idriss, night in Meknes
Leave Fez by car, with the luggage on board, and drive straight to **Volubilis**: about 70 km, an hour and a half by road. From Meknes it's about half an hour. It was already a town in the 3rd century BC, and may have been the capital of the kingdom of Mauretania. Rome annexed it in 44 AD, and by the late 2nd century it had about 20,000 inhabitants, who lived mainly on olive oil. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1997. Walk along the **Decumanus Maximus** to the **Arch of Caracalla**, from 217, pass the basilica and the Capitol, and above all look at the **mosaics** left in the houses, such as the Labours of Hercules and Orpheus. The site is open and has almost no shade: hat and water.

About 4 km away is **Moulay Idriss Zerhoun**, the holy town built around the mausoleum of Idris I. He founded the Idrisid dynasty at Volubilis in 787-788, and was killed there in 791. Walk around the town and climb to a viewpoint to see it from above. The mausoleum is a place of pilgrimage and is not open to non-Muslims. Then drive down to **Meknes**, about 30 km, for the night.

**In practice:** Volubilis about 70 dirhams, official guides available at the entrance for a fee · open from morning to late afternoon, with hours that change with the season · Fez-Volubilis about 1.5 hours, Volubilis-Meknes about half an hour · have lunch in Moulay Idriss, but go down to Meknes to sleep.

### Day 8 — Meknes, the city of Moulay Ismail, then by car to Rabat
**Meknes** is the city of Moulay Ismail, the sultan who made it his capital and reigned from 1672 to 1727. It has been a UNESCO site since 1996. You start from **Bab Mansour**, the monumental gate completed in 1732 by his son, and from **el-Hedim** square in front of it, whose name means "rubble": it comes from the demolitions carried out to open it. Then the **Mausoleum of Moulay Ismail**, a religious site also open to visitors, and the **Heri es-Souani**, the huge granary with rows of arches, next to the Agdal basin. In the medina is the **Bou Inania Madrasa** of Meknes, from the same period as the one in Fez, smaller and almost always quiet, with a roof from which you can see the minarets. *The mausoleum was closed for a long time for restoration: ask at your hotel whether it can be visited before going.*

Meknes is calmer than Fez, and easier to explore on your own. Lunch is around el-Hedim square, looking at the gate. In the afternoon you drive to **Rabat**: about 140 km, almost 2 hours, nearly all on the motorway. You arrive in time to settle in and take a stroll in Rabat's small, orderly medina, or along the Bou Regreg riverfront before dinner.

**In practice:** Meknes-Rabat about 140 km on the toll motorway · Heri es-Souani and the madrasa with inexpensive tickets · to sleep in Rabat, the medina or the area between the medina and the Hassan Tower.

### Day 9 — Rabat: the Kasbah of the Udayas, the Hassan Tower and the Chellah
**Rabat** has been the capital of Morocco since 1912, and stayed so after independence in 1956; it has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2012, with both the old and the modern city. Compared with Fez it's tidier and you get around without pressure. The morning is for the **Kasbah of the Udayas**, at the mouth of the Bou Regreg river: enter through the Almohad gate of **Bab Oudaya**, walk among the white and blue houses to the viewpoint over the Atlantic and Salé, and go down into the **Andalusian Gardens**. Entry to the kasbah is free.

In the afternoon, the **Hassan Tower**, the unfinished minaret of a mosque commissioned by the Almohad sultan Yaqub al-Mansur in the 12th century, with the **Mausoleum of Mohammed V** in front of it, completed in 1971, where Hassan II and Prince Abdallah are also buried. Then the **Chellah**, where the ruins of the Roman town of Sala and the Marinid necropolis stand together, with storks nesting on the old minaret. To get around there's the tram.

**In practice:** Kasbah of the Udayas free entry · Hassan Tower and Mausoleum of Mohammed V free, with modest clothing · Chellah with a paid ticket, open until late afternoon · the tram links the medina, the Hassan Tower and Salé.

### Day 10 — From Rabat to Casablanca and the Hassan II Mosque
From Rabat to **Casablanca** it's about 95 km along the coast, a little over an hour by road. In Casablanca there's only one visit not to miss: the **Hassan II Mosque**, on the sea, which non-Muslims can only see on a guided tour. The tour goes through the prayer hall, the minaret hall and the ablution halls. Tickets cost 140 dirhams for foreign adults (November 2026). There are several tours a day, in the morning and early afternoon, and fewer on Fridays: up-to-date times are on fmh2.ma, where they're given in GMT. Shoulders, torso and legs must be covered to below the knees, and shoes come off at the entrance.

After the mosque you can go through the **Habous quarter**, the "new medina" built by the French in the 1930s, tidy and full of bookshops and copperware shops, and through the center with its Art Deco buildings along Boulevard Mohammed V. Casablanca is a big, congested city, better covered by taxi than on foot. In the evening, a walk along the seafront for the last dinner of the trip. *The Corniche seafront towards Ain Diab is the liveliest in the evening.*

**In practice:** Rabat-Casablanca a little over an hour on the motorway · Hassan II Mosque 140 dirhams, visit of about an hour, guarded parking nearby · in town, red petit taxis, metered fare · the last night is best spent not far from the airport road.

### Day 11 — Departure from Casablanca
Transfer to Mohammed V airport, about 30 km from the center (a good half hour by car or taxi), and flight home. Before leaving, leftover dirhams should be changed back, with the exchange receipts: they can't be taken out of Morocco. With Casablanca traffic, half an hour can become an hour: leave with a wide margin, especially at the morning and late-afternoon rush hours.

If your flight is in the afternoon, you can use the morning for one last visit to the **Marché Central**, the covered market in the center with its stalls of fish, olives and flowers, or for a quiet walk along the seafront. At the airport, checks start at the terminal entrance, followed by passport control and security: you queue several times. *It's best to change your dirhams back as soon as you reach the airport, before the checks, rather than leaving it to the last minute.*

**In practice:** Mohammed V airport about 30 km from the center, 40 minutes to an hour with traffic · at the airport at least 3 hours before the flight · keep the exchange receipts to convert dirhams back.

## How it combines with Marrakech and the desert
The two itineraries split at **Fez**. This one continues west, by road, to the Atlantic. The other is all in the south: Marrakech, Aït Benhaddou, the Todra Gorge, the Erg Chebbi desert and the coast at Essaouira.

To join them, cut Rabat and Casablanca and head down from Fez toward the Sahara. Fly into Tangier and out of Marrakech, in about 13 days:

- **Days 1-6:** as above, from Tangier to Chefchaouen and then to Fez.
- **Day 7:** Volubilis, Moulay Idriss and Meknes as a day trip from Fez, with a car and driver. Night in Fez.
- **Day 8:** from Fez to **Merzouga**, across the Middle Atlas: about 465 km, more than 8 hours of driving without stops. It's a full day on the road, to do with a van and a driver. Night in a tented camp at the foot of the Erg Chebbi dunes.
- **Days 9-10:** the southern itinerary in reverse. The Todra Gorge and the night in Aït Benhaddou, then the Telouet Kasbah and the Atlas pass to Marrakech.
- **Days 11-13:** Marrakech, with the medina, Jemaa el-Fna and the Jardin Majorelle, and the flight home.

With 3-4 more days you can add the Draa valley, Agadir and Essaouira, as in the southern itinerary. Routes, times, tented camps and tips for the desert are in the itinerary [Morocco: from Marrakech to the Sahara](/viaggi/marocco-itinerario).

## What to see / what to do
- **Chefchaouen:** the blue medina at dawn, Outa el-Hammam in the evening, sunset from the Spanish Mosque. With an extra day, go to Akchour.
- **Fez:** the Bou Inania and al-Attarine madrasas, the Chouara tanneries from the terraces, Bab Bou Jeloud, the medina seen from the Marinid Tombs.
- **Volubilis:** the Decumanus Maximus, the Arch of Caracalla and the mosaics in the Roman houses.
- **Meknes:** Bab Mansour, el-Hedim square and the Heri es-Souani.
- **Rabat:** the Kasbah of the Udayas and the Andalusian Gardens, the Hassan Tower, the Mausoleum of Mohammed V, the Chellah.
- **Casablanca:** the Hassan II Mosque on the guided tour.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **In Fez, an official guide on the first day.** Ask to see the badge before setting off. Whoever offers on the street usually ends up taking you to partner shops.
- **Don't believe anyone who says a street is closed.** It's the most common way to steer tourists into a shop.
- **Book a riad you can reach.** Cars can't enter the Fez medina: ask the riad where the taxi drops you and whether they send someone for the bags.
- **Allow plenty of time on the road.** The driving times given are without stops: Chefchaouen-Fez, on the Rif's bends, takes up most of the day.
- **Covering clothes for mosques and religious sites.** At the Hassan II Mosque you don't get in wearing shorts or sleeveless tops.
- **Volubilis early in the morning.** The site is almost entirely in the sun.
- **No drugs, not even in the Rif.** Around Chefchaouen someone will offer them, but Morocco has zero tolerance: prison and heavy fines.
- **Change back dirhams before the flight, with the receipts.** They can't be taken out of Morocco.

## Chiusura personale
This was my northern Morocco: Chefchaouen, Fez, Meknes with Volubilis, Rabat and Casablanca, eleven days all by road. I prefer it that way, because you stop wherever you want. The south, from Marrakech to the dunes of Erg Chebbi, is the other trip.
