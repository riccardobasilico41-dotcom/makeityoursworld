# Brazil in 22 Days: Rio, Ilha Grande, Iguazú, Salvador, Chapada Diamantina and Lençóis Maranhenses

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (itinerary, extensions and practical information checked through research in October 2026)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 22 days, 21 nights, plus two alternative extensions of 4-6 days (Pantanal or Amazon)
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independent, with domestic flights between regions, buses along the Costa Verde and to the Chapada, 4x4 transfers in the Lençóis, guided tours where needed
**Categoria:** Sud America · Brasile · Rio de Janeiro · Iguazú · Bahia · natura · mare · cascate

---

## Apertura personale
Brazil is as big as Europe and you can't see it all in three weeks: you have to choose. This itinerary brings together the most varied landscapes you can reach with just a few domestic flights. Rio de Janeiro, between granite mountains, forest and ocean. The Costa Verde, with the colonial town of Paraty and the car-free island of Ilha Grande. The Iguazú Falls, to be seen from two countries. Salvador, the most African city in the Americas, and behind it the plateau of canyons and caves of the Chapada Diamantina. Finally the Lençóis Maranhenses, white dunes as far as the eye can see, with rainwater lagoons between one dune and the next, which really exist only from June to September.

July-August is chosen for those lagoons, and it is also the time when the two extensions proposed at the end open up. The first is the **Pantanal**, the largest wetland in the world, where from July to October you can see jaguars on the riverbanks. The second is the **Amazon** from Manaus, with the option of continuing by boat along the Amazon River to the border with Colombia and Peru. The trip is for people who want nature and cities together, accept a few transfer days and want a Brazil more varied than Rio and the beach.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** the itinerary is built around **July-August**. Month by month: **June-September** is the season of full lagoons in the Lençóis (peak in July-August, the water drops in September); **April-September** is the dry season in the Chapada Diamantina, with passable trails and waterfalls still fed until June-July; in **Rio** May to September is winter, 68-81 °F (20-27 °C), little rain and cool sea; at **Iguazú** there is water all year, more powerful with the summer rains (December-March); in **Salvador** the heaviest rains fall between April and June. **December-March** is full summer: strong heat, afternoon rain, Carnival (February) with doubled prices. **October-November** is fine for Rio and Iguazú, but the Lençóis lagoons are nearly empty.
- **Documents:** Italian citizens enter without a visa for tourism for up to 90 days every 180. You need a passport with at least 6 months of remaining validity on entry and a round-trip plane ticket, which the Brazilian authorities ask for. To continue to Colombia or Peru you need exit and entry stamps at the border posts (see the Amazon extension). It's worth signing up to **Dove Siamo nel Mondo** and reading the **Viaggiare Sicuri** page.
- **Health:** the Brazilian authorities recommend the **yellow fever** vaccination for a good part of the itinerary: western Paraná with Foz do Iguaçu, all of Maranhão with the Lençóis Maranhenses, Mato Grosso with the Pantanal, Amazonas, and western and southern Bahia. It must be done at least 10 days before departure; the international certificate may be requested when entering Colombia or Peru at the Amazon border. Dengue is widespread in the cities: repellent and long sleeves at dusk. Bottled water, high-SPF sunscreen.
- **Currency and payments:** Brazilian real (BRL, R$), roughly **€1 = R$5.6-6** in 2026. Cards and contactless are accepted almost everywhere, even at beach kiosks; **Pix**, the Brazilian instant payment system, is widespread but requires a local account. Cash for small towns, boats and markets. Better to withdraw during the day, from ATMs inside banks and shopping centers.
- **Getting around:** the distances call for **domestic flights** (LATAM, GOL, Azul), which cost less if booked in advance and often go through São Paulo or Brasília. Comfortable buses on medium routes (Rio-Paraty, Salvador-Lençóis in the Chapada). In the Lençóis Maranhenses only **4x4s (jardineiras)** with authorized drivers: you can't drive yourself in the park. In Rio, taxis and apps (Uber, 99) are the safest choice.
- **Safety:** in Rio and Salvador the main problem is petty crime: phone thefts on the beach and in the street, snatchings from motorbikes, robberies in deserted areas in the evening. The rules are simple: no jewelry or phone in hand on the street, little cash (but a small amount to hand over in case of a robbery, without resisting), apps instead of walking at night. With Uber and 99 you check the license plate and the driver's name before getting in, because the Italian foreign ministry reports fake drivers, especially at the airport. Viaggiare Sicuri advises against any visit to the favelas, even the "pacified" ones and even with guides or specialized operators. The natural areas of the itinerary are quiet, but the Pantanal and areas far from inhabited centers are always visited with an experienced guide.
- **Language:** Portuguese. Outside hotels and agencies English is limited; Spanish helps you get by.
- **Time zone:** Rio, Salvador, Foz do Iguaçu and São Luís are on UTC-3, 5 hours behind Italy in summer. Manaus and Cuiabá (Pantanal) are on UTC-4. Since 2019 Brazil no longer uses daylight saving time.
- **Electricity:** 127 V or 220 V depending on the city (127 V in Rio and Foz do Iguaçu, 220 V in much of the Northeast), type N sockets; Italian 10 A plugs often fit, but an adapter is useful and chargers must be 100-240 V.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Rio de Janeiro
We landed at Galeão international airport and took an official taxi or an app to the South Zone. The best base is **Ipanema**, **Leblon** or **Copacabana**: you sleep close to the sea, with the lit-up promenade and services, and from there you get around by metro. The first afternoon, the beach is enough.

We watched the sunset from the **Pedra do Arpoador**, the rocky spur between Copacabana and Ipanema, where people applaud when the sun goes down behind the Dois Irmãos hill.

### Day 2 — Christ the Redeemer and Santa Teresa
**Christ the Redeemer** on Corcovado is reached by the cog train from Cosme Velho or by the official vans from Paineiras; the van ticket costs about R$87, with a single price all year. It's best to take the first run of the morning, before the clouds and the crowds arrive. From above you understand the shape of the city: the beaches, the Rodrigo de Freitas lagoon, Sugarloaf at the entrance to the bay.

In the afternoon we went up to **Santa Teresa**, the hillside neighborhood with nineteenth-century villas, studios and the yellow tram (bondinho), and came down the **Escadaria Selarón**, the staircase covered in colorful tiles that leads to Lapa. You go in the daytime and head back by app.

### Day 3 — Sugarloaf, Copacabana and Ipanema
The **Sugarloaf** cable car goes up in two stages, first to Morro da Urca and then to the top. The best moment is late afternoon, for the sunset over the bay and the city lighting up; the full ticket costs about R$205 (the promotional rate of R$160 is only for Brazilians). Those who like to walk can hike up to Urca on a short trail and take the cable car only for the second stage.

The morning was free for the beaches: **Copacabana**, with its wavy Burle Marx promenade, and **Ipanema**, divided into "postos", the lifeguard towers that also mark the tribe of each stretch of beach. On your towel, bring only things you're willing to lose.

### Day 4 — The historic center and the night in Lapa
Rio's **center** is best visited in the daytime, on weekdays: the Confeitaria Colombo, the Theatro Municipal, the cone-shaped cathedral, the Real Gabinete Português de Leitura, one of the most beautiful libraries in the world. At the port, Praça Mauá with the **Museu do Amanhã** and the Boulevard Olímpico full of murals.

In the evening we went back to **Lapa**, under the arches of the aqueduct, for samba in the clubs and the streets packed on Fridays and Saturdays. You go and come back by app, with only your phone and the cash for the evening in your pocket.

**In practice**
- Many attractions in the center close on Sunday, and the center empties out.
- By metro from Ipanema and Copacabana to the center takes 20-30 minutes.
- In Lapa watch out for snatching in the crowd, especially late in the evening.

### Day 5 — To Paraty on the Costa Verde
From Rio to **Paraty** it's about 4 and a half hours by bus (Novo Rio bus station) along the **Costa Verde**, the road between the Atlantic Forest and the sea. Paraty is an eighteenth-century colonial town, a port for the gold of Minas Gerais, a UNESCO site since 2019 together with Ilha Grande and the Serra da Bocaina.

The historic center is closed to cars, with white houses and colorful doors and streets of big cobblestones that sometimes flood at high tide. The evening is quiet: locally made cachaça, restaurants and live music.

### Day 6 — Paraty: the bay by boat and the waterfalls
Paraty Bay has dozens of islands and beaches reachable only by sea. The **escunas**, wooden boats for groups, do a 4-5 hour tour with swimming stops; private **lanchas** cost more but choose where to stop. In the afternoon we went up to the **waterfalls** along the Cunha road, like the Cachoeira do Tobogã, where local kids slide down the rock, and to the cachaça **alambiques**.

*Good to know: if you have an extra day you can go to **Trindade**, 30 minutes south, with wild beaches and the Cachadaço natural pool.*

### Day 7 — Ilha Grande: arrival in Abraão
From Paraty you reach **Ilha Grande** by van and boat (about 3 hours), via Angra dos Reis or Conceição de Jacareí. The island has no cars: you walk on trails in the forest or get around by boat. The main village, **Vila do Abraão**, has pousadas, restaurants and nightlife.

In the afternoon we did a first short trail to Abraãozinho beach or to the **Aqueduto** and the **Cachoeira da Feiticeira**.

### Day 8 — Ilha Grande: Lopes Mendes
**Lopes Mendes** is considered one of the most beautiful beaches in Brazil: three kilometers of fine white sand, open sea, forest behind. You get there by boat to Pouso and then a 20-minute walk, or on a trail of about three hours through the forest. Alternatively there's the boat tour of the **Lagoa Azul** and the **Lagoa Verde**, for snorkeling.

*Good to know: there are no facilities at Lopes Mendes, so water and food must be brought along. The open sea has waves and currents, so swim with caution.*

### Day 9 — From Ilha Grande to Foz do Iguaçu
A transfer day: boat and transfer to Rio airport (3-4 hours in all, to be booked the day before in Abraão) and flight to **Foz do Iguaçu**, often with a stop in São Paulo. We arrived in the evening. You can sleep on the Brazilian side, in Foz, or in Puerto Iguazú in Argentina; this itinerary uses Foz, more convenient for flights.

### Day 10 — Iguazú, the Brazilian side and the Parque das Aves
The **Iguazú Falls** are a front of about 275 drops stretching almost three kilometers, on the border between Brazil and Argentina. The **Brazilian side** gives the overall view: a trail of about a kilometer and a half along the canyon, up to the walkway that goes in beneath the **Garganta do Diabo**, where you get completely soaked. 2-3 hours are enough; entrance for foreigners costs R$134 (fare in force since 19 December 2025, updated every year with inflation) and is bought online by choosing day and time.

Right across from the entrance is the **Parque das Aves**, with toucans, macaws and Atlantic Forest birds in large aviaries. If you like, add the **Macuco Safari**, the inflatable-boat ride right under the falls (roughly R$450-550).

### Day 11 — Iguazú, the Argentine side and the Garganta del Diablo
The **Argentine side** is where you walk inside the falls: three walkway circuits (Superior, Inferior and the **Garganta del Diablo**, reached by a little train in the forest) lead above the biggest drop, where the water mist rises tens of meters. It takes the whole day.

The border is crossed by taxi, bus or transfer, showing your passport and getting exit and entry stamps. Since June 1, 2026, entry to the Argentine park for non-resident foreigners costs 60,000 Argentine pesos (about 40 dollars, €35), with a reduced rate for the second consecutive day.

**In practice**
- Bring your passport, and cash in pesos or a card for the Argentine park.
- Arrive at opening: the Garganta walkway fills up by mid-morning.
- A rain cape or waterproof bag for your phone and documents.

### Day 12 — Flight to Salvador de Bahia
Flight from Foz do Iguaçu to **Salvador**, almost always with a stop in São Paulo or Brasília (4-6 hours in all). Salvador was the first capital of Brazil, from 1549 to 1763, and the main port of arrival of enslaved Africans: its culture (music, Candomblé religion, capoeira, food) is deeply Afro-Brazilian.

The best base is the **Pelourinho**, the historic center, or the **Barra** and **Rio Vermelho** neighborhoods, by the sea, safer in the evening. We watched our first sunset from the **Farol da Barra**.

### Day 13 — Salvador: Pelourinho and the Lower City
The **Pelourinho**, a UNESCO site, is a set of uphill squares with pastel-colored colonial houses and baroque churches; the most famous is the **Igreja de São Francisco**, covered in gold inside. We went down to the lower city by the **Elevador Lacerda**, the public elevator from 1873, to the **Mercado Modelo**. On Tuesday evenings the Pelourinho throws a party, with concerts and the drums of Olodum.

*Good to know: in the daytime the Pelourinho is patrolled, but in the evening the side streets empty out early. Better to stick to the main streets and head back by app.*

### Day 14 — Salvador: Bonfim, Rio Vermelho and Bahian food
In the morning we went to the **Igreja do Senhor do Bonfim**, the church of the colored ribbons tied to the gate and to the wrist as a vow. Then the **Solar do Unhão** and the modern art museum by the sea, and in the afternoon **Rio Vermelho**, the nightlife neighborhood, where the baianas fry **acarajé** in palm oil on the street.

If you want to see **capoeira** you can watch a roda in one of the schools in the Pelourinho (ask first), and if you have an extra day you can take the boat trip in the **Baía de Todos os Santos**.

### Day 15 — To the Chapada Diamantina
From Salvador to **Lençóis** (Bahia) it's about 6-7 hours by bus inland; where available, you can take a short flight. Lençóis is a town born in the nineteenth century with the diamond rush, today the base for the **Parque Nacional da Chapada Diamantina**: a plateau of mesas, canyons, caves and waterfalls as big as an Italian region.

The town is small, with colorful houses, agencies and restaurants. The first afternoon, the short trail along the river to the **Ribeirão do Meio**, a natural rock slide with a pool, is enough.

### Day 16 — Chapada: Morro do Pai Inácio and the caves
We did the classic tour with a guide and a car: the **Gruta da Lapa Doce** or the **Gruta da Pratinha**, with turquoise water where you snorkel inside the cave; the **Poço do Diabo**, a waterfall with a dark pool; and the sunset on the **Morro do Pai Inácio**, the mesa that overlooks the valley, with a steep 20-30 minute climb.

In the Chapada guides are not mandatory everywhere, but they are on the long trails and in the caves, and the routes are poorly marked: it's best to go with a local guide anyway.

### Day 17 — Chapada: the Cachoeira da Fumaça or the Poço Azul
For the last day there are two choices. The **Cachoeira da Fumaça**, one of the tallest waterfalls in Brazil (about 340 meters), is reached from the Capão valley by a 6 km round-trip trail, with a steep initial climb; in the dry season the water turns to smoke before touching the ground, hence the name. The alternative is the **Poço Azul** and the **Poço Encantado**, flooded caves where sunlight comes in and lights the water blue (at Poço Encantado roughly from April to September, in the middle hours).

*Good to know: if you have time and legs, you can replace these days with the **Vale do Pati trek**, 3-5 days through remote valleys sleeping in farmers' houses.*

### Day 18 — From Lençóis to São Luís
A long transfer day: bus back to Salvador and flight to **São Luís**, capital of Maranhão, almost always with a stop. We arrived in the evening. São Luís is the only Brazilian capital founded by the French (1612), with a historic center of houses covered in Portuguese azulejos, a UNESCO site; if you arrive early you can take a walk in the Praia Grande.

### Day 19 — Barreirinhas and the Lagoa Azul circuit
By van from São Luís to **Barreirinhas** (about 4 hours), the town on the Rio Preguiças that is the gateway to the **Parque Nacional dos Lençóis Maranhenses**. In the afternoon we did our first ride in a jardineira, the off-road truck with benches on the bed that crosses the river on a raft and takes the sand track to the dunes.

The **Lagoa Azul circuit** leads to the first lagoons: clear, warm rainwater collected between white sand dunes up to 40 meters high. You walk barefoot, swim and stay for the sunset, when the sand changes color. There is nothing inside the park: water and a hat must be brought from Barreirinhas.

### Day 20 — Lagoa Bonita and the dunes at dawn
The second circuit is the **Lagoa Bonita** one, reached by climbing a steep dune with a rope: from the top you see the sea of dunes and lagoons to the horizon. You can do it at dawn, with the lower light, or at sunset. In your free time, there's also the scenic flight in a small plane over the park, from which you understand how vast the lagoons are.

**In practice**
- Jardineira for one circuit: roughly R$100-150 per person, guide included.
- Park entrance: free. ICMBio does not sell tickets and the plan to concession the park to private operators was withdrawn at the end of 2023; the R$10 tourist tax introduced in 2023 by the municipality of Santo Amaro was suspended by the Maranhão court.
- The lagoons are full from June to September, at their peak in July-August.
- At midday the sand is scorching: go in the early hours or in the afternoon.

### Day 21 — The Rio Preguiças, Caburé and Atins
We spent the last day by boat along the **Rio Preguiças**, down to the sea. You pass **Vassouras**, where capuchin monkeys come close to the shore, and **Mandacaru**, with the lighthouse from which you look out over the dunes; then **Caburé**, a tongue of sand between the river and the ocean. If you like, you can stop at **Atins**, the village at the far end of the park, a kitesurfing base, and sleep there instead of going back to Barreirinhas.

### Day 22 — Return from São Luís
Transfer to São Luís (about 4 hours from Barreirinhas, more from Atins) and return flight, with a stop in São Paulo, Brasília or Fortaleza and a connection to Europe. It's best to book the transfer for the early morning hours and an afternoon or evening flight.

## Extension A — The Pantanal, Land of Jaguars (4-6 days)
The **Pantanal** is the largest tropical wetland in the world, between Mato Grosso, Mato Grosso do Sul, Bolivia and Paraguay. In the dry season the water retreats into the rivers and animals gather on the banks: caimans, capybaras, giant otters, toucans, jabirus and above all the **jaguar**, which in the northern Pantanal is seen with a regularity unmatched anywhere in the world.
- **When:** from **July to October**, with August-September the best months. From November to April the rains flood the Transpantaneira and boat outings become less certain.
- **How to get there:** flight to **Cuiabá** (capital of Mato Grosso), then about 2 hours to **Poconé** and the **Transpantaneira**, a 147 km dirt road with over a hundred wooden bridges that ends at **Porto Jofre**, on the Cuiabá River. The stretch takes a whole day, with stops for animals.
- **Typical 5-day program:** day 1 flight to Cuiabá and road to a pousada along the Transpantaneira; day 2 safari by car, on horseback or on foot around the pousada, and descent to Porto Jofre; days 3-4 boat outings at dawn and in the afternoon on the Cuiabá, Três Irmãos and Piquiri rivers for jaguars; day 5 return to Cuiabá and flight. From the Pantanal you can continue to the Amazon with a Cuiabá-Manaus flight.
- **The budget option:** two nights in a simple pousada along the Transpantaneira and one or two boat days from Porto Jofre, sharing the boat with other travelers. Some travelers report a boat with a piloteiro at around **R$1,400-1,600 for the full day, for 4-6 people**, and around R$1,000 for half a day; with a rental car from Cuiabá (you need a high-clearance car, preferably 4x4) you stay under R$1,000-1,500 a day per person.
- **The more expensive option:** complete packages from Cuiabá with a naturalist guide, transfers, full board and a private boat, in river lodges or in the **floating hotel** moored near Porto Jofre: roughly **R$9,000-13,000 per person for 3-4 nights**, more for photography expeditions.
- **On respect:** in July 2026 a video with dozens of boats around a jaguar reopened the debate on wildlife-watching tourism. It's best to choose operators that respect distances and limit the number of boats per animal.
- **The southern alternative:** the southern Pantanal, from Campo Grande toward Miranda, pairs well with **Bonito**, with crystal-clear rivers for snorkeling, but jaguars are seen less regularly.

## Extension B — The Amazon from Manaus, and Beyond to Peru or Colombia (4-10 days)
**Manaus** is a city of over two million people in the middle of the forest, reachable only by air or by boat, with the Teatro Amazonas built in the rubber boom. It is the most convenient gateway to the Brazilian Amazon.
- **When:** in the Amazon it rains all year. With **high water** (April-July) you go by canoe inside the flooded forest, the igapó; with **low water** (September-December) sand beaches emerge on the rivers and you walk more. July-August, the period of this itinerary, is the transition between the two seasons, with the water still high and less rain.
- **Typical 4-5 day program:** day 1 Manaus, Teatro Amazonas and Mercado Adolpho Lisboa; day 2 the **Meeting of the Waters**, where the dark Rio Negro and the mud-colored Solimões run side by side for kilometers without mixing, and transfer to a lodge in the forest; days 3-4 at the lodge, with canoeing at dawn, a walk in the forest with a guide, piranha fishing, night spotting of caimans, a visit to a riverside community and, on the Rio Negro, pink dolphins; day 5 return to Manaus. On the Rio Negro, with its dark, acidic water, there are far fewer mosquitoes. The **Anavilhanas** river archipelago is one of the most beautiful areas.
- **Indicative costs:** lodge with full board, guide and activities from about R$800-1,500 per person per night in the simple places, over R$2,500 in the high-end ones.
- **Continuing to Peru (Tabatinga-Leticia-Iquitos):** from Manaus you go up the Solimões to **Tabatinga**, at the triple border with Colombia (Leticia) and Peru (Santa Rosa). By air it's about an hour and a half; by **speedboat** about 30-36 hours (in April 2026 one departure a day, around R$1,000); by **slow boat**, in a hammock on the deck, 6-7 days going up the river. In Tabatinga you get the Brazilian exit stamp from the Polícia Federal, then from Santa Rosa the speedboats leave for **Iquitos** (about 10-12 hours, a few times a week, roughly 150-200 soles), where you do the Peruvian entry. Iquitos is not connected by road: you continue by air to Lima or Cusco. It's the way to link this trip to an itinerary in Peru.
- **Continuing to Colombia:** Tabatinga and **Leticia** are adjacent and you cross on foot or by moto-taxi. You get the Brazilian exit stamp and Colombian entry at the airport or the immigration office in Leticia, then fly to **Bogotá** (about 2 hours). From Leticia you visit **Amacayacu National Park** and Puerto Nariño, the car-free town on the river.
- **Good to know:** ports and border offices have limited hours, and stamps must be done before boarding; boat services change often, so it's best to ask on the spot. The yellow fever certificate may be requested.

## Variants by length
- **15 days:** Rio (4), Ilha Grande (2), Iguazú (2), Lençóis Maranhenses (4), with one or two transfer days. Paraty, Salvador and the Chapada are cut.
- **3 weeks with the Pantanal:** drop the Chapada Diamantina and add extension A after Iguazú (Foz-Cuiabá by air with a stop), then Salvador and the Lençóis.
- **4 weeks:** the full itinerary plus one of the two extensions.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
Indicative ranges per night for a double room in July-August (in Brazil it's winter, low-to-mid season outside of July).
- **Rio de Janeiro:** Ipanema, Leblon or Copacabana. Budget: hostels and rooms R$200-350. Mid-range: hotels near the sea R$450-800. High-end: seafront hotels over R$1,200. Santa Teresa has charming pousadas but it's less convenient in the evening.
- **Paraty:** inside the historic center or next to it, pousadas R$300-700; hostels from R$120 per bed.
- **Ilha Grande:** Vila do Abraão for services and boats (pousadas R$250-600); isolated beaches like Aventureiro or Saco do Céu for those who want peace and quiet.
- **Foz do Iguaçu:** the center or the road to the parks; mid-range R$300-600. The only hotel inside the park, on the Brazilian side, is high-end.
- **Salvador:** Pelourinho and Santo Antônio Além do Carmo for atmosphere (pousadas R$250-600), Barra and Rio Vermelho for the sea and the evenings.
- **Chapada Diamantina:** Lençóis as a base (pousadas R$200-500); the Vale do Capão for a more alternative atmosphere.
- **Lençóis Maranhenses:** Barreirinhas for convenience (pousadas R$250-600), Atins for kitesurf resorts and beachfront pousadas (mid-to-high range), Santo Amaro for a less frequented entrance.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
- **Rio:** feijoada on Saturday, pastel and caldo de cana in the markets, Globo biscuits and iced mate on the beach, rodízio churrascarias, botecos with caipirinha and petiscos.
- **Costa Verde:** fish and seafood, the craft cachaça of Paraty, the fishermen's peixe com banana of Ilha Grande.
- **Iguazú:** churrasco on the Brazilian side, Argentine beef and wine in Puerto Iguazú.
- **Salvador:** acarajé and abará from the baianas, Bahian moqueca with dendê oil and coconut milk, vatapá, bobó de camarão, cocadas.
- **Chapada Diamantina:** sertão cooking with carne de sol, cassava and the dishes of the restaurants in Lençóis; in the houses of the Vale do Pati, the farmers' simple meals.
- **Maranhão:** arroz de cuxá, fried fish, crabs and Guaraná Jesus, the local pink soft drink.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book domestic flights early**: booked in advance they cost half, and almost all go through São Paulo or Brasília.
- **Don't trust "it's close"**: Brazilian distances are continental and almost every change of region is a day of travel.
- **Phone in your pocket and apps for getting around in the evening**, in Rio and Salvador.
- **Yellow fever vaccine before leaving**, at least 10 days before, especially with the extensions.
- **Lençóis Maranhenses only from June to September**: out of season the lagoons are empty or nearly so.
- **Two full days at Iguazú**: one side alone shows only half of the falls.
- **Local guides in the Chapada**: poorly marked trails and caves with guided access.
- **Cash for islands, boats and small towns**, even though cards work almost everywhere.

## Scams and common annoyances
- **Phone thefts on the beach and in the street**, including snatchings from bicycles or motorbikes: phone in your pocket, never left on the towel.
- **Organized distractions**: someone stains your clothes or asks for help while an accomplice takes your bag.
- **Unofficial taxis at the airport**: only taxis from the official counters, or apps.
- **"Tourist" prices at beach kiosks**: ask the price first, especially for chairs, umbrellas and coconut.
- **Bonfim ribbons handed out for free** in front of the church and in the Pelourinho, followed by an insistent request for money.
- **Unauthorized agencies in the Lençóis and in the Pantanal**: choose registered drivers and piloteiros.
- **Isolated ATMs** with skimmers: withdraw inside banks and shopping centers.

## Chiusura personale
This is an itinerary of contrasts: the metropolis under the Christ and the car-free island, the falls that separate two countries, the baroque center of Salvador and the empty canyons of the Chapada, and finally a desert full of water. It holds a lot together because it accepts a few transfer days, which in Brazil are unavoidable. The two extensions are the way, for those with more time, to see the interior of the country, the jaguars or the forest, and for those who want to leave Brazil by going up the Amazon River.
