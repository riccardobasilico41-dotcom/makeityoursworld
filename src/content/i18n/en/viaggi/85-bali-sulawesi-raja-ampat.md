# Bali, Sulawesi and Raja Ampat in 12 Days: From Rice Terraces to the Richest Reefs in the World

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (Rick has seen Bali, Sulawesi and Raja Ampat, Raja Ampat in August 2026; flights, ferries and prices checked on November 5, 2026 against official and industry sources, to be rechecked before you go. The Raja Ampat permit amount for foreigners is not confirmed)
**Periodo del viaggio:** designed for July to October. Bali and Sulawesi are in the dry season from April-May to October; in Raja Ampat the best window runs from October to April, but in summer the sea stays beautiful, with more wind and fewer people
**Durata:** 12 days, 11 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** independently, with a driver on Bali, a local operator in North Sulawesi, three domestic flights, a fast ferry and homestay boats in Raja Ampat
**Categoria:** Asia · Indonesia · Bali · Sulawesi · Raja Ampat · mare · snorkeling · immersioni · natura

## Apertura personale
Raja Ampat is the most beautiful place I've seen underwater, and also the hardest to reach. This itinerary puts it after Bali and North Sulawesi, because there's a direct flight from Manado to Sorong, and another from Sorong to Bali. That way three very distant islands fit into twelve days without backtracking.

Twelve days is short for three islands, and I'll say it right away: every island deserves at least a week, if not more. This is the loop for people who have little time and want to see how different Indonesia is from one island to the next. Further down there are also the two versions with just one island besides Bali: Sulawesi only or Raja Ampat only. You choose based on the season.

We went in August, which every guide marks as the wrong season for Raja Ampat. In part it is, but not in the way you'd expect: the sea was spectacular anyway, ten days without a drop of rain. The wind, though, made itself felt, and stretched out a couple of transfers between the islands.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** on Bali the dry season runs from April to October, on Sulawesi from May to October. In Raja Ampat the best window is October to April, with calmer seas and more manta rays and big pelagics. From May to September the southeast winds blow: transfers can take longer and outings to exposed areas can be canceled. The month that suits all three is **October**. Summer works too, if you accept the wind.
- **Documents:** passport valid for at least 6 months, **e-VOA** online at evisa.imigrasi.go.id and the **All Indonesia Arrival Card** before entry.
- **Bali tourist levy:** 150,000 IDR per person, paid online on the official Love Bali site (lovebali.baliprov.go.id).
- **Domestic flights:** from Denpasar to Manado there are direct flights (TransNusa) and Lion Air flights via Makassar. Lion Air and TransNusa fly from Manado to Sorong. From Sorong to Denpasar there's a direct Garuda flight, and Sorong is also connected to Jakarta and Makassar. Routes change often: check them when you book and always leave a margin between flights.
- **Sorong-Waisai ferry:** about 2 hours, departing at 9 am and 2 pm according to Indonesia's official tourism site, from 137,000 IDR. In Waisai the homestay boat picks you up.
- **Raja Ampat permit (PIN):** it's mandatory and you buy it in Waisai. Keep the receipt, because it's checked at several spots. The amount for foreigners needs to be checked at the time: the figures you find don't match.
- **Cash:** there are no ATMs on the Raja Ampat islands. Withdraw in Sorong or Waisai, before heading out to the islands.
- **Time zones:** Bali and Sulawesi are on WITA (UTC+8), Raja Ampat is on WIT (UTC+9). You lose an hour flying to Sorong.
- **Health:** no mandatory vaccinations. For Raja Ampat and Papua, see a travel medicine clinic before you go. Hospitals in the Papua region are few: according to gov.uk a serious emergency may require evacuation. You need insurance that covers it.
- **Safety:** gov.uk advises caution in the Papua region, especially in Highland Papua and Central Papua, far from Raja Ampat, which is in Southwest Papua province. Before you leave, check the latest travel advice: viaggiaresicuri.it for Italians, or your own government's.
- **At sea:** Raja Ampat's currents are strong and must be taken seriously. Some spots are only done at the right times, with a guide.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Bali and the road to Ubud
You land in Denpasar and head to **Ubud**, inland. Best to have paid the tourist levy online already. In Ubud I stayed at **Villa Jacques**, and it was beautiful. If you want an Italian-speaking guide, you can message **Pande Wirta**.

### Day 2 — Ubud, the rice terraces and Sidemen
The **Tegalalang rice terraces** are best early in the morning, before the crowds. For more peace, go to **Sidemen**, at the foot of Mount Agung, 1h15-1h30 from Ubud: the terraces are less touristy and tour buses barely get there. Just twenty kilometers from the south, the flower offerings reappear in front of the houses and the temples are in use rather than on display.

### Day 3 — Uluwatu and the Kecak dance at sunset
You head south to **Uluwatu**, all cliffs and beaches at the bottom of stairways cut into the rock. At sunset, in the cliff temple, there's the **Kecak dance**: seventy men sitting in a circle make all the music with their voices. It stuck with me more than any rice field in Bali. You sleep in the south, near the airport.

### Day 4 — Flight to Manado and up to Tomohon
Flight from Denpasar to **Manado**, in North Sulawesi, direct or via Makassar. You drive up to **Tomohon**, on the plateau between the volcanoes, cooler than the coast. To organize North Sulawesi I recommend **Wale Adventure**: ask for Alvaro. They're kind and good at what they do, they're based in Tomohon, run private tours and also have a vanilla farm you can visit. For transparency: I built their website.

### Day 5 — Tangkoko, the forest of the tarsiers
A day in the **Tangkoko** reserve, on the northeast coast, a lowland forest at the foot of a volcano. You walk with a local guide looking for **tarsiers**, tiny primates with huge eyes, and **crested black macaques**. It's a long outing: Wale Adventure offers it as a day trip or with a night near the reserve.

### Day 6 — Bunaken and the walls that drop into the blue
By boat from the Manado pier to **Bunaken National Marine Park**. Coral walls drop sheer for hundreds of meters: you turn onto your back and there's no bottom below. You can snorkel or dive. Diving packages in Bunaken cost more than the Indonesian average: compare operators.

### Day 7 — Flight to Sorong, ferry to Waisai and Kri
Morning flight from Manado to **Sorong**, then the 2 pm ferry to **Waisai**, about 2 hours. In Waisai you buy the park permit, and the homestay boat takes you to **Kri**. On Kri we stayed at **Paparisa Homestay**, the right mix of comfort and local stay: prices are above the average of other homestays, but dolphins and dugongs often swim right off the beach. Staying in homestays instead of resorts is the choice worth making, because that model keeps the park's protection going.

### Day 8 — Kri and the Arborek reef
Snorkeling day. A density of fish like the one on the **Arborek** reef, twenty meters from shore, no longer exists elsewhere. Divers go out with **Soul Scuba Divers**, on Kri island. The currents are strong: get in the water when the guide says so.

### Day 9 — To the Fam Islands and Piaynemo at sunset
Boat transfer to the **Fam Islands**. We stayed at **Tanjung Mefiyai Homestay**: very simple huts, but in a stunning spot. Reno is super kind and helpful, and he took care of us on the trips and transfers. In the afternoon you climb **Piaynemo**, the viewpoint over the rock islands. At sunset, with the wooden stairs and the crowded platform, it's the only moment you run into anyone else.

### Day 10 — Fam: Rufas and the Fam Wall
A day on the boat with Reno between **Rufas** and the **Fam Wall**, snorkeling. If the wind picks up, outings to the most exposed areas can be canceled: whoever knows the sea decides. In August the wind made us skip an outing to a more exposed area.

### Day 11 — Back to Sorong and flight to Bali
Early boat from Fam to Waisai, ferry to **Sorong** and flight to Denpasar, direct with Garuda or with a stopover. It's a full travel day, and with the wind the boat can take longer. If you can, spend a night in Sorong and fly the next day.

### Day 12 — Departure
Last day in Bali or Jakarta, depending on your international flight. With Indonesian domestic flights it's always better not to have tight connections on the day you fly home.

## Sulawesi only or Raja Ampat only: the two 12-day versions
Three islands in twelve days can be done, but every island deserves at least a week. The destinations are interchangeable: with the same time you can pick just one island besides Bali, depending on the season.

**Bali + Raja Ampat (October to April).** Three days on Bali, then a flight from Denpasar to Sorong and eight days between Kri, Arborek and the Fam Islands, with more time for diving and for slow transfers. It's the best version for manta rays and big pelagics, which arrive mostly in the winter months. Misool, in the south, needs a liveaboard or an extra week.

**Bali + Sulawesi (May to October).** Three days on Bali, then a flight to Makassar and the road to **Tana Toraja**: 8-10 hours by car or bus, including overnight. There are flights between Makassar and Toraja airport, but they're few and can be canceled: check them first. Three days in Toraja among tongkonan villages, rock tombs and, if it happens, a funeral. Then a flight from Makassar to Manado for Tangkoko and Bunaken, and back from Manado to Bali. Tana Toraja is the most different thing I've seen in Indonesia: a Toraja funeral lasts days, gathers hundreds of people and includes the sacrifice of buffalo in front of everyone. As an outsider you can attend only with a guide, bringing a gift, usually sugar or cigarettes. Toraja can also be organized with Wale Adventure.

**Toraja instead of the north, with Raja Ampat.** If you want Toraja and Raja Ampat together, go through Makassar, which has flights to Sorong. But the road to Toraja eats up two days: in twelve days there's little left for everything else.

## What to see / what to do
- **Bali's rice terraces**, Tegalalang at dawn or Sidemen for peace and quiet.
- **The Kecak dance at Uluwatu** at sunset.
- **The tarsiers of Tangkoko**, with a local guide.
- **The walls of Bunaken**, snorkeling or diving.
- **The Arborek reef** and the dives around Kri.
- **Piaynemo** and the Fam Islands, with Rufas and the Fam Wall.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't book tight connections** between domestic flights: routes and times change.
- **Buy the Raja Ampat permit in Waisai** and keep the receipt.
- **Withdraw cash in Sorong or Waisai:** there are no ATMs on the islands.
- **Stay in Raja Ampat homestays:** they cost less than resorts and the money reaches the families.
- **Factor in the wind** from May to September: some longer transfers and some canceled outings.
- **Respect the currents:** in Raja Ampat some spots are only done at the right times.
- **Never show up at a Toraja funeral without a guide:** they're private events with rules of respect.
- **Insurance with evacuation** for the Papua part.
- **Pay the Bali levy online** before you leave.

## Chiusura personale
Twelve days like this are a taste, and should be taken for what they are. Bali is the gateway. North Sulawesi adds the forest and the walls of Bunaken. Raja Ampat closes with the most beautiful place I've seen underwater. With reefs full of fish and hardly any other tourists, even summer was a compromise I don't regret. If you can, though, pick one island and give it the week it deserves.
