# Bali, Sulawesi and Raja Ampat in Two Weeks: From Rice Terraces to the Richest Reefs in the World

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (Rick has seen Bali, Sulawesi and Raja Ampat, Raja Ampat in August 2026; flights, ferries and prices checked on November 5, 2026 against official and industry sources, to be rechecked before you go. The Raja Ampat permit amount for foreigners is not confirmed)
**Periodo del viaggio:** Raja Ampat in August 2026
**Durata:** 12 days, 11 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** independently, with a driver on Bali, a local operator in North Sulawesi, three domestic flights, a fast ferry and homestay boats in Raja Ampat

## Apertura personale
Raja Ampat is the most beautiful place I've seen underwater, and also the hardest to reach. This itinerary puts it after Bali and North Sulawesi, because there's a direct flight from Manado to Sorong, and from Sorong you fly back towards Bali. That way three very distant islands fit into twelve days without backtracking.

I went back to Indonesia for the people, the places and the smiles, a bit like what happens to me with Thailand.

Twelve days is short for three islands, and I'll say it right away: every island deserves at least a week, if not more. This is the loop for people who have little time and want to see how different Indonesia is from one island to the next. Further down there are also the two versions with just one island besides Bali: Sulawesi only or Raja Ampat only. You choose based on the season.

We went in August, which every guide marks as the wrong season for Raja Ampat. In part it is, but not in the way you'd expect: the sea was spectacular anyway, ten days without a drop of rain. The wind, though, made itself felt, and stretched out a couple of transfers between the islands.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** on Bali the dry season runs from April to October, on Sulawesi from May to October. In Raja Ampat the best window is October to April, with calmer seas and more manta rays and big pelagics. From May to September the southeast winds blow: transfers can take longer and outings to exposed areas can be canceled. The month that suits all three is **October**. Summer works too, if you accept the wind.
- **Documents:** passport valid for at least 6 months, **e-VOA** online at evisa.imigrasi.go.id and the **All Indonesia Arrival Card** before entry.
- **Bali tourist levy:** 150,000 IDR per person, paid online on the official Love Bali site (lovebali.baliprov.go.id).
- **Domestic flights:** from Denpasar to Manado you fly direct, when the route is running, or via Makassar. Sorong has direct flights from Manado, Makassar and Jakarta. Garuda's direct Sorong-Denpasar flight is seasonal: in 2026 it was suspended from April and reinstated from October 25 until March 2027. In the months without it, you connect through Makassar or Jakarta. Routes change often: check them when you book and always leave a margin between flights.
- **Sorong-Waisai ferry:** about 2 hours. The early-afternoon departure (2 pm) usually runs every day, the morning one not always. Tickets are bought at the port, economy or VIP: check times and prices on the spot. In Waisai the homestay boat picks you up.
- **Raja Ampat permit (PIN):** it's mandatory and you pay it in Sorong, at the tourism office, or at Waisai port; it's often included in dive packages. Keep the receipt, because it's checked at several spots. The amount for foreigners changed in 2026 and the figures you find don't match: check it at the time.
- **Cash:** there are no ATMs on the Raja Ampat islands. Withdraw in Sorong or Waisai, before heading out to the islands.
- **Time zones:** Bali and Sulawesi are on WITA (UTC+8), Raja Ampat is on WIT (UTC+9). You lose an hour flying to Sorong.
- **Health:** no mandatory vaccinations. For Raja Ampat and Papua, see a travel medicine clinic before you go. Hospitals in the Papua region are few: according to gov.uk a serious emergency may require evacuation. You need insurance that covers it.
- **Safety:** gov.uk advises caution in the Papua region, especially in Highland Papua and Central Papua, far from Raja Ampat, which is in Southwest Papua province. Before you leave, check the latest travel advice: viaggiaresicuri.it for Italians, or your own government's.
- **At sea:** Raja Ampat's currents are strong and must be taken seriously. Some spots are only done at the right times, with a guide.
- **From the airport to the centre:** at **Bali (DPS)** there are no trains: at the official taxi counter in arrivals the fare is fixed by zone, about 300,000-500,000 IDR to Ubud (1-1.5 hours), while Grab and Gojek, from the signposted pickup zone, cost about 200,000-300,000 IDR; the most relaxed option is a driver sent by your accommodation · whoever offers «transport?» just past customs is not official, and money changers outside banks often cheat · at **Manado (MDC)** the centre is about half an hour away and Grab works · at **Sorong (SOQ)** the ferry port for Waisai is 10-15 minutes by taxi, price to be agreed first.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Bali and the road to Ubud
You land in Denpasar and head to **Ubud**, inland. Best to have paid the tourist levy online already: keep the receipt on your phone, because it can be checked on arrival. Outside arrivals it's better to have a transfer already booked with the hotel, a trusted driver or an app like Grab, rather than haggling with whoever approaches you at the exit. The road to Ubud is short on paper, but traffic in south Bali stretches it a lot, especially in the late afternoon.

The first day is for getting over the time difference. Central Ubud is walkable, between the **Royal Palace**, the market and the lanes with family temples, and the evening is the best time to go out, when the heat drops. In Ubud I stayed at **Villa Jacques**, and it was beautiful. If you want an Italian-speaking guide, you can message **Pande Wirta**, best contacted before you leave to set up the driving days that follow.

**In practice:** airport to Ubud by car about 1-1.5 hours, more in traffic · Bali tourist levy 150,000 IDR, paid on lovebali.baliprov.go.id · cash: withdraw at the airport or in Ubud, preferably at ATMs inside banks · driver for the next days to be booked in advance.

### Day 2 — Ubud, the rice terraces and Sidemen
The **Tegalalang rice terraces** are best early in the morning, before the crowds. They're about twenty minutes' drive north of Ubud: arriving around sunrise the light is soft, it's cooler and the paths between the terraces are almost empty. From ten o'clock on come the groups, the swings and the queues for photos. *Along the paths you'll find small farmers' booths asking for a donation: they're normal and worth budgeting for, with a few small notes.*

For more peace, go to **Sidemen**, at the foot of Mount Agung, a drive away from Ubud: the terraces are less touristy and tour buses barely get there. Just twenty kilometers from the south, the flower offerings reappear in front of the houses and the temples are in use rather than on display. You walk through the rice fields with a village guide, have lunch at one of the warungs overlooking the valley and head back to Ubud in the afternoon, before the traffic jams up.

**In practice:** Tegalalang entry about 25,000 IDR, plus small donations along the paths · Ubud to Sidemen by car about 1-1.5 hours · day driver to be agreed beforehand, with clear price and hours · temples require a sarong and covered shoulders, often lent at the entrance.

### Day 3 — Uluwatu and the Kecak dance at sunset
You head south to **Uluwatu**, all cliffs and beaches at the bottom of stairways cut into the rock. It's best to leave Ubud in late morning, after checking out, and spend the afternoon on one of the beaches of the Bukit peninsula, like **Padang Padang** or **Bingin**, reached on foot down steep steps. The tide changes the beach a lot: at high tide the sand shrinks to a strip.

At sunset, in the cliff temple, there's the **Kecak dance**: dozens of men sitting in a circle make all the music with their voices. It stuck with me more than any rice field in Bali. The dance ticket is separate from the temple ticket, and the best seats go to those who arrive well ahead. *The temple monkeys steal glasses, hats and phones: keep everything inside a closed backpack.* You sleep in the south, near the airport.

**In practice:** Ubud to Uluwatu by car about 1.5-2 hours · Uluwatu temple entry about 50,000 IDR, Kecak dance about 150,000 IDR, show around 6 pm · traffic is slow on the way out: keep the driver waiting · night in Jimbaran or the Bukit for the morning flight.

### Day 4 — Flight to Manado and up to Tomohon
Flight from Denpasar to **Manado**, in North Sulawesi, direct or via Makassar. The direct flight, when it runs, takes about two and a half hours; with the stopover the day gets a few hours longer. A morning flight is best, so you reach Manado with daylight left to drive up into the hills. North Sulawesi has a different language, a different main religion and a different cuisine from Bali, and you notice it from the airport.

You drive up to **Tomohon**, on the plateau between the volcanoes, cooler than the coast. To organize North Sulawesi I recommend **Wale Adventure**: ask for Alvaro. I did Tomohon and Tangkoko with them. They're kind and good at what they do, they're based in Tomohon, run private tours and also have a vanilla farm you can visit. For transparency: I built their website. In the afternoon you can see the surroundings, between **Lake Linow** and the Lokon and Mahawu volcanoes, weather permitting.

**In practice:** Denpasar to Manado direct about 2.5 hours, route not always running; via Makassar about 4-5 hours with the stopover · Manado airport to Tomohon about 1-1.5 hours by car · in Tomohon you need a sweater in the evening · transfers and tours to be agreed beforehand with Wale Adventure.

### Day 5 — Tangkoko, the forest of the tarsiers
A day in the **Tangkoko** reserve, near Bitung on the northeastern tip of Sulawesi: a lowland forest below Mount Tangkoko. You walk with a local guide looking for **tarsiers**, tiny primates with huge eyes, and **crested black macaques**. Timing matters: early morning is best for the macaques and for birds such as hornbills, while the tarsiers are mostly seen at dusk, when they come out of the hollow trees where they sleep.

It's a long outing: Wale Adventure offers it as a day trip or with a night near the reserve. A night in **Batuputih**, the village at the entrance, lets you do both walks without driving back in the dark. *In the forest you need closed shoes, long trousers and repellent: mosquitoes and tiny mites are plentiful. Tarsiers should not be lit up with a torch or flash.*

**In practice:** Tomohon to Tangkoko by car about 2-2.5 hours · entry for foreigners about 100,000 IDR per day, more at weekends, local guide compulsory and paid separately · walks last 2-3 hours · night in Batuputih or back towards Manado.

### Day 6 — Bunaken and the walls that drop into the blue
By boat from the Manado pier to **Bunaken National Marine Park**. The crossing is short and boats leave in the morning, when the sea is usually calmer. Coral walls drop sheer into the blue: you turn onto your back and there's no bottom below. You can snorkel or dive. Along the walls you often meet turtles, schools of fish and soft corals, a few meters below the surface.

For diving, compare operators, looking at safety, small groups and the state of the gear more than the price. Snorkelers get dropped at the more sheltered parts of the walls, because in the middle of the channel the current can be strong. Lunch is on the island or on the boat, and you're back in Manado in the afternoon. *There's a flight the next day: divers must respect the safety interval between the last dive and take-off.*

**In practice:** boat Manado to Bunaken about 30-60 minutes · park entry for foreigners about 50,000 IDR per day, often included in tours · reef-safe sunscreen and a UV shirt · night in Manado, near the airport.

### Day 7 — Flight to Sorong, ferry to Waisai and Kri
Morning flight from Manado to **Sorong**, then the 2 pm ferry to **Waisai**, about 2 hours. The flight takes just under an hour and a half, but flying east you lose an hour to the time zone. From the airport to Sorong port is a short taxi ride: get there with time to spare, buy the tickets and make a last withdrawal, because there are no ATMs on the islands. In Waisai you buy the park permit, and the homestay boat takes you to **Kri**.

On Kri we stayed at **Paparisa Homestay**, the right mix of comfort and local stay: prices are above the average of other homestays, but dolphins and dugongs often swim right off the beach. Staying in homestays instead of resorts is the choice worth making, because that model keeps the park's protection going. You arrive in the late afternoon, with time for a first swim off the jetty before dark.

**In practice:** Manado to Sorong direct about 1.5 hours with Lion Air or TransNusa, schedules to check · Sorong-Waisai ferry at 2 pm, ticket at the port in economy or VIP · park permit in Waisai or Sorong, keep the receipt · Waisai-Kri boat to be arranged with the homestay.

### Day 8 — Kri and the Arborek reef
Snorkeling day. The reefs around Kri are among the most species-rich in the world, and many can be reached straight from the jetty or with short boat trips. Morning is the best time, with high light and an often flatter sea, while the middle of the day is for the shade. From Kri boats also go to the **Arborek** reef, an island with a village and several homestays, known for the schools of fish under its jetty.

If you want to stay there, you'll find them on the local homestay portal, stayrajaampat.com: for example **Worisun** and **Adefiro**, with over-water bungalows and very good reviews. Divers go out with **Soul Scuba Divers**, on Kri island. The currents are strong: get in the water when the guide says so. *Never touch the corals and never rest your fins on the bottom: a single kick breaks colonies that have been growing for decades.*

**In practice:** boat trips to be arranged with the homestay the day before, price per boat to be shared · dives to be booked ahead in high season · in Arborek village always ask before photographing people · cash in rupiah for everything.

### Day 9 — To the Fam Islands and Piaynemo at sunset
Boat transfer to the **Fam Islands**, further northwest, where the landscape changes: limestone islets covered in forest, turquoise lagoons and very few places to stay. The crossing from Kri takes a few hours and can get longer with the wind, so it's best to leave in the morning and keep phones and documents dry in a dry bag.

We stayed at **Tanjung Mefiyai Homestay**: very simple huts, but in a stunning spot. Reno is super kind and helpful, and he took care of us on the trips and transfers. In the afternoon you climb **Piaynemo**, the viewpoint over the rock islands. At sunset, with the wooden stairs and the crowded platform, it's the only moment you run into anyone else. There are about three hundred steps, with rest platforms along the way: the climb takes a quarter of an hour at an easy pace.

**In practice:** Kri-Fam boat to be arranged with the arrival homestay, duration depends on the sea · Piaynemo covered by the park permit, possibly a small donation to the village · shoes with grip for the wet steps · Fam homestays include meals and often have power only in the evening.

### Day 10 — Fam: Rufas and the Fam Wall
A day on the boat with Reno between **Rufas** and the **Fam Wall**, snorkeling. Rufas is a bay among the rock islets with shallow, clear water, while the Fam Wall is a coral wall dropping towards the blue, with soft corals and gorgonian fans. The tide decides the order of the stops: some spots are done with the right current, others only at high water.

If the wind picks up, outings to the most exposed areas can be canceled: whoever knows the sea decides. In August the wind made us skip an outing to a more exposed area. That's why it's best to keep the day flexible and not lock in anything rigid. In the late afternoon you go back to the homestay, with time for a last swim off the beach. *In the evening you pack and agree with the homestay on the boat time for the next day.*

**In practice:** boat trip with the homestay, price to be settled beforehand and paid in cash · bring your own mask and fins, because borrowed ones aren't always in good shape · water and a hat on the boat, you're outdoors all day · no ATMs in Fam.

### Day 11 — Back to Sorong and flight to Bali
Early boat from Fam to Waisai, ferry to **Sorong** and flight to Denpasar, direct with Garuda in the months the route runs, otherwise with a stopover. It's a full travel day, and with the wind the boat can take longer. If you can, spend a night in Sorong and fly the next day. To be safe, agree with the homestay on a dawn departure, when the sea is often calmer.

In Waisai the morning ferry doesn't run every day, so the timetable should be checked locally before booking the flight. From Sorong the flight to Bali puts you back an hour, and with a stopover in Makassar or Jakarta the arrival can slip to late evening. *With a night's margin in Sorong, a delayed boat won't cost you the international flight.*

**In practice:** Fam-Waisai boat to be arranged with the homestay, at dawn · Waisai-Sorong ferry about 2 hours, schedules to check in Waisai · Garuda direct Sorong-Denpasar is seasonal, in 2026 running from late October to March 2027 · a margin night in Sorong is recommended.

### Day 12 — Departure
Last day in Bali or Jakarta, depending on your international flight. With Indonesian domestic flights it's always better not to have tight connections on the day you fly home. With an evening flight you can leave your bags at the hotel and spend the morning near the airport, on the beach at **Jimbaran** or **Sanur**, without going too far because of the traffic.

The way home is the moment to tidy up the last things: rupiah to spend before leaving, permit receipts, flight documents downloaded to your phone. For international flights from Denpasar it's best to be at the airport at least three hours ahead, because security and check-in queues can be long at peak times.

**In practice:** hotel with luggage storage or day-use near the airport · Jimbaran and Sanur are the handiest beaches before the flight · at the airport at least 3 hours before international flights · leftover rupiah to change or spend before security.

## Sulawesi only or Raja Ampat only: the two 12-day versions
Three islands in twelve days can be done, but every island deserves at least a week. The destinations are interchangeable: with the same time you can pick just one island besides Bali, depending on the season.

**Bali + Raja Ampat (October to April).** Three days on Bali, then a flight from Denpasar to Sorong and eight days between Kri, Arborek and the Fam Islands, with more time for diving and for slow transfers. It's the best version for manta rays and big pelagics, which arrive mostly in the winter months. Misool, in the south, needs a liveaboard or an extra week.

**Bali + Sulawesi (May to October).** Three days on Bali, then a flight to Makassar and the road to **Tana Toraja**: 8-10 hours by car or bus, including overnight. There are flights between Makassar and Toraja airport, but they're few and can be canceled: check them first. Three days in Toraja among tongkonan villages, rock tombs and, if it happens, a funeral. Then a flight from Makassar to Manado for Tangkoko and Bunaken, and back from Manado to Bali. Tana Toraja is the most different thing I've seen in Indonesia: a Toraja funeral lasts days, gathers hundreds of people and includes the sacrifice of buffalo in front of everyone. It's best to go with a local guide who knows the rules of respect, and to bring a gift for the family, usually cigarettes. Funeral season is mainly July-August, after the harvest. Toraja can also be organized with Wale Adventure.

**Toraja instead of the north, with Raja Ampat.** If you want Toraja and Raja Ampat together, go through Makassar, which has flights to Sorong. But the road to Toraja eats up two days: in twelve days there's little left for everything else.

## What to see / what to do
- **Bali's rice terraces**, Tegalalang at dawn or Sidemen for peace and quiet.
- **The Kecak dance at Uluwatu** at sunset.
- **The tarsiers of Tangkoko**, with a local guide.
- **The walls of Bunaken**, snorkeling or diving.
- **The Arborek reef** and the dives around Kri.
- **Piaynemo** and the Fam Islands, with Rufas and the Fam Wall.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't book tight connections** between domestic flights: routes and times change.
- **Buy the Raja Ampat permit only at official points** (Sorong or Waisai port) and keep the receipt.
- **Withdraw cash in Sorong or Waisai:** there are no ATMs on the islands.
- **Stay in Raja Ampat homestays:** they cost less than resorts and the money reaches the families.
- **Factor in the wind** from May to September: some longer transfers and some canceled outings.
- **Respect the currents:** in Raja Ampat some spots are only done at the right times.
- **Go to a Toraja funeral with a local guide:** there are rules of respect to know, and a gift for the family.
- **Insurance with evacuation** for the Papua part.
- **Pay the Bali levy online** before you leave.

## Chiusura personale
Raja Ampat is the most beautiful place I've seen underwater. I went in August, which every guide marks as the wrong season: the wind stretched a couple of transfers, but the sea was stunning and in ten days I didn't see a drop of rain. With reefs full of fish and hardly any other tourists, for me it was a compromise I don't regret.

I went back to Indonesia for the people, the places and the smiles. Twelve days for three islands is not much: if you can, pick one and give it the week it deserves.
