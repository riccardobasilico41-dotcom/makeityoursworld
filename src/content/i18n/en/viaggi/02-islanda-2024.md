# Iceland by Tent: 9 Days on the Island of Ice and Fire (Ring Road)

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress
**Periodo del viaggio:** late July 2024
**Durata:** 9 days, 8 nights (6 in a tent, 2 in a hotel)
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, shared rental car, a common kitty for meals along the way
**Categoria:** Europa · Islanda · on the road · campeggio · Ring Road

---

## Apertura personale
At two in the morning, in front of the plane wreck at Sólheimasandur, there was still light. We had set out after dinner, with four kilometers of black sand to cover on foot, and the late-July sun had never quite made up its mind to disappear. Then there's waking up in the tent to the sound of waterfalls. And above all the kayak on the icy lagoon of Jökulsárlón, among the icebergs, with seals all around: there, all I could say was "wow." This is the trip we did: nine days on the Ring Road, six nights in a tent and a rental car, from Keflavík to Keflavík.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** summer. The weather stays changeable anyway, between 10 and 20°C by day,
  and at night it can drop to 0°C, with a lot of humidity.
- **Documents/visa:** none, Iceland is in the Schengen area; an ID card valid
  for travel abroad is enough (better to bring your passport too). Driver's license for
  whoever drives.
- **How I got around:** flight to Keflavík, rental car (Sixt), rented tent
  on site.
- **Budget:** meals and groceries handled with a common kitty (useful when traveling
  with others). In Iceland alcohol is extremely expensive (best to bring it from home or buy it at
  duty-free), and the same goes for coffee and moka pots.
- **Weather:** very strong wind: car doors must always be held shut, so as not to
  risk breaking them. At night it gets close to 0°C.
- **Driving:** dirt roads, rough, often without guardrails, with stretches of
  2-3 hours at a time (up to 7h in a day). Best to have two people who can
  drive, to take turns on the longest stretches.
- **From the airport to the centre:** you land at **Keflavík (KEF)**, about 50 km from Reykjavík. **Flybus** to the BSÍ terminal in about 45 minutes, about 4,000 ISK, with departures timed to arrivals (online, at the desk or on board; Flybus+ takes you to the hotels) · **Airport Direct**, to its own terminal or to hotels, about 5,000 ISK · **Strætó bus 55**, the cheapest (about 2,400 ISK, on board or in the app), but slow and with few runs · **taxi** about 20,000-25,000 ISK, no Uber · if you rent a car, you pick it up right at the airport, and that's the easiest option.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 0
Departure day from Italy. There are direct flights to Keflavík, mostly seasonal, and connections via Northern Europe; the direct flight takes about four hours. This is the day to finish the logistics you can't improvise in Iceland: car and tent booked weeks ahead, the Ring Road campsites marked on the map, a credit card with a PIN for the fuel pumps, which are almost all self-service and often unstaffed.

Pack warm, waterproof layers even in July, because at night it drops close to 0°C, and a sleep mask: with the midnight sun the tent never gets dark. On arrival, before leaving the baggage hall, there's the Keflavík duty-free, where alcohol costs much less than at Vínbúðin, the state shops that hold the monopoly on the island. *The day before, check the weather, roads and the state of the volcanic area on safetravel.is and vedur.is.*

**In practice:** direct flight Italy-Keflavík about 4 hours · book car and tent well ahead for July and August · card with a PIN for fuel · alcohol from the arrivals duty-free.

### Day 1 — Arrival in Keflavík and the eruption seen up close
We landed in Keflavík in the late afternoon, and the first Iceland was the unromantic one of the airport area. Bus 55 takes you in about twenty minutes to the *Kellir* stop (€4 per person), a stone's throw from the Konvin Hotel: just long enough to drop the backpacks and we went out for dinner.

We ate at **Kaffi Duus**, mixed fish for about €30. Here I tried **whale fillet**. It's a dish that sparks debate: in Iceland whaling is still allowed but highly contested, and it's worth forming an opinion before ordering it. You still get there with the 55: five minutes' walk to the stop, ten by bus, another ten on foot from Hringbraut & Melteigur. Afterwards we did a round of the bars in Keflavík and went back, again by bus, with an eye on the clock: the last run comes just after 11 p.m. and can't be missed.

It's in these first days, on the Reykjanes peninsula, that I saw the **erupting volcano** up close: the Grindavík and Fagradalsfjall area, a few minutes from the airport, became active several times between 2023 and 2024. I went on my own, without a tour. It was one of the most powerful experiences of the whole trip, especially on the way up, when we walked alongside the lava flows: you could see them from a safe distance, the lava slowly creeping down. Glowing rock: wow. Eruptions come and go, though, and the area is often closed: before leaving, check the status of the area on safetravel.is.

**In practice:** bus 55 from the airport to the Kellir stop about €4 (to Reykjavík about 2,400 ISK), last bus just after 23:00 · dinner at Kaffi Duus about €30 · before heading to the eruption, check closures and air quality on safetravel.is · on Reykjanes you need hiking shoes and a windproof jacket even in summer.

### Day 2 — Golden Circle: Geysir and Gullfoss
The day started early and with logistics: the car from Sixt at Keflavík airport, then the tent from RENTATENT (Vesturvör 32b, Kópavogur, opens at 9). From there the trip really began, toward the Golden Circle.

At **Geysir** the water shoots up every six or seven minutes: in a quarter of an hour you see it two or three times. A bit further on is **Gullfoss**, the "golden waterfall," with its two drops of 11 and 21 meters on the Hvítá river. Packed lunch, then a timed half hour at **Krónan** in Hvolsvöllur (Austurvegur 4) for groceries for the camping days.

The afternoon was a string of waterfalls. At **Seljalandsfoss** (parking 700 ISK) you walk behind the curtain of water; the nearby Gljúfrabúi is reached by walking inside the river. Half an hour by car and there's **Skógafoss**, with its roughly 470 steps up to the top of the fall. In the evening we pitched the tent for the first time at a campsite at the foot of a big waterfall and had dinner there. I don't remember its name: along this stretch of the south coast there are the ones at Skógafoss and Seljalandsfoss.

Then the decision made almost as a joke. At the end of July the sun doesn't set, tiredness isn't enough, and so, instead of sleeping, we set off again for the **DC-3 wreck at Sólheimasandur**, abandoned on the sand since 1973. Twenty minutes by car to the parking lot on the Ring Road, then 4 km on foot over black sand, flat and with no shelter from the wind, between 40 and 60 minutes each way. *A real slog after an already full day, but probably the most absurd and memorable moment of the whole trip.* We got back to the campsite in the dead of night, with a light that never fully faded.

**In practice:** Geysir and Gullfoss are free · Seljalandsfoss parking 700 ISK · the campsites closest to the waterfalls are at Skógafoss and Seljalandsfoss; further on, the Vík one costs about 1,950 ISK, shower 300 ISK · cars can't go past the parking lot for the DC-3: 4 km on foot each way · shop in Hvolsvöllur, further on supermarkets are few.

### Day 3 — Dyrhólaey, Reynisfjara and the glacial lagoon
The alarm came later than planned, because of the wreck. The best night in the tent was actually the first one: we'd arrived exhausted and mentally unprepared, and waking up in the morning right next to the waterfall was something else, wow. We set off unhurriedly toward **Dyrhólaey**: the lighthouse, the black lava arch over the sea, the sea stacks and, in summer, puffins. Right after is **Reynisfjara**, the black beach with basalt columns. Here the waves are unpredictable and dangerous: follow the signs to the letter. Quick lunch at the beach stand, hot dog or soup for 2-3,000 ISK.

In the afternoon we headed inland. An hour and a quarter of driving takes you to the **Fjaðrárgljúfur** canyon, about a hundred meters deep and two kilometers long, much less crowded than the other places in the area. Another three quarters of an hour and you enter **Skaftafell**, in Vatnajökull park, the largest in Europe, where trails also lead to the black waterfall of Svartifoss.

From Skaftafell I did the **trek on the Vatnajökull glacier** with **[Troll Expeditions](https://troll.is/)**, who I did a couple of excursions with: really nice, with crampons, ice axe and a mountain guide: crevasses, blue ice and absolute silence. It's one of the most intense activities of the trip. An hour further east is **Jökulsárlón**, the lagoon where the glacier calves its icebergs, which float almost out to sea. I saw it by **kayak**, paddling among blocks of ice with seals often just a few meters away: a completely different thing from the crowded viewpoint. Next to it is **Diamond Beach**, where ice washes up on the black sand.

The last hour of driving leads to **Höfn**, where we arrived late in the evening. You sleep at a campsite, either Vestrahorn at the foot of the mountain, 18 minutes from town, or the municipal one in the center. For dinner there's fish soup from the "capital of Icelandic lobster," or you cook at the tent.

**In practice:** at Reynisfjara keep away from the waterline, sneaker waves have killed people · paid parking at Skaftafell and Jökulsárlón · glacier hike and kayak only with a guide, book ahead for July and August · from Skaftafell to Höfn about two hours of driving with the lagoon stop.

### Day 4 — Viking Village, the East Fjords and Egilsstaðir
Breakfast at the campsite and a short walk to the **Viking Village**, the film set built in 2010 next to Vestrahorn: a reconstructed Viking village, with horses grazing around it.

Then the day belonged to the road. Route 1 along the **East Fjords** is about 250 km: three or four hours on paper, which easily become five or six, because the road follows every single fjord and you travel at 50-60 km/h. It's one of the least touristy areas of the island, the wildest, and in summer colonies of puffins live there. Those with time can detour to **Seyðisfjörður**, a small town tucked among the mountains and known for its art scene.

In the late afternoon we arrived in **Egilsstaðir**, the capital of the east with about 2,500 inhabitants, and pitched the tent at **Camp Egilsstaðir**: bathrooms, showers, laundry, indoor and outdoor kitchens, free wifi, about 2,000 ISK per person. Ten minutes away are the **Vök Baths**, the hot pool floating on Lake Urriðavatn: the first real hot bath after two days of hiking and wind.

**In practice:** the Viking Village is visited with a ticket paid at the Viking Café, at the start of the road to Stokksnes · East Fjords about 250 km, allow five or six hours · Camp Egilsstaðir about 2,000 ISK per person · book Vök Baths online, especially in the late afternoon.

### Day 5 — Stuðlagil, Dettifoss and Ásbyrgi
After an hour of driving, with the last stretch on dirt road, we reached **Stuðlagil Canyon**: basalt columns 30 meters high and a turquoise river. It only became famous after 2017, when the Kárahnjúkar hydroelectric plant lowered the water level, and it remains one of the less-visited stops.

Back on Route 1, in two hours you reach **Dettifoss**, the most powerful waterfall in Europe: a 44-meter drop and up to 500 m³ of muddy water per second in summer. You hear the roar before you see it. The best view is from the west side, with a parking lot and trail off Route 862, which is paved.

Forty minutes later comes **Ásbyrgi**, the horseshoe-shaped canyon that legend attributes to a hoof of Odin's eight-legged horse. We walked among the green walls to the Botnstjörn pond and slept at the **Ásbyrgi campsite**, in the heart of the park (open from mid-May to the end of October, with electricity), from which several trails start. Dinner at the campsite.

**In practice:** at Stuðlagil the west side has the viewing platform near the parking lot, the east side needs a walk of a few kilometers but reaches the canyon floor · Dettifoss west side from the paved Route 862 · Ásbyrgi campsite open mid-May to late October · fill up when you can, fuel stations are sparse in the northeast.

### Day 6 — Húsavík, whale watching and Lake Mývatn
An hour's drive brought us to **Húsavík**, which calls itself the whale watching capital of the world. The classic outing lasts three hours and leaves from the old harbor, on one of the restored traditional oak boats. In Skjálfandi Bay you look for humpbacks and minke whales; with luck, orcas or blue whales. We saw whales and puffins really up close, but it was freezing cold, even with the extra suits they gave us.

After a quick lunch, another hour by car took us to **Lake Mývatn**, one of the most active geothermal areas on the island. In a few hours you go from the **Grjótagjá** cave, with the hot spring from a Game of Thrones scene (not swimmable today, the temperature is unpredictable), to the ring crater of **Hverfjall**, to the fumaroles and boiling mud of **Hverir**, where the smell of sulfur is overpowering, to the lava mazes of **Dimmuborgir**, with the natural "church" of Kirkjan. The last stop is **Goðafoss**, the "waterfall of the gods," tied to the Christianization of Iceland in the year 1000.

In the evening, three quarters of an hour later, we arrived in **Akureyri**. We slept at the **Hamrar** campsite, at the edge of the Kjarnaskógur forest, and the groceries bought in town turned into a barbecue.

**In practice:** whale watching about 3 hours, book the day before in high season, usually with a windproof suit provided on board · at Mývatn in summer there are clouds of midges: a head net helps · at Grjótagjá you look, you don't bathe · from Goðafoss to Akureyri about 45 minutes.

### Day 7 — Toward the Snæfellsnes peninsula
It was almost all transfer: about 390 km from Akureyri to the **Snæfellsnes** peninsula, five actual hours via Route 1 and Borgarnes, six or seven with stops. Along the road in the northwest you meet the **turf houses** with grass roofs and, if there's time, a little-visited natural hot pool like **Guðrúnarlaug**, toward the Westfjords: basic changing room and free entry.

In the late afternoon **Kirkjufell** appeared, the 463 most photographed meters in Iceland, with the Kirkjufellsfoss waterfall in front. The parking lot is right next to the waterfall and the classic photo requires no walking; with the long summer light it's the right place for sunset. We spent the last night in the tent at the **Grundarfjörður** campsite, at the foot of the mountain, with a view of the fjord. Groceries at the town's shops, ten minutes on foot, and dinner at the campsite.

**In practice:** Akureyri-Snæfellsnes about 390 km, five hours of actual driving · Guðrúnarlaug is free · at Kirkjufellsfoss the parking lot is right by the waterfall · Grundarfjörður campsite at the foot of Kirkjufell · shop in Akureyri or Borgarnes, there are few stores along the way.

### Day 8 — Thermal relaxation and return to Reykjavík
We took down the tent for the last time, unhurriedly. On the way back there's room for one last hot stop in West Iceland, in the area of Deildartunguhver, the most powerful hot spring in Europe, and Krauma. Then two and a half hours, 180 km via Borgarnes, to **Reykjavík**. **Deildartunguhver** can be seen for free from the boardwalks, with steam pouring out nonstop; next to it is **Krauma**, the spa whose pools are filled with the same water, for a fee. *Before returning the car, fill up and photograph it from every side: damage disputes are the most common hassle.*

In the late afternoon we returned the car and tent and checked into the hotel: after six nights in a tent, a real bed. The rest of the day is free in the center: the **Kolaportið** flea market if it's Saturday or Sunday, the colorful streets, the Hallgrímskirkja church. In the evening, a last dinner downtown to close out the trip. If you still have energy, go up the Hallgrímskirkja tower, with a separate ticket, for the view over the colorful roofs, or take a dip in a municipal pool, where Icelanders go: it costs much less than the thermal lagoons.

**In practice:** Deildartunguhver free, Krauma paid with booking recommended · from the thermal area to Reykjavík about two and a half hours · Kolaportið open only on Saturdays and Sundays · central Reykjavík is all walkable.

### Day 9 — Return
Check-out and transfer to **Keflavík**, about 45 minutes from Reykjavík, depending on the flight time. Without a car the easiest option is the **Flybus**, the shuttle that leaves from the BSÍ terminal in the center, with pick-ups at some hotels on request. Get to the airport at least two hours early: in summer the security lines at Keflavík are long and the check-in desks open in waves.

With an afternoon flight you can use the morning for one last hot soak: the **Blue Lagoon** is about twenty minutes from the airport, entry is by online booking only and there's luggage storage, so you can go straight there with your backpack. The area is close to the Reykjanes eruptions, though, and is sometimes closed. If you've made major purchases, there's a VAT refund desk at the airport. *Keep the receipt and the item at hand, because they may ask to see it.*

**In practice:** Flybus from BSÍ about 45 minutes, ticket online · at the airport at least two hours early, more in July and August · Blue Lagoon by booking only, check on safetravel.is that the area is open · VAT refund at the airport desk.

## What to see / what to do
- **Erupting volcano on the Reykjanes peninsula:** seen up close during the trip, one of the most powerful experiences in all of Iceland.
- **Vatnajökull glacier trek** from Skaftafell, with crampons and a guide: crevasses and blue ice a few steps away.
- **Kayaking among the icebergs at Jökulsárlón:** you experience the glacial lagoon much more from the inside than from the viewpoint alone.
- **Geysir and Gullfoss:** the classic Golden Circle, not to be skipped.
- **Seljalandsfoss** (you walk behind it) and **Skógafoss**: the most photogenic waterfalls
  in the south.
- **Plane wreck at Sólheimasandur:** I went **on foot, at
  night**, thanks to the late-July light: 4 km each way on
  black sand, a good hour each direction. *The real gem of the trip, but only if
  you're willing to take on a slog after an already full day.*
- **Reynisfjara:** black sand beach, beautiful but to be respected for
  safety.
- **Jökulsárlón and Diamond Beach:** the glacial lagoon with seals and
  icebergs, and the beach where they run aground on the black sand.
- **Stuðlagil Canyon:** a less-visited gem, basalt columns and turquoise
  water.
- **Mývatn area:** geothermal and volcanic phenomena concentrated in a few hours
  of visit (Hverir, Dimmuborgir, Grjótagjá, Goðafoss).
- **Dettifoss:** the most powerful waterfall in Europe, striking even just for
  the roar.
- **Kirkjufell:** the most photographed mountain in Iceland, great at sunset.
- **Vök Baths:** floating hot pool near Egilsstaðir, a great break
  midway through the trip.
- **Whale watching in Húsavík:** the "world capital" of whale
  sightings, classic 3-hour boat tour.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
- **Keflavík — Konvin Hotel (Day 1).** One of the largest hotels in the airport
  area, convenient for arrival thanks to the shuttle, with a generous breakfast.
  Online reviews (about 3/5 across nearly 940 Tripadvisor reviews)
  give an average picture for a one-night airport stop: those who choose it
  for its proximity to the airport find it practical and the staff friendly, others
  mention cold rooms or bathrooms that aren't spotless. Nothing special, but
  functional for the first night.
  #### Alternatives in Keflavík / Reykjanesbær
  - Budget: guesthouses and small B&Bs in the Reykjanesbær area.
  - Mid-range (the Konvin Hotel falls here): other hotels near the airport
    with shuttle included.
  - More refined: boutique hotels in central Reykjavík (~45 min by car), for those who
    prefer to give up airport convenience on the first or last night.

- **First night — a campsite at the foot of a waterfall (Day 2).** I don't remember which one: along this stretch there are the ones at Skógafoss and Seljalandsfoss, with the waterfall a few steps from the tent.
- **Vík — equipped campsite, 1,950 ISK + shower 300 ISK (alternative for Day 2).** The
  Vík campsite (electricity, showers, shared kitchen) is effectively the only large
  facility in the area: convenient and well equipped, but in the most recent reviews
  people complain about overcrowding in high season.
  #### Alternatives in Vík
  - Budget: **The Barn** (one of the best hostels in Iceland) or **Farmhouse
    Lodge Guesthouse**.
  - Mid-range: **Hotel Kría**, Nordic design on the town's main street.
  - More refined: **Hótel Vík í Mýrdal**.

- **Höfn — Vestrahorn campsite or municipal campsite (Day 3).** The Vestrahorn campsite (~2,800 ISK per person/night)
  includes entry to the Viking Village and the view over Stokksnes, but some
  reviews mention only two showers, available at limited hours; the
  municipal campsite on Hafnarbraut (~1,800 ISK) is more central, near the
  town pool.
  #### Alternatives in Höfn
  - Budget: **Höfn HI Hostel**, **Guesthouse Dyngja**.
  - Mid-range: **Hotel Höfn**, **The Old Airline Guesthouse**.
  - More refined: **Fosshotel Vatnajökull**, **Brunnhóll Country Guesthouse**.

- **Egilsstaðir — Camp Egilsstaðir (Day 4).** Well-reviewed campsite
  (4/5 on Tripadvisor): clean bathrooms, washers and dryers, indoor
  kitchens, electricity, about 2,000-2,400 ISK a night.
  #### Alternatives in Egilsstaðir
  - Budget: **Hafaldan HI Hostel** (Harbour building).
  - Mid-range: **Við Lónið Guesthouse**.
  - More refined: **Gistihúsið Lake Hotel Egilsstaðir** or **Hotel Valaskjálf**.

- **Ásbyrgi — national park campsite (Day 5).** Large campsite
  (up to 350 pitches), open from mid-May to the end of October, with good
  facilities according to reviews (clean bathrooms, showers, laundry). In high
  season it's best to book.
  #### Alternatives in Ásbyrgi
  A very remote area, with few real alternatives nearby: in Kópasker
  (about 35-40 minutes) there are **Kópasker Guesthouse**, **MELAR Guesthouse**
  and **Kópasker HI Hostel** (budget/mid-range). There are no luxury options in the surroundings: in this area the choice stays between the
  campsite itself (which also offers small studios) and the guesthouses of Kópasker.

- **Akureyri area — Hamrar campsite (Day 6).** Among the best-reviewed campsites
  in Iceland (4/5 on Tripadvisor), at the edge of the Kjarnaskógur
  forest, with nearly new showers and excellent facilities; indicative price
  around 1,500 ISK per person plus a fee for the tent.
  #### Alternatives in Akureyri
  - Budget: **Akureyri Backpackers**, **Guesthouse Bægisá**.
  - Mid-range: **Centrum Hotel**.
  - More refined: **Hotel Kea**, **Bryggjan Boutique Hotel**.

- **Grundarfjörður — campsite at the foot of Kirkjufell (Day 7).** Simple and
  scenic, with a direct view of the fjord and the mountain.
  #### Alternatives in Grundarfjörður
  In town the options are few: the **Kirkjufell Guesthouse and Apartments**
  (from about $180/night) is effectively the main mid-range choice, and
  the town itself lacks real budget or luxury alternatives. For more
  choice it's worth looking toward **Stykkishólmur**, about 25 minutes by car.

- **Reykjavík — hotel, last night (Day 8).**
  #### Alternatives in Reykjavík
  - Budget: **KEX Hostel**, **Loft HI Eco Hostel**.
  - Mid-range: hotels in the Þingholt area, **Hlemmur Square**.
  - More refined: **The Reykjavik EDITION**, **Canopy by Hilton Reykjavík**.

## Practical tips / what to pack
- **Backpack, not a wheeled suitcase**: 50/60 liters, to check in (not as carry-on, especially
  with low-cost airlines).
- **Sleeping bag, sleeping pad, camping pillow** (if you don't rent them on site).
  Luckily, when we went it never rained hard at night, but a couple of evenings were seriously cold. The least pleasant part? Climbing out of a nice warm sleeping bag to go to the bathroom. Brutal, ha.
- **Thermal underwear**, waterproof windbreaker with a hood (no umbrellas, the
  wind is too strong), hiking shoes + a spare pair.
- **Swimsuit that isn't new or precious**: the geothermal waters of the Blue Lagoon
  ruin fabrics if you don't rinse them right away.
- Hat, scarf/neck warmer, gloves, tall thick socks.
- Changes of clothes for ~9 days: few campsites have a washer/dryer.
- Small daypack/waist pack for excursions (the main luggage stays in the car).
- Personal medical kit, plus a basic one to share if you're in a group
  (anti-diarrheal, anti-nausea, fever reducer, painkiller).
- Refillable bottle (Icelandic tap water is excellent), power bank (at campsites you can only
  recharge once a day), personal mug, Swiss
  army knife, waterproof pouch for "wet" outings (e.g. whale watching).
- **If traveling in a group:** it's worth sharing the hair dryer (one for every 2-3
  people) and remembering to bring alcohol and coffee/moka pots from home (in Iceland
  they're extremely expensive and hard to find).
- Data roaming is included in EU/EEA plans; usually the plans also include
  calls to Italy.
- Car rules: always two people who can drive per car, always close the
  doors because of the wind, respect the limits (fines are steep and, if the car is a shared rental,
  they have to be settled with whoever was driving).

## Chiusura personale
If I did it again, I'd be much more careful about distances and the unexpected. It takes very little here: a flat tire or, as happened to us, a dead car battery. I don't remember exactly where we were, I think near where you see the whales, but I couldn't say for sure. We called a tow truck and paid a fortune, because we didn't have roadside assistance insurance. We had to postpone everything and skip some stops. That's why my advice is to plan without cramming everything in and to leave a margin between one stop and the next. And bring food from home, because in Iceland it costs a lot, really a lot.
