# Complete Egypt in 21 Days: Pyramids, Siwa, Abu Simbel, the Nile and Diving in the Red Sea

**Durata:** 21 days, 20 nights

## Apertura personale
Three weeks is the time it takes to see Egypt without having to choose. The trip takes in Cairo with the three pyramid necropolises, Giza, Saqqara and Dahshur; then heads west to Siwa, the Berber oasis on the edge of the Great Sand Sea, which few itineraries include because it's far from everything. Then you go down to Upper Egypt: Aswan, Abu Simbel at dawn, three nights of Nile cruise up to Luxor, two full days on the west bank. It ends with four days of diving at Marsa Alam, in the southern stretch of the Red Sea, the one with the most intact reefs.

It's a trip for people who have already traveled outside Europe and can accept a bit of logistics: a bus or a car for eight hours in the desert, checkpoints, 3 am wake-ups for Abu Simbel, lots of haggling. In return it offers a variety that few countries have within such a small radius. Anyone with less time will find the 11-day version in the guide "Egypt in 11 Days: Cairo, the Pyramids, Luxor and the Red Sea".

## Scheda pratica

### When to go, month by month
- **October:** the best choice for the whole circuit. Luxor and Aswan at 30-35 °C (86-95 °F), Siwa pleasant, sea at 27-28 °C (81-82 °F), oceanic whitetip sharks at Elphinstone and the Brothers. On October 22 there's the solar phenomenon at Abu Simbel, with crowds and high prices.
- **November:** ideal temperatures everywhere, sea still at 25-26 °C (77-79 °F). Last good month for pelagic sharks.
- **December-February:** high season at the sites, cool days, cold nights (close to 0 °C / 32 °F in Siwa and the desert). Sea at 22-23 °C (72-73 °F), strong wind, 5-7 mm wetsuit. Christmas and New Year are the priciest period, with cruises full. On February 22 the second solar phenomenon at Abu Simbel.
- **March-April:** again a good compromise, with the risk of sandy wind (khamsin). Ramadan 2027 roughly from February 8 to March 9: reduced hours at the sites, restaurants closed during the day outside tourist areas.
- **May-September:** warm sea and, from June to August, hammerhead sharks at the Brothers and Daedalus, but Aswan, Luxor and Abu Simbel often exceed 40-45 °C (104-113 °F). It can only be done with dawn visits and afternoons off. Siwa in summer is not recommended.

### Documents, visa and permits
- **Documents:** passport with at least 6 months of remaining validity on arrival. For tourism, the electronic ID card valid for travel abroad with more than 6 months remaining and two passport photos is also accepted, but not one extended with the paper slip; for this itinerary, with checkpoints, Siwa and a cruise, the passport is much more practical.
- **Visa:** $30 on arrival in Cairo (increased as of March 1, 2026; Viaggiare Sicuri still reports $25) or e-visa on the official portal visa2egypt.gov.eg (roughly $25-30 single, $60-65 multiple). The free Sinai stamp isn't valid for this trip.
- **Siwa and the Western Desert:** the oasis can be reached freely with documents in order, but along the road there are several military checkpoints that register passports. To go out into the **Great Sand Sea** and the areas toward the Libyan border you need a permit, obtained through an authorized local operator, usually within 24 hours: you hand over the passport the evening before. Viaggiare Sicuri asks to avoid the areas near the border with Libya, strongly advises against desert excursions outside the tourist areas (in many there's no GPS coverage either) and recommends not traveling by road at night: in Siwa you move around only with authorized operators, and by day.
- **Diving:** certification and logbook, specific diving insurance (DAN type). See the dedicated section.

### Currency, payments and tips
- **Currency:** Egyptian pound (EGP), roughly 58-62 EGP to the euro in October 2026. Cruises, drivers and diving often in dollars or euros.
- **Cards:** accepted in hotels, cruises, dive centers and mid-range restaurants; the ticket offices of state sites accept cards only, and tickets can also be bought online at egymonuments.com. In Siwa few places accept cards and ATMs are few: you arrive with cash for the whole stay.
- **Baksheesh:** tipping is part of the system. Roughly 10-20 EGP for restrooms and small services, 50-100 EGP a day for the driver, 100-200 EGP a day for a guide, $15-25 per person for the crew of a 3-4 night cruise, 5-10% of the cost for diving. The envelope for the crew is left at reception at the end.

### Getting around
- **Cairo:** Uber and Careem; private driver for Saqqara and Dahshur ($30-50 a day).
- **Cairo-Siwa:** about 750 km. By car with a driver it takes two days, passing through Alexandria and El Alamein and sleeping in Marsa Matruh (roughly $250-400 for the car in total, depends on the operator). There's also the West Delta bus from Cairo, about 10-12 hours and a few tens of euros, but it travels at night: Viaggiare Sicuri recommends not moving by road in the dark, so here we recommend a car with a driver, by day, both ways.
- **Cairo-Aswan:** EgyptAir flight (1 hour 25 minutes, roughly €70-140) or the **overnight sleeper train** by Abela Trains from Cairo Upper Egypt (Bashtil) or Giza stations (no longer from Ramses), about 13-14 hours, $110-125 per person in a shared double cabin and $150-170 alone, dinner and breakfast included, booking at abelatrains.com or with their app. The ordinary day trains cost very little but are slow and less comfortable.
- **Aswan-Abu Simbel:** 280 km, 3-3.5 hours by road. Minivans leave at 3-4 am; there's no longer a mandatory convoy as there used to be, but there are checkpoints. Alternative: 45-minute EgyptAir flight, useful only if the schedules let you see the site without rushing.
- **Aswan-Luxor:** 3-night cruise (ships going the other way take 4 nights), or a 4-5 night sailing **dahabiya**, or 3 hours by car for those who prefer the hotel.
- **Luxor-Marsa Alam:** car with a driver, 4-4.5 hours ($100-150 per vehicle).
- **Return:** direct flights from Marsa Alam or Hurghada to Italy, or a domestic flight to Cairo.

### Classic cruise or dahabiya
- **Cruise ship (3-4 nights):** 60-150 cabins, deck pool, group guide included in most packages. Roughly $450-1,200 per person for 4 nights, all inclusive except tips and a few entrance fees. Downside: dozens of ships dock at the same places at the same times, and at Kom Ombo and Edfu you visit in a crowd.
- **Dahabiya (4-5 nights):** sailing boat with 6-12 cabins, mooring at islands and banks where ships don't stop. Roughly $250-500 per person per night. Downside: it costs double, and without wind you're towed.
- **The choice for this itinerary:** a classic 3-night Aswan-Luxor ship, the easiest to fit in. Anyone who can spend more and has two extra days chooses the dahabiya.

### Diving in the Red Sea
- **The north (Hurghada, Sharm, El Gouna):** famous wrecks and reefs. The **Thistlegorm**, a British ship sunk in 1941 with its cargo of motorcycles and trucks, is done in a day from Hurghada or Sharm or on a cruise; the wrecks of **Abu Nuhas**; the **Ras Mohammed** park (Shark and Yolanda Reef) and the **Tiran** strait from Sharm. The Blue Hole in Dahab is beautiful from the shallow rim, while the deep arch is a technical dive that has claimed many victims.
- **The south (Marsa Alam, Port Ghalib, Hamata):** intact reefs and pelagics. **Elphinstone** (oceanic whitetip sharks from October to December, sometimes in spring too), **Abu Dabbab** (turtles and dugong, great for beginners too), **Dolphin House (Samadai)** with limited numbers, the reefs of **Fury Shoals** and **St. John's** on a cruise.
- **Brothers and Daedalus:** islets in open sea, reachable only by cruise. Schooling hammerhead sharks from June to August, oceanic whitetips from October to December, gray reef sharks and thresher sharks all year. Strong currents.
- **Certifications:** with the **Open Water** (18 meters / 60 ft) you can dive Abu Dabbab, the shore reefs and many boat outings just fine. The **Advanced** (30 meters / 100 ft) is needed for Thistlegorm, Elphinstone and almost all southern cruises. For **Brothers, Daedalus and Elphinstone on a cruise** operators normally require Advanced, at least 50 logged dives and sometimes a buoyancy and surface marker buoy test. **Nitrox** is recommended. A 4-5 day Open Water course costs roughly $400-550.
- **Liveaboard:** 6-7 nights, departing from Hurghada (north and wrecks) or from Port Ghalib/Marsa Alam (south, BDE). Roughly €1,000-1,800 per person plus marine park fees (tens of euros a day) and Nitrox. Book months ahead for October-November and June-July.
- **Rules:** gloves banned in marine parks, no touching coral and no feeding fish; some sites have time limits and night dives are prohibited. Between the last dive and the flight you need **at least 18-24 hours**: the itinerary leaves a day without diving before the return.

### Safety and common scams
Tourist Egypt is safe with respect to violent crime; the real problem is commercial pressure and small scams, much more organized than elsewhere. The script repeats: a friendly contact, an unrequested service, then the request for money.
- **Camels and horses at Giza:** the starting price is low (even 50-100 EGP "for a photo"), and halfway through they ask for much more to bring you back to the entrance. Agree on everything beforehand, in writing if need be, with currency, duration, route and number of people, and pay only at the end. Many prefer the internal electric bus.
- **"Closed" entrance:** taxis or tuk-tuks are stopped before the entrance by someone who says "you can't get in from here" or that the ticket must be bought elsewhere. The official entrances are the signposted ones: go straight to the ticket office.
- **Fake guides and "guardians":** someone who introduces himself as an official guide, an Egyptology student or a site employee walks with you for a few minutes and asks for money. Licensed guides have the Ministry ID card and are booked through hotels or agencies with reviews.
- **Papyrus, perfumes, alabaster, rugs:** the driver or guide "just stops for a moment" at a shop where he gets a 30-50% commission. The papyrus sold is often banana leaf, the essences diluted perfumes. Make clear at the start of the day that you don't want to enter any shop.
- **Taxis:** in Cairo and Luxor taxis rarely use the meter. Uber and Careem eliminate the problem; otherwise agree on the price beforehand, in pounds and not in euros or dollars, for the whole group.
- **Baksheesh not owed:** whoever points the way, whoever "opens" a hall, whoever offers to take a photo. A gentle but firm "no, shukran" (no, thank you) closes almost every conversation.
- **Photos in tombs:** the guard offers to look the other way on the ban, then asks for a tip or threatens a fine.
- **Change and small bills:** 50-piastre notes or notes from another country given as change, and the classic "I have no change." Count your money on the spot and keep small bills.
- **Feluccas and horse carriages:** the price agreed "per person" becomes "per person per hour." Clear everything up beforehand.
- **Last-minute cruises and tours on the street:** offers that are too low, sold by touts at the port or in hotels, often hide old ships or excluded entrance fees.
- **Gifts:** bracelets, scarabs or scarves put in your hand "for free" should be returned immediately.
- **Actual safety:** northern Sinai is off limits, the border areas with Libya and Sudan are not recommended; Siwa and the Western Desert are visited with authorized agencies. Tourist areas are policed by the tourist police. Traffic is the biggest physical risk: no night driving outside the city, and seat belts in the back seats too.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Cairo
We landed in Cairo, bought the visa at the counter before the checks (needed if you don't have the e-visa) and took an Uber to **Giza**, where it's best to sleep for the first three days: you're a few minutes from the pyramids, the Grand Egyptian Museum and the road to Saqqara. In the evening there's the sound and light show in front of the Sphinx, which you can also see from many hotel terraces.

**In practice**
- Visa on arrival $30, cash or card
- Uber airport-Giza: 45-75 minutes
- SIM or eSIM in arrivals, with passport

### Day 2 — Giza and the Grand Egyptian Museum
The plateau opens at 7 and it's the only time of day without groups and without heat. With the internal **electric bus** we did the Great Pyramid (the inside is paid separately and is worth it for the corridor and the Grand Gallery, not for the chambers), the panoramic viewpoint of the nine pyramids and the **Sphinx** with the Valley Temple of Khafre.

We spent the afternoon at the **Grand Egyptian Museum**, the largest archaeological museum in the world dedicated to a single civilization. The **Tutankhamun** galleries display the entire tomb assemblage for the first time; the monumental staircase, with dozens of colossal statues, deserves to be taken slowly. You need at least four hours, and the time-slot ticket must be bought online before leaving.

**In practice**
- Giza 700 EGP, inside of Khufu about 1,500 EGP
- GEM 1,830 EGP (about $35), online booking
- No shade on the plateau: water, hat, sunscreen

### Day 3 — Saqqara, Dahshur and Memphis
We spent the day with a driver heading south. At **Saqqara** there's the **step pyramid of Djoser**, the first great stone building in history, and around it the mastabas of Old Kingdom officials, with hunting, fishing and market scenes carved into the limestone. The **Serapeum**, with the granite sarcophagi of the sacred bulls, is one of the most impressive visits in the Cairo area.

At **Dahshur**, almost deserted, there are the **Bent Pyramid** with its change of slope halfway up and the **Red Pyramid**, which you enter down a sixty-meter corridor. On the way back we stopped briefly at **Memphis**, the ancient capital, with the reclining colossus of Ramesses II.

**In practice**
- Driver for the day: roughly $30-50 per vehicle
- Saqqara 600 EGP, Dahshur 200 EGP, Memphis 200 EGP
- Lunch at one of the country restaurants on the road to Saqqara

### Day 4 — Islamic Cairo, Coptic Cairo and the Egyptian Museum
Here we change base: we sleep in **Zamalek** or downtown. In the morning we visited **Islamic Cairo**: the **Citadel** with the Muhammad Ali Mosque, the **Ibn Tulun Mosque**, the oldest intact one in the city, the **Sultan Hassan** complex and **al-Muizz** street as far as Khan el-Khalili. Those who prefer can go to **Coptic Cairo**, by metro to Mar Girgis.

In the afternoon we went to the **Egyptian Museum on Tahrir Square**, which even after Tutankhamun's move to the GEM remains full of masterpieces, in an early-20th-century display. In the evening we had tea at El Fishawy café, in the bazaar. Shoulders and knees covered all day, scarf for women in the mosques.

**In practice**
- Citadel 550 EGP, Egyptian Museum 550 EGP
- Mosques: shoes off at the entrance, tip the shoe keeper
- Tomorrow we leave at dawn: suitcase ready

### Day 5 — From Cairo to Marsa Matruh via Alexandria
We did the first leg toward Siwa by car with a driver. **Alexandria**, three hours from Cairo, deserves a half-day stop: the new **Bibliotheca Alexandrina**, the catacombs of **Kom el-Shoqafa** and the **Qaitbay** citadel on the site of the Lighthouse. Then the coastal road passes through **El Alamein**, with the war cemeteries and the battle museum, and we arrived in **Marsa Matruh** in the evening.

It's a long day, around eight hours of driving with stops. The direct night bus to Siwa would save a day, but it travels in the dark, which Viaggiare Sicuri advises against: better to break up the trip and drive only by day.

**In practice**
- Car with driver Cairo-Siwa and back: roughly $250-400 in total
- Bibliotheca Alexandrina about 200 EGP, Kom el-Shoqafa 200 EGP
- Italian war cemetery at El Alamein: free entry

### Day 6 — From Marsa Matruh to Siwa
Another four hours southwest, through a flat, empty desert, with a couple of military checkpoints where you hand over passports. We arrived in **Siwa** for lunch. In the afternoon we got our bearings by bicycle among the palm groves and at sunset we climbed the **fortress of Shali**, the old center built of kershef, a mix of mud and salt that the rains of 1926 partly melted.

In the evening you hand your passport to the operator for tomorrow's or the next day's desert permit.

**In practice**
- Bicycle rental: a few euros a day
- Shali fortress: cheap entry
- Cash for the whole stay, ATMs are few and sometimes empty

### Day 7 — Siwa: the Oracle, the Mountain of the Dead and Cleopatra's Spring
We spent the day in the oasis. In the morning we visited the **temple of the Oracle of Amun** at **Aghurmi**, where in 331 BC Alexander the Great was recognized as the son of the god; not far away, the remains of the temple of Umm Ubayda. Then **Gebel al-Mawta**, the Mountain of the Dead, with Ptolemaic and Roman tombs, some still painted.

In the afternoon we swam in **Cleopatra's Spring**, a pool of lukewarm water in the middle of the palm groves, and at sunset we went to the **salt lakes**, where you float as in the Dead Sea. Siwa is a conservative community: even at the springs you swim covered, in a T-shirt and shorts.

**In practice**
- Tuk-tuk or cart for the day: to be agreed, a few euros
- Siwa site entrance fees: cheap
- At the springs: swimsuit covered with a T-shirt, women all the more so

### Day 8 — The Great Sand Sea
The 4x4 excursion into the **Great Sand Sea** is the reason you come all this way. We left in the early afternoon with an authorized operator, crossed dunes tens of meters high, stopped at hot and cold springs in the middle of the sand, went **sandboarding** and watched the sunset from the crest of a dune. Those who want to sleep in a tent camp in the desert, with dinner and a fire.

**In practice**
- Half-day excursion: roughly $20-35 per person, permit included
- Night in the desert: roughly $50-90 per person with dinner and breakfast
- In winter nights get close to 0 °C (32 °F): heavy jacket

### Day 9 — Back to Cairo
It was a transfer day, eight to ten hours of road to Cairo, passing again through Marsa Matruh or along the more direct inland road, depending on the driver. You sleep near the airport or in Heliopolis, to be convenient for tomorrow morning's flight. It's best to leave early, to reach Cairo before dark.

**In practice**
- Car: 8-10 hours with stops
- Night bus: it exists (10-12 hours, a few tens of euros), but it's not recommended because it travels in the dark
- Hotel near the airport for tomorrow's flight

### Day 10 — Flight to Aswan and felucca at sunset
We took the morning flight to **Aswan**, an hour and twenty-five minutes. Anyone with an extra day, or wanting to save a hotel night, can take the **overnight train** from Giza on the evening of the return from Siwa, if you arrive in time, and wake up in Aswan. Aswan is the calmest city in Upper Egypt, with the Nile narrowing between granite islands and dunes. In the afternoon we visited the **Nubian Museum** and, around 4 pm, took a **felucca** around Elephantine Island and Kitchener's Island, with the botanical garden, until sunset over the tombs of the nobles.

**In practice**
- Cairo-Aswan flight: roughly €70-140
- Nubian Museum 400 EGP
- Felucca: roughly 300-600 EGP per hour per boat, to be agreed beforehand

### Day 11 — Abu Simbel at dawn
Wake-up at 3, minivan at 4 and three and a half hours of Nubian desert to **Abu Simbel**. The two temples, the great one of **Ramesses II** with its four colossal statues and the one of **Nefertari**, were cut into blocks and rebuilt 65 meters higher between 1964 and 1968, when the Aswan Dam created Lake Nasser. Arriving early means seeing them in the morning light and before the 9 o'clock groups.

We were back in Aswan by early afternoon. Those who don't want to do seven hours of road in one day take the 45-minute EgyptAir flight, or sleep in Abu Simbel and see the sound and light show.

**In practice**
- Shared minivan: roughly $20-35 per person; private $100-150 per vehicle
- Abu Simbel: 822 EGP (1,272 EGP on February 22 and October 22)
- Packed breakfast from the hotel: there's only one rest stop along the way

### Day 12 — Philae and boarding the Nile cruise
In the morning we visited the **temple of Philae**, dedicated to Isis, also moved to a higher island to save it from the water; you reach it by boat from the Shellal dock. On the way you see the **High Dam** and the **Unfinished Obelisk**, left in the quarry because it cracked during cutting.

In the early afternoon we boarded the cruise ship, moored along the Corniche. The first night the ship stays in Aswan; in the evening many packages include a visit to a **Nubian village** on the west bank.

**In practice**
- Philae 550 EGP, boat roughly 300-500 EGP per boat
- Aswan-Luxor 3-night cruise: roughly $400-1,000 per person depending on the ship
- Check what's included: entrance fees, guide, drinks, tips

### Day 13 — Cruise: Kom Ombo and Edfu
The ship set sail at dawn heading north. Mid-morning we docked at **Kom Ombo**, the only double temple in Egypt, dedicated to Sobek, the crocodile god, and to Horus; next to it is the small museum with crocodile mummies. In the afternoon we reached **Edfu**, the temple of Horus, the best preserved in the country: roof, pylon, hypostyle hall and sanctuary almost intact.

The ships all dock at the same time, and the temples fill up. Anyone who can get off first or visit Edfu the following morning finds far fewer people.

**In practice**
- Kom Ombo 450 EGP and Edfu 550 EGP, often included in the package
- At Edfu you reach the temple by horse carriage: price already set by the ship, tip separate
- On deck it's cool in the evening even in October

### Day 14 — Esna and arrival in Luxor: Karnak and Luxor Temple
The ship went through the **Esna lock**, where sometimes vendors throw tablecloths and galabeyas from their boats up to the deck. We arrived in **Luxor** at the end of the morning. We gave the afternoon to the east bank: the **Karnak** complex, with the 134-column hypostyle hall and the sacred lake, and at sunset **Luxor Temple**, linked to Karnak by the avenue of sphinxes reopened in 2021.

**In practice**
- Karnak 600 EGP, Luxor Temple 500 EGP
- Luxor Temple is open until evening and is more beautiful lit up
- Last night on board

### Day 15 — Luxor, west bank: Valley of the Kings, Hatshepsut and Nefertari
We disembarked and at dawn we were already on the west bank. The **Valley of the Kings** with the basic three-tomb ticket, plus **Tutankhamun** or **Seti I** for those who want to spend; the terraced **temple of Hatshepsut** beneath the mountain; and the **tomb of Nefertari** in the Valley of the Queens, the most beautiful in Egypt, with a limited visiting time and a very expensive ticket. We slept on the west bank, among the sugarcane fields.

**In practice**
- Valley of the Kings 750 EGP, Tutankhamun about 700, Seti I about 2,000
- Nefertari: separate ticket and short visit, but in 2026 the tomb is often reported closed; Valley of the Queens 220 EGP
- Car with driver for the west bank: $25-40

### Day 16 — Hot-air balloon, Medinet Habu and Deir el-Medina
At dawn we took the **hot-air balloon flight** over the west bank, about forty minutes above temples and villages. Then we saw the less crowded sites of the Theban necropolis: **Medinet Habu**, the temple of Ramesses III, with reliefs that still keep their reds and blues; **Deir el-Medina**, the village of the workers who dug the royal tombs, with their small painted tombs; the **Ramesseum** and the **tombs of the nobles**. Afternoon free by the pool.

**In practice**
- Hot-air balloon roughly $50-100 per person
- Medinet Habu 220 EGP, Deir el-Medina about 200-300 EGP
- With strong wind balloon flights are canceled: better to book it for the first available day

### Day 17 — Day trip to Dendera or Abydos
Last day in Luxor, with a trip north. **Dendera**, an hour and a half away, has the temple of Hathor with its cleaned and brilliantly colored astronomical ceiling and the decorated crypts. **Abydos**, three hours away, has the temple of **Seti I**, the most refined reliefs of the New Kingdom and the king list. Those with the whole day do both with a driver. In the evening we returned to Luxor.

**In practice**
- Dendera 300 EGP, Abydos 260 EGP
- Car with driver for both: roughly $80-120
- The road passes through checkpoints: bring your passport

### Day 18 — From Luxor to Marsa Alam
We transferred to the Red Sea: four and a half hours of road, first along the Nile as far as **Edfu**, then across the Eastern Desert. We arrived in **Marsa Alam** in the early afternoon and checked in at the dive center with certifications, logbook and insurance; then there's time for a trial dive or a first snorkel on the reef in front of the resort.

**In practice**
- Private car Luxor-Marsa Alam: roughly $100-150 per vehicle
- At the dive center: certification, logbook, diving insurance, medical certificate if required
- Packages of 6-10 dives are better value than single dives

### Day 19 — Diving: Abu Dabbab and the Marsa Alam reefs
Two or three dives on the coastal reefs. **Abu Dabbab** is a sandy bay with green turtles and, if you're lucky, the **dugong** grazing on the seagrass; it can also be done from shore and suits every level. The boat outings go to the reefs around **Marsa Alam** and **Port Ghalib**, with hard coral walls, Napoleon wrasse and moray eels. Those with only the Open Water stay within 18 meters (60 ft), and here they miss almost nothing.

**In practice**
- Single dive roughly $35-55, 10-dive package $300-400
- Full equipment rental: roughly $20-30 a day
- Nitrox: extra per dive

### Day 20 — Diving at Elphinstone or snorkeling at Dolphin House
For divers with an **Advanced** certification and experience, **Elphinstone**: a long, narrow reef in open sea, with currents, vertical walls and, from October to December, oceanic whitetip sharks. For those who don't dive, or who want to respect the time before the flight, **Dolphin House (Samadai)**, the horseshoe reef where you swim near spinner dolphins, in a protected area with limited access.

The last dives must be done by this morning: between the last dive and the flight you need at least 18-24 hours.

**In practice**
- Elphinstone: only with Advanced, often with a minimum of logged dives
- Dolphin House: roughly $40-60 with lunch
- Last afternoon without diving, on the reef with a mask

### Day 21 — Return
Transfer to **Marsa Alam** airport or Hurghada and flight to Italy. At security check coral, shells and sand are confiscated, and real antiquities as souvenirs are banned from export: the scarabs sold in the bazaars are reproductions, and that's fine.

**In practice**
- At the airport 3 hours early: checks at the entrance and at the gate
- Spend any leftover pounds before leaving

## Where to stay
- **Giza (nights 1-3):** budget: guesthouses with a terrace on the pyramids, €30-50 · mid-range: boutique hotels near the GEM, €70-120 · luxury: Marriott Mena House.
- **Cairo (night 4):** budget: Downtown hotels, €30-50 · mid-range: Zamalek, €70-120 · luxury: grand hotels on the Nile in Garden City.
- **Marsa Matruh (night 5):** seafront hotels, €40-80.
- **Siwa (nights 6-8):** budget: small hotels in the center, €20-40 · mid-range: Siwan-style eco-lodges, €60-120 · luxury: Adrère Amellal, with no electricity, by candlelight · alternatively one night in a tent camp in the desert.

- **Aswan (nights 10-11):** budget: Nubian guesthouses on Elephantine Island, €25-45 · mid-range: hotels on the Corniche, €60-110 · luxury: Old Cataract.
- **Cruise (nights 12-14):** 4 or 5 star ship, or dahabiya.
- **Luxor, west bank (nights 15-17):** budget: guesthouses among the fields, €25-40 · mid-range: small hotels with a pool, €50-90 · luxury: Al Moudira.
- **Marsa Alam (nights 18-20):** mid-range: resorts with a reef and in-house diving, €70-130 · for divers: eco-lodges like Wadi Lahami.

## Where to eat
- **Cairo:** koshari at Abou Tarek, ful and taameya for breakfast, **molokhia** and **fattah** in Egyptian restaurants like Zooba or Abou El Sid, mezze and fish in Zamalek.
- **Alexandria:** grilled fish chosen at the counter in the harbor restaurants, and the sweets of Délices, the historic pastry shop.
- **Siwa:** dates and olives from the oasis, **abu mardam** (meat cooked under the sand), bread baked in mud ovens, very sweet mint tea.
- **Aswan:** Nubian cooking in village homes, with bread, vegetables and meat tagine; cold karkadè.
- **Luxor:** stuffed pigeon, tagine and baladi bread in the west bank eateries; sugarcane juice.
- **Red Sea:** grilled fish of the day; in all-inclusive resorts, at least one dinner out.

## Variations by length
- **14 days, without Siwa:** Cairo (4 days), flight to Aswan, Abu Simbel, cruise to Luxor, Luxor (2 days), Marsa Alam (3 days). It's the classic circuit, easier to organize.
- **17 days, with Siwa but without the sea:** like the itinerary up to day 17, returning from Luxor via Cairo.
- **With the White Desert instead of Siwa:** from Cairo, two or three days in the Bahariya oasis and the **White Desert**, with a night in a tent among the chalk formations. Closer than Siwa (about 5 hours), still with an authorized agency.
- **With the dahabiya:** replace the classic cruise with 4-5 nights on a dahabiya Esna-Aswan or Aswan-Esna; one extra day needed.
- **For divers:** drop Dendera-Abydos and the resort sea days, and end with a week-long dive cruise from Port Ghalib on the Brothers-Daedalus-Elphinstone route. The trip becomes 24-25 days.
- **Lake Nasser:** for an Egypt that's even more remote, take the 3-4 night cruise on Lake Nasser between Aswan and Abu Simbel, with the Nubian temples moved along the shores.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book well in advance:** GEM tickets, cruise, overnight train, liveaboard and domestic flights in peak months.
- **Do the sites at opening**, always: in Luxor and Abu Simbel it changes everything, for heat and crowds.
- **Say no to shops right away** when booking a driver or guide, and repeat it at the start of the day.
- **Agree on every price beforehand**, clarifying currency, duration, per person or per vehicle.
- **Use Uber or Careem** in Cairo and Luxor instead of taxis.
- **Bring cash for Siwa**, where cards are of little use.
- **Always have your passport with you** on desert routes: there are many checkpoints.
- **Dress with shoulders and knees covered** outside the resorts, and in Siwa at the springs too.
- **Don't skip the break between diving and flying:** 18-24 hours, the itinerary allows for it.
- **Check the certification and number of dives** required for Elphinstone and the southern cruises before booking.
- **No drones:** they're confiscated on arrival.
- **Don't buy "real" antiquities or coral:** it's a crime, and they check at the airport.

## Chiusura personale
Three weeks in Egypt leave you above all with the sense of contrast between one place and the next: Cairo with its twenty million people and Siwa with its mud and salt houses, the green, narrow Nile and the desert right beyond the palms, the crowded temples of Karnak and the silence of Dendera, the colors of the tombs and those of the reef. It's a tiring trip to organize and to do, but a consistent one: every stop adds a piece the others don't give.
