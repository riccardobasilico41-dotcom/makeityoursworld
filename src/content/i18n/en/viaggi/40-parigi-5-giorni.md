# Paris in 5 Days (with a 3-Day Version)

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 5 days, 4 nights — with a 3-day version
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, by metro and on foot
**Categoria:** Europa · Francia · Parigi · arte · architettura

---

## Apertura personale
Paris is the city where the plan goes wrong most often, for a specific reason: the metro compresses distances in a deceptive way, and putting three big museums in one day seems reasonable until you try it. Five days are enough to do the big things without rushing (one big museum a day, never two) and leave the third real day for the neighborhoods that aren't on the postcards.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** April-June and September-October. In August many restaurants and neighborhood shops close for weeks.
- **Museums to book:** **Louvre** (time slot effectively mandatory, closed on Tuesday), **Orsay** (closed on Monday), **Versailles** (time slot mandatory, closed on Monday).
- **Transport:** metro with a simplified single fare since 2025 (**amounts change: check them before leaving**); from Orly, metro line 14 is now the easiest way to get to the center.
- **Where to stay:** Marais, Latin Quarter, Canal Saint-Martin/République for the best value for location.
- **Food:** the midday menu is always better value than the evening one; coffee at the counter costs half as much as at a table.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Île de la Cité and the old town
In the morning we went to the **Sainte-Chapelle** (only if it's sunny: otherwise it's better to move it), then we saw Notre-Dame from the outside; if it's open, you can also do the interior visit. We had lunch in the Latin Quarter.

We spent the afternoon in the **Marais**: Place des Vosges, the Musée Picasso in the Hôtel Salé and the narrow streets of the 3rd and 4th arrondissements.

For the evening, Saint-Germain or the Marais again.

### Day 2 — The Louvre
We devoted the day to the Louvre, with a strategy decided the evening before: two or three wings and ten works, no more. The Winged Victory of Samothrace on the Daru staircase, the Egyptian and Mesopotamian antiquities, the basement with the foundations of the medieval castle.

The afternoon stays free, in the Tuileries gardens or at the Jeu de Paume.

### Day 3 — Orsay, Orangerie and the Eiffel Tower
In the morning we went to the **Musée d'Orsay**, with the Impressionist collection on the top floor. We had lunch nearby, then moved on to the **Orangerie**, with the two oval rooms of the Water Lilies.

In the afternoon we headed toward the **Eiffel Tower**. You look at it from Trocadéro or the Champ de Mars, and going up is not essential: from the top, the panorama is missing the tower itself.

### Day 4 — Montmartre at dawn and Versailles
We saw dawn at **Montmartre**: the Sacré-Cœur at seven in the morning, then the side streets away from Place du Tertre, such as Rue de l'Abreuvoir and the Clos Montmartre.

In the early afternoon we took the train to **Versailles**: royal apartments and Hall of Mirrors, then the Domaine de Marie-Antoinette with the Hameau de la Reine, which is the least visited part and the most surprising.

### Day 5 — Neighborhoods and a closing note
In the morning we went to **Père-Lachaise** with a map in hand: the famous tombs and above all the park, with the Mur des Fédérés.

For the afternoon you choose between **Canal Saint-Martin**, **Belleville** or the **Catacombs** (with a timed reservation). We spent the last evening in a neighborhood bistro far from the monuments.

## The three-day version
- **Day 1:** Sainte-Chapelle, Marais, Louvre in the afternoon (strategy reduced to one wing).
- **Day 2:** Orsay in the morning, Orangerie, Montmartre at dawn the next day or at sunset.
- **Day 3:** Versailles for the whole day.
- **What you give up:** Père-Lachaise, the "off-the-postcard" neighborhoods, the Catacombs. It's the right sacrifice: over three days, the big museums and Versailles use up all the useful time.

## What to see / what to do
- **The Sainte-Chapelle on a sunny day.**
- **The Louvre with a strategy**, not a complete route.
- **Montmartre at seven in the morning.**
- **The Hameau de la Reine at Versailles**, which almost everyone skips.
- **Père-Lachaise**, not only for the famous tombs.

## Possible extensions
- **Giverny (+1 day):** Monet's garden, an hour by train.
- **Chantilly or Fontainebleau (+1 day):** châteaux less crowded than Versailles.
- **Normandy (+3-5 days):** direct train toward Bayeux and the D-Day beaches.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
Marais or Canal Saint-Martin/République for the best balance between price, neighborhood life and connections.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
The midday menu in neighborhood bistros, the bouillons for classic cuisine at low prices, steak-frites, confit de canard, a freshly baked baguette de tradition.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Without booking the Louvre and Versailles you can't get in** in peak periods.
- **Check the closing days** before building the schedule.
- **Don't go up the Eiffel Tower** expecting the best panorama: the tower itself is missing.
- **One big museum a day**, never two.

## Chiusura personale
What stays with you from Paris isn't the first two days in places you already knew before leaving: it's the third, when you come out of a museum tired and take a random direction instead of heading to the next monument, and end up in a neighborhood with nothing on your list to see, where people sit on the edges of the canal and nobody photographs anything.
