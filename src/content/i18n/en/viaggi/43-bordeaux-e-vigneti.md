# Bordeaux and Its Vineyards in 7 Days

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:** **September-October (harvest) or May-June**
**Durata:** 7 days, 6 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, with a car and a designated driver for the tastings
**Categoria:** Europa · Francia · Bordeaux · vino · costa atlantica

---

## Apertura personale
Bordeaux combines a city you can cover on foot with a territory you can't see without a car. The itinerary covers both banks of the vineyard (Médoc and Saint-Émilion) plus Sauternes, and ends with the ocean at the Arcachon Basin.

## Scheda pratica
- **Base:** Bordeaux city.
- **Driving and wine don't go together:** a designated driver who doesn't taste, guided excursions, or spitting at tastings (it's the norm, not an insult).
- **Appointment always mandatory** for the big châteaux.
- **Crit'Air sticker** to enter the city.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 — Bordeaux city
We did the first day on foot, because that's how you cover Bordeaux: Place de la Bourse with the Miroir d'eau, the eighteenth-century center, the Chartrons district. In the evening, an entrecôte à la bordelaise. To enter the city by car you need the Crit'Air sticker.

### Day 2 — The Cité du Vin and the Chartrons
In the morning we were at the **Cité du Vin**, the most orderly way to understand the vineyard before going there. In the afternoon we went back to the Chartrons, among the former wine merchants' warehouses now converted, with a canelé for a snack.

### Day 3 — Saint-Émilion
A whole day on the right bank. At **Saint-Émilion** the monolithic church carved into the rock can only be visited with a guide; then the medieval village and a tasting by appointment. From here on the rule of the vineyard days applies: a driver who doesn't taste, a guided excursion, or spitting at tastings, which is the norm and not an insult.

### Day 4 — Pomerol and the Graves
We crossed **Pomerol**, tiny, with the most expensive wines in the world, and then went down into the **Graves**, south of the city.

### Day 5 — The Médoc
It's the day of the road of châteaux lined up on the left bank, with two visits at classified growths. You don't show up at the big châteaux without an appointment: you don't get in.

### Day 6 — Sauternes
An estate in **Sauternes**, and the thing that stays with you most from the trip: clusters with noble rot that look like they should be thrown away, hand-picked five or six times in the same row (in season you can see the harvest in successive passes), for a sweet wine born from what to a normal farmer would look like a disease.

### Day 7 — Arcachon Basin
We spent the last day on the ocean. The train takes you to Arcachon, the **Dune du Pilat** is climbed at sunset (in summer the sand burns) and dinner was freshly opened oysters in a cabane on the pier.

## What to see / what to do
- The monolithic church of Saint-Émilion, with a guide.
- The botrytized clusters of Sauternes.
- The Dune du Pilat at sunset.
- Freshly opened oysters on a pier in Arcachon.

## Possible extensions
- **Loire (+4-6 days):** continuing toward the northeast.
- **Occitania and the Pyrenees (+4-5 days):** toward the south.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
In Bordeaux city, to explore it on foot and use it as a base for all the trips.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
Entrecôte à la bordelaise, Arcachon oysters, canelé and the midday menu, always good value.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't show up without an appointment** at the big châteaux.
- **Don't drive after tastings.**
- **Don't underestimate the Dune du Pilat**: in summer the sand burns.
