# The Tour du Mont Blanc in 9 Days

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 9 days, 8 nights in stages
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independent, overnight in a hut every night
**Categoria:** Europa · Francia · montagna · trekking

---

## Apertura personale
The Tour du Mont Blanc circles the highest massif in the Alps in full, about a hundred and seventy kilometers, crossing France, Italy and Switzerland. It isn't technical, but the stages are long: the constraint that structures everything is booking the huts, to be done in January-February for the summer.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **mid-June to mid-September, with September the best month**
- **Huts booked months in advance**, otherwise the itinerary falls apart.
- **Mountain rescue is free** from the French public services, but insurance still makes sense.
- **Sleeping bag liner required** in all the huts.
- **Cash** for the huts with no card line.
- **Regular afternoon thunderstorms**: start early to reach the hut before early afternoon.
- **From the airport to the centre:** you fly into **Geneva (GVA)**, 1h15-1h40 from Chamonix: direct buses (FlixBus, AlpyBus, Swiss Tours and others) about €15-30 per person, to book online, with few evening runs · shared door-to-door shuttles about €40-50 per person, private transfers over €200 per vehicle · from Chamonix to Les Houches local bus or the Mont-Blanc Express train in 10-15 minutes, free with the guest card given by hotels and campsites · alternatively **Turin (TRN)** or **Milan Malpensa (MXP)** for those starting from Courmayeur, 2-3 hours away · the typical mistake is booking the last bus of the evening: with a delayed flight you are stranded.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 — Les Houches, start
We set out from **Les Houches**, near Chamonix, toward Les Contamines-Montjoie. The loop is about a hundred and seventy kilometers long and not technical, but the stages are long and the pace is set by two things: the huts booked in January-February and the afternoon thunderstorms. Every day we started early, to be at the hut before early afternoon.

The first stage climbs to the **Col de Voza**, at about 1,650 meters, then drops toward Bionnassay and the valley floor of **Les Contamines**. Anyone who wants to skip the first climb can take the Bellevue cable car from Les Houches; those with the legs and the time can choose the **Col de Tricot** variant, harder, which passes below the Bionnassay glacier and over the suspension bridge. In all it is sixteen to twenty kilometers depending on the variant, with a lot of downhill: the first day is also when you find out whether your pack is too heavy. *Better to find out here, where there is still a village with shops, than on day four.*

**In practice:** Les Houches is a few minutes from Chamonix by train or bus · six to seven hours of walking, longer with the Tricot variant · Les Contamines has shops and ATMs: stock up on cash before the more isolated huts.

### Day 2 — Toward the Col du Bonhomme
With the climb to the **Col du Bonhomme** came the first big views of the massif. Here stages with 800-900 meters of elevation gain are the norm. From Les Contamines you leave early along the old Roman road that climbs past the church of **Notre-Dame de la Gorge**, then through pastures and small tarns up to the pass, at about 2,330 meters. Just above is the **Col de la Croix du Bonhomme** with its hut, the high point of the day; from there you descend to the village of **Les Chapieux**, or stay high and cross the more exposed Col des Fours.

It is a long stage, around eighteen to twenty-one kilometers, and the high section is where the weather changes fastest. Aim to reach the Col de la Croix du Bonhomme by late morning, take your break at the hut and drop toward Les Chapieux before the afternoon clouds build. *In early summer steep snowfields often linger below the passes: with hard snow in the morning, go carefully, and use poles if you have them.*

**In practice:** over a thousand meters of ascent, more than the average for the loop, and seven to eight hours of walking · avoid the Col des Fours in fog or snow · Les Chapieux is a tiny village with few beds, which need booking well ahead, like the huts.

### Day 3 — Entering Italy, Val Veny
We crossed into Italy, and the slope opened onto huge meadows, with the wall of Mont Blanc seen in full from afar. From Les Chapieux you head up the valley past Ville des Glaciers and the Refuge des Mottets, then a long series of switchbacks leads to the **Col de la Seigne**, at about 2,520 meters, the border with Italy.

It is the most anticipated moment of the first half of the loop: from the pass you see the whole south side of the massif. You descend toward **Rifugio Elisabetta** and the plain of **Lago Combal**, in **Val Veny**, where the landscape becomes wide and green. Do the climb to the pass in the early hours, while the light is still low and the clouds have not covered the peaks.

**In practice:** six to eight hours depending on where you sleep in Val Veny · Rifugio Elisabetta is one of the most sought-after huts on the Italian side, so book it first · in summer a bus links the bottom of Val Veny with Courmayeur, useful if the weather turns.

### Day 4 — Courmayeur
In **Courmayeur** you can stay longer or take a rest day. From Val Veny the trail climbs toward Col Checrouit and Maison Vieille, with views of the Miage glacier, then drops steeply into town. Anyone wanting to spare their knees takes the valley bus.

The afternoon in Courmayeur is for practical things: washing clothes, restocking food and cash, changing socks. With energy left and a clear day, take the **Skyway Monte Bianco** up to Punta Helbronner, above 3,400 meters, to look down on the massif after walking around it. *With low cloud it is not worth the ticket: keep it in reserve for a sunny day.*

**In practice:** Courmayeur has pharmacies, mountain shops, supermarkets and ATMs, the last easy chance before Switzerland · the Skyway costs about €59-63 return to Punta Helbronner, so check the summit weather before going up · it has the widest choice of beds on the whole loop, but book for August.

### Day 5 — Rifugio Bonatti
We climbed to the **Rifugio Bonatti**, one of the best-known viewpoints on the loop. From Courmayeur a steep climb through the woods starts straight away and leads to **Rifugio Bertone**, at about 2,000 meters, best done in the cool of the morning. From there begins the finest stretch of the Italian side: a balcony path along the slopes of Mont de la Saxe, almost level, with the **Grandes Jorasses** and the Dent du Géant in front of you all morning.

Those who want more climb to the Tête de la Tronche, a higher and harder variant. The **Bonatti**, at about 2,025 meters, looks straight down Val Ferret: the sunset from the terrace is why it is so sought after. The stage is short, so enjoy it: you arrive in the early afternoon and stay outside watching the light change on the Jorasses.

**In practice:** about five to six hours from Courmayeur, with most of the climbing in the first two · the Bonatti fills up months in advance: if it is full, sleep in Val Ferret and walk up for the day · a summer bus runs along Val Ferret and can shorten the stage if needed.

### Day 6 — Crossing into Switzerland
The **Grand Col Ferret** took us onto the Swiss side, orderly and green. From the Bonatti you descend to the floor of Val Ferret near Arnouvaz, and from there the climb begins, passing close to Rifugio Elena and reaching the pass, at about 2,540 meters, on the border between Italy and Switzerland.

The slope is exposed and without shelter: with a storm coming, this is not a place to be. On the other side the descent is long and gentle, through the pastures of La Peule and the village of Ferret, down to **La Fouly**, with its wooden houses and the face of Mont Dolent above the village. *Cross the pass by late morning: afternoon storms here come often and fast.*

**In practice:** six to seven hours, about 800 meters of ascent · in Switzerland you pay in francs; euros are often accepted, but change comes in francs · Switzerland is outside EU roaming: check your phone plan before crossing the pass.

### Day 7 — La Fouly, Champex-Lac
We came down toward **Champex-Lac** and stopped at the alpine lake. It is the quietest stage of the loop and a chance to recover. From La Fouly the trail follows the stream down the valley, through woods and small villages such as Praz-de-Fort and Issert, with wooden houses and flowers at the windows.

The last hour is a climb through the woods that comes out at **Champex**, a resort village on the shore of a lake at about 1,470 meters. You arrive early and spend the afternoon by the water: a walk around the shore, a rowing boat, a swim for those who can take cold water. *Make the most of it and rest: tomorrow's stage is the longest.*

**In practice:** four to five hours, with little climbing compared to the other stages · if anything goes wrong you can do the stretch by bus via Orsières on the Swiss lines · Champex has shops, restaurants and hotels as well as gîtes, but in high season it fills up too.

### Day 8 — Back toward France
The last big stage brought us back to the French side, and the massif came to dominate the view again. To keep this day from turning into an eight-to-ten-hour marathon, the Champex to Trient section is skipped by public transport: bus to Orsières, train to Martigny, then the summer bus up to the **Col de la Forclaz** and **Trient**. What's left on foot is the best part, the one that takes you back into France.

From **Trient** you climb through woods and pastures to the **Col de Balme**, at about 2,190 metres, the border with France, where Mont Blanc and the Chamonix valley suddenly reappear. The descent to **Le Tour** or Tré-le-Champ is easy but long: better not to rush. *With an extra day, walk the Champex section too, via the Bovine pastures or, only in settled weather, over the Fenêtre d'Arpette, the hardest variant of the tour.*

**In practice:** about five to six hours on foot from Trient to Le Tour · bus and train times on sbb.ch, check them the evening before: the bus to the Col de la Forclaz only runs in summer · to shorten the descent you can use the Le Tour lifts, when they're open.

### Day 9 — Chamonix
The last stage closed the loop in **Chamonix**. In the end this is what stays with you: the same massif changes face on every side, vertical and icy in France, open and wide in Italy, orderly in Switzerland. The last day climbs onto the side of the **Aiguilles Rouges**, facing Mont Blanc. From Tré-le-Champ the classic trail goes up a series of metal ladders and walkways on the rock to **Lac Blanc**, at about 2,350 meters, where the water reflects the whole range.

From there you descend to La Flégère and reach Chamonix on foot or by cable car. If you still have half a day, keep it for the **Aiguille du Midi**, at 3,842 meters, the most spectacular ride in the valley. If the sky is clear, go up early, before clouds form on the peaks. *The ladders are not difficult, but anyone afraid of heights can avoid them on the trail via La Flégère.*

**In practice:** six to seven hours if you walk all the way down to Chamonix · Lac Blanc is crowded in the middle of the day: get there early · Aiguille du Midi about 83 euros return in the summer high season, best booked online.

## What to see / what to do
- The three sides of the massif, each with a different character.
- The Rifugio Bonatti for the view.
- The Aiguille du Midi from Chamonix, to close the trip.

## Possible extensions
- **Vallée Blanche (+1 day):** from Chamonix, with a guide, for skiers (in a separate winter season).
- **Écrins or Vanoise (+4-7 days):** another French alpine park, less crowded.
- **GR20 in Corsica:** for those looking for a harder, more technical trek, as a trip of its own.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
Every night in a hut, booked from January-February for the summer.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
Soups and polenta in the huts; in the valley raclette, tartiflette and the Savoyard cheeses.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book the huts in January-February**, not in May.
- **Start early every day** because of the afternoon thunderstorms.
- **Don't underestimate the elevation gain**: stages of 800-900 meters are the norm.

## Chiusura personale
We did the loop starting from Les Houches, then into Italy over the Col de la Seigne, up to the Rifugio Bonatti and down into Switzerland as far as the lake at Champex. Every day an early start, to be at the hut before early afternoon.
