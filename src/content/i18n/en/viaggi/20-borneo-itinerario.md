# Borneo in Two Weeks: Kinabalu, Orangutans, the River and Sipadan

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (itinerary and practical information complete and verified; the first-person passages are written as a draft to be reread and personalized)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 12 days, 11 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, with lodges and permits booked months in advance
**Categoria:** Asia · Borneo · Malesia · fauna selvatica · trekking · diving

---

## Apertura personale
Borneo isn't an appendix of Malaysia: it's a trip of its own, with timing, costs and logic completely different from the peninsula, which is why it gets its own itinerary here. In twelve days you climb the highest mountain in Southeast Asia between the Himalayas and New Guinea, you see orangutans in two opposite ways, you go up a river where wildlife is concentrated like nowhere else in the region, and you dive down a coral wall many consider the best on the planet. It's also the trip where you understand what palm oil has done to this island: you land over a neat green carpet that looks like forest, and isn't. You see it better from here than in any documentary.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** from **March to October**, with the best months between April and September. It holds true for everything at the same time, which is rare: calm sea and high visibility at Sipadan, lower chance of rain at the top of Kinabalu and a low river on the Kinabatangan, which is the condition for the animals to stay concentrated on the banks instead of scattering into the flooded forest.
- **Documents:** passport with 6 months of validity and the mandatory **MDAC**, as for all of Malaysia. But there's one thing that throws almost everyone: **Sabah and Sarawak have their own immigration**, a legacy of the terms on which they joined the Federation in 1963. Arriving from the peninsula you go through a passport check and get a new stamp, with a separate stay permit. The same happens going from Sabah to Sarawak. Nothing extra is needed, but it's good to know.
- **Bookings, the part that decides the trip:** nothing here can be improvised.
  - **Mount Kinabalu:** just over **a hundred and sixty permits a day**, tied to the beds at the Laban Rata hut, the only place to stay at altitude. Book **months** ahead, not weeks. A guide is mandatory by law.
  - **Sipadan:** just over **two hundred and fifty permits a day**, assigned by Sabah Parks to authorized operators (**not to individuals**) and distributed among their guests on a rotation, rewarding longer stays. Hence the rule: you book a 4-5 night package with a resort on Mabul, six to twelve months ahead in peak months. Today each permit allows three dives at the island on the same day.
  - **Kinabatangan:** lodges are booked as a package, with meals, outings and transfers included. In high season, a few months ahead.
- **Cash:** cards work in the cities; **elsewhere they don't**. Lodges on the river, villages and many Semporna operators accept only ringgit, and ATMs become rare and unreliable. Stock up in Sandakan or Kota Kinabalu before leaving: forgetting this is one of the most common mistakes of people arriving from the peninsula.
- **Health, where the story changes:** Borneo is the only part of Malaysia where **malaria** needs to be taken seriously with a doctor before leaving, including the one from *Plasmodium knowlesi* transmitted by macaques, present right here. Dengue is widespread all year. You need high-concentration repellent, long sleeves in the evening and **leech socks** for forest treks. The good hospitals are in Kota Kinabalu: from a lodge on the river, evacuation takes hours, and insurance should be chosen accordingly.
- **Safety:** Sabah and Sarawak are calm. The only area with a different history is the **east coast around Semporna**, where in the past there were kidnappings by armed groups from the southern Philippines. Today there's a patrolled security zone, with a nighttime maritime curfew, and tourism runs normally, but it's wise to check updated travel advisories before booking.
- **Transport:** you fly. Kota Kinabalu is 2h30 from Kuala Lumpur, and on the ground distances are long and roads slow: from Kota Kinabalu to Sandakan it takes six hours, from Sandakan to the Kinabatangan another two. Almost everything is organized as a package with transfers included, and it's the sensible choice.
- **Budget:** this is the expensive part of any trip to Malaysia. Between package lodges, capped permits and domestic flights, you work out the bill beforehand, not along the way.
- **What to bring:** binoculars (on the Kinabatangan they change the day more than any lens), real layers for the summit of Kinabalu, leech socks, headlamp, a light rain shell and cash.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Kota Kinabalu
We flew in from Kuala Lumpur, about 2h30, and on arrival there was the **Sabah passport check**, even though we came from the peninsula.

The first day was for settling in: the waterfront, the fish market and the sunset, which in Kota Kinabalu is a collective ritual and one of the best in Southeast Asia. We had dinner at the **Filipino Market**, picking fish at the stall and having it grilled on the spot.

*It's also the day to stock up on cash: from here on, ATMs thin out.*

### Day 2 — On to Kinabalu Park
You move to **Kundasang** or into the park, about two hours by road. You sleep at altitude to be at the Timpohon Gate at dawn: starting from Kota Kinabalu would mean leaving at three in the morning.

The afternoon was easy, in **Kinabalu Park**, a UNESCO site, along the low trails and the botanical garden. It serves, if nothing else, to start getting used to the altitude.

### Day 3 — Kinabalu, first day
We set off from the **Timpohon Gate**, at 1,866 meters, with the mandatory guide: six kilometers almost all of uneven steps, inside the rainforest, with the humidity on your skin and the feeling they'll never end.

In the early afternoon we reached **Laban Rata**, 3,270 meters. You have an early dinner and try to sleep at eight in the evening, which few manage at that altitude.

### Day 4 — Summit at dawn and descent
Wake-up at one thirty. You head out in the dark, the forest is over, and you walk on the **bare granite plateau** holding a fixed rope, with a line of headlamps above and below, along the mountainside.

You feel the altitude: in a little over twenty-four hours you go from under two thousand to over four thousand meters with no acclimatization, and a headache is the norm more than the exception. Then you reach **4,095 meters** and the sky goes from black to orange, with the clouds beneath your feet.

The descent is all done in one day, and it's harder on the knees than the climb. Then we transferred toward **Sandakan**.

### Day 5 — Sepilok
In the morning we went to the **Sepilok Orangutan Rehabilitation Centre**, for the first feeding. These are animals orphaned or confiscated from the illegal trade, in rehabilitation, and the center doesn't pretend it's nature: in this part of the world that's not a given.

In the afternoon there's the **second feeding**, and it's better to try twice instead of once: in certain seasons the forest offers enough fruit and almost nobody shows up.

In between are the two stops almost everyone skips: the **sun bear center**, two hundred meters away, with the world's smallest bears rescued from the pet trade, and the **Rainforest Discovery Centre**, with an excellent canopy walkway for birdwatching.

*Keep backpacks closed: the macaques around the walkways snatch bottles and bags without hesitation.*

### Day 6 — Sandakan and remembrance
We spent the morning at the **Sandakan Memorial Park**, on the site of the Japanese prison camp from which the death marches to Ranau set out in 1945: of about two thousand four hundred Allied prisoners, six survived. It's a quiet, little-visited place, and it tells one of the hardest pages of the war in the Pacific.

In the afternoon we moved toward the **Kinabatangan River**: about two hours by road to Sukau or Bilit, then the boat to the lodge. The first **sunset outing** was already the evening we arrived.

### Day 7 — Kinabatangan
It's the full day on the river: **dawn outing**, with mist still on the water and the engine idling, **sunset outing** and a **night outing** with torches for crocodiles and owls.

In two hours of cruising you pass proboscis monkeys leaping between branches, hornbills in pairs, macaques, crocodiles motionless on the banks and, with luck, a wild orangutan. Then the boat turns into a tributary, the strip of green thins out and behind it the **neat rows of oil palms** appear, to the horizon. That's where you understand why the animals are all concentrated on a strip a few hundred meters wide.

*Here binoculars are worth more than any lens: you're almost always looking at twenty or thirty meters.*

### Day 8 — Kinabatangan and on to Semporna
Last **dawn outing** (every outing is a lottery, and the more tickets you have, the better). Alternatively there's a guided forest trek behind the lodge or the **Gomantong Caves**, with millions of bats and the swiftlet nests harvested for soup.

In the afternoon we took the long transfer toward **Semporna** and the boat to **Mabul** (about 45 minutes).

### Day 9 — Mabul: muck diving
The first diving day was around **Mabul and Kapalai**. Almost everyone treats them as just a base for Sipadan, but they're actually among the best places in the world for **muck diving**: shallow sandy bottoms where you look for pygmy seahorses the size of a fingernail, mimic octopuses, nudibranchs and frogfish.

It's also the day you see a contrast worth stopping to think about: around the stilt houses, fifty meters from the resorts, live the **Bajau Laut**, the "sea nomads", often without citizenship of any state.

### Day 10 — Sipadan
It's the day the whole trip was built for, **if the permit has come through**. The operator's rotation rewards long stays, which is why the itinerary has several nights at Mabul instead of two.

You descend along a wall that continues into the blue with no visible bottom. In a few minutes a school of **barracuda** passes, twisting into a spiral as wide as a house; the turtles are so many that after the fifth you stop photographing them. The permit allows three dives at the island on the same day.

*You can't sleep on the island: the facilities were removed in 2004 precisely to protect it.*

### Day 11 — Mabul or Bohey Dulang
Second diving day. Those who don't dive can climb **Bohey Dulang**, a volcanic crater with a steep trail above the lagoon and one of the best views in Sabah.

In the afternoon we returned to Semporna for a last seafood dinner on the waterfront, excellent and cheap.

### Day 12 — Tawau and return
We transferred to **Tawau** (about an hour and a half by road). From there the return flight goes to Kota Kinabalu or straight to Kuala Lumpur.

## What to see / what to do
- **Book Kinabalu and Sipadan months ahead.** It's the one thing that truly decides this trip.
- **Dawn from the granite plateau** at 4,095 meters.
- **Both feedings at Sepilok**, not one.
- **The sun bear center**, which almost everyone skips.
- **The Sandakan Memorial Park**, which almost nobody includes.
- **At least two nights on the Kinabatangan**, to get four outings instead of two.
- **Binoculars**, on the river.
- **Muck diving at Mabul**, which is worth the trip even without Sipadan.
- **Looking past the strip of forest on the river**, to understand where the rest has gone.

## Possible extensions
- **Danum Valley (+3 nights):** primary forest never logged, trees over eighty meters, a canopy walkway at twenty-six meters. It's the most expensive stop in Borneo and orangutan sightings remain unpredictable: you come for the forest, not for one species. Fewer than three nights makes no sense, because you'd spend almost all of it in transfers.
- **Sarawak: Kuching and Bako (+3-4 days):** another state, with another immigration and another history: for a century it was the private kingdom of the white rajahs. Kuching is the most pleasant city in Malaysia, Bako park is the most accessible place in the world to see proboscis monkeys, and Semenggoh has free-ranging orangutans that between November and March may not show up at all.
- **Gunung Mulu (+3 days):** a UNESCO site, reachable **only by air**, with the largest known underground chamber in the world and the evening exit of millions of bats from Deer Cave. Beds inside the park are few and limit visitors, so you book the opposite of usual: bed first, then the flight.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
- **Kota Kinabalu:** on the waterfront, handy for the airport and transfers.
- **Kinabalu:** Kundasang or inside the park the evening before; **Laban Rata**, at 3,270 meters, the night at altitude, in a dorm, where sleeping little is the norm.
- **Sepilok:** in the lodges in the middle of the forest, a few minutes from the centers. That's what lets you be at the first feeding and on the canopy walkway at dawn.
- **Kinabatangan:** lodges along the river around Sukau and Bilit, packaged with meals and outings. The price difference lies more in the room than in the guides.
- **Semporna:** on the island of **Mabul**, to already be at sea. Semporna town costs less, but takes away that advantage.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
- **Kota Kinabalu:** fish picked at the stall in the Filipino Market and grilled on the spot; hinava, the raw fish marinated in lime of the Kadazan-Dusun; tuaran mee, the hand-made noodles of the area.
- **Sandakan:** Chinese food and seafood restaurants on the waterfront, both of good quality, because the city has a historic Chinese community.
- **Kinabatangan and Mabul:** full board in the lodges and resorts, with no alternatives. Simple Malay food, good and repetitive. There are no shops: take water, snacks and cash beforehand.
- **Semporna:** fish on the waterfront, the catch of the day picked at the stall, at low prices.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book months ahead, not weeks.** A hundred and sixty permits a day for Kinabalu, two hundred and fifty for Sipadan: whoever tries on the spot doesn't climb and doesn't dive.
- **Don't book two nights at Mabul hoping for Sipadan.** The rotation rewards long stays, which is why the itinerary has three or four.
- **Stock up on cash** in Kota Kinabalu and Sandakan. Along the river and on the islands there are no ATMs, or they don't work.
- **Talk to a doctor about malaria before leaving.** Here, unlike the peninsula, it's not a theoretical precaution.
- **Leech socks** for every forest trek.
- **Don't sleep in Kota Kinabalu the night before the climb** of Kinabalu.
- **At least two nights on the Kinabatangan**, never one.
- **Bring binoculars**, the most useful gear of the whole trip.
- **Check travel advisories** for Sabah's east coast before booking Semporna.

## Chiusura personale
Borneo leaves two images that sit side by side and don't reconcile. The first is dawn on the granite plateau of Kinabalu, with the clouds beneath your feet and the whole island revealing itself as the sky changes color. The second is the boat turning into a tributary of the Kinabatangan: ahead there are proboscis monkeys, hornbills and an orangutan on a branch, and behind that strip of green, three hundred meters away, the neat rows of oil palms begin and run on to the horizon. You see the animals so well precisely because they have nowhere else to go. It's the reason this trip is worth doing now, and also the reason why, coming back, something stays with you that isn't just enthusiasm.
