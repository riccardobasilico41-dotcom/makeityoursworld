# The Amalfi Coast and the Islands of the Bay in 9 Days

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 9 days, 8 nights — can be split into two blocks of 4-5
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independent, **no car**, everything by ferry, bus and on foot
**Categoria:** Europa · Italia · Campania · mare · trekking · isole

---

## Apertura personale
This is the most photographed stretch of coast in Italy and, statistically, the one where the most people spend their vacation stuck in traffic. The two things are connected: the Amalfi state road is a narrow two-lane road cut into the rock, and in summer it handles a fraction of the traffic that pours onto it. Anyone arriving by car in August sees the Coast from the window, standing still.

The choice that changes everything is simple and almost nobody makes it: **no car, everything by ferry**. It's cheap, there's no traffic, and you see the towns from the sea, which is the viewpoint they were built for. The second choice that changes everything is **going up**: above the road there's a network of trails and mule tracks that linked the towns before the state road existed, and up there there's nobody.

The islands are the second half, and they need a rule: **they're not interchangeable.** Capri is vertical and spectacular, Ischia is big, green and thermal, Procida is small and real. Doing two well is better than doing three in a rush, and if you do only one as a day trip, you do it badly by definition.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **May-June and mid-September to early October.** July and August are saturated on every front (road, ferries, prices, beaches), and in August the ratio between what you spend and what you get is the worst of the year. **April and October** are ideal for the trails, with the sea still or already out of play. **From November to March** the coast truly empties out: it's beautiful and quiet, but many places are closed.
- **No car. Seriously.** The SS163 is narrow, winding and without parking; where there is any, it costs absurd amounts. **In high season an alternating license plate system applies on some stretches**, and it changes from year to year. **In Capri, cars of non-residents are banned**, and **Procida and Ischia have seasonal limits on landing vehicles**: these are all rules to check before leaving.
- **How you get around, in practice:**
  - **Ferries and hydrofoils** connect Salerno, Amalfi, Positano, Sorrento, Capri, Ischia, Procida and Naples. They cost from about €10 per leg (Salerno-Amalfi) to over €25 (Naples-Capri by hydrofoil), and are fast and scenic. **The limit is the sea:** in strong wind runs are cancelled, especially on minor routes and off season. **Always plan a plan B by land.**
  - **SITA buses** run along the whole coast, are cheap, and are the only way to reach the upper towns like Ravello and Agerola. In summer they're full and you get left behind: it's best to board at the terminus.
  - **In Naples there are two ports:** **Molo Beverello** for the fast hydrofoils and **Calata Porta di Massa** for ferries carrying vehicles. They're close but distinct, and going to the wrong one costs an hour.
  - **For Ischia and Procida it's often better to leave from Pozzuoli** rather than Naples: the crossing is shorter and costs less.
- **Where to sleep, the decision that weighs most on the budget:** **in season, staying in Praiano, Minori or Maiori costs a fraction of the same thing in Positano or Amalfi**, on the same coast and ten minutes away by ferry. **Salerno** is the budget base par excellence, well connected by sea to the whole coast. **Ravello** is another category: higher up, quieter, more expensive.
- **The trails:** the Coast is one of the best coastal trekking areas in the Mediterranean, and the routes are **free**. **You need real hiking shoes** (not sandals, not city sneakers), **water** and an **early morning start** in the warm season, because on much of the routes there's no shade.
- **Steps:** this coast is measured in steps, not kilometers. In Positano you walk down and back up; the Path of the Gods ends with **about 1,700 steps** of descent into Positano; Sorgeto on Ischia is reached by **over two hundred steps** down that then have to be climbed back up. If you have knee problems, you need to know this beforehand.
- **Food:** the tradition here is the fishermen's, so **blue fish** and not noble fish. The **colatura di alici from Cetara**, direct heir of the Roman **garum**, made by maturing anchovies under salt in wooden barrels, is used **raw** on spaghetti with garlic, oil and parsley, and **must never be cooked**. Then **scialatielli**, **mozzarella in myrtle leaves**, **pezzogna** and squid, and the **Amalfi sfusato lemon**, huge, with a thick peel, which you can even eat in a salad. The **limoncello** on sale is almost always industrial: the good stuff you buy from producers.
- **On Ischia, the thermal baths chapter:** the island is volcanic and springs are everywhere. There are **big equipped thermal parks** on the western side, paid, with day admission, and there are **free springs** like **Sorgeto**, where boiling water comes out directly into the sea in a bay of stones.
- **From the airport to the centre:** you land at Naples Capodichino (NAP) · the Alibus goes to Centrale station (Piazza Garibaldi) in about 15-20 minutes and to Molo Beverello, for ferries and hydrofoils, in about 35-45, from about 6:00 to midnight, €5 · the Curreri bus goes straight to Sorrento in about 75-90 minutes, about €10, with few runs between 9:00 and 19:30 · taxis at a predetermined fare, about €21 to the historic centre or the station and about €24 to Molo Beverello: ask for it before getting in, otherwise the meter runs · avoid the unlicensed drivers touting in arrivals.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival and the first evening on the Coast
You arrive in Naples and transfer to the coast: by sea in season, or by Circumvesuviana to Sorrento and then by bus or ferry.

For sleeping, the earlier recommendation holds: **Praiano or Minori** if you want the coast at a human price, **Salerno** if you want to spend little and use the ferries, **Positano** only knowing what it costs.

The first evening, take it slow. **Positano** at sunset, if you're there: it's vertical, you walk down among stairs and bougainvillea to the Spiaggia Grande and **you climb back up on your legs**. There's no alternative, except a bus that takes a long loop. The church of Santa Maria Assunta has the dome of yellow and green majolica tiles that is the image of the town.

**In practice:** from Naples by sea from Molo Beverello in season, or Circumvesuviana to Sorrento then SITA bus or ferry · no car · in Positano you walk down and back up, travel light · book dinner for the first evening in high season.

### Day 2 — The Path of the Gods
It's the best day of the itinerary, and it has to be done early.

We took the bus to **Bomerano**, a hamlet of **Agerola**, at about 600 meters. From there starts the **Path of the Gods**, which runs high along the ridge with **the coast below, Positano ahead and Capri on the horizon**. It's about three hours of walking, with an overall **downhill** elevation change, on a trail that's exposed in several places but not technical.

**Direction matters:** from **Bomerano toward Nocelle** you go down; the other way you climb the whole thing, with the same view and a lot more effort. It's the most common mistake on this trail.

We arrived at **Nocelle**, a hamlet hanging above Positano. From there you can take the bus or **go down the more than 1,700 steps** that lead to Positano. The next day your legs remember it.

Along the way you see the **lemon and vine terraces on impossible slopes**: they're exactly why this coast is a UNESCO site **as a cultural landscape** and not as a natural landscape. Someone built those dry-stone walls, by hand, over the centuries.

In the afternoon, beach or ferry, and rest.

**In practice:** SITA bus to Agerola-Bomerano early in the morning · about three hours from Bomerano to Nocelle, downhill · free trail, trekking shoes and water, no guaranteed fountain along the way · from Nocelle bus to Positano or about 1,700 steps.

### Day 3 — Amalfi, Atrani and Ravello
We went by ferry to **Amalfi**, which was a **Maritime Republic** when Venice was still a gamble and which today is a small town with a disproportionate past.

There's the **Duomo**, with its monumental staircase and striped facade, and above all the **Cloister of Paradise**, with its interlaced arches of Arab-Norman taste, the most beautiful thing in town. Then the **Paper Museum**, in a historic paper mill inside the valley: **Amalfi made "bambagina paper" with the Arab method** as far back as the Middle Ages, and the museum shows the machines and vats still in place.

**Two hundred meters from Amalfi**, behind the headland, is **Atrani**: **the smallest municipality in Italy by area**, a little square with the church and the houses packed against it, and **almost nobody**, while Amalfi is full. You get there on foot in ten minutes along the road or through a pedestrian passage, and it's the most instructive contrast on the Coast.

In the afternoon we went up to **Ravello**, about four hundred meters above the coast, where the air, the noise and the pace change completely. There's **Villa Rufolo**, with the terrace overlooking the bay (the one with the summer concerts, and the one that inspired Wagner's Klingsor garden in *Parsifal*), and there's **Villa Cimbrone** with the **Terrace of Infinity**, a balcony lined with marble busts, sheer above the sea: probably the most beautiful viewpoint in Campania.

The evening in Ravello, if you manage to stay, is something else: after the last buses have gone down, the town changes.

**In practice:** ferry to Amalfi, about €9-15 each way from Salerno · Cathedral and Cloister of Paradise with a ticket · Atrani ten minutes on foot · SITA bus to Ravello, which fills up in summer: better to board at the terminus · Villa Rufolo and Villa Cimbrone on separate tickets.

### Day 4 — The eastern coast and the Valle delle Ferriere
It's the day of the things almost nobody does.

**Option A — The Valle delle Ferriere.** A nature reserve above Amalfi, going up the mill valley. There's a damp, constant microclimate in which **giant ferns of pre-glacial origin** survive, and along the route remain the ruins of the **ironworks and paper mills** that used the stream's water. You walk in shade, among waterfalls, twenty minutes from one of the most crowded coasts in Italy. **Some stretches of the reserve have regulated access:** better to find out beforehand.

**Option B — The coast from Minori to Cetara and Vietri.** **Minori** has a **Roman villa** with frescoes, in the middle of town, free and empty. From there starts the **Path of the Lemons**, which leads to **Maiori** through the terraces: a short, beautiful walk. Then **Cetara**, a real fishing village, where you buy the **colatura** from producers, and **Vietri sul Mare**, the capital of Campanian **ceramics**, with its tiled church domes.

Anyone with energy left can add the **Fiordo di Furore**, a very narrow cut in the rock with a bridge above and a tiny beach at the bottom, and the **Emerald Grotto** at Conca dei Marini, reachable by boat or by an elevator from the road, **with access that depends on sea conditions**.

**In practice:** Valle delle Ferriere with regulated access, check beforehand · shoes with grip on wet ground · Roman villa in Minori free · Sentiero dei Limoni short and easy · Emerald Grotto only in calm sea.

### Day 5 — Transfer to Capri
In the morning we took the ferry to **Capri**. You land at **Marina Grande** and go up to the center with the **funicular**, or on foot if you want to start well.

We spent the afternoon on the upper part: the **Piazzetta**, tiny, which lives on being watched, and then the **Gardens of Augustus** with the view over the **Faraglioni** and over **Via Krupp**, the switchback road cut into the rock that goes down to Marina Piccola. **Via Krupp has been closed in stretches for rockfall risk: better to check beforehand whether it's open.**

Toward evening, when the day excursions have left, **Capri changes**. In the evening the island empties by two thirds and suddenly becomes quiet, and that's why it's worth sleeping there instead of doing it as a day trip.

**In practice:** ferry or hydrofoil to Capri from Amalfi, Positano, Sorrento or Naples · funicular from Marina Grande to the Piazzetta, tickets at the station · Gardens of Augustus with a ticket · check the status of Via Krupp · sleeping on the island is expensive, book early.

### Day 6 — Capri, from above and from the sea
In the morning we went up to **Anacapri**, the upper town, the other face of the island: fewer shop windows, more life. From there we took the **chairlift to Monte Solaro**, a single-seat, open chairlift that climbs slowly over the low walls and gardens: it's the best experience on the island, at a ridiculous price. At the top, at **589 meters**, there's all of Capri below and the whole bay around.

In Anacapri there's also **Villa San Michele**, the house built by the Swedish doctor **Axel Munthe** on the remains of an imperial villa, with its loggia, the Egyptian sphinx on the parapet and the view dropping sheer to Marina Grande.

We spent the afternoon **on a boat**: the **tour around the island** is the right way to see Capri, which from land you understand only halfway. You pass **under the arch of the middle Faraglione**, you see the caves and coves that can't be reached from land, and you swim.

**The Blue Grotto** is the chapter with the most misunderstandings. **You enter only on small rowboats, lying down**, through a very low opening, **and access depends entirely on the tide and sea conditions**: with rough seas it's closed, and on good days the queue of waiting boats can be very long. The blue light is a real optical phenomenon (the light enters through a submerged opening), but the visit lasts a few minutes. **It's worth it if conditions are right and there's no line; it's not worth a two-hour wait.**

For those who prefer walking there's the **Path of the Fortini** along the western coast, or the climb to **Villa Jovis**, the villa of **Tiberius** on the eastern tip: it's the largest of the twelve imperial villas on the island and from there you command the whole bay.

**In practice:** bus to Anacapri from Capri or Marina Grande · Monte Solaro chairlift, stops in strong wind · Villa San Michele with a ticket · boat trip around the island to book at Marina Grande · Blue Grotto only in calm sea and without a queue, entry paid separately on the boat.

### Day 7 — Ischia: the Castle and the thermal baths
We took the ferry to **Ischia**, **the largest island in the bay** and the one least like its reputation: it's not a popular Capri, it's something else entirely. It's volcanic, green, home to sixty thousand people, and has **a thermal tradition documented since antiquity**.

The first stop was the **Aragonese Castle** at **Ischia Ponte**, on a rock islet connected by a stone bridge. You go up by an elevator cut into the rock or by the ramp, and above there's a whole citadel, with a view over the whole bay: churches, terraces, a convent with the **cemetery of the Poor Clare nuns**, where bodies were left to decompose on stone seats while the sisters prayed right beside them.

We spent the afternoon in a **thermal park** on the western side. They're big gardens sloping down to the sea, with pools at different temperatures, from forty degrees up, natural saunas cut into the rock and direct access to the sea. You enter for the day and spend the entire afternoon doing nothing else, which is exactly the point.

**In practice:** ferry from Capri, Naples or Pozzuoli · Aragonese Castle with a ticket, lift or ramp · thermal parks with day entry, check opening season and prices · swimsuit, flip-flops and towel for the spas.

### Day 8 — Ischia: Epomeo, Sorgeto and La Mortella
In the morning we climbed **Mount Epomeo**, the volcanic peak at the center of the island, **789 meters**. The classic route starts from **Fontana**: it's a climb of about an hour and a half, with the last stretch in a **corridor cut into green tuff** that ends on a summit with the hermitage carved into the rock. From up there you see **all of Ischia at once**, which never happens from the island, and on clear days you reach Capri and Vesuvius.

As an alternative or addition there are the **La Mortella Gardens** in Forio: a subtropical botanical garden created from the 1950s in a stone quarry, with greenhouses, tree ferns, giant water lilies and two open-air theaters. It comes from a lifetime project, and it's one of the most beautiful gardens in Italy.

In the late afternoon we went to **Sorgeto**. You go down to a bay of stones near **Panza** by **over two hundred steps** (which then have to be climbed back up, and it's no joke) and arrive at a cove where **boiling springs come out directly under the stones and mix with the sea**. You look for the natural pools with the right temperature, moving one meter between water at forty degrees and the cold. **It's free, it's always open, and the best moment is sunset or a winter night.** You need **rubber shoes**, because near the vents the stones really burn, and caution, because in some spots the water is scalding.

**In practice:** Epomeo from Fontana, about an hour and a half uphill · La Mortella gardens open only in some months and days, to check · Sorgeto free, over two hundred steps, rubber shoes · island bus or taxi between Forio and Panza.

### Day 9 — Procida and the return
The last ferry was to **Procida**, the smallest: **four square kilometers**, the least touristy of the three and **Italian Capital of Culture in 2022**, which brought it attention without changing it much.

**Marina Corricella** is the reason to come: a fishing village packed against a natural amphitheater, with houses painted **pink, yellow, ochre, light blue** (**solid colors chosen so fishermen could recognize their own home from the sea**), outdoor staircases, arches and boats pulled up on shore. Cars don't get there: you walk down.

Above is **Terra Murata**, the highest point, with the **Abbey of San Michele Arcangelo**, the former Bourbon prison and the view dropping down onto Corricella. On the other side are **Chiaiolella** and the islet of **Vivara**, a nature reserve connected by a bridge, **with limited and regulated access: check the rules beforehand**.

You can see all of Procida in one day, and it's the right note to close on: after Capri, it reminds you that in this bay there's still a place where people fish for a living.

In the afternoon we returned to Naples.

**In practice:** ferry from Ischia to Procida, a few minutes · Corricella and Terra Murata on foot or by micro-taxi · Vivara with capped access, check the rules beforehand · back to Naples from Procida port, check whether it arrives at Beverello or Calata Porta di Massa.

## How to shorten the itinerary
- **In 5 days:** Coast only (Days 1-4) plus one day on Capri, sleeping there.
- **In 5 days, islands version:** Capri two nights, Ischia two nights, Procida as a day trip on the way back.
- **In 6-7 days:** Coast in three days with the Path of the Gods, then two islands out of three: **Capri and Procida** if you want concentrated beauty, **Ischia and Procida** if you want thermal baths, walks and human prices.
- **What should never be done:** Capri as a day trip from Naples in high season. You arrive when there are the most people, leave before it empties, and see the worst version of a beautiful island.

## What to see / what to do
- **The Path of the Gods from Bomerano to Nocelle**, in the right direction.
- **The Cloister of Paradise** in Amalfi.
- **Atrani**, two hundred meters from Amalfi and with nobody there.
- **The Terrace of Infinity** at Villa Cimbrone in Ravello.
- **The Valle delle Ferriere**, with its pre-glacial ferns.
- **The chairlift to Monte Solaro** in Anacapri.
- **The boat tour around Capri**, passing under the arch of the Faraglioni.
- **The Aragonese Castle** and the Poor Clare cemetery on Ischia.
- **Sorgeto at sunset**, with the boiling springs in the sea.
- **Marina Corricella on Procida**, where people still fish.

## Possible extensions
- **Naples (+3 days):** the dedicated itinerary, the natural complement to this one.
- **Paestum and the Cilento (+2-3 days):** the three Doric temples and then a completely different coast, much wilder and with much lower prices.
- **Pompeii and Herculaneum (+1-2 days):** they fit well on the way in or out from Sorrento.
- **Sorrento Peninsula and Punta Campanella (+2 days):** the marine reserve at the end of the peninsula, with trails and the view of Capri within reach.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
- **Praiano or Minori** on the coast: same coast, half the price of Positano and Amalfi.
- **Salerno** as a budget base, with ferries leaving from the waterfront.
- **Capri**, at least one night, to see the island after the day-trippers have left.
- **Ischia on the western side** (Forio-Panza area) to be near thermal baths, sunsets and Sorgeto.
- **Procida** for those who want a quiet, well-connected base, with prices much lower than the other two islands.
- **Good to know:** on all the islands and on the coast **in August prices double** compared to May and September, for the same property.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
- **Spaghetti with Cetara anchovy colatura**, raw.
- **Scialatielli with seafood** and blue fish, the real tradition of this coast.
- **Mozzarella in myrtle leaves**, wrapped in sprigs of myrtle.
- **Ischia-style rabbit** on Ischia, an inland dish and not a sea one, which surprises anyone expecting only fish.
- **Lemon delight** and the **sfusato lemon** even in salad.
- **To drink:** the volcanic whites of the **Campi Flegrei** and **Ischia DOC**, Falanghina, and limoncello **only if artisanal**.
- **General rule:** on the waterfront of Positano, Amalfi and in Capri's Piazzetta you pay for the location. Going up two flights of stairs, anywhere, changes the bill and often improves the plate.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't come by car**, and least of all in July and August.
- **Get around by ferry** and always keep a plan B by land, because in rough seas runs are cancelled.
- **Check which port you leave from in Naples** (Beverello or Porta di Massa) and consider Pozzuoli for Ischia and Procida.
- **Do the Path of the Gods from Bomerano toward Nocelle**, not the other way around.
- **Real hiking shoes** for the trails and **rubber shoes** for Sorgeto.
- **Don't do Capri as a day trip** if you can sleep there.
- **Don't wait two hours for the Blue Grotto**, and check the sea conditions first.
- **Don't cook the anchovy colatura.**
- **Check the access rules**: Via Krupp, the Valle delle Ferriere, Vivara and the Emerald Grotto all have rules that change.
- **Factor in the steps.** It's not a flat beach vacation: it's a vertical coast.

## Chiusura personale
What stays with you from this coast isn't Positano from the water, as much as it looks exactly like the photographs. It's the moment, on the Path of the Gods, when you stop on an exposed stretch five hundred meters above the sea and look down: below are the terraces, one above the other, for hundreds of meters of drop, held up by dry-stone walls built stone upon stone. On those two-meter-wide patches of earth there are lemons and vines. No machine could ever get up there; everything you see was carried on shoulders.

Then you lower your gaze a few degrees and there's the state road, with its line of stopped cars, and buses passing each other with mirrors folded in. The two things are two hundred meters of elevation apart and don't talk to each other. The Coast is a World Heritage site for the one above, and it is visited almost entirely from the one below: which is, all things considered, good news for anyone who decides to climb.
