# Andalusia in 9 Days: Seville, Córdoba, Granada and Ronda

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (trip taken by Rick; itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:** **March-May or September-early November**
**Durata:** 9 days, 8 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, AVE train between the big cities, rental car from Granada onward
**Categoria:** Europa · Spagna · Andalusia · cultura moresca · flamenco · città

---

## Apertura personale
There is no single way to tell the story of Andalusia, but there is a thread that ties its most famous cities together: all of them, in different ways, are what remains of Al-Andalus, the eight centuries of Islamic rule over the Iberian Peninsula that ended in 1492 right here, with the fall of Granada. Seville has the largest Gothic cathedral in the world, built on top of a mosque; Córdoba has a mosque into which, quite literally, a Renaissance cathedral was planted; Granada has the Alhambra, the last Moorish palace in Europe to survive almost intact. Ronda, at the end, is something else entirely: the mountains, the wind over the Tajo gorge, the necessary counterpoint to three cities that, beautiful as they are, risk looking too alike if taken one after the other.

Nine days are enough to do all four stops at a human pace, without the rushing that reduces the Alhambra or the Mezquita to a photo and off you go. The only real constraint is the heat: this itinerary is meant for spring or autumn, not summer, when Seville and Córdoba regularly hit 40°C.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **March-May** and **September-early November**. **June-August** is best avoided because of the heat: Seville and Córdoba are among the hottest cities in Spain, with averages of 36-37°C in July-August and peaks that in 2021 reached the all-time Spanish record of 47.6°C in Córdoba. In midsummer many Córdoba attractions cut their hours. **Watch out for Holy Week and the Feria de Abril** in Seville (March/April): moments of great cultural weight, but with lodging booked months ahead and much higher prices.
- **Alhambra, booking:** tickets online only at **alhambra-patronato.es**, up to **three months ahead**. In high season (June-September, Holy Week) they sell out **weeks, sometimes months**, in advance: book as soon as you set the date of your trip, don't leave it for last. The ticket assigns a binding time slot for the Nasrid Palaces.
- **AVE trains:** Madrid-Seville (~2h30), Seville-Córdoba (~45 min), Córdoba-Madrid (~1h40-2h) are among the most frequent and fastest routes on the Spanish network. **Córdoba-Granada has no direct AVE as fast as the other routes**: the bus (~2h) is often more practical than the regional train.
- **Rental car:** not needed until Granada, but almost essential for Ronda and the pueblos blancos, where buses between villages are scarce. It's best to rent it in Granada and return it in Málaga at the end of the trip.
- **The tapeo:** in Seville you often have dinner going from bar to bar, tapa after tapa. In **Granada the tapa comes free with every drink**: it is one of the few Spanish cities where this still always happens.
- **Flamenco, how to choose:** the more touristy tablaos tend toward standardized, amplified shows for large groups; the smaller ones without amplification, often in Triana (Seville) or in the cave houses of Sacromonte (Granada), give you something closer to the tradition. Price is not always a guarantee of authenticity.
- **The heat:** even in the recommended window (March-May, September-October) the middle hours in Seville and Córdoba can be heavy: plan outdoor monuments for early morning or late afternoon.
- **Electricity and currency:** euro, type C/F sockets identical to Italy. No surprises on that front.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Seville: Cathedral and Giralda
We landed in Seville (San Pablo airport) and transferred to the center. In the afternoon we saw the **Seville Cathedral**, the largest Gothic church in the world, built over the old Almohad mosque; inside is the (disputed) tomb of Christopher Columbus. Then we climbed the **Giralda**, the former minaret turned bell tower, which you go up on ramps rather than stairs. The view from the top over the red-and-white city is the first taste of the trip.

In the evening, the **Barrio de Santa Cruz**, the former Jewish quarter, among narrow alleys and flowered patios.

### Day 2 — Seville: Real Alcázar, Triana and flamenco
In the morning we visited the **Real Alcázar**, the royal palace still in use, a Mudéjar masterpiece with its patios, gardens and the Hall of the Ambassadors. The ticket has a time slot and it's best to book it online ahead.

In the afternoon we crossed the Guadalquivir to **Triana**, the Gypsy neighborhood that is the cradle of flamenco and of Sevillian ceramics: the Mercado de Triana on the riverfront for a first tapeo, then free time among the alleys.

The evening was for a **flamenco show** in a small acoustic tablao (there is a dedicated experience page).

### Day 3 — From Seville to Córdoba: the Mezquita
In the morning we took the AVE to **Córdoba** (about 45 minutes). The afternoon went entirely to the **Mezquita-Catedral**: the forest of over eight hundred columns of marble and onyx with double-tiered two-tone arches, the Mihrab and, at the center, the Renaissance Capilla Mayor grafted into the heart of the mosque. An architectural contrast no photo can convey.

In the evening, the **Judería**, the old Jewish quarter around Calleja de las Flores.

### Day 4 — Córdoba and transfer to Granada
In the morning we went to the **Alcázar de los Reyes Cristianos** and the **Roman Bridge** over the Guadalquivir, with the classic view of the Mezquita from the opposite bank. If the trip falls in May, you can detour to the **Patios de Córdoba** open to the public.

In the early afternoon, bus or regional train to **Granada** (about 2-3 hours). The evening served to get our bearings, with a first walk around the center near the Cathedral.

### Day 5 — Granada: the Alhambra and the Generalife
The day went almost entirely to the **Alhambra**: the **Nasrid Palaces** with the Patio de los Leones and the Hall of the Ambassadors (access tied to the time slot on the ticket), the **Alcazaba**, the oldest fortress in the complex, and the **Generalife**, the sultans' summer gardens with hidden fountains. For booking, which is mandatory well in advance, there is a dedicated experience page.

### Day 6 — Granada: Albaicín and Sacromonte
In the morning we got deliberately lost in the alleys of the **Albaicín**, the Moorish quarter and UNESCO World Heritage site, among carmens (houses with an enclosed garden) and small mosques, all the way to the **Mirador de San Nicolás**, with the view of the Alhambra and the snow-capped Sierra Nevada behind.

In the afternoon we climbed up to **Sacromonte**, the historically Gypsy cave-house neighborhood. The evening was for a **zambra**, the neighborhood's traditional flamenco show, in one of the cuevas (there is a dedicated experience page).

### Day 7 — From Granada to Ronda: the Serranía
We picked up the rental car and set off for **Ronda** (about 2h30-3h), through the Serranía de Ronda. Arriving in the afternoon, we took a first walk onto the **Puente Nuevo**, the 18th-century bridge that spans the Tajo gorge more than 100 meters up, and made a (partial) descent toward the **Mirador de Aldehuela** for the view from below.

### Day 8 — Ronda: the Puente Nuevo and the pueblos blancos
In the morning we visited the **Plaza de Toros de Ronda**, among the oldest bullrings in Spain, and the **Baños Árabes**. The afternoon is for a drive to one or two nearby **pueblos blancos**, **Setenil de las Bodegas**, with its houses carved under overhanging rock, or **Grazalema**, in the natural park of the same name, and for a stop at a winery in the Ronda wine region.

### Day 9 — Ronda and end of the trip toward Málaga
We left the last morning in Ronda free for the Ciudad, the Moorish quarter on the side of the bridge opposite the center. Then by car to **Málaga** (about 1h30) for the flight home.

## What to see / what to do
- **The Cathedral and the Giralda** of Seville, the largest Gothic church in the world over an old minaret.
- **The Real Alcázar** of Seville, a Mudéjar palace still in use.
- **The Mezquita-Catedral** of Córdoba, the forest of columns with the Renaissance cathedral at the center.
- **The Alhambra and the Generalife** of Granada, booked months in advance.
- **The Albaicín and the Mirador de San Nicolás**, the classic view of the Alhambra.
- **The Puente Nuevo in Ronda** over the Tajo gorge.
- **An acoustic flamenco show**, in Seville or in Sacromonte.

## Possible extensions
- **Costa del Sol (+2-3 days):** Marbella, Málaga city (with the Picasso museum, he was a native) or the coast between Nerja and Frigiliana, for those who want to add some sea to the tour of the cities.
- **Cádiz (+1-2 days):** the oldest city in Spain according to tradition, with its Atlantic center and nearby Jerez de la Frontera, home of sherry.
- **Sierra Nevada (+1 day):** from Granada, skiing in winter or hiking in summer on the highest peaks of mainland Spain.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
In Seville, the **Barrio de Santa Cruz** for convenience or **Triana** for a more lived-in atmosphere and lower prices. In Córdoba, the **Judería** for its proximity to the Mezquita. In Granada, the **Albaicín** for the view (with steep climbs to factor in) or the center around the Cathedral for convenience. In Ronda, the historic center near the Puente Nuevo.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
The **tapeo** is an institution in Seville (salmorejo, espinacas con garbanzos, pescaíto frito) and in Granada, where the tapa comes free with every drink. In Córdoba, **salmorejo** (thicker than gazpacho, born right here) and **rabo de toro**. In Ronda, mountain cooking with grilled lamb and local cheeses, often with wines from the Sierras de Málaga appellation.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book the Alhambra as soon as you set the date of your trip**, not close to departure: it sells out weeks, in high season months, ahead.
- **Avoid June-August**: the heat in Seville and Córdoba is a serious factor to build into your planning.
- **Don't pick the first tablao advertised in the center**: between tourist formats and more authentic venues, it's worth checking the reputation of the place before booking.
- **Rent the car only from Granada onward**: in the three big cities the AVE is more convenient and a car in the center is just a parking problem.
- **Dress in layers even in spring/autumn**: temperature swings between day and evening remain marked.
- **Check the reduced summer hours** of the Córdoba monuments if you travel anyway in late June or early September.

## Chiusura personale
What stays with me is how much the four stops complete each other instead of repeating: Seville is the great Baroque capital on Moorish foundations, Córdoba is the most disorienting monument in its single hall of columns, Granada is the intact palace on the hill, and Ronda is the mountain air that the other three, closed within their cities, don't have. A trip in four stops that rarely disappoints anyone who takes it outside the hottest months.
