# Andalusia in 9 Days: Seville, Córdoba, Granada and Ronda

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (trip taken by Rick; itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 9 days, 8 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, AVE train between the big cities, rental car from Granada onward
**Categoria:** Europa · Spagna · Andalusia · cultura moresca · flamenco · città

---

## Apertura personale
There is no single way to tell the story of Andalusia, but there is a thread that ties its most famous cities together: all of them, in different ways, are what remains of Al-Andalus, the eight centuries of Islamic rule over the Iberian Peninsula that ended in 1492 right here, with the fall of Granada. Seville has the largest Gothic cathedral in the world, built on top of a mosque; Córdoba has a mosque into which, quite literally, a Renaissance cathedral was planted; Granada has the Alhambra, the last Moorish palace in Europe to survive almost intact. Ronda, at the end, is something else entirely: the mountains, the wind over the Tajo gorge, the necessary counterpoint to three cities that, beautiful as they are, risk looking too alike if taken one after the other.

Nine days are enough to do all four stops at a human pace, without the rushing that reduces the Alhambra or the Mezquita to a photo and off you go. The only real constraint is the heat: this itinerary is meant for spring or autumn, not summer, when Seville and Córdoba regularly hit 40°C.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **March-May** and **September-early November**. **June-August** is best avoided because of the heat: Seville and Córdoba are among the hottest cities in Spain, with averages of 36-37°C in July-August and peaks that in 2021 reached the all-time Spanish record of 47.6°C in Córdoba. In midsummer many Córdoba attractions cut their hours. **Watch out for Holy Week and the Feria de Abril** in Seville (March/April): moments of great cultural weight, but with lodging booked months ahead and much higher prices.
- **Alhambra, booking:** tickets online only at **alhambra-patronato.es**, up to **three months ahead**. In high season (June-September, Holy Week) they sell out **weeks, sometimes months**, in advance: book as soon as you set the date of your trip, don't leave it for last. The ticket assigns a binding time slot for the Nasrid Palaces.
- **AVE trains:** Madrid-Seville (~2h30), Seville-Córdoba (~45 min), Córdoba-Madrid (~1h40-2h) are among the most frequent and fastest routes on the Spanish network. **Córdoba-Granada** has direct high-speed trains (about 1h30), more convenient than the bus (~2h40): book them together with the other legs.
- **Rental car:** not needed until Granada, but almost essential for Ronda and the pueblos blancos, where buses between villages are scarce. It's best to rent it in Granada and return it in Málaga at the end of the trip.
- **The tapeo:** in Seville you often have dinner going from bar to bar, tapa after tapa. In **Granada the tapa comes free with every drink**: it is one of the few Spanish cities where this still always happens.
- **Flamenco, how to choose:** the more touristy tablaos tend toward standardized, amplified shows for large groups; the smaller ones without amplification, often in Triana (Seville) or in the cave houses of Sacromonte (Granada), give you something closer to the tradition. Price is not always a guarantee of authenticity.
- **The heat:** even in the recommended window (March-May, September-October) the middle hours in Seville and Córdoba can be heavy: plan outdoor monuments for early morning or late afternoon.
- **Electricity and currency:** euro, type C/F sockets identical to Italy. No surprises on that front.
- **From the airport to the centre:** at Seville San Pablo (SVQ) Tussam's EA bus goes to Santa Justa, Prado de San Sebastián and Plaza de Armas in about 35 minutes, every 20-30 minutes from about 5:20 to 1:15, about €4, payable on board, contactless card included · fixed-fare taxi to the centre, about €25 by day and €28 at night and on holidays · Uber, Cabify and Bolt work · leaving from Málaga (AGP), the C1 Cercanías train links María Zambrano and the centre in about 12 minutes, about €2, every 20 minutes; the A Express bus costs about €4, a taxi about €20-25 · to avoid trouble, always check that the taxi applies the fixed fare.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Seville: Cathedral and Giralda
We landed in Seville (San Pablo airport) and transferred to the center. The airport bus links the terminals with the center in about half an hour, with stops near the Cathedral and Santa Justa station; taxis have a fixed fare to the center. In the old town a car is no use and actually complicates things: many streets are pedestrian or restricted.

In the afternoon we saw the **Seville Cathedral**, the largest Gothic church in the world, built on top of the old Almohad mosque; inside is the (disputed) tomb of Christopher Columbus. Then we climbed the **Giralda**, the former minaret turned bell tower, which you go up by ramps rather than stairs: there are thirty-five of them, wide enough for a horse to pass. The view from the top over the red and white city is the first taste of the trip. You leave through the **Patio de los Naranjos**, the orange-tree courtyard that belonged to the mosque.

In the evening, the **Barrio de Santa Cruz**, the former Jewish quarter, among narrow alleys and flower-filled patios. The finest route passes along the **Callejón del Agua**, by the walls of the Alcázar, and through the small Plaza de Doña Elvira. *After nine the alleys empty of groups and only the tapas bars remain: the right time to walk them.*

**In practice:** Cathedral about €13 online and €14 at the ticket office, Giralda and the church of El Salvador included · open in the afternoon until about 7 pm, on Sundays only in the afternoon · to sleep, Santa Cruz is convenient, Triana more lived-in.

### Day 2 — Seville: Real Alcázar, Triana and flamenco
In the morning we went to the **Real Alcázar**, the royal palace still in use, a Mudéjar masterpiece with its patios, gardens and Hall of Ambassadors. The ticket has a time slot and it is worth booking it online in advance. The 9:30 slot, at opening, is the best: the **Patio de las Doncellas**, with its long pool and pierced arches, is almost empty. Then the gardens, worth an hour on their own, with the peacocks and the Grutesco galleries.

In the afternoon we crossed the Guadalquivir to **Triana**, the gitano quarter that is the cradle of flamenco and Sevillian ceramics: the Mercado de Triana on the riverfront for a first round of tapas, then free time in the alleys. Beneath the market lie the foundations of the **Castillo de San Jorge**, seat of the Inquisition, now a small museum. Around Calle Alfarería there are still azulejo workshops. Come back at sunset across the **Puente de Isabel II**, with the view of Seville lit up.

The evening was for a **flamenco show** in a small, acoustic tablao (there's a dedicated experience page). *Shows last about an hour and small venues have few seats: book the day before.*

**In practice:** Real Alcázar about €15.50, opens at 9:30, closes earlier in winter · Calle Betis, on the Triana riverfront, is the place for an aperitivo · tablao to be booked ahead, dinner before or after.

### Day 3 — From Seville to Córdoba: the Mezquita
In the morning we took the AVE to **Córdoba** (about 45 minutes). From the station to the old town is about a twenty-minute walk, or a taxi; drop the bags straight away and plan to tackle the Mezquita in the cooler hours, because in Córdoba the heat comes earlier and weighs more than in Seville.

The afternoon went entirely to the **Mezquita-Catedral**: the forest of more than eight hundred marble and onyx columns with two-tiered, two-tone arches, the Mihrab and, at the center, the Renaissance Capilla Mayor grafted into the heart of the mosque. An architectural contrast no photo captures. You enter from the **Patio de los Naranjos**, free to access, and walk among the columns following the phases of expansion, from the oldest eighth-century part toward the Mihrab. The bell tower is climbed with a separate ticket at set times.

In the evening, the **Judería**, the old Jewish quarter around Calleja de las Flores, with the medieval **Synagogue** and the Puerta de Almodóvar. *Calleja de las Flores is tiny and crowded at all hours: the photo with the bell tower at the end works best late in the evening.*

**In practice:** Mezquita about €13, open all day except during services · free entry early in the morning Monday to Saturday, in a short slot before tourist opening · in Córdoba, sleep in the Judería, near the Mezquita.

### Day 4 — Córdoba and transfer to Granada
In the morning we went to the **Alcázar de los Reyes Cristianos** and onto the **Roman Bridge** over the Guadalquivir, with the classic view of the Mezquita from the other bank. The Alcázar is the fortress from which the Catholic Monarchs prepared the conquest of Granada; inside are Roman mosaics and, outside, terraced gardens with pools and cypresses. At the far end of the bridge stands the **Torre de la Calahorra**, the medieval watchtower. If the trip falls in May, you can detour to the **Patios de Córdoba** open to the public.

In the early afternoon, direct high-speed train to **Granada** (about an hour and a half). Book it in advance: services are not very frequent, and Granada station is about twenty minutes on foot from the center. The evening served to settle in, with a first walk around the center near the Cathedral. Next to it is the **Capilla Real**, where Isabella and Ferdinand are buried. *In Granada a free tapa comes with every drink: two or three bars already make a dinner.*

**In practice:** Alcázar de los Reyes Cristianos closed on Mondays, ticket about €7 · Patios festival in the first half of May, free entry to most houses · book the direct train to Granada on Renfe or the private high-speed operators.

### Day 5 — Granada: the Alhambra and the Generalife
The day went almost entirely to the **Alhambra**: the **Nasrid Palaces** with the Patio de los Leones and the Hall of the Ambassadors (access tied to the ticket's time slot), the **Alcazaba**, the oldest fortress in the complex, and the **Generalife**, the sultans' summer gardens with their hidden fountains. For booking, mandatory well in advance, there's a dedicated experience page.

The complex is large and the routes are long: you need at least three or four hours, and comfortable shoes. Build the visit around the Nasrid Palaces slot, the only one that cannot be moved, and fit the rest before or after. The **Palace of Charles V**, the Renaissance palace with its great circular courtyard, can be visited without a ticket. From the towers of the Alcazaba you see the Albaicín opposite and the Sierra Nevada. *The name on the ticket must match your ID: they check it at the entrance to the Palaces.* Those still keen can come back in the evening for the night visit, on a separate ticket.

**In practice:** general ticket about €21-22, only on the official website and up to three months ahead · walk up the Cuesta de Gomérez or take the city minibuses from the center · water and a hat, there is little shade inside.

### Day 6 — Granada: Albaicín and Sacromonte
In the morning we deliberately got lost in the alleys of the **Albaicín**, the UNESCO-listed Moorish quarter, among carmens (houses with walled gardens) and small mosques, up to the **Mirador de San Nicolás**, with its view of the Alhambra and the snow-capped Sierra Nevada in the background. You climb from the **Carrera del Darro**, the street along the river below the Alhambra, past the **Bañuelo**, the eleventh-century Arab baths, and the Paseo de los Tristes. The climbs are steep: those who prefer not to walk them use the minibuses that go up from the center.

In the afternoon we went up to **Sacromonte**, the quarter of cave houses that was historically gitano. The **Museo Cuevas del Sacromonte** shows how people lived in the caves dug into the hill, and from the path you see the Alhambra from a different angle. At sunset, go back to San Nicolás, when the palace turns red. The evening was for a **zambra**, the neighbourhood's traditional flamenco show, in one of the cuevas (there's a dedicated experience page). *The San Nicolás sunset is crowded: arrive half an hour early to find a spot on the wall.*

**In practice:** Albaicín and the Mirador are free, the quarter is best explored by day · zambra to be booked, often with transfer included · in the Albaicín at night, move in a group and along the main streets.

### Day 7 — From Granada to Ronda: the Serranía
We picked up the rental car and set off toward **Ronda** (about 2h30-3h), through the Serranía de Ronda. The road passes near **Antequera**, where the Menga and Viera dolmens, a UNESCO World Heritage site, make a one-hour stop; then the olive-covered hills give way to mountains. The final stretch, through bends and high plateaus, is the finest drive of the trip. Leave in the morning, to arrive in daylight.

Arriving in the afternoon, we took a first walk across the **Puente Nuevo**, the 18th-century bridge spanning the Tajo gorge at over 100 meters high, and a (partial) descent toward the **Mirador de Aldehuela** for the view from below. The path down toward the old mills shows the bridge head-on, with the waterfall beneath. From the **Paseo de Blas Infante** and the Alameda del Tajo gardens you see the open valley instead. *The path below the bridge is unpaved and steep: wear closed shoes, and the way back is all uphill.*

**In practice:** Granada-Ronda by car about 2-2.5 hours without stops · Antequera dolmens free for EU citizens, closed on Mondays · in Ronda, sleep in the old town near the Puente Nuevo, parking in public garages.

### Day 8 — Ronda: the Puente Nuevo and the pueblos blancos
In the morning we went to the **Plaza de Toros de Ronda**, among the oldest bullrings in Spain, and to the **Baños Árabes**. The eighteenth-century ring is visited together with its museum and stables; even those who dislike bullfighting will find the stone architecture interesting, with its two tiers of arcades. The Arab baths, down in the San Miguel quarter, are among the best preserved in the peninsula, with vaults pierced by star-shaped skylights.

The afternoon is for a car trip to one or two nearby **pueblos blancos**, **Setenil de las Bodegas**, with houses carved under rock overhangs, or **Grazalema**, in the natural park of the same name, and for a stop at a winery in the Ronda wine area. Setenil is about half an hour away, Grazalema about forty minutes on mountain roads. *Whoever drives to the winery should taste sparingly: the Serranía roads are narrow and winding.* Wineries almost always receive visitors by appointment.

**In practice:** Plaza de Toros about €9 · Baños Árabes cheap ticket, included in Ronda's municipal tourist pass · wineries to be booked a day ahead.

### Day 9 — Ronda and end of the trip toward Málaga
We left the last morning in Ronda free for La Ciudad, the Moorish quarter on the opposite side of the bridge from the center. The streets are quieter than those around the Puente Nuevo: you pass the church of **Santa María la Mayor**, built over the main mosque, and the **Palacio de Mondragón**, with its patios and valley view. Next to the bridge is the **Casa del Rey Moro**, with a staircase cut into the rock down to the river.

Then by car to **Málaga** (about 1h30) for the return flight. You descend from the mountains toward the coast; allow a margin for traffic around Málaga and for returning the car at the airport. *The tank almost always has to be returned full: there are few, busy petrol stations near the airport, so fill up before.* Those with an afternoon flight can stop for lunch in one of the villages along the way.

**In practice:** Ronda-Málaga airport about 1h30-1h45 · Casa del Rey Moro with a ticket, the staircase is long and damp · to return the car, arrive at the airport at least two and a half hours before the flight.

## What to see / what to do
- **The Cathedral and the Giralda** of Seville, the largest Gothic church in the world over an old minaret.
- **The Real Alcázar** of Seville, a Mudéjar palace still in use.
- **The Mezquita-Catedral** of Córdoba, the forest of columns with the Renaissance cathedral at the center.
- **The Alhambra and the Generalife** of Granada, booked months in advance.
- **The Albaicín and the Mirador de San Nicolás**, the classic view of the Alhambra.
- **The Puente Nuevo in Ronda** over the Tajo gorge.
- **An acoustic flamenco show**, in Seville or in Sacromonte.

## Possible extensions
- **Costa del Sol (+2-3 days):** Marbella, Málaga city (with the Picasso museum, he was a native) or the coast between Nerja and Frigiliana, for those who want to add some sea to the tour of the cities.
- **Cádiz (+1-2 days):** the oldest city in Spain according to tradition, with its Atlantic center and nearby Jerez de la Frontera, home of sherry.
- **Sierra Nevada (+1 day):** from Granada, skiing in winter or hiking in summer on the highest peaks of mainland Spain.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
In Seville, the **Barrio de Santa Cruz** for convenience or **Triana** for a more lived-in atmosphere and lower prices. In Córdoba, the **Judería** for its proximity to the Mezquita. In Granada, the **Albaicín** for the view (with steep climbs to factor in) or the center around the Cathedral for convenience. In Ronda, the historic center near the Puente Nuevo.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
The **tapeo** is an institution in Seville (salmorejo, espinacas con garbanzos, pescaíto frito) and in Granada, where the tapa comes free with every drink. In Córdoba, **salmorejo** (thicker than gazpacho, born right here) and **rabo de toro**. In Ronda, mountain cooking with grilled lamb and local cheeses, often with wines from the Sierras de Málaga appellation.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book the Alhambra as soon as you set the date of your trip**, not close to departure: it sells out weeks, in high season months, ahead.
- **Avoid June-August**: the heat in Seville and Córdoba is a serious factor to build into your planning.
- **Don't pick the first tablao advertised in the center**: between tourist formats and more authentic venues, it's worth checking the reputation of the place before booking.
- **Rent the car only from Granada onward**: in the three big cities the AVE is more convenient and a car in the center is just a parking problem.
- **Dress in layers even in spring/autumn**: temperature swings between day and evening remain marked.
- **Check the reduced summer hours** of the Córdoba monuments if you travel anyway in late June or early September.

## Chiusura personale
Nine days are enough for Seville, Córdoba, Granada and Ronda at a human pace, without reducing the Alhambra or the Mezquita to a quick photo. Ronda, with the mountains and the wind over the Tajo gorge, is the counterpoint the other three need. The only real constraint is the heat: it's a spring or autumn trip, not a summer one.
