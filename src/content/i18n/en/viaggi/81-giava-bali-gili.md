# Java, Bali and the Gilis in 14 Days: Temples, Volcanoes, Rice Terraces and Islands Without Engines

**Durata:** 14 days, 13 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, with one domestic flight, trains, minivans, 4x4 jeeps, a ferry, a driver by the day and a fast boat

## Apertura personale
This is the stretch of Indonesia I covered in the first half of the long trip, from Yogyakarta to the Gilis. Here it's squeezed into two weeks, which is about all the time most people flying from Italy have.

Java is the part that feels least like a vacation and leaves the sharpest memory. At Ijen we climbed in the dark with gas masks, and the sulfur miners walked past us with baskets on their shoulders. Sunrise at Bromo is spectacular, even if you share it with hundreds of jeeps. I took the aerial photos on this page with the drone: Bromo at dawn, the Handara gate, the Jatiluwih rice terraces, the little islands of the Gilis.

I do Bali away from the south. I sleep inland, in Munduk and Ubud, and only head down to the coast for Uluwatu and the Kecak dance. The Gilis close the trip with three slow days, with no cars and no scooters.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** May to September for Java, when the sky at sunrise is clearest. In Bali and the Gilis the dry season runs from April to October.
- **Documents:** passport valid for at least 6 months. Apply for the visa online as an **e-VOA** at evisa.imigrasi.go.id, at least a week ahead (about 500,000 IDR, roughly 35€). Since October 2025 you also need the **All Indonesia Arrival Card**, an electronic form to fill out before every entry.
- **Currency and payments:** Indonesian rupiah (IDR), roughly 1€ = 17,000-18,000 IDR. In Yogyakarta and Bali cards work almost everywhere. At Bromo, Ijen and the Gilis it's better to have cash.
- **Time zone:** Java is on WIB (UTC+7), Bali and the Gilis on WITA (UTC+8). You gain an hour on the ferry to Bali.
- **Getting around:** domestic flight to reach Yogyakarta, train to Probolinggo, minivan and jeep for Bromo, Ketapang-Gilimanuk ferry, private driver or scooter in Bali, fast boat to the Gilis. In Yogyakarta and Bali, Grab and Gojek are very handy. There are no motor vehicles on the Gilis.
- **Scooter or driver:** a scooter costs a few euros a day, but only ride one if you already have real experience, because traffic is dense and the roads are narrow. A private driver costs more (roughly 300,000-600,000 IDR per leg) and takes all the worry away.
- **Health:** no vaccinations required. Hepatitis A and B and typhoid are recommended, to be discussed with a travel doctor. Always drink bottled water.
- **Electricity:** 230V, type C and F sockets: Italian plugs fit.
- **Nyepi:** the Balinese New Year (March, date varies) shuts the island down for 24 hours and closes the airport. If it falls on your dates, the Bali block has to be moved.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Jakarta and flight to Yogyakarta
From Italy you usually land in Jakarta. From there a domestic flight of just under an hour and a half takes you to **Yogyakarta**, the cultural heart of Java. The most convenient base is the **Prawirotaman** area: quieter than Malioboro, but close to everything. The first day is just for arriving and, if you haven't already, booking your ticket to climb Borobudur.

### Day 2 — Sunrise at Borobudur, sunset at Prambanan
Wake up in the dark for **sunrise at Borobudur**, the largest Buddhist temple in the world. It's one of the few things that lives up to its reputation. The ticket to climb the structure is capped and sells out well in advance: buy it before you leave. In the afternoon you go to **Prambanan**, the largest Hindu complex in Indonesia. At sunset the stone spires light up with golden light.

### Day 3 — Yogyakarta: the kraton, batik and Malioboro
A day in the city. In the morning, the **kraton**, the sultan's palace, still in use. Then a **batik** workshop, to see wax and color applied by hand. In the evening you stroll along **Malioboro**, among shops and street food carts. Try the **gudeg**, the city's sweet jackfruit stew: you either like it right away or not at all. Grab and Gojek are all you need to get around.

### Day 4 — By train toward Probolinggo and Cemoro Lawang
A transfer day. The train from Yogyakarta to **Surabaya Gubeng** takes 4-5 hours. From Surabaya a second train of about 2 hours reaches **Probolinggo**, the Bromo station. Touts wait outside: they'll tell you the public minivans have already left or are full, and offer private rides at inflated prices. The public minivan to **Cemoro Lawang** costs around 35,000 IDR per person. Just take your time looking for it and don't let anyone rush you. You reach the caldera rim in the late afternoon. Eat dinner early and go to bed, because the alarm goes off in the middle of the night.

### Day 5 — Sunrise at Bromo and on to Ijen
You set off around 3 a.m. in a **4x4 jeep** for the Penanjakan viewpoint. A shared jeep costs about 700,000-900,000 IDR **per vehicle**, to be split among 4-6 people. Anyone asking double at dawn is simply taking advantage of the rush. Sunrise with Bromo smoking and Semeru behind it is spectacular. It's also crowded: hundreds of jeeps and rows of raised phones. It's still worth it. Then you go down into the **Sea of Sand** and walk up to the crater rim. Bring heavy layers, because it really is cold at altitude at night. If you still have energy, make a detour to the **Madakaripura** waterfall, about 45 minutes by car. In the afternoon you head toward **Banyuwangi** or **Licin**, the bases for Ijen.

### Day 6 — The blue fire of Ijen and the ferry to Bali
You leave around 1 a.m. The **gas mask** is mandatory, because the sulfur fumes are genuinely dangerous. Inside the crater the blue flames are smaller than they look in photos. What's huge are the **sulfur miners**, who climb the same trail carrying eighty kilos on their shoulders, barefoot or nearly so. In daylight you can see the turquoise acid lake. Once down from the volcano, you go to the port of **Ketapang**: the ferry to **Gilimanuk** takes 30-60 minutes and leaves every fifteen minutes, day and night. From Gilimanuk you drive toward northern Bali, to sleep in **Lovina** or **Munduk**. It's the longest day of the trip: you go to bed early that night.

### Day 7 — North Bali: Munduk, Lake Beratan and the Handara gate
Northern Bali is a green plateau of coffee, cacao and waterfalls. In **Munduk** you walk a few minutes through the forest to reach **Munduk Waterfall** and nearby **Melanting**. Then you head down toward **Lake Beratan**, with its temple on the water. Not far away is the **Handara gate**, the Balinese stone portal facing an avenue between lawns: I photographed it from above with the drone. If you're staying in Lovina, you can add the dawn outing for dolphins. It's a very touristy activity, with lots of boats in the same stretch of sea.

### Day 8 — The Jatiluwih rice terraces and arrival in Ubud
Morning among the **terraced rice fields of Jatiluwih**, which cover the hills for miles. From above they look like the contour lines on a map. In the afternoon you head down to **Ubud**, the base for the next few days. For this transfer and the one the day before, a private driver is worth it: the mountain roads are long and narrow.

### Day 9 — Ubud and the Tegalalang rice terraces
A day in Ubud. The **Tegalalang rice terraces** are the most famous and also the most crowded: they look best early in the morning. If you want a challenge, climb **Gunung Batur** for sunrise, leaving in the night. If you'd rather have a slower day, stay in town among temples and markets. Here, far from the concrete of the south, there are flower offerings in front of the houses, renewed every morning.

### Day 10 — Sidemen and the Gunung Agung valley
Day trip to **Sidemen**, at the foot of Gunung Agung. The terraces are less touristy than Tegalalang and the tour buses hardly ever come. You walk among the rice fields, pass through the villages where **songket** is woven with gold and silver threads, and stop at a warung with a view of the volcano. From Ubud it's 1h15-1h30 by road. With a driver, a round trip costs roughly 350,000 IDR (about 20€).

### Day 11 — Uluwatu and the Kecak dance at sunset
You go south to **Uluwatu**, all cliffs and beaches at the bottom of staircases cut into the rock: **Padang Padang**, **Bingin**, **Suluban** with the bar inside the cave. The waves are reef breaks for people who already know how to surf, but the local schools also teach beginners. In the late afternoon you climb up to the clifftop temple and at sunset there's the **Kecak dance**: seventy men sitting in a circle make all the music with their voices. It stayed with me more than any rice terrace in Bali.

### Day 12 — By fast boat to the Gilis
The fast boat to the **Gilis** leaves from Sanur or Padang Bai. The first boat in the morning is best, so you arrive with calmer seas and the whole day ahead. On the Gilis there are no cars or scooters: only **cidomo**, the horse-drawn carts, and bicycles. You can walk around an island in under an hour. The afternoon is for settling in and the first swim.

### Day 13 — Gilis: turtles, bikes and sunset
**Snorkeling with turtles** starts right from the beach, with no boat and no guide. It's the simplest and best thing about the Gilis. The three islands have different personalities. **Gili Trawangan** is more of a party place in the evening than you'd expect. **Gili Air** sits halfway between amenities and calm. **Gili Meno** is the quietest. You watch the sunset from the west coast, facing the neighboring islands.

### Day 14 — Back to Bali and departure
Fast boat back to Bali and transfer to Denpasar airport. The crossing depends on the sea, so a flight in the afternoon or evening is best. If your flight leaves early, it's better to spend the last night near the airport and leave the Gilis on day 13.

## What to see / what to do
- **Borobudur at sunrise**, with the ticket for the structure bought before you leave.
- **Prambanan at sunset**, after Borobudur on the same day.
- **Sunrise at Bromo** from Penanjakan and the walk up to the crater from the Sea of Sand.
- **The blue fire of Ijen**, only with a gas mask and a guide.
- **North Bali**: the Munduk waterfalls, Lake Beratan, the Handara gate.
- **The rice terraces**: Jatiluwih for the scale, Tegalalang for the fame, Sidemen for the calm.
- **The Kecak dance at Uluwatu** at sunset.
- **Turtles on the Gilis**, snorkeling from the beach.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Buy the Borobudur ticket right away:** access to the structure is capped.
- **Ignore the touts in Probolinggo:** the public minivan exists and costs around 35,000 IDR.
- **The Bromo jeep is paid per vehicle**, roughly 700,000-900,000 IDR, to be split.
- **Bring warm clothes** for Bromo and Ijen: it's cold at altitude at night.
- **Gas mask at Ijen**, no exceptions.
- **Scooter only if you can really ride:** otherwise a driver by the day, Grab or Gojek.
- **Skip generic southern Bali**, around Seminyak: Uluwatu has the same coast with more character.
- **Leave a buffer on the last day:** when the sea is rough, boats from the Gilis run late.
- **Complete the e-VOA and the All Indonesia Arrival Card** before departure, on the official portals.

## Chiusura personale
In two weeks this loop shows you the most famous Indonesia without the rush of a package tour. Java gives the strongest memories, the kind you earn by waking up at night. Bali is better if you see it from Munduk and Ubud rather than from the south. The Gilis are there to slow you down before the flight. With a few extra days, I'd stretch the Gilis or add a night in Sidemen.
