# Java, Bali and the Gilis in Two Weeks: Temples, Volcanoes, Rice Terraces and Islands Without Engines

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (stops done by Rick on the long trip through Indonesia; routes, times and costs taken from the 35-day itinerary, to be rechecked before you go)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 14 days, 13 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, with one domestic flight, trains, minivans, 4x4 jeeps, a ferry, a driver by the day and a fast boat
**Categoria:** Asia · Indonesia · Giava · Bali · Gili · vulcani · templi · risaie · isole

## Apertura personale
This is the stretch of Indonesia I covered in the first half of the long trip, from Yogyakarta to the Gilis. Here it's squeezed into two weeks, which is about all the time most people flying from Italy have.

If I keep going back to Indonesia, the credit goes to the people even before the places: the smiles, just like in Thailand.

Java is the part that feels least like a vacation and leaves the sharpest memory. At Ijen we climbed in the dark with gas masks, and the sulfur miners walked past us with baskets on their shoulders. Sunrise at Bromo is spectacular, even if you share it with hundreds of jeeps. I took the aerial photos on this page with the drone: Bromo at dawn, the Handara gate, the Jatiluwih rice terraces, the little islands of the Gilis.

I do Bali away from the south. I sleep inland, in Munduk and Ubud, and only head down to the coast for Uluwatu and the Kecak dance. The Gilis close the trip with three slow days, with no cars and no scooters.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** May to September for Java, when the sky at sunrise is clearest. In Bali and the Gilis the dry season runs from April to October.
- **Documents:** passport valid for at least 6 months. Apply for the visa online as an **e-VOA** at evisa.imigrasi.go.id, at least a week ahead (about 500,000 IDR, roughly 35€). Since October 2025 you also need the **All Indonesia Arrival Card**, an electronic form to fill out before every entry.
- **Currency and payments:** Indonesian rupiah (IDR), roughly 1€ = 17,000-18,000 IDR. In Yogyakarta and Bali cards work almost everywhere. At Bromo, Ijen and the Gilis it's better to have cash.
- **Time zone:** Java is on WIB (UTC+7), Bali and the Gilis on WITA (UTC+8). You gain an hour on the ferry to Bali.
- **Getting around:** domestic flight to reach Yogyakarta, train to Probolinggo, minivan and jeep for Bromo, Ketapang-Gilimanuk ferry, private driver or scooter in Bali, fast boat to the Gilis. In Yogyakarta and Bali, Grab and Gojek are very handy. There are no motor vehicles on the Gilis.
- **Scooter or driver:** a scooter costs about IDR 60,000-100,000 a day, but only ride one if you already have real experience, because traffic is dense and the roads are narrow. A private driver costs more (roughly 300,000-600,000 IDR per leg) and takes all the worry away.
- **Health:** no vaccinations required. Hepatitis A and B and typhoid are recommended, to be discussed with a travel doctor. Always drink bottled water.
- **Electricity:** 230V, type C and F sockets: Italian plugs fit.
- **Nyepi:** the Balinese New Year (March, date varies) shuts the island down for 24 hours and closes the airport. If it falls on your dates, the Bali block has to be moved.
- **From the airport to the centre:** you land at **Jakarta Soekarno-Hatta (CGK)** and connect to the domestic flight: the free Skytrain links the terminals, but with immigration you need a generous margin. **Yogyakarta (YIA)** is on the coast, about 45 km from the city: **KA Bandara train** to Tugu station, about 40 minutes, about IDR 20,000 (about 50,000 for the faster YIA Xpress), ticket at the station or in the Access by KAI or C-Access apps; **DAMRI** bus about IDR 80,000; Grab, Gojek or taxi about IDR 250,000-350,000, over an hour. **Bali (DPS)**, where the trip ends: fixed-price taxi from the official desk, about IDR 150,000-250,000 to Kuta and 300,000-450,000 to Ubud; Grab and Gojek cost less, but only from the pickup point shown in the app · ignore drivers touting at the exit.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Jakarta and flight to Yogyakarta
From Italy you usually land in Jakarta. From there a domestic flight of just under an hour and a half takes you to **Yogyakarta**, the cultural heart of Java. Book the connection with a few hours' margin, because delays on the long-haul flight and immigration queues at Soekarno-Hatta are common. Yogyakarta's airport (**YIA**) is far from the city, on the west coast: the airport train reaches the central **Tugu** station in about 40 minutes, while Grab and taxis take an hour or a little more, depending on traffic.

The most convenient base is the **Prawirotaman** area: quieter than Malioboro, but close to everything. It's full of guesthouses, small hotels with pools and cafés, and almost all the temple transfers leave from here. The first day is just for arriving and, if you haven't already, booking your ticket to climb Borobudur. At the same time, it's worth arranging a car and driver for the next day, which starts while it's still dark. *The six-hour jet lag hits hard: better an early dinner nearby and no plans for the evening.*

**In practice:** Jakarta-Yogyakarta flight about 1 hour 20 minutes, many departures a day · YIA-Tugu train about 40 minutes, tickets at the station or on the KAI Access app · car and driver for Borobudur and Prambanan to be arranged at the hotel the evening before.

### Day 2 — Sunrise at Borobudur, sunset at Prambanan
Wake up in the dark for **sunrise at Borobudur**, the largest Buddhist temple in the world. It's one of the few things that lives up to its reputation. The ticket to climb the structure is capped and sells out well in advance: buy it before you leave. The climb runs in time slots, with a local guide and a daily limit of about 1,200 people; you go up wearing the woven sandals handed out at the entrance. From Prawirotaman it's about an hour and a half by car. *Times and ticket types change often, including for the sunrise climb: check them on the official site when you buy. If you can't get a slot, watch the sunrise from Punthuk Setumbu hill, with the temple in the mist below, and visit Borobudur right after.*

Around midday you head back toward Yogyakarta for a break in the hottest hours. In the afternoon you go to **Prambanan**, the largest Hindu complex in Indonesia, about twenty kilometers east of the city. Arrive at least an hour and a half before closing, to walk among the temples of Shiva, Vishnu and Brahma and then move on to the nearby **Sewu** temple, in the same park. At sunset the stone spires light up with golden light.

**In practice:** Borobudur and Prambanan tickets on the official portal ticket.borobudurpark.com, a few hundred thousand rupiah each for foreigners (Prambanan about 25 dollars) · on Mondays the temple areas at Borobudur and Prambanan may be closed for maintenance: avoid them · shoulders and legs covered, a sarong is given at the entrance.

### Day 3 — Yogyakarta: the kraton, batik and Malioboro
A day in the city. In the morning, the **kraton**, the sultan's palace, still in use. Go early, because it closes in the early afternoon, and in the mornings there are often gamelan and dance performances in the pavilions. A few minutes' walk away is **Taman Sari**, the court's old water garden, with its pools and underground passages. Then a **batik** workshop, to see wax and color applied by hand. *Around the kraton, touts talk about a "students' batik exhibition, today only": it's the classic way of steering tourists into a shop with inflated prices.*

The afternoon is free for resting or for a walk among the silver workshops of **Kotagede**, the oldest district in the city. In the evening you stroll along **Malioboro**, among shops and street food carts. Try the **gudeg**, the city's sweet jackfruit stew: you either like it right away or not at all. Grab and Gojek are all you need to get around. Before bed, pack for two days in the mountains: tomorrow you leave early and arrive in Cemoro Lawang in the cold.

**In practice:** kraton open in the morning until about mid-afternoon, closed on Mondays and during court ceremonies, foreigner entry about 25,000 IDR · Taman Sari separate, about 25,000 IDR for foreigners · tickets for tomorrow's trains on the KAI Access app or at the station.

### Day 4 — By train toward Probolinggo and Cemoro Lawang
A transfer day. The train from Yogyakarta to **Surabaya Gubeng** takes 4-5 hours. The first morning departure is best, leaving margin for the connection and for reaching the mountains in daylight. From Surabaya a second train of about 2 hours reaches **Probolinggo**, the Bromo station. Touts wait outside: they'll tell you the public minivans have already left or are full, and offer private rides at inflated prices. The public minivan to **Cemoro Lawang** costs around 35,000 IDR per person. Just take your time looking for it and don't let anyone rush you.

*The public minivan leaves from the Bayuangga terminal only when it's full: on quiet days the wait can be long, and a group can offer to pay for the empty seats.* The ride up takes about an hour and a half, on a road climbing through potato and cabbage fields. You reach the caldera rim in the late afternoon. Accommodation in Cemoro Lawang is basic, often with hot water only at certain times, and at night the temperature drops below ten degrees. In the evening you book the jeep for sunrise, through your guesthouse or with the village drivers. Eat dinner early and go to bed, because the alarm goes off in the middle of the night.

**In practice:** book trains a few days ahead during holidays · Probolinggo-Cemoro Lawang minivan about 1 hour 30 minutes · few places to stay in Cemoro Lawang and prices above average: book ahead · Bromo park entry paid separately (see next day).

### Day 5 — Sunrise at Bromo and on to Ijen
You set off around 3 a.m. in a **4x4 jeep** for the Penanjakan viewpoint. A shared jeep costs about 700,000-900,000 IDR **per vehicle**, to be split among 4-6 people. Anyone asking double at dawn is simply taking advantage of the rush. Sunrise with Bromo smoking and Semeru behind it is spectacular. It's also crowded: hundreds of jeeps and rows of raised phones. It's still worth it. Then you go down into the **Sea of Sand** and walk up to the crater rim. Bring heavy layers, because it really is cold at altitude at night.

The last stretch to the crater is a staircase of about 250 steps, often covered in sand; from the bottom you can ride up on horseback, but agree the price first. You're back in Cemoro Lawang for breakfast around 8-9. If you still have energy, make a detour to the **Madakaripura** waterfall, about 45 minutes by car: you walk along the river and get wet, so bring sandals and a pouch for your phone. In the afternoon you head toward **Banyuwangi** or **Licin**, the bases for Ijen. The simplest transfer is a private car, booked in Cemoro Lawang.

**In practice:** Bromo Tengger Semeru park entry for foreigners about 255,000 IDR, booked online · *in 2026 the park was closed for weeks after a fire: check on the official site that it's open before you go* · Cemoro Lawang-Banyuwangi by car about 5-6 hours.

### Day 6 — The blue fire of Ijen and the ferry to Bali
You leave around 1 a.m. The **gas mask** is mandatory, because the sulfur fumes are genuinely dangerous. From the **Paltuding** car park to the crater rim it's about 3 kilometers of steady climbing, an hour and a half to two hours on foot, then a steep descent over rocks to the bottom. Inside the crater the blue flames are smaller than they look in photos. What's huge are the **sulfur miners**, who climb the same trail carrying eighty kilos on their shoulders, barefoot or nearly so. In daylight you can see the turquoise acid lake.

*The wind changes direction suddenly and pushes the fumes onto the trail: anyone with asthma or breathing problems shouldn't go down into the crater.* Once down from the volcano, around 8-9 a.m., you go to the port of **Ketapang**: the ferry to **Gilimanuk** takes 30-60 minutes and leaves every fifteen minutes, day and night. From Gilimanuk you drive toward northern Bali, to sleep in **Lovina** or **Munduk**. It's the longest day of the trip: you go to bed early that night.

**In practice:** Ijen gate open from 2 a.m. to noon, foreigner entry about 100,000-150,000 IDR, online ticket and medical check at Paltuding · closures for bad weather, volcanic activity and maintenance days, check beforehand · on entering Bali you pay the 150,000 IDR tourist levy, best done online on Love Bali · Gilimanuk-Lovina about 2-2.5 hours by road.

### Day 7 — North Bali: Munduk, Lake Beratan and the Handara gate
Northern Bali is a green plateau of coffee, cacao and waterfalls. In **Munduk** you walk a few minutes through the forest to reach **Munduk Waterfall** and nearby **Melanting**. The trails are damp and slippery, so you need shoes with some grip. Start in the morning, because on the plateau the clouds roll in almost every day after lunch and the light fades early.

Then you head down toward **Lake Beratan**, with its temple on the water, **Pura Ulun Danu Beratan**, quieter in the early hours. Not far away is the **Handara gate**, the Balinese stone portal facing an avenue between lawns: I photographed it from above with the drone. At the resort entrance there's usually a small fee for photos, and in the middle of the day there's a queue. If you're staying in Lovina, you can add the dawn outing for dolphins. It's a very touristy activity, with lots of boats in the same stretch of sea. *Evenings in Munduk are cool: a light sweater comes in handy here too.*

**In practice:** Munduk waterfalls with small entry tickets, a few euros in all · Ulun Danu Beratan with a foreigner ticket of about 100,000 IDR (from July 2026), cash or card at the entrance · day driver to be arranged at the hotel · in Munduk you stay in guesthouses and small lodges overlooking the valley.

### Day 8 — The Jatiluwih rice terraces and arrival in Ubud
Morning among the **terraced rice fields of Jatiluwih**, which cover the hills for miles. From above they look like the contour lines on a map. They're part of the **subak** landscape, the irrigation system shared between villages that UNESCO has listed as World Heritage. There are marked walking trails of different lengths, from half an hour to a few hours: the shortest is enough to get the idea, but the best views come once you leave the main road and the restaurants with terraces behind.

From Munduk it takes about an hour and a half. *The color changes a lot with the rice cycle: bright green in the growing weeks, gold near harvest, mud and water afterwards. No month is guaranteed.* In the afternoon you head down to **Ubud**, the base for the next few days. For this transfer and the one the day before, a private driver is worth it: the mountain roads are long and narrow. On the way you can add a stop at the **Batukaru** temple, on the slopes of the volcano of the same name.

**In practice:** Jatiluwih entry for foreigners about 75,000 IDR · Jatiluwih-Ubud about 1.5-2 hours depending on traffic · in Ubud it's better to stay off the main road, in the green areas around the center, which are quieter at night.

### Day 9 — Ubud and the Tegalalang rice terraces
A day in Ubud. The **Tegalalang rice terraces** are the most famous and also the most crowded: they look best early in the morning. They're about twenty minutes by road north of the center; before 8 a.m. the light is soft and the groups haven't arrived yet. Along the paths between the terraces, farmers sometimes ask for a small donation to pass, and there are paid swings and photo frames.

If you want a challenge, climb **Gunung Batur** for sunrise, leaving in the night. The climb takes about two hours and a local guide is effectively mandatory. If you'd rather have a slower day, stay in town among temples and markets: Ubud's **royal palace**, the **Saraswati temple** with its water lilies and the **Monkey Forest**. Here, far from the concrete of the south, there are flower offerings in front of the houses, renewed every morning. *In the Monkey Forest the macaques steal glasses, hats and food: go in with your bag closed and nothing in your hands.* In the evening there are Balinese dance shows almost every day at the palace and in the temples in the center.

**In practice:** Batur trek with hotel pickup around 2-3 a.m., permit about 100,000 IDR per person plus the guide · Tegalalang with a small entry ticket · tickets for the evening dances sold at the entrance in the afternoon.

### Day 10 — Sidemen and the Gunung Agung valley
Day trip to **Sidemen**, at the foot of Gunung Agung. The terraces are less touristy than Tegalalang and the tour buses hardly ever come. You walk among the rice fields, pass through the villages where **songket** is woven with gold and silver threads, and stop at a warung with a view of the volcano. Gunung Agung, Bali's highest and most sacred volcano, is clearest in the morning, before the clouds cover it.

From Ubud it's 1h15-1h30 by road. With a driver, a round trip costs roughly 350,000 IDR (about 20€). On the way back you can have the driver stop at **Tirta Gangga**, the water garden of the old king of Karangasem, or at **Klungkung**, with its hall of justice and painted ceilings. *If you want to walk the Sidemen rice fields, it's worth taking a village guide for a couple of hours: the paths aren't marked and cross private land.* Sidemen is also a quiet place to spend a night, if the schedule allows.

**In practice:** driver from Ubud about 350,000 IDR round trip, more if you keep him all day · Tirta Gangga and Klungkung with small entry tickets · temples require a sarong and sash, often lent at the entrance.

### Day 11 — Uluwatu and the Kecak dance at sunset
You go south to **Uluwatu**, all cliffs and beaches at the bottom of staircases cut into the rock: **Padang Padang**, **Bingin**, **Suluban** with the bar inside the cave. The waves are reef breaks for people who already know how to surf, but the local schools also teach beginners. From Ubud the drive takes an hour and a half to two hours, much longer at rush hour: leave in the morning and spend the day on the beach, where low tide uncovers the reef and leaves more room on the sand.

In the late afternoon you climb up to the clifftop temple and at sunset there's the **Kecak dance**: seventy men sitting in a circle make all the music with their voices. It stayed with me more than any rice terrace in Bali. Be at the temple around 5 p.m. to get a seat on the stands with the sea behind the dancers. *The monkeys at Uluwatu temple are known for stealing glasses, hats and phones: keep them in your bag.* For the night, stay in Uluwatu or Sanur, depending on which port the next day's boat leaves from.

**In practice:** Kecak show around 6 p.m., about 150,000 IDR, temple entry separate · limited seats in peak months, better to buy ahead · Uluwatu-Padang Bai about 2 hours by road, Uluwatu-Sanur about 45 minutes without traffic.

### Day 12 — By fast boat to the Gilis
The fast boat to the **Gilis** leaves from Sanur or Padang Bai. The first boat in the morning is best, so you arrive with calmer seas and the whole day ahead. From Padang Bai the crossing is the shortest, about an hour and a half to two hours; from Sanur and Serangan it usually takes a couple of hours more. Almost all companies include the hotel transfer, and you land in shallow water: keep your bag up high and your phone in a pouch.

On the Gilis there are no cars or scooters: only **cidomo**, the horse-drawn carts, and bicycles. Air and Meno are quick to walk around; Trawangan is the largest and you go round it by bike, in under an hour. The afternoon is for settling in and the first swim. *ATMs on the Gilis are few and sometimes empty: arrive with cash for the first few days.*

**In practice:** Padang Bai-Gili fast boat about 350,000-475,000 IDR one way, more from Sanur · buy the ticket directly from the company or from well-known platforms, not from sellers at the port · in rough seas boats are cancelled or delayed · you hop between the three islands on the public boat, a few minutes and a few euros.

### Day 13 — Gilis: turtles, bikes and sunset
**Snorkeling with turtles** starts right from the beach, with no boat and no guide. It's the simplest and best thing about the Gilis. The turtles are usually found over the seagrass close to shore, on the east side of Trawangan and around Gili Meno; masks and fins can be rented everywhere for a few euros. Currents between the islands are strong: stay close to the shore and don't try to swim across.

The three islands have different personalities. **Gili Trawangan** is more of a party place in the evening than you'd expect. **Gili Air** sits halfway between amenities and calm. **Gili Meno** is the quietest. With the morning public boat you can spend a day on a different island from the one you're sleeping on and come back in the afternoon. You watch the sunset from the west coast, facing the neighboring islands. *The Gilis are mostly Muslim: away from the beach, cover up a little and, during Ramadan, keep an eye on opening hours.*

**In practice:** bike rental about 50,000 IDR a day, mask and fins a few euros · shared snorkeling boat trips around the three islands, bookable the evening before · cheaper places to stay on Gili Air and Trawangan, quieter but with less choice on Meno.

### Day 14 — Back to Bali and departure
Fast boat back to Bali and transfer to Denpasar airport. The crossing depends on the sea, so a flight in the afternoon or evening is best. From Padang Bai to the airport it's about an hour and a half by road, often more in the traffic of the south; many boat companies include the transfer to the airport or the Kuta area.

If your flight leaves early, it's better to spend the last night near the airport and leave the Gilis on day 13. *For a long-haul flight you need to be at the airport at least three hours ahead: with the morning boat and the transfer, a flight before 5 p.m. is a risk.* If you have a few spare hours, stop in **Sanur** for lunch on the seafront before check-in.

**In practice:** Gili-Padang Bai boat about 1.5-2 hours, then a car to the airport · large luggage: check the company's limits and surcharge · flights to Europe with one stop, usually in Singapore, Doha, Dubai or Istanbul.

## What to see / what to do
- **Borobudur at sunrise**, with the ticket for the structure bought before you leave.
- **Prambanan at sunset**, after Borobudur on the same day.
- **Sunrise at Bromo** from Penanjakan and the walk up to the crater from the Sea of Sand.
- **The blue fire of Ijen**, only with a gas mask and a guide.
- **North Bali**: the Munduk waterfalls, Lake Beratan, the Handara gate.
- **The rice terraces**: Jatiluwih for the scale, Tegalalang for the fame, Sidemen for the calm.
- **The Kecak dance at Uluwatu** at sunset.
- **Turtles on the Gilis**, snorkeling from the beach.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Buy the Borobudur ticket right away:** access to the structure is capped.
- **Ignore the touts in Probolinggo:** the public minivan exists and costs around 35,000 IDR.
- **The Bromo jeep is paid per vehicle**, roughly 700,000-900,000 IDR, to be split.
- **Bring warm clothes** for Bromo and Ijen: it's cold at altitude at night.
- **Gas mask at Ijen**, no exceptions.
- **Scooter only if you can really ride:** otherwise a driver by the day, Grab or Gojek.
- **Skip generic southern Bali**, around Seminyak: Uluwatu has the same coast with more character.
- **Leave a buffer on the last day:** when the sea is rough, boats from the Gilis run late.
- **Complete the e-VOA and the All Indonesia Arrival Card** before departure, on the official portals.

## Chiusura personale
Java is the part that left me the sharpest memory: Ijen climbed in the dark with a gas mask, with the miners walking past us carrying baskets of sulphur, and sunrise on Bromo, even if shared with hundreds of jeeps. Of Bali, more than any rice terrace, what stuck with me is the Kecak dance at Uluwatu. The Gilis close with three slow days, no cars or scooters.

I keep going back to Indonesia above all for the people: the smiles, like in Thailand.
