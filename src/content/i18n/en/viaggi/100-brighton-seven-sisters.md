# Brighton, the Seven Sisters and the Thames at Kingston and Richmond: Three Days South of London

## Apertura personale
*My memory: Brighton and the Seven Sisters are doable as a day trip or a weekend from London. Brighton is really beautiful, but at the weekend it turns into a sort of open-air stag party, with lots of booze and bar hopping, so people really go for it. Still, it's very beautiful. The Seven Sisters, on the other hand, are nature: if you get a good day they're incredible, while if it rains or it's windy it might not be so pleasant. And then there's Kingston upon Thames, where I studied, which has a beautiful park, and Richmond Park.*

The first day stays close to London, along the Thames south-west of the city: **Kingston upon Thames**, with its riverside and bridge, then **Richmond Park**, the largest of the Royal Parks, with free-roaming deer, and the view from **Richmond Hill**. You go back to sleep in London.

The second day is in **Brighton**, the seaside city an hour by train from London: the Palace Pier, the Indian-style Royal Pavilion, the alleys of the Lanes and the shops of North Laine. In the evening you stay in town, or go back to London.

On the third day you head east along the coast to the **Seven Sisters**, the white chalk cliffs that drop sheer into the Channel, inside the South Downs National Park. You walk on the grass along the clifftops, from Cuckmere Haven to Birling Gap, and the bus takes you back to Brighton for the train.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** May to September, with long days. For the Seven Sisters the weather matters more than the month: pick a day with no rain and little wind.
- **Documents:** the United Kingdom is not in the European Union. Italian citizens need an **ETA**, which you apply for on gov.uk or with the official UK ETA app and which costs £20 (2026), and a **passport**.
- **Currency and payments:** pound sterling. Cards and contactless almost everywhere, buses included. The Royal Pavilion and Preston Manor only take cards.
- **Trains to Kingston and Richmond:** South Western Railway from Waterloo; Richmond is also the end of the District line and the London Overground. Kingston is in zone 6 and Richmond in zone 4: you pay with a contactless card, as in the rest of London. The 65 and 371 buses run between the two towns.
- **Trains to Brighton:** Southern from Victoria and Thameslink from London Bridge, Blackfriars and St Pancras, in about an hour. Seats aren't reserved. Off-Peak tickets save up to a third; with a dated train ticket the Royal Pavilion has a two-for-one offer.
- **Buses to the Seven Sisters:** from Brighton, Brighton & Hove's 12 and 12X buses stop outside the Seven Sisters Country Park visitor centre at Exceat; the 12X runs up to every 12 minutes. On Sundays and bank holidays the 13X calls at Birling Gap and Beachy Head every 15 minutes.
- **Driving:** you don't need a car. If you do use one, there are paid car parks at Seven Sisters Country Park and Birling Gap. You drive on the left.
- **Health:** the EHIC covers necessary treatment in the public NHS service; travel insurance is still recommended.
- **Sockets:** type G, with three rectangular pins. You need an adapter.
- **Weather:** on the coast the wind changes everything. A windproof jacket and walking boots for the cliffs.
- **From the airport to the centre:** **Gatwick (LGW)** is the closest: Thameslink and Southern trains reach Brighton in about half an hour, without going through London · for London, the **Gatwick Express** reaches Victoria in 31 minutes, with 10% off if you buy online or with the app; Southern and Thameslink cost less and take only a little longer · from **Heathrow** you go into central London on the Elizabeth line or the Piccadilly line and set off again from Victoria or London Bridge · the mistake to avoid is going through London when you land at Gatwick: Brighton is closer to the airport than central London is.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — The Thames south-west of London: Kingston, Richmond Park and Richmond Hill
From Waterloo the South Western Railway train reaches **Kingston upon Thames** in about half an hour. You walk down to the river, to **Kingston Bridge**, and along the Thames, past the boats and the riverside pubs. In the centre, outside the Guildhall, is the **Coronation Stone**, where tradition has it that seven Anglo-Saxon kings were crowned, and in Old London Road the red phone boxes toppled against each other in **Out of Order**, the sculpture by David Mach.

Then you enter **Richmond Park** by Kingston Gate. The park covers about 1,000 hectares and is a nature reserve: around 630 red and fallow deer live here, roaming free. You cross it northwards via the **Isabella Plantation**, the woodland garden with its azaleas, and reach **Pembroke Lodge**, with its café. From **King Henry's Mound**, in the garden, a gap in the trees frames the dome of St Paul's, more than fifteen kilometres away. You leave by Richmond Gate, at the top of **Richmond Hill**: the view of the Thames from here is protected by an Act of Parliament of 1902. You walk down through the **Terrace Gardens** to the river and **Richmond Bridge**, and the train takes you back to Waterloo. *Keep at least 50 metres from the deer, and don't feed them: from September to November, in the rutting season, the stags can charge.*

**In practice:** Waterloo-Kingston about 30 minutes, Richmond-Waterloo under 20 · Richmond Park free; the pedestrian gates are usually open day and night, but during the deer cull, in November and February, they close at night · the 371 bus stops at Richmond Gate · night in London.

### Day 2 — From London to Brighton: the Pier, the Royal Pavilion and the Lanes
The train from Victoria or London Bridge reaches Brighton in about an hour. From the station to the sea it's about fifteen minutes on foot, downhill, through **North Laine**, the district of independent shops, cafés and little markets, with its low, colourful houses.

The **Royal Pavilion** is King George IV's palace, with domes and minarets outside and Chinese-style interiors. You visit with a ticket, and the gardens around it are free. Then the **Lanes**, the narrow alleys of the old town, with their jewellers and pubs, and the seafront as far as the **Brighton Palace Pier**, with its rides and amusement arcades. To the west are the remains of the burnt-out **West Pier** and the **Brighton i360** tower, with its pod that rises to 138 metres. *On Friday and Saturday nights the centre fills up with stag and hen parties: if you want a quiet evening, have dinner towards Kemp Town or Hove.*

**In practice:** Royal Pavilion £21.50, 5% less online, open 10 am-5:15 pm from October to March and 9:30 am-5:45 pm from April to September · Palace Pier free, except £1 at peak times from March to October · Brighton i360 from about £18.50 online, a flight of about 25 minutes.

### Day 3 — The Seven Sisters: from Cuckmere Haven to Birling Gap
In the morning you take the 12 or 12X bus to **Seven Sisters Country Park**, at Exceat, where the visitor centre has a café and toilets. From here a flat path follows the meanders of the River Cuckmere down to the sea at **Cuckmere Haven**, the shingle beach from which you can see the cliffs. The most famous view, with the coastguard cottages in front of the Seven Sisters, is on the other side of the river, on the **Seaford Head** headland.

Then you climb onto the cliffs and walk east, up and down the seven "sisters", on the grass on top of the chalk. It's about 6 kilometres to **Birling Gap**, with the National Trust café and a staircase of 50 steps down to the beach. If you still have the legs, carry on to the **Belle Tout** lighthouse and **Beachy Head**. From the village of East Dean, a couple of kilometres from Birling Gap, the bus takes you back towards Brighton or Eastbourne, and from there the train goes to London. *The cliffs retreat by up to a metre a year and can collapse without warning: walk well back from the edge, even for a photo, and don't stop below the cliff face.*

**In practice:** Seven Sisters Country Park, parking from £3.50 for two hours, visitor centre 10 am-4 pm in winter · Birling Gap with a National Trust car park, café and toilets 10 am-4 pm · on Sundays and bank holidays the 13X bus calls at Birling Gap · walking boots and a windproof jacket.

## As a day trip from London
Kingston and Richmond Park make a half-day or full-day trip from London, by train from Waterloo or on the District line. Brighton and the Seven Sisters can be done in a single day, but you have to choose. If you want the city, take an early train to Brighton, see the Royal Pavilion, the Lanes and the Pier, and come back in the evening. If you want nature, go straight to the Seven Sisters: by train to **Seaford**, then on foot to Seven Sisters Country Park, in about an hour, or by the 12 bus. Then the cliffs to Birling Gap and the bus to Eastbourne, where the trains leave for London Victoria. The full route from Seaford to Eastbourne is about 22 kilometres, up and down the cliffs: only do it if you're fit and have a long day.

## How it changes with more or fewer days
- **One day:** just Kingston and Richmond Park, just Brighton, or just the Seven Sisters, as above.
- **Two days:** Brighton and the Seven Sisters, with a night in Brighton, without the first day on the Thames.
- **More time on the Thames:** **Hampton Court Palace**, Henry VIII's palace, is a few minutes from Kingston on the 111 and 216 buses, next to **Bushy Park**. North of Richmond are **Ham House** and the gardens at **Kew**.
- **Four days:** add Hove, with its colourful beach huts on the seafront, **Devil's Dyke** in the South Downs hills, reached in summer on the 77 bus, and the village of **Alfriston**, in the Cuckmere valley.
- **With London:** it combines with the itinerary [*London and the Cotswolds*](/viaggi/londra-cotswolds), before or after.
- **With the north:** if you also want Manchester and Liverpool, there's the itinerary [*Manchester and Liverpool in a long weekend*](/viaggi/manchester-liverpool-premier-league).

## What to see / what to do
- **Kingston and Richmond:** the Kingston riverside, the Coronation Stone, Richmond Park, King Henry's Mound and the view from Richmond Hill, on the [Kingston and Richmond Park page](/destinazioni/inghilterra/kingston-richmond-park).
- **Brighton:** the Royal Pavilion, the Lanes, North Laine, the Palace Pier, the West Pier, the Brighton i360, Kemp Town and Hove. All on the [Brighton page](/destinazioni/inghilterra/brighton).
- **Seven Sisters:** Seven Sisters Country Park, Cuckmere Haven, Seaford Head, Birling Gap, Belle Tout and Beachy Head, on the [Seven Sisters page](/destinazioni/inghilterra/seven-sisters).

## Where to stay
- **The first day:** you sleep in London; if you want to stay on the river, there are hotels in Kingston and Richmond.
- **Brighton, the centre and the Lanes:** everything on foot, the sea a stone's throw away. On Fridays and Saturdays it's the noisiest area.
- **Kemp Town:** east of the Pier, quieter, with Georgian houses and the LGBTQ+ bars of St James's Street.
- **Hove:** to the west, residential, with a calmer seafront. Good if you want an early night.
- **Near the station and North Laine:** handy if you arrive late from London or leave early.

## Where to eat
- **On the Thames:** the riverside pubs in Kingston, near the bridge, and around Richmond Bridge; in Richmond Park, the cafés at Pembroke Lodge and the kiosks at Pen Ponds and Kingston Gate.
- **Fish and chips on the seafront**, eaten sitting on the shingle.
- **North Laine and the Lanes** for cafés, brunch and vegetarian food, very popular in Brighton.
- **The pub after the cliffs:** around East Dean and Alfriston there are country pubs; at Birling Gap there's the National Trust café.
- **On weekend evenings** the places in the centre are packed: book dinner.

## Budget
- **ETA:** £20 per person (2026).
- **Kingston and Richmond Park:** free; you only pay for the train or the Tube, with a contactless card.
- **Entry fees in Brighton:** Royal Pavilion £21.50, Brighton Museum £10.50, Brighton i360 from about £18.50 online. The Palace Pier is free almost all the time.
- **Seven Sisters:** the path is free. Car parks cost from £3.50 to £7 at Seven Sisters Country Park and from £2 to £8 at Birling Gap.
- **Train and accommodation:** prices vary by day and time, with no verified figure to put here. Off-Peak tickets cost up to a third less.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Choose your Seven Sisters day by the forecast**, not the calendar. In rain and strong wind, a museum day in Brighton is better.
- **Keep away from the deer in Richmond Park**, at least 50 metres, especially in autumn.
- **Keep away from the cliff edge.** The chalk collapses without warning.
- **Buy Off-Peak tickets** and travel after the morning rush hour.
- **Weekend nights in Brighton are party nights.** If you want calm, pick a Sunday or a weekday.
- **Check the 13X bus times**: it only calls at Birling Gap on certain days.
- **Bring water and something to eat**: there's nothing between Exceat and Birling Gap.
