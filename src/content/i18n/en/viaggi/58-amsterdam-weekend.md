## Apertura personale
Amsterdam is a city best read from the handlebars of a bicycle rather than the window of a taxi: more bicycles than inhabitants, bike lanes with real priority over cars, and a historic center (the Grachtengordel canal ring, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2010) designed in the seventeenth century for the water and left almost intact since.

It's also a city weighed on, in different ways, by two opposite weights: on one side the Anne Frank House, which every year reminds millions of visitors of one of the hardest pages of twentieth-century European history; on the other the Red Light District, which should be walked through with the same respect as any lived-in neighborhood, not photographed as an attraction.

Four days are enough to do the things that matter well: the canal ring and Jordaan, the two great museums (Rijksmuseum and Van Gogh Museum), the Anne Frank House, and time to get around the way Amsterdammers do, by bicycle.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **April-May** (tulips, mild weather) and **September-October**, with fewer crowds than in high summer. **June-August** is high season: it's hotter, by Dutch standards, and the canals and main museums are very crowded. **November-March** is cold, often rainy and windy, but lines are shorter and in December there are Christmas markets.
- **Currency:** **euro**, as in Italy: no need to exchange.
- **Cash almost useless:** the Netherlands is among the most advanced countries in the world on digital payments. Many shops, bars and restaurants accept **only card or contactless**, and some don't take cash at all. It's the opposite surprise from the most common one elsewhere in Europe: here, rather than carrying a lot of cash, it's better to make sure your card works well.
- **Anne Frank House, booking:** tickets **exclusively online** at **annefrank.org**, released weeks in advance (usually the first Tuesday of the month for the following two months) and sold out quickly, especially on weekends. A small last-minute quota is released online every day, but it's not a reliable plan for a short weekend.
- **Bicycles, the unwritten rules:** bike paths (red asphalt, distinct from the sidewalk) have **real priority**, not just on paper. You don't walk or stop distractedly on a bike path: it's the most common cause of accidents and arguments with tourists. On a bike you keep to the right, signal with your arm before turning, and never go the wrong way.
- **Red Light District:** a red-light area regulated for centuries in the medieval heart of the city, not an attraction. **Photographing the windows or the people who work there is prohibited** in much of the area; where it isn't prohibited, it remains a matter of basic respect.
- **Getting around the city:** center on foot or by bike; GVB trams, buses and metro for the rest, payable also by contactless card directly on board. You don't need a car: in the center parking is expensive and scarce.
- **From the airport to the centre:** from **Schiphol (AMS)** the NS train reaches Central Station in 15-20 minutes, about €6-7, with trains every few minutes and one an hour even at night; pay with a contactless card at the gates (check in and out with the same card) or with a ticket from the machines · bus 397 Amsterdam Airport Express goes to Museumplein and Leidseplein in about 30 minutes, about €6.50 · taxi from the official rank, about €50-60 to the centre · Uber and Bolt work from the pick-up area · the typical mistake is forgetting to check out at the gates: you get charged the maximum fare.
- **Food:** **bitterballen** (fried stuffed meatballs) with a beer; **haring** (marinated herring) from the street stalls; warm **stroopwafel** from the market stalls; **rijsttafel**, a legacy of the Dutch colonization of Indonesia, now part of the city's cuisine.
- **Museums, booking:** the Rijksmuseum and the Van Gogh Museum sell timed-slot tickets online: in high season it's best to book them days ahead to avoid the longest lines.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Grachtengordel and Jordaan
We spent the first day on the ring of canals, the **Grachtengordel**, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2010: **Herengracht**, **Keizersgracht** and **Prinsengracht**, dug from the seventeenth century onwards during the Dutch Golden Age, with their typical tall, narrow terraced houses and their **gables** (decorated façades) that once signalled the owner's trade or wealth. It is best walked slowly, passing through the **Negen Straatjes**, the "nine little streets" that cut across the three main canals with independent shops and cafés, and looking up: many houses still have the beam with a hook under the gable, used to hoist furniture and goods through the windows because the staircases inside are too narrow. *Watch out for the bike lanes, usually red: cross them looking both ways, because bikes come fast and don't slow down.*

Then we walked to the **Jordaan**, the former working-class district west of the center, now one of the most lived-in parts of the city: narrow streets, the **hofjes** (small inner courtyards that were once almshouses for elderly people and widows) and a more residential feel than the historic center. On Monday mornings there is a flea market on the **Noordermarkt** square, and on Saturdays an organic produce market: if you happen to be there on the right day, stop by before lunch. In the late afternoon we took a **canal cruise** (see the dedicated experience page) to see the Grachtengordel ring from the water, with the best light towards sunset.

**In practice:** the center is entirely walkable, Centraal station to the Jordaan takes about 20 minutes · canal cruise of about an hour, book online for the sunset slot · the handiest areas to sleep in are the Grachtengordel and the Jordaan, expensive and in demand: book well ahead.

### Day 2 — Anne Frank House and Dam Square
In the morning we went to the **Anne Frank House (Anne Frank Huis)**, on the Prinsengracht: the building where the Frank family and four other refugees hid from July 1942 to August 1944, before being discovered and deported. The route goes through the **Achterhuis**, the secret annex behind the revolving bookcase, left largely bare at Otto Frank's wish. We had booked our tickets weeks in advance: there's no alternative. Tickets are sold only on the official website and never at the door: they are released every Tuesday for the week that falls six weeks later, and the best slots go within hours. A morning slot leaves the rest of the day free.

In the afternoon we passed through **Dam Square**, the central square with the Royal Palace and the National Monument, and walked to the **Red Light District (De Wallen)**, in the medieval heart of the city: you walk through it like any other neighborhood, without photographing windows or people. On the way it's worth a detour to the **Begijnhof**, the beguines' courtyard hidden behind a door on the Spui, one of the quietest places in the center, where you go in quietly because the houses are still lived in. *Since 2023 smoking cannabis on the street has been banned in the Red Light District, and the fines are real.* We had dinner in one of the many places in the center, with no plan.

**In practice:** Anne Frank House about €16.50, online only at annefrank.org · the Royal Palace can be visited when it isn't in use for ceremonies, with a separate ticket · De Wallen is calmer in the afternoon than late in the evening, when it fills up with groups.

### Day 3 — Rijksmuseum and Van Gogh Museum
We spent the day at the city's two great museums, both facing the **Museumplein**. In the morning we were at the **Rijksmuseum**, the national museum with the world's largest collection of Dutch art and history, from Rembrandt's *Night Watch* onwards. It's huge, and you can't see it all in one morning: start from the **Gallery of Honor**, with Rembrandt, Vermeer and the great seventeenth-century painters, then choose rooms according to your interests, from ship models to dolls' houses. Tickets are timed and sold online; the first morning slot is the least crowded.

The afternoon was for the **Van Gogh Museum**, the world's largest collection of works by Vincent van Gogh, arranged largely in chronological order through the different phases of his short career. Booking matters even more here: afternoon slots often sell out days ahead, and no tickets are sold at the door. Between the two museums, the Museumplein lawn and the nearby **Vondelpark** are the place for a break. The evening ends with a quiet walk along the canals of the center, or in one of the bars (*bruine kroegen*, the traditional "brown cafés" in dark wood) around the Jordaan.

**In practice:** Rijksmuseum and Van Gogh Museum about €25 each, online only with a set entry time · under-18s get in free but still need to book a ticket · from the center to the Museumplein it's a 20-25 minute walk, or take the tram.

### Day 4 — By Bicycle Around the City
On the last day we got around the way Amsterdammers do: we **rented a bike** (see the dedicated experience page) and rode freely around the city, from the canals of the center to the more residential neighborhoods, with time to stop wherever we liked instead of following a fixed route. Many Dutch bikes brake only by pedalling backwards: if you're not used to it, ask the rental shop for a model with hand brakes. *A few rules matter: stay in the bike lane, signal turns with your arm, keep away from tram tracks, where the wheel gets stuck, and always lock the bike to something fixed.*

One possible loop starts at the **Vondelpark**, goes through the **De Pijp** neighborhood and the **Albert Cuyp** street market, and heads back towards Centraal station. Behind the station, free ferries leave for **Amsterdam-Noord**, and they take bikes too: across the IJ are the A'DAM tower and the EYE film museum, with a view of the center from the water. In the early afternoon, before heading home, one last walk around the center for final shopping.

**In practice:** day bike rental usually under €20, often with a deposit or ID · ferries to Noord free and frequent, a few minutes' crossing · Albert Cuyp market closed on Sundays · from Centraal to Schiphol airport about 15-20 minutes by train.

## What to see / what to do
- **The Grachtengordel**, the seventeenth-century canal ring and UNESCO site.
- **Jordaan**, among hofjes and narrow streets.
- **The Anne Frank House**, with a ticket booked well in advance.
- **The Rijksmuseum and the Van Gogh Museum**, on the Museumplein.
- **A canal cruise**, best at sunset.
- **A bike ride around the city**, to get around like Amsterdammers.
- **The Red Light District**, walked through with respect, without taking photos.

## Possible extensions
- **Zaanse Schans (+half a day):** an open-air village-museum about 20 minutes by train, with the traditional windmills still working on the Zaan river.
- **Rotterdam (+1 day):** about 40 minutes by high-speed train, for the contemporary architecture that contrasts with Amsterdam's historic center, rebuilt almost from scratch after the bombings of 1940.
- **Keukenhof (+half a day, March-May only):** the large tulip park near Lisse, open only a few weeks a year in spring.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
The **Grachtengordel** is the most central and characteristic area, convenient on foot for almost everything. **Jordaan**, just to the west, is the more residential alternative and often a bit cheaper, ten minutes' walk from the heart of the historic center.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
**Bitterballen** with a Dutch beer in the *bruine kroegen* of Jordaan; **haring** from the street stalls around the canals; warm **stroopwafel** from the market stalls; a **rijsttafel** in one of the Indonesian restaurants in the center, a direct legacy of the Dutch colonial past.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book the Anne Frank House online weeks in advance**: you can no longer buy on site.
- **Don't walk or stop on the bike paths** (red asphalt): it's the most common cause of accidents with tourists.
- **Don't count on cash**: many places accept only card or contactless.
- **Don't photograph the Red Light District**: it's prohibited in much of the area, and anyway it's a matter of respect.
- **Book the Rijksmuseum and the Van Gogh Museum online**, with a time slot, to avoid the longest lines.
- **Dress in layers and bring a rain jacket**: it rains often, even in summer, and you feel the wind on the canals.

## Chiusura personale
We got around Amsterdam on foot and by bike: the canal ring and the Jordaan, the Anne Frank House, the two big museums on Museumplein. On the last day we rented bikes and rode around freely, stopping wherever we felt like it, the way Amsterdammers do.
