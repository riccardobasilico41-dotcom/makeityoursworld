# Peru in Two Weeks: Lima, Arequipa, Lake Titicaca, Cusco and Machu Picchu

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (two-week version of the long trip through Peru: routes, times, costs and warnings come from there and need to be rechecked before you go)
**Periodo del viaggio:** three trips to Peru, in 2018, 2021 and 2024
**Durata:** 14 days, 13 nights, plus 3-4 optional days for the Amazon or for Huaraz
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, with domestic flights, tourist buses, the train to Machu Picchu and organized tours in the Colca and on Titicaca
**Categoria:** Sud America · Perù · Ande · alta quota · cultura inca · Machu Picchu · lago Titicaca

---

## Apertura personale
The moment I carry with me from Peru is Machu Picchu at sunrise. Getting in among the first, with the citadel still almost empty, pays you back for the wake-up in the dark. I've been to Peru three times, and the complete loop I wrote up takes a month. Here I keep the Andean part, the one almost everyone does on a first trip: two weeks from Lima to Arequipa, the Colca, Titicaca, Cusco, the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu.

Compared to the long trip, I reversed the direction. You start in the south and climb one step at a time: first Arequipa at 2,300 meters (about 7,500 ft), then the canyon, then Puno and finally Cusco. Machu Picchu comes at the end, when your body has gotten used to the altitude.

I've also been to Peru twice as a volunteer: in Cusco with Proyecto Cusimaky, a project I support and invite you to visit, and in Arequipa as a volunteer osteopath.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** May to September, the dry season in the Andes. In February the Inca Trail closes for maintenance. On June 24 there's Inti Raymi in Cusco: the city is packed and prices are high, so book well ahead.
- **Documents:** passport with at least 6 months of remaining validity. No visa for Italian citizens for up to 183 days.
- **Currency and payments:** nuevo sol (PEN). Cards work in Lima, Arequipa and Cusco and at tourist businesses, much less in markets, on Titicaca boats and in villages. Arrive in Puno with cash already in your pocket, in small bills.
- **Altitude:** it's the theme of the trip. Arequipa is at 2,300 meters, Cusco at 3,400, Puno at 3,812. Soroche, altitude sickness, is a real risk. This loop climbs in steps on purpose, and the slow days are there for that.
- **Transport:** two domestic flights (Lima-Arequipa on the way out, Cusco-Lima on the way back), tourist buses from Arequipa to Puno and from Puno to Cusco, PeruRail or IncaRail train to Machu Picchu. Flights are cheap if you book early.
- **Safety:** the most concrete risk is fake taxi drivers outside airports. Only booked taxis or apps. In Cusco, no street money changers: they pass counterfeit or out-of-circulation bills. For long bus rides, only the main companies.
- **Connectivity:** Claro, Movistar, Entel and Bitel, SIM at the airport with your passport. Good coverage in cities and along the tourist circuit.
- **Electricity and time zone:** 220V, type A and C sockets; a universal adapter helps. UTC-5 all year: 7 hours behind Italy during daylight saving time, 6 in winter.
- **From the airport to the centre:** at **Lima Jorge Chávez (LIM)**, with its new terminal, the official Airport Express Lima bus reaches Miraflores in about 45-75 minutes, about 20 soles (15 each for two or more, or when bought online), roughly hourly from 7:00 to 21:00 · taxis only from the official desks inside arrivals · Uber and Cabify about 55-105 soles, only from the pick-up zone shown in the app · at **Arequipa (AQP)** and **Cusco (CUZ)** official airport taxi or hotel transfer, 15-30 minutes · the mistake to avoid is following the fake taxi drivers at the arrivals exit.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Lima
You land at **Jorge Chávez**, the country's only intercontinental airport, almost always after a long flight with a connection. The first mistake to avoid is already here: never an unauthorized taxi at the exit. Only booked taxis or apps. *Fake taxi drivers at arrivals are the most concrete risk of the whole trip: it's worth arranging the transfer with your accommodation before you leave.* Then the transfer to **Miraflores** or **Barranco**, the most convenient and safest areas, which in Lima traffic takes from three quarters of an hour to more than an hour.

In the evening a walk along the **Malecón**, the cliff above the ocean, is enough to shake off the flight. The Miraflores seafront runs for kilometres past parks and gardens perched above the Pacific; the easiest stretch is around the **Parque del Amor** and Larcomar, the shopping centre built into the cliff. For dinner it's best to stay in the area and go to bed early: with a 6-7 hour time difference, the first day is mostly for recovering. If you need one, buy a SIM card at the airport with your passport.

**In practice:** booked transfer or app from the airport, from about 45 minutes to over an hour to Miraflores · Claro, Movistar, Entel or Bitel SIM at the airport with your passport · first night in Miraflores or Barranco, booked before you leave.

### Day 2 — Lima: historic center, Miraflores and Barranco
People often skip Lima, and they miss the food. Peruvian cooking is one of the things I remember most from these trips, and in Lima I found it better than anywhere else. In the morning the **historic center**, a UNESCO World Heritage site, between the **Plaza Mayor**, with the cathedral and the Government Palace, and the **convent of San Francisco**, visited with a guide together with the catacombs beneath the church. Get there by taxi or app from Miraflores and walk the main streets between the two squares. At lunch your first ceviche at a neighborhood cevichería: here you eat at midday, with fish that arrived that morning. *Many cevicherías work mainly at lunchtime: don't put it off until the evening.*

The afternoon in **Miraflores**, along the Malecón to **Huaca Pucllana**, the pre-Inca pyramid in the middle of the city, built of mud bricks by the Lima culture many centuries before the Incas. You finish in **Barranco**, the district of colourful colonial houses, galleries and murals, on the **Bridge of Sighs**, at sunset. From June to September Lima's sky is often covered by a grey haze, and the sunset over the ocean can be more muted than expected. The city is dangerous, though: outside Miraflores and Barranco, get around with care, and in the evening use app taxis even for short trips.

**In practice:** Huaca Pucllana about 15 soles, closed on Tuesdays · convent of San Francisco with catacombs, paid guided tour · historic center by taxi or app from Miraflores, about 30-45 minutes in traffic · ceviche at lunch, dinner in Barranco or Miraflores.

### Day 3 — Flight to Arequipa, the white city
Morning flight to **Arequipa**, about an hour and a half. The city is built of **sillar**, the pale volcanic stone, with the **Misti** volcano in the background, and at 2,300 metres it's the first altitude step of the trip: keep the morning light and drink plenty of water. The afternoon goes to the **Santa Catalina Monastery**, a small walled city with blue and orange lanes, founded in 1579 and closed to the world for almost four centuries; part of it is still home to nuns. I didn't expect it to be so big, and it's the place in Arequipa that stayed with me most. With one of the monastery's guides you get a better sense of the nuns' life in the cells and kitchens.

In the evening the **Plaza de Armas** with the cathedral lit up and arcades on the other sides of the square, and for dinner **arequipeña** cooking, different from the rest of Peru: rocoto relleno and chupe de camarones in the picanterías. *The most traditional picanterías often open only at lunchtime: if they're closed in the evening, catch up when you're back from the Colca.* The historic center around the square is the place to sleep, as you can walk everywhere. It's also the right evening to book the two-day Colca tour, comparing prices and reviews of several agencies.

**In practice:** Lima-Arequipa flight about 1 hour 30 · Santa Catalina about 40 soles, optional guide extra, open every day except a few holidays · sleep in the historic center · book the Colca tour here.

### Day 4 — Toward the Colca Canyon
You set off for the **Colca Canyon**, one of the deepest in the world, on a two-day tour or by the regular bus to **Chivay**, about 3-4 hours by road. It's the first real jump in altitude of the trip: the road crosses the **Salinas y Aguada Blanca** reserve, where vicuñas and alpacas graze, and at the **Patapampa** viewpoint, the Mirador de los Andes, it goes above 4,800 metres. Go up slowly and drink a lot; coca tea at the stops helps. *Some people get a headache at the pass: it's normal, and it usually eases as you descend towards Chivay.*

Stop at the **La Calera** hot springs, in Chivay, which feel good after the cold of the pass. Along the valley you already see colonial villages and pre-Inca terraces, many still farmed. You sleep in Chivay or Cabanaconde: Chivay has more services, Cabanaconde is closer to the Cruz del Cóndor and to the starting point of the treks down into the canyon. In the evening, at this altitude, it's cold even in summer. *The day tour from Arequipa costs less, but you leave at 3 a.m. and spend almost all the time on the bus.*

**In practice:** Arequipa-Chivay about 3-4 hours · Colca Tourist Ticket about 70 soles for foreigners, checked along the road, in cash · La Calera entry extra · 2-day tour with night and transport to book in Arequipa.

### Day 5 — The Cruz del Cóndor and back to Arequipa
Early start for the **Cruz del Cóndor**, about an hour's drive from Chivay: the condors rise on the morning thermals, usually between 8 and 10. Know beforehand that it's not guaranteed and that the viewing platform is crowded. When it happens, watching them glide a few metres away is impressive, and it's worth the dawn alarm anyway. *Along the canyon rim there are other viewpoints a few minutes' walk from the main one, usually less crowded.* You stay an hour or a little more, then the tours move on.

On the way back tours usually stop in the villages of **Maca** and **Yanque**, with their stone colonial churches, and at a few viewpoints over the terraces. Back in Arequipa in the afternoon. If there's time, the **Museo Santuarios Andinos** with the Juanita mummy, found on the Ampato volcano in 1995: an Inca girl sacrificed on the summit and preserved for centuries by the ice. The visit is only with a museum guide and explains the Incas' high-altitude rituals well. The evening is the chance for the picantería you missed on the first day.

**In practice:** condors most active between 8 and 10, no guarantee · Chivay-Arequipa about 3-4 hours · Museo Santuarios Andinos about 20-30 soles with compulsory guide, check opening hours beforehand, especially on Sundays.

### Day 6 — From Arequipa to Puno, on Titicaca
About 6 hours by bus to **Puno**, on Lake Titicaca, at 3,812 metres. You leave in the morning with one of the main companies: the road crosses the altiplano, past high-altitude lagoons and herds of alpacas, and climbs above 4,000 metres before dropping towards the lake. It's the highest altitude you sleep at on the whole trip. *Keep water, snacks and a warm layer within reach, because on the bus the temperature changes a lot with altitude.*

Take the afternoon slowly: a walk in the center, between the **Plaza de Armas** and the pedestrian **Jirón Lima**, a coca tea, an early dinner. Altitude sickness is more likely here than in Cusco, even after the Colca. A headache and shortness of breath in the first hours are normal; if they get worse, rest, and if they don't pass, see a doctor. It's also the right evening to confirm the two-day lake tour and to withdraw cash: on the islands you need cash, in small notes.

**In practice:** Arequipa-Puno about 6 hours by bus, main companies only · Puno at 3,812 metres, a day without exertion · withdraw cash in town before the islands · 2-day Uros-Taquile-Amantaní tour to book in Puno or beforehand.

### Day 7 — Titicaca: the Uros and Taquile islands, night on Amantaní
By boat from the port of Puno to the floating islands of the **Uros**, built of totora, the lake reed, about half an hour away. To be honest: it's a tourist show, an interesting half hour on how the island is built and little more. The ride on the totora boat is optional and paid separately, in cash. Then **Taquile**, UNESCO-listed for its weaving, with lunch and a view of the Bolivian side of the lake. *On the island you climb a long path on foot: at almost 4,000 metres take it slowly, stopping often.*

In the afternoon you reach **Amantaní**, where we slept in a family's home. We had dinner with them and climbed to the temple of **Pachatata** for sunset. The climb is short but takes you above 4,000 metres, and you need a torch to come down in the dark. The island's families take turns hosting the tour travellers; a small useful gift for the house, such as fruit, rice or notebooks for the children, is always welcome. *At 3,800 metres with no heating the night is freezing: you need heavy clothes.*

**In practice:** 2-day tour with boat, meals and family homestay about 65-75 dollars · island entry fees and totora boat in cash, ask whether included · torch, hat, sunscreen and warm layers.

### Day 8 — Amantaní at dawn and back to Puno
The morning with the family, then back by boat to Puno, arriving around midday. Amantaní isn't comfortable: shared bathroom, hot water not guaranteed. But it's the only point on the trip where you step off the tourist circuit, and it's the moment on the lake that stayed with me most. The crossing takes a few hours. *On the lake, at this altitude, the sun burns even when it's cold: keep sunscreen and a hat in your daypack.*

In the afternoon, if you still have energy, go to the **funerary towers of Sillustani**, pre-Inca, just outside town: the chullpas, tall stone towers built by the Colla for their dead, on a peninsula above the **Umayo** lagoon. Tours usually leave in the early afternoon and return around sunset, when the light on the towers is at its best. If you'd rather rest, stay in Puno and pack for tomorrow's long transfer, which leaves early in the morning.

**In practice:** back in Puno around midday · Sillustani about 35 km away, half day with an early-afternoon tour, entry extra · early dinner: the bus to Cusco leaves around 7.

### Day 9 — From Puno to Cusco across the altiplano
A transfer day, best done on the **tourist bus** (about 10 hours with stops) rather than the flight from Juliaca. The bus leaves early in the morning and stops along the way: the **Pukará** museum, the **La Raya** pass at over 4,300 metres, the temple of Wiracocha at **Raqchi** and the church of **Andahuaylillas**, the "Sistine Chapel of the Andes", with its walls and ceiling entirely painted. Halfway there's a buffet lunch, usually included in the price. *Entry to the sites is often paid separately: have a few dozen soles in cash.*

You reach **Cusco**, at 3,400 metres, in the evening. After Puno the altitude is a bit lower, and your body handles it better than flying in from Lima. To sleep, the center or San Blas: San Blas is prettier but all uphill, and with luggage it's best to have the taxi drop you at the door. Take the first evening slowly even if you feel fine: a light dinner, no alcohol and an early night. Tomorrow you walk all day.

**In practice:** Puno-Cusco tourist bus about 45-60 dollars with lunch, book the day before · departure around 7, arrival in the late afternoon · entry to the sites along the way often extra · sleep in the center or San Blas.

### Day 10 — Cusco: Qorikancha, Sacsayhuamán and San Blas
The day for the city. Start with the **Qorikancha**, the Inca sun temple on which the conquistadors built the convent of Santo Domingo: the interlocking Inca walls are still there, inside the colonial cloister. Then climb to **Sacsayhuamán**, the fortress above the city with blocks fitted together without mortar: it's the best way to understand in advance what you'll see at Machu Picchu. On foot from the Plaza de Armas it's a steep half-hour climb, which you feel at this altitude; a taxi is a sensible alternative. The **Boleto Turístico del Cusco** (about 130 soles) covers Sacsayhuamán, the Sacred Valley sites and several museums: buy it today. *The Qorikancha has its own ticket, outside the Boleto.*

Lunch at the **San Pedro market**, among juices at the counter and soups for a few soles. The afternoon in **San Blas**, the artisans' quarter, with workshops along the uphill streets and the small square with its church. For souvenirs, in Cusco I go to **Assunta**. In the evening head back to the **Plaza de Armas**, with the cathedral and the church of La Compañía lit up. *Give no money to street money changers: they pass fake or out-of-circulation notes.* If you want to add Rainbow Mountain or Humantay lagoon, the tours are booked here for the next day.

**In practice:** full Boleto Turístico about 130 soles, valid 10 days, sold only in Cusco or at the site entrances · Qorikancha ticket separate · San Pedro best in the morning or at lunch · change money only at a bank or authorized casa de cambio.

### Day 11 — Sacred Valley: Pisac, Maras and Moray
You drop into the **Sacred Valley**, 500-600 metres lower than Cusco: from here on you sleep better. A car with driver for the day is the way to go, arranged the day before in Cusco with an agreed price. In the morning **Pisac**, the market and the hilltop site, with stepped terraces and the ceremonial sector at the top. *At the site, drive up to the upper entrance and walk down to the village: it's all downhill and the driver waits at the bottom.*

Then the **Maras salt pans**, thousands of pools carved into the mountain and worked for generations by the same families (separate ticket, about 10-20 soles). For some years now you can no longer walk among the pools: you view them from the paths and viewpoints above the valley. Nearby is **Moray**, the circular terraces the Incas used as an agricultural laboratory, included in the Boleto Turístico. You sleep in **Ollantaytambo**, which in the evening, once the tour buses have left, goes back to its residents.

**In practice:** car with driver from Cusco for the day, price agreed beforehand · Pisac and Moray in the Boleto Turístico, Maras extra in cash · sleep in Ollantaytambo, close to the station for tomorrow's train.

### Day 12 — Ollantaytambo and the train to Aguas Calientes
The morning for **Ollantaytambo**, the only Inca settlement still inhabited along its original layout, with the terraced fortress above the town. Go up early, before the groups from Cusco arrive, then wander the narrow streets of the center, where Inca water channels still run along the walls. The site is included in the Boleto Turístico. *The climb to the terraces is steep but short: from the top you see the whole valley and the route to Machu Picchu.*

In the afternoon the train to **Aguas Calientes** (PeruRail or IncaRail): from here departures are more frequent and cheaper than from Cusco. The ride takes about an hour and a half along the Urubamba river, with the valley narrowing and the vegetation getting ever denser. I've reached Machu Picchu in three ways: on a five-day trek, by minibus to Hidroeléctrica and then on foot, and by train in economy class. Aguas Calientes is a place you pass through: early dinner and bed, because tomorrow you get up in the dark.

**In practice:** Ollantaytambo-Aguas Calientes train about 1 hour 30, book weeks ahead in high season · luggage on the train limited to a small backpack, leave the rest at your hotel · buy Machu Picchu bus tickets in town the evening before.

### Day 13 — Machu Picchu at opening
In line for the first **shuttle bus** (25 minutes, about $24 round trip), or on foot in an hour and a half to two hours, up a steep stairway through the forest. *In 2025 the bus service changed operator: check the price and where to buy on the spot.* The tip that matters more than any other: enter on the first time slot. The light is better, and above all there are forty minutes when the site is almost silent, before the groups arrive.

The citadel is visited along the circuit assigned with your ticket, one-way, so stop for photos at the high points at the start, because you can't go back. I also did **Huayna Picchu** and it's beautiful, but it has to be booked months ahead, and anyone who suffers from vertigo should think hard about it. Tripods, drones and large bags aren't allowed inside. In the afternoon the train to Ollantaytambo and from there the transfer to Cusco for the night, about an hour and a half to two hours by road.

**In practice:** entry about 160 soles for foreign adults, only on tuboleto.cultura.pe · Huayna Picchu and Montaña with a combined ticket, book months ahead · book the return train together with the outbound one · sleep in Cusco.

### Day 14 — Flight to Lima and journey home
Cusco-Lima flight, about an hour and twenty minutes, and connection with the intercontinental flight. Leave a few hours' margin between the two flights: weather delays in Cusco are not rare, and a morning domestic flight leaves more time to recover. If you have a few free hours before leaving, take a last walk in the center or at the San Pedro market.

In Lima, with an evening flight, you have a few hours left: the **Museo Larco**, the country's most important collection of pre-Columbian art, is in Pueblo Libre, closer to the airport than Miraflores, and a couple of hours is enough for it. *Leave for the airport well ahead: Lima's rush-hour traffic stretches travel times a lot.* If you have more days, don't fly home from here: the two best extensions are below, the Amazon with the flight from Cusco to Puerto Maldonado and Huaraz with the night bus from Lima.

**In practice:** Cusco-Lima flight about 1 hour 20 · best a morning domestic flight and a few hours' margin · Museo Larco paid entry, in Pueblo Libre · app taxi to the airport, leaving early.

## What to see / what to do
- **Lima:** ceviche at lunch, the historic center, the Malecón of Miraflores and Barranco at sunset. If you have time, add the Museo Larco, the country's most important collection of pre-Columbian art.
- **Arequipa and the Colca:** the Santa Catalina Monastery, the mummy Juanita, the Cruz del Cóndor at dawn. With two extra days, do the trek to the Sangalle oasis, with a climb back out of about 1,100 meters to do before the sun gets strong.
- **Titicaca:** the night with a family on Amantaní, the rest of the lake is background.
- **Cusco:** Qorikancha, Sacsayhuamán, the San Pedro market. With one extra day, **Rainbow Mountain (Vinicunca)** above 5,000 meters or **Humantay Lake** at 4,200: I did both. Arriving from Puno you're already acclimatized, so you can slot them in between Day 10 and Day 11.
- **Sacred Valley:** Pisac, Maras, Moray, Ollantaytambo. If you have time, add **Chinchero** for a weaving workshop with a women's cooperative.
- **Machu Picchu:** the first shift, and Huayna Picchu or Montaña Machu Picchu if you can get tickets. With 4-5 extra days you can get there on foot via the Salkantay or the Inca Trail.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Climb in altitude by steps.** Never strenuous effort the day after a jump in altitude. In Puno and Cusco, take the first moments easy, even if you feel fine.
- **Book Machu Picchu months in advance.** Huayna Picchu and Montaña Machu Picchu sell out 3-4 months ahead in high season, the dawn shifts even earlier. The Inca Trail has to be booked 5-6 months ahead, 8-12 for June-August. Tickets only at tuboleto.cultura.pe: the others are resellers with a markup.
- **Sleep in the Colca**, don't do it as a day trip from Arequipa.
- **Sleep in Ollantaytambo** before the train, not in Cusco.
- **Taxis only booked or via app**, in Lima as in Cusco, especially to and from airports.
- **Change money only at a bank or authorized casa de cambio.**
- **Only buses from the main companies** on long routes.
- **Don't get ripped off by agencies**, which are on every corner in Cusco: compare prices and reviews before paying for a tour.

## If you have 3-4 more days
**The Amazon from Puerto Maldonado (3-4 days).** After Machu Picchu, instead of flying to Lima, you fly from Cusco to **Puerto Maldonado**, a little over an hour, and continue by boat to a lodge in the **Tambopata** reserve. Three days are enough for the canopy walkway among the treetops, **Lake Sandoval** with giant otters and sunrise at the **collpa**, the clay wall where hundreds of parrots and macaws gather. It was the moment that turned the visit around: in the dense forest you hear far more than you see, and there for twenty minutes we couldn't look at anything else. Choose the lodge for its distance from the city more than for luxury: from the nearby ones you see very little. Roughly $300-600 for 3 nights, all inclusive. Then Puerto Maldonado-Lima flight and home. Yellow fever vaccination is recommended, and required by some lodges. The other gateways, Iquitos and Manu, take a full week: they're covered in the [Amazon section](/amazzonia).

**Huaraz and the Cordillera Blanca (4 days).** From Lima, at the end of the loop, overnight bus to **Huaraz** (about 8 hours), only with the main companies. After coming down to sea level you have to start acclimatizing again: a light day in town, one at **Laguna Churup** at about 4,450 meters, then **Laguna 69** at 4,600, a turquoise that looks retouched. The last forty minutes above 4,500 meters were a fight for breath, twenty steps and a rest. It costs a few tens of dollars with a Huaraz agency, at a fraction of the crowds on the Cusco circuit. On the evening of the fourth day, overnight bus to Lima. The four-day Santa Cruz trek takes a week: it's in the [long trip through Peru](/viaggi/peru-itinerario).

## Chiusura personale
The mistake I wouldn't make again is underestimating the altitude. And you need comfortable shoes, always. To anyone going, I say don't get ripped off by agencies and, if you can, add a few days for the Amazon: it's incredible.
