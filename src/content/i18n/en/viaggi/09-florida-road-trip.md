# Florida in 10 days: from the Keys to Orlando, on the road among alligators, beaches and rockets

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 10 days, 9 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, self-drive rental car (Sixt)
**Categoria:** Nord America · USA · Florida · on the road · road trip · mare

---

## Apertura personale
The blast of humid heat as soon as we stepped off the plane in Miami was the first taste of ten days spent almost always on the move: nearly 1,800 km at the wheel, between the primeval green of the Everglades, the turquoise of the Keys, the golden kitsch of Orlando and the NASA countdown at Cape Canaveral. But the moment I remember most clearly is still the first view of the Seven Mile Bridge, literally suspended between two shades of blue: sea on one side and the other, and the road that seems to float in the middle.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** hot and humid climate all year, with very rainy summers; the tour can change in case of bad weather (hurricanes and tropical storms in season).
- **Documents/visa:** passport with remaining validity beyond the return date. For EU/UK citizens no visa: the **ESTA** is enough (electronic travel authorization, valid for 2 years; **since September 30, 2025 it costs $40.27**, almost double the previous $21), to apply for online at [esta.cbp.dhs.gov](https://esta.cbp.dhs.gov/esta/) when you book the flight. The form asks for lodging and contact details in the USA, but you can leave them blank. Anyone who has been to Cuba since 2011 has to apply for a proper visa instead (€150-250, 30-45 days): in that case the ESTA is not valid.
- **Driver's license:** the Italian one is valid for driving in Florida for up to 30 days (the Spanish one too, the British one up to 3 months).
- **Estimated budget:** recommended starting shared kitty €270 per person (covers activities and car-related expenses), to which are added the Orlando theme park ticket ($130-260 depending on season and number of parks) and the Kennedy Space Center (about $70). Average personal spending in the rest of the trip: $900-1,000 each. Food in Florida is expensive: fast food or a simple dinner around $20, cheap restaurant $35, a real restaurant $50 and up, plus 7% taxes and an 18-20% tip (often already included in the bill: always check and ask for separate checks).
- **How I got around:** rental car (main supplier Sixt) for almost the whole trip, picked up on Day 2 and returned on the last day in Miami; Uber/Lyft for evening trips in town (about $30-40 from the airport to Miami Beach, $15 between Mid Beach and South Beach).
- **Insurance/SIM/Wi-Fi:** medical-baggage insurance included in the package from start to finish of the tour (to be supplemented with a private one if you stay beyond the dates). Wi-Fi always available in hotels and in the Orlando parks. It's best to activate a US data plan before departure (e.g. T-Mobile SIM: 9 days/€31 or 15 days/€40, to activate a few days before leaving) or buy the SIM on the spot. With these SIMs you can't make international calls, only Skype/WhatsApp.
- **Luggage:** compact 40/50/60 liter backpack or duffel bag, no wheeled suitcase. Mosquito repellent, sunscreen, swimsuit, a sweatshirt and a light rain jacket: it's hot all year, but in summer it can be very humid and rainy.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 — Arrival in Miami
From the airport to the hotel it's a quarter of an hour, with the free shuttle or with Uber and Lyft. In the afternoon there's the welcome meeting, better in the lobby than in a noisy restaurant.

For the first evening the most convenient area is **Little Havana**, ten minutes by Uber, full of Cuban restaurants like **Doce Provisions**. Alternatively, **CVI.CHE 105** in Downtown for the best Peruvian ceviche in the city or **Garcia's** for fish. After dinner you dance at **Cubaocho** in Little Havana, go up to the **Sugar** rooftop in Downtown or head to Miami Beach, between Lincoln Rd and Ocean Dr, to **Kill Your Idol**, **Abbey Brewing Company** or **Mango's**: often pricey, some with a dress code.

### Day 2 — Key West
We picked up the car from **Sixt** (the exact pickup point depends on the hotel; no extras to add, the cars are already insured for everything) and headed down to Key West: about 160 miles, three and a half or four hours. The closer you get to the islands, the more spectacular the road becomes, and before and after the Seven Mile Bridge there are scenic parking areas to stop at.

Along the way you can have lunch in Marathon, visit the **Turtle Hospital** ($30, better to book) or look for the miniature deer on **Big Pine Key** with a few short walks. For eating there are **Robbie's**, two hours from Miami, **Burdine's Waterfront** in Marathon, two and a half hours, and **Blue Heaven** in Key West, only without a reservation.

The afternoon in Key West is spent at **Fort Zachary Taylor Beach**, between swimming and the fort ($6 per car, $2.50 on foot), or kayaking, to book. At sunset you go to **Mallory Square** for the golden hour, with a cocktail and a slice of key lime pie at the **Sunset Pier** (free parking around Truman Ave), or on a $75 party boat. In the evening you walk to the **Green Parrot** for a concert, then on Duval St to the **Tiki House** and dancing at **Rick's**.

### Day 3 — Key West and Marathon
After breakfast you leave the room, but the bags can stay at the hotel until the afternoon. The morning is all on foot. The **Hemingway Home and Museum** (an hour, $17, discount when booking for groups of twelve) is the house where Hemingway lived, with the study and the gardens full of the famous six-toed cats. Then the **Butterfly & Nature Conservatory** (half an hour, $15), the photo at the **Southernmost Point**, where there's always a bit of a line, and the **Mel Fisher Maritime Museum** (half an hour, $17.50, closed Mondays), with the treasures of two wrecked Spanish galleons.

After a quick lunch you collect the bags and drive an hour to **Bahia Honda State Park** ($10 per car), with another photo stop at the Seven Mile Bridge. Here there are the beach, kayaking ($18 an hour for a double, $12 for a single, first come first served) or snorkeling on the reef ($30 plus the mask, booking the afternoon tour and being there by noon). For real diving there is **Captain Hook Diving** in Marathon, to contact beforehand: $80-90 plus $30-40 for equipment.

Twenty minutes later you're in Marathon for the sunset at the **Sunset Grille & Raw Bar**. For dinner, the cheap Cuban **La Nina** or the beach bar **Burdine's Waterfront**, which closes early; for a drink, **Porky's Bayside** or the **Overseas Pub**.

### Day 4 — Everglades and Naples
Very early breakfast, before the heat: the day is long. The Everglades are an enormous swamp, and repellent, sunscreen, a rain jacket and long pants are almost mandatory.

The **Everglades Airboat Tour** is included in the GoCity Pass: it has to be booked beforehand on [gocity.com](https://gocity.com/en/miami/attractions/everglades-alligator-farm-and-airboat-ride) and you show the pass at the Everglades Alligator Farm ticket office, arriving no later than early afternoon.

Route 40 takes you to Naples, an hour and a half and 80 miles, and along the way you can stop at **Shark Valley** ($30 per car, bike at $22 or tram at $28 on a road closed to cars, better to book), at the **Oasis Visitor Center**, with the alligators in the canal, or in winter see the manatees at the **Nathaniel Reed Visitor Center** and the birds on the **Marsh Trail** of the Ten Thousand Islands Wildlife Refuge.

**Naples** is a very elegant seaside town, with expensive restaurants and shops. Check-in happens before or after the beach, near the **Naples Pier** or **Lowdermilk Park**; watch out for parking, often reserved for the customers of the shop in front. For dinner, **Alberto's on 5th** (excellent pasta, mediocre pizza), **Taqueria San Julian** or **Michelbob's Ribs and Steaks**, and afterward a stroll among the bars of 5th Ave.

### Day 5 — From Naples to Orlando via Clearwater
It's the longest day at the wheel: five hours and 280 miles, with many possible stops. In Fort Myers, 45 miles from Naples, there are the beautiful historic house of the **Edison and Ford Winter Estates** ($25) and the **Lee County Manatee Park**, free, where in winter you see manatees and can rent a kayak. In Sarasota, two hours from Naples, the **John and Mable Ringling Museum of Art** ($25) is an enormous area with the circus museum, the art museum and a Venetian-style villa. In St. Petersburg the **Dalí Museum** ($29, to book) holds the largest private collection of Salvador Dalí in the world, in a building that is already an icon. And at **Clearwater Beach**, almost three hours from Naples, there's a huge white beach where you can stop and rest.

From there two hours and a hundred miles to **Orlando**. After check-in you have dinner downtown at **Gringos Locos** and stroll around **Lake Eola**, or go to the theme park you won't be visiting the next day, to walk its outer areas with shops and restaurants, which are worth it anyway.

### Day 6 — Orlando (theme park)
It's the most intense day of the trip: crowded parks, heat, long lines and expensive tickets, so it has to be organized. It's best to arrive at least an hour before opening and stay until the evening show (times on the website), with a small backpack (big ones don't fit in the lockers), water, light snacks and the park's official app to follow wait times in real time.

You have to choose between two worlds. **Universal Studios** has two parks, Universal Studios and Islands of Adventure: $130-160 for one day in one park, $160-210 for one day in both. **Disney World** has four, Magic Kingdom, Epcot, Hollywood Studios and Animal Kingdom, with tickets of at least two days starting from €250 and a reservation for the date. On [getyourguide.com](https://www.getyourguide.com/) tickets cost a bit more but can be canceled up to 24 hours before. Parking is expensive, $26 per car at Universal Studios: better to check whether the hotel has a free shuttle.

At Universal the attractions are of three types: the **thrill** ones, outdoor roller coasters not recommended for people who get motion sick, the **medium** ones, indoor roller coasters and simulators, and the **3D** ones, with light special effects for the stomach. Doing them all is impossible even in a single park, so it's best to choose beforehand. With the two-park ticket you also take the **Hogwarts Express**, between Diagon Alley and Hogwarts.

At **Universal Studios** there are Harry Potter Escape from Gringotts, Transformers, Hollywood RIP Ride Rockit, Race Through NY with Jimmy Fallon, Revenge of the Mummy, Fast & Furious, MIB, Despicable Me and The Simpsons Ride. At **Islands of Adventure** Harry Potter and the Forbidden Journey, Hagrid's Motorbike Adventure, VelociCoaster, Skull Island, Incredible Hulk Coaster, Dr. Doom's Fearfall and Spider-Man.

You have dinner inside the park, at the big Hard Rock Café shaped like the Colosseum or at Willy Wonka's Chocolate Factory, or head back toward the hotel for the **Mango's Tropical Café**, with a show and dancing until late ($39 menu plus tax).

### Day 7 — Orlando and Kennedy Space Center
Check-out at dawn, a quick breakfast at **Keke's** or Starbucks and an hour's drive to **Cape Canaveral**. The **Kennedy Space Center** ($57, on site or online) is an impressive complex that needs at least half a day; it's worth checking beforehand whether a launch is scheduled that day. It's divided into two sites linked by a free bus, and the photo with the big NASA globe is not to be skipped.

The main stops: the **Rocket Garden**, ten minutes; the **Apollo/Saturn V Center**, two hours with the bus, with the recreation of the control room that launches the rocket and then the real rocket, enormous, with authentic space suits and moon rocks; the **Atlantis Building**, an hour and a half, with a real shuttle, its history and the simulator; the temporary exhibits, the **IMAX** cinema, **Heroes and Legends**, with the story of the American astronauts in 3D, and the **meet an astronaut** session, half an hour (times change: it's worth checking them).

From Cape Canaveral to Miami Beach it's three hours and a quarter, 200 miles on the Florida Turnpike, which has tolls: the "toll by plate" lanes are for those with a SunPass and should be avoided. You can have lunch at **Cocoa Beach**, half an hour from the Space Center, then stop at **West Palm Beach**, with the luxurious Worth Avenue and the Norton Museum of Art ($18, to book), at **Boca Raton** for the Morikami Japanese Garden ($15, closed Mondays) or at **Fort Lauderdale** for shopping at the Florida Swap Shop.

In Miami you park: on the street in Mid Beach it costs $3 an hour at any hour, in the covered lots of the area $2 an hour, free from late night to early morning (often you need an app and pay by card). The evening is quiet or, for those who want, again between Lincoln Rd and Ocean Dr.

### Day 8 — Miami Beach
First of all the car has to be returned on time: date, time and place are on the voucher, and punctuality avoids extra costs for delays or damage.

The morning is at the sea, toward the hotel's neighborhood or toward South Beach, by car or renting bikes and scooters. Among the busiest spots are **Muscle Beach**, with the free outdoor gym, and beach clubs like **Nikki Beach**. The alternative I recommend is an hour and a half on foot with the **Art Deco Welcome Center** ($30, to book), which tells the story of Miami Beach passing by the most famous hotels, Versace Mansion included.

The **Speed Boat Tour "Thriller Miami"** is in the GoCity Pass: you book on [gocity.com](https://gocity.com/en/miami/attractions/thriller-miami-speedboat-adventures), show up 45 minutes early and sign a waiver, better filled in online in advance.

For dinner, the real Italian pizza of **Fratelli la Bufala**, the Greek cooking of **Santorini by Georgios** or the burgers of **Michael Mina** and **Fat Ronnie's**. The night continues in Downtown, at **E11EVEN** (excessive), **Club Space** (house) or **The Hangar**: prices vary a lot on special nights and the dress code is often strict; in some venues you don't get in without heels.

### Day 9 — Inland Miami
It's the day of continental Miami; anyone who prefers the sea can skip it entirely. You start in **Wynwood**, fifteen minutes by car, with breakfast at **Zak the Baker** or the **Salty Donut** and a walk among the murals of the former industrial district, where the **Wynwood Walls** also run guided tours. Then **Little Havana**: Calle 8, the **Domino Park** where the elderly play dominoes and the Walk of Stars with the stars of Cuban actors. In **Coconut Grove** there is the **Vizcaya Museum and Gardens** ($25, last entry in the late afternoon), a villa with a private harbor, period European furnishings and splendid gardens. In the afternoon you go back to the sea, or to surfing with the **South Beach Dive & Surf Center** ($40 for half a day, better to book).

In the evening there's **Salsa Night! Lessons, Mojitos & Dancing**, in the GoCity Pass, with mandatory reservation: your name stays at the door, and for information you can call Salsa Mia at 305-458-4558. Dress code "tropical glam", no baseball caps, tank tops or swimsuits; dinner at the table is paid separately. To finish, **Mango's Tropical** or, more elegant, the **Clevelander**.

### Day 10 — Check-out and return
Check-out and goodbyes.

## What to see / what to do
- The **Seven Mile Bridge** between the Keys, with its photo stops before and after the bridge.
- The sunset at **Mallory Square** in Key West.
- Wildlife spotting in the **Everglades** (alligators, manatees, herons).
- The **Kennedy Space Center**, and with a bit of luck a live launch: the calendar has to be checked beforehand.
- **Little-touristed gem:** the Domino Park in Little Havana, where elderly Cubans meet every day to play: a piece of everyday life far from the beaten paths.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
**Miami (Day 1 and Day 8-9) — Generator Miami**
I slept at Generator Miami, the Generator chain's design hostel in South Beach. It's a property with dorms and also private rooms, different from a traditional hotel, made for people who travel light without giving up a good level of design and services. Convenient location near the sea, pool and common area very busy, a polished and relaxed atmosphere rather than a "party hostel" one; 24-hour reception and breakfast included in the higher rates. It costs a bit more than the average hostel in the area, but it has excellent value for money for those looking for socializing and location without spending as much as in a South Beach hotel.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- Always phone restaurants and attractions to book: they rarely answer emails.
- Watch out for the "toll by plate" lanes on the highway: they're reserved for people with a SunPass; without the device they should be avoided.
- The speed limit is strictly enforced, but 5-10 miles per hour over the limit is generally tolerated.
- Parking is almost always paid and never cheap: it's best to download in advance the app required by many parking lots.
- Check with each hotel whether breakfast is included and whether there's a **resort fee** (an extra charge on the room, in Key West up to $30/room) to be paid from the shared kitty.
- For the security deposit hotels ask for (about $50/night/room) you need a personal credit/debit card as a guarantee: never cash or tools like the agency's group card.
- All rooms have double beds to share: to keep in mind when forming the rooms.

## Chiusura personale
Ten days and 1,800 km are a lot for a single trip, and honestly a slower pace would have rewarded the Keys more, which alone would deserve a few more days. Between Universal and Disney I chose Universal, and I have no regrets: the Harry Potter attractions are worth the day on their own. The strongest memory, though, is the Kennedy Space Center: seeing up close the history of the American space missions, with authentic suits and moon rocks a few inches away, was a bigger surprise than I expected from a stop I considered secondary.
