# Manchester and Liverpool in a Long Weekend: the Premier League, the Red Brick and an Extension into the Lake District and the Peak District

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (areas Rick has visited many times, put together into one practical route; practical information checked in November 2026, to recheck before departure; first-person memories only those given by Rick, none invented)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 4 days, 3 nights, plus an optional 3-day extension in the Peak District and the Lake District
**Compagni di viaggio:** itinerary designed for a couple or a group of friends, all by train, tram and bus; a car is only needed if you want to explore the national parks freely
**Categoria:** Europa · Inghilterra · Manchester · Liverpool · calcio · Premier League · Lake District · Peak District · natura · treno

## Apertura personale
*My memory: my brother lived in the north of England for four years, so I went to visit those parts many, many times. I like Manchester because you still breathe a more authentic England than in London, which by now is a fully cosmopolitan city. The same goes for Liverpool, where there's even more of a "working-class" feel, in a good way, with its red-brick buildings.*

The long weekend starts in **Manchester**: the Northern Quarter and Ancoats, the old cotton warehouses, the free museums and the stadium, for a match or for the tour. Then less than an hour on the train takes you to **Liverpool**, with the docks on the River Mersey, the Beatles, the two cathedrals and Anfield. The two cities are close and well connected: you don't need a car.

If you have a few more days, you can carry on into the countryside. The **Peak District** is three quarters of an hour by train from Manchester, with the hills of Edale and Castleton. The **Lake District** is further, just over two hours, but it has the lakes, the boats and the finest climbs in the north.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** May to September, with long days and a better chance of sun in the parks. The Premier League runs from mid-August to May: if you're coming for a match, pick those months.
- **Documents:** the United Kingdom is not in the European Union. Italian citizens need an **ETA**, the electronic travel authorisation: you apply on gov.uk or with the official UK ETA app, it costs £20 (2026) and is valid for 2 years. You need a **passport**.
- **Currency and payments:** pound sterling. Cards and contactless work almost everywhere, trams and buses included.
- **Trains:** Manchester Piccadilly-Liverpool Lime Street in about an hour, with Northern trains roughly every half hour and TransPennine Express too. For the Lake District there are direct Northern trains from Manchester Piccadilly and the airport to Windermere; for the Peak District, the Hope Valley line, also Northern, to Edale and Hope. Times and fares on nationalrail.co.uk.
- **Trams and buses in Manchester:** the Bee Network. The Metrolink tram is paid with a contactless card, with a daily cap of £7.10 across all zones (£4.90 outside the morning peak); buses cost at most £2 a journey. Fares are frozen for the whole of 2026.
- **Buses in Liverpool:** a single journey costs at most £2 with any operator; the Solo day ticket for all Merseyside buses costs £5.90 (2026).
- **Match tickets:** only from the clubs' official channels. In the UK, selling a football match ticket without the club's authorisation is a criminal offence. Full details in the Premier League section below.
- **Driving:** only for the extension, and only if you want to. You drive on the left, and the Italian driving licence is valid with no international permit. Whoever drives doesn't drink. *My memory: driving on the left is fine for me, but it takes practice. And in the countryside the roads can be narrow.*
- **Health:** the EHIC covers necessary treatment in the public NHS service; travel insurance is still recommended.
- **Sockets:** type G, with three rectangular pins. You need an adapter.
- **Weather:** it rains often, in every season. Always a waterproof jacket, and walking boots for the extension.
- **From the airport to the centre:** **Manchester (MAN)**. · **train** to Manchester Piccadilly with Northern and TransPennine Express, roughly every 10 minutes, a journey of about twenty minutes; at night there is still one train an hour · **Metrolink tram** to the centre and Victoria, every 12 minutes, £4.60 for a single: it's cheap but slower than the train · **black cabs** from the official rank outside each terminal, metered · **Liverpool John Lennon (LPL)**: Arriva's **500** bus runs up to every 15 minutes, from 4 am to midnight, to Liverpool ONE bus station and Great Charlotte Street, near Lime Street; it costs at most £2. The 80A and 86A buses call at Liverpool South Parkway station, where you can continue by train · the usual mistake is looking for a train in Liverpool: the airport has no station, you need the bus.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Manchester: the Northern Quarter and Ancoats
From the airport the train reaches Piccadilly in about twenty minutes. It's best to stay in the centre, between Piccadilly, the Northern Quarter and Deansgate: you can do almost everything on foot, and the tram covers the rest. Drop the bags and head out.

The afternoon is for the **Northern Quarter**, the district of old textile warehouses, with murals, record shops and bars. To the east you cross into **Ancoats**, where the red-brick mills along the canal have become homes, restaurants and bakeries. It was the world's first industrial suburb, and it still shows. In the evening you have dinner between the two districts: it's the liveliest part of the city.

**In practice:** airport-Piccadilly train about 20 minutes · Metrolink tram with a contactless card, always the same one · the Northern Quarter and Ancoats are explored on foot, for free.

### Day 2 — Manchester: museums, Castlefield and the match
The morning goes to the **John Rylands Library**, the neo-Gothic library on Deansgate, free and open Wednesday to Saturday. Then the **National Football Museum**, near the **cathedral**. If you prefer the city's history, go to the **Science and Industry Museum**, in the old Liverpool Road station: it's free, but some parts are closed for restoration work. Next to it is **Castlefield**, with the canals, the railway viaduct and the remains of the Roman fort.

The afternoon is for the stadium. If there's a home match and you have a ticket, get to Manchester United's **Old Trafford** or Manchester City's **Etihad Stadium** well ahead of kick-off, for the atmosphere outside the ground. If there's no match, do the **stadium tour**: Old Trafford also has the club museum. *There are no tours on home match days: check the fixture list before booking.*

**In practice:** John Rylands Library free, Wednesday-Saturday 10 am-5 pm · National Football Museum 10 am-5 pm, paid, ticket valid for 12 months · Old Trafford tour from £28 online, tram to Old Trafford or Wharfside · Etihad Stadium by tram to Etihad Campus.

### Day 3 — By train to Liverpool: the Albert Dock, Pier Head and the Mersey
From Manchester Piccadilly the train reaches **Liverpool Lime Street** in about an hour. You come out in front of **St George's Hall**, the great neoclassical building, and walk down to the river. At the **Royal Albert Dock** there are the red-brick warehouses around the basin, **The Beatles Story** and, not far away, the free **Museum of Liverpool**.

At **Pier Head** stand the "Three Graces", the three port buildings, with the **Royal Liver Building** and the two birds on top of its towers. This is where the Mersey cruise leaves from, on the old ferry: an hour on the river, with the view of the city from the water. *The Merseyside Maritime Museum and the International Slavery Museum are closed for works: Tate, meanwhile, has moved into the RIBA North building at Mann Island.* In the evening head to Ropewalks and Bold Street to eat.

**In practice:** Manchester-Liverpool about an hour, Northern trains roughly every half hour · Beatles Story £20 · Museum of Liverpool free, in winter 10 am-4 pm and closed on Mondays · Mersey cruise £14, hourly departures from Pier Head.

### Day 4 — Liverpool: the cathedrals, the Cavern Club and Anfield
Hope Street links the two cathedrals. Start with the **Anglican cathedral**, the largest in the United Kingdom, with its tower: two lifts and 108 steps. At the other end of the street is the Catholic **Metropolitan Cathedral**, round, from the 1960s, with its lantern of coloured glass. Then you head down into the centre, to **Mathew Street**, with the **Cavern Club**, where the Beatles used to play, rebuilt next to the original.

The afternoon is for **Anfield**, Liverpool's stadium, with the tour and the club museum. Tours run from 10 am to 3 pm, every day. Everton fans go to the **Hill Dickinson Stadium**, the new ground on the river at Bramley-Moore Dock, with tours bookable online only. In the evening you go back to Manchester for the extension, or fly home from Liverpool or Manchester.

**In practice:** Anglican cathedral free, tower paid · Cavern Club £6 in the daytime · Anfield tour £26, museum alone £14 · for Anfield, the 26 bus from Liverpool ONE or the 17 from Queen Square; on match days also the 917.

### Day 5 — Extension: the Peak District, from Edale to Castleton over Mam Tor
From Manchester Piccadilly the Northern train on the Hope Valley line reaches **Edale** in about three quarters of an hour. Edale is a village at the head of the valley, where the Pennine Way begins, the trail that climbs north. From here you go up **Mam Tor**, the "shivering mountain", and walk along the ridge with the Vale of Edale on one side and the Hope Valley on the other.

You come down to **Castleton**, the village of the caves. **Peak Cavern** has the largest cave entrance in the country, and **Speedwell Cavern** is visited by boat through a flooded mine. Peveril Castle, above the village, is closed until March 2027. To get back you walk to Hope station, or take the 272 bus to Hathersage and the train. *Since 2026 you can't stop on the roads around Mam Tor and Winnats Pass: if you're driving, use the car parks.*

**In practice:** Manchester Piccadilly-Edale about 45 minutes, one train an hour · Peak Cavern £22.25 online, Speedwell Cavern £23.50 · boots and a jacket: the ridge is exposed to the wind · night back in Manchester.

### Day 6 — Extension: the Lake District, Windermere, Ambleside and Grasmere
From Manchester Piccadilly or the airport, Northern trains run direct to **Windermere** in just over two hours. You go down to **Bowness**, on the lake, and take the Windermere Lake Cruises boat to **Ambleside**, at the other end: it's the best way to see the longest lake in England.

From Ambleside the 599 or 555 bus takes you to **Grasmere**, the village of the poet William Wordsworth. You visit **Dove Cottage**, the house where he lived, with the museum next door, and his grave in St Oswald's churchyard. Around Grasmere lake there are easy walks, some of just an hour. Night in Ambleside or Grasmere. *For Hill Top, Beatrix Potter's house, you need timed tickets booked in advance, and without a car it's hard work to get there.*

**In practice:** Bowness-Ambleside boat from £19.20 · Dove Cottage £16, Tuesday to Saturday, 10 am-4 pm · 599 and 555 buses between Windermere, Ambleside and Grasmere.

### Day 7 — Extension: Keswick, Derwentwater and Catbells, then home
The 555 bus takes you from Grasmere to **Keswick**, the little town on **Derwentwater**. From the jetty the Keswick Launch sails round the lake: you get off at **Hawes End** and climb **Catbells**, the best-loved fell in the Lake District, with views over the lake and the mountains around it. The climb is short but steep, with a final scramble over rock.

Back in Keswick, a half-hour walk takes you to **Castlerigg Stone Circle**, the prehistoric ring of stones in the middle of the mountains, free and always open. In the afternoon the 555 bus takes you back to Windermere, and from there the train goes to Manchester and the airport. *If your flight is the same evening, count the bus and the train: it's better to sleep one more night and leave the next morning.*

**In practice:** 555 bus Grasmere-Keswick · Keswick Launch stopping at Hawes End for Catbells · Castlerigg free · Windermere-Manchester Airport by direct train, just over two hours.

## The Premier League match: tickets and stadiums
Seeing a match in England is an experience in itself: the pub beforehand, the street full of scarves, the stadium singing. Tickets are scarce, though, because the grounds are almost always full of season-ticket holders, and they have to be bought by the right rules.

**Official channels only.** Under the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994, in the UK it is a criminal offence to sell or offer for sale a ticket for a match without the club's written authorisation. Tickets bought from unauthorised resellers can be cancelled, and you won't get into the stadium.

**Club membership.** To buy tickets for home matches you almost always need to be a member. For the 2026-27 season:
- **Manchester United:** Official Membership £37.50, Premium £70. One ticket per member, on tickets.manutd.com; for the most sought-after matches there is a ballot.
- **Manchester City:** Matchday Membership £38 plus postage. Tickets range from £20 to £60 depending on the match category. For the most sought-after matches you need previous attendance or the ballot.
- **Liverpool:** All Red membership, Full at £42.99 or Light at £18.99: both give access to ticket sales. Without membership you can't buy tickets.
- **Everton:** Forever Blue membership, with priority in the ballots for home matches. Tickets from £55 to £75, with prices frozen for 2026-27.

**Ticket exchanges.** These are the clubs' official resale platforms, where season-ticket holders put up the seats they won't use. At City, Ticket Exchange tickets can be bought from three weeks before the match; at Liverpool they are resold at face value to All Red members. It's the best way to find a seat at the last minute.

**Hospitality.** The official packages with a meal and a guaranteed seat, sold by the clubs (hospitality.manutd.com, hospitality.mancity.com, hospitality.liverpoolfc.com; for Everton, Seat Unique). They cost a lot more, but you don't need membership: it's the safest route if you want one particular match.

**Stadium tours.** Without a match, the tour is the way into the dressing rooms, the tunnel and pitchside:
- **Old Trafford:** tour and museum from £28 online, Sunday to Thursday 9:30 am-4 pm, Friday and Saturday until 5 pm. Closed on home match days.
- **Etihad Stadium:** tour of about 75 minutes, starting from the South Stand, tram to Etihad Campus.
- **Anfield:** tour £26, every day from 10 am to 3 pm; museum 9 am-5 pm. There is also a special match-day tour.
- **Hill Dickinson Stadium:** Everton's new stadium on the Mersey, opened for the 2025-26 season. Tours by online booking only, with the dressing room, tunnel and press room. Tours have also returned at **Goodison Park**, the old ground, where Everton Women now play.

## How it changes with more or fewer days
- **3 days:** a day and a half in Manchester and a day and a half in Liverpool, with the match or the tour of just one stadium.
- **Just the Peak District:** Day 5 is enough, as a day trip from Manchester. With a car, add **Chatsworth** and **Bakewell**.
- **Just the Lake District:** Days 6 and 7, with an extra night to do everything at a gentle pace, or for Ullswater and Aira Force.
- **With a car:** useful in the parks, for Hill Top, Tarn Hows, Chatsworth and Dovedale. Better to pick it up at the end of the city days: in Manchester and Liverpool you don't need it.
- **With London:** from Liverpool Lime Street there are direct Avanti West Coast trains to London Euston, and from there the itinerary [*London and the Cotswolds*](/viaggi/londra-cotswolds).

## Where to stay
- **Manchester:** in the centre, between Piccadilly, the Northern Quarter and Deansgate, so you have everything on foot and the trains to the airport and Liverpool close by. Salford Quays is handy for Old Trafford but far from the evening life.
- **Liverpool:** around Liverpool ONE and the waterfront, walking distance from the Royal Albert Dock and Lime Street station. On home match weekends prices go up: book as soon as you know the date.
- **Peak District:** you go back to sleep in Manchester; if you stay, there are pubs with rooms in Castleton and the Hope Valley.
- **Lake District:** Ambleside or Grasmere the first night, Keswick the second. In the summer months and at weekends the B&Bs fill up early.

## Where to eat
- **In Manchester** the Northern Quarter and Ancoats are the areas for eating and drinking. Mackie Mayor, the old meat market turned food hall, is a stone's throw from the Northern Quarter.
- **In Liverpool** Bold Street and the Ropewalks area have food from all over the world; the Baltic Triangle, in the old warehouses, is the place for the evening.
- **Liverpool's dish** is **scouse**, the meat and potato stew that gives the locals their name, "Scousers".
- **On match day** you eat at a pub near the stadium, or at the pie stall outside the gates.
- **In the parks** the village pubs serve hot lunches; in the Peak District, try the **Bakewell pudding**.

## Budget
- **ETA:** £20 per person (2026).
- **Transport:** trams in Manchester with the £7.10 daily cap, buses at £2 a journey. The Manchester-Liverpool train is the biggest cost of the city part.
- **Football:** membership from £18.99 to £70 depending on the club, tickets from £20 to £75. Stadium tours from £26 to £28.
- **Museums:** many free, from the John Rylands Library to the Museum of Liverpool.
- **Extension:** Windermere boat from £19.20, Dove Cottage £16, Castleton caves from £22.25. For accommodation in the parks and any car hire there are no verified prices to put here.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Apply for the ETA before booking everything**, only on gov.uk or with the official app.
- **Buy match tickets only from the club**, with membership, the ticket exchange or hospitality. No resale sites.
- **Check the fixture list before the stadium tour**: regular tours don't run on home match days.
- **Always pay for trams and buses with the same contactless card**, to get the daily cap.
- **Book your nights in Liverpool as soon as you know the match date**: prices go up on football weekends.
- **Check the weather before Mam Tor and Catbells**: in fog and wind the ridges are no fun, and you need walking boots.
- **Whoever drives doesn't drink**, not even a pint at the pub after the walk.
