# The Loire Châteaux in 6 Days

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 6 days, 5 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, car plus one day by bicycle
**Categoria:** Europa · Francia · Loira · castelli · giardini

---

## Apertura personale
The golden rule here is not to see too many: five or six châteaux in six days, chosen by type and not by fame, with a whole day left for the bicycle along the river, which is how this UNESCO landscape should really be crossed.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** April-June for the gardens in bloom, September-October for the colors and fewer crowds.
- **Base:** Amboise, Tours or Blois.
- **One château a day**, not two: after the third masterpiece in two days you stop telling them apart.
- **Bicycle:** the Loire à Vélo cycling route links the châteaux along the embankment, on the flat.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 — Arrival and Blois
We started from **Blois**, and it is a good way into the subject: the château sums up four centuries of architectural styles in a single courtyard, a kind of index of what you see in the days that follow. The Gothic wing of Louis XII in red brick, the Renaissance wing of François I with the great spiral staircase open onto the courtyard, the classical wing of Gaston d'Orléans: turning on the spot you move from one century to the next. Inside are the royal apartments and the rooms linked to the murder of the Duke of Guise, killed on the orders of Henri III in 1588. A couple of hours are enough for the visit.

In the afternoon you walk down into the old town: Saint-Louis cathedral, the **Évêché gardens** with the view over the Loire and the roofs, the stairways between half-timbered houses. Opposite the château is the Maison de la Magie, the museum of the illusionist Robert-Houdin, with dragons that lean out of the windows at set times. On evenings in the warmer months the courtyard hosts a sound and light show. For sleeping Blois is fine, but the best base for the week is Amboise, more central and with a real village life. *Those moving on in the evening can see the show and reach Amboise after dark.*

**In practice:** Blois château ticket under 20 euros, two hours' visit · sound and light show on evenings from April to September, separate ticket · Blois-Amboise about 40 minutes by car or around twenty minutes by regional train · combined tickets with Chambord and Cheverny exist.

### Day 2 — Chambord
**Chambord** took up our whole day. From Amboise it is about three quarters of an hour by car, and it pays to arrive at opening time, before the coaches. It is the largest château in the Loire, built by François I from 1519 as a hunting residence, with hundreds of rooms and fireplaces. Inside is the double-helix staircase, where two people climb without crossing paths, then the apartments and the terrace at the top, a forest of chimneys and chimney pots. The staircase is at the center of the building and can be seen well from the landings; the idea is often attributed to Leonardo, who was living in Amboise at the time.

Outside is the walled park, to be explored by bike or on foot: without it, the visit is only half done. It is the largest walled park in Europe, more than five thousand hectares enclosed by a wall of about 32 kilometers, with free access. Bikes can be rented near the château, and there are also small boats on the Cosson canal. Between September and October, at dusk, the stags' bellowing can be heard from the observation towers. *Lunch is in the village of Chambord or a picnic in the park: there is nowhere to eat inside the château.*

**In practice:** château open 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. in winter and until 6 p.m. from late March to late October · ticket around 20 euros, with a higher rate for non-European visitors from 2026 · park freely accessible all year · bike rental near the château, by the hour, from spring to autumn.

### Day 3 — Chenonceau and Amboise
In the morning we went to **Chenonceau**, the château built over the river Cher, with the gallery that crosses it from one bank to the other and the story of the demarcation line that ran right there. During the Second World War the Cher marked the border between occupied France and the free zone: the château's entrance was on one side, the exit at the end of the gallery on the other. It is also the château of the women who lived in it, Diane de Poitiers and Catherine de' Medici, with their two gardens on either side. From Amboise it is about twenty minutes; *at opening time the gallery is almost empty, by mid-morning it no longer is*.

In the afternoon, **Amboise** and the **Clos Lucé**, the house where Leonardo da Vinci lived. Leonardo spent the last three years of his life there as the guest of François I; the house keeps his bedroom, study and kitchens, and the park displays scale models of his machines. The royal château of Amboise, with the Saint-Hubert chapel where Leonardo is buried, can be admired from the bridge over the Loire or left for another evening. Two visits in one day, but different from each other. That is the right limit, because after the third masterpiece in two days you stop telling them apart.

**In practice:** Chenonceau open every day, ticket around 18 euros, more with the audio guide, entry time to be booked · Clos Lucé about 20 euros, at least two hours with the park · Amboise-Chenonceau about 20 minutes by car, or train from Tours to Chenonceaux station · dinner in Amboise, in the streets below the château.

### Day 4 — Cycling along the Loire
It is the day without châteaux, and perhaps the most important one. We rented a bike and followed the **Loire à Vélo**, the cycle route that runs along the embankment, completely flat, from Amboise toward Chaumont-sur-Loire or toward Tours. Upstream, to Chaumont, it is about 22 kilometers, a little over an hour of easy pedaling on minor roads and stretches of cycle path; downstream, to Tours, about 25. In Chaumont, from April to early November, there is the international garden festival. You set off early in the morning, when the wind is light and the light on the river is at its best.

The stops are in villages that don't have a château, and perhaps in a troglodyte cellar dug into the tufa. The cellars are the signature of this stretch of valley: between Amboise and Tours, around Vouvray, the tufa has been dug out for centuries and today houses cellars of Chenin to taste. It is the right way to see this landscape: the Loire is one of the few great European rivers left largely unchanneled, and the châteaux are here because of it. *On the way back, anyone who is tired can put the bike on the regional train: bike spaces are limited.*

**In practice:** Amboise-Chaumont about 22 km, Amboise-Tours about 25 km, all flat and signposted · bike and e-bike rental in Amboise, best booked at weekends in spring and summer · water, a hat and a picnic: along the embankment there isn't always somewhere to stop · Chaumont garden festival from April to early November.

### Day 5 — Villandry and Azay-le-Rideau
**Villandry** is for the Renaissance gardens, laid out in geometric patterns. The most famous is the ornamental kitchen garden, where cabbages, lettuces and leeks are planted in patterned beds and replanted twice a year, in spring and summer; above it are the love garden, the water garden and the maze. From the château's keep you can see the whole design from above, and that is where it pays to start. Out of season they are bare, so check the period before going: the best time runs from May to October. From Amboise it is about three quarters of an hour by car via Tours.

Then **Azay-le-Rideau**, the château on the island that is mirrored in the water. It is a quarter of an hour from Villandry, built in the early sixteenth century on an island in the Indre, and it is one of the most elegant in the valley, with an English-style park around it and the best reflection in the afternoon, when the water is still. The interiors are furnished as a nineteenth-century residence. In the evening it is worth stopping in **Tours**, in the old town around place Plumereau, full of half-timbered houses and outdoor tables, before going back to Amboise. *With two châteaux in one day, an evening in town does you good.*

**In practice:** Villandry about 14 euros for château and gardens, less for the gardens only · gardens at their best from May to October · Azay-le-Rideau ticket under 15 euros, run by the Centre des monuments nationaux · Amboise-Villandry about 45 minutes, Villandry-Azay about 15, Tours-Amboise about 30.

### Day 6 — Cheverny or Fontevraud, and departure
The last visit is a choice. **Cheverny** is still lived in by the same family; **Fontevraud** holds the tombs of the Plantagenets. Cheverny is about three quarters of an hour east of Amboise: a classical château with furnished, lived-in interiors, which inspired Hergé's Marlinspike Hall in Tintin. The kennels house the hounds of the hunting pack, and around midday there is the dogs' feeding, the «soupe des chiens», which draws a crowd; the time changes with the season and is shown at the entrance.

Fontevraud is in the other direction, about an hour and a half west, toward Saumur and Chinon. It is one of the largest abbeys in Europe, later a prison until the 1960s; in the abbey church lie the funeral effigies of Henry II of England, Eleanor of Aquitaine, Richard the Lionheart and Isabella of Angoulême. Then we headed back in the afternoon, with rillettes and a bottle of Chenin or Cabernet Franc in the suitcase. *Those who choose Fontevraud pass the cellars of Chinon on the way, the Cabernet Franc country.*

**In practice:** Cheverny about 15 euros, soupe des chiens usually in the late morning, time to be checked on the day · Fontevraud about 13 euros · for Paris, TGV from Saint-Pierre-des-Corps station, on the edge of Tours, in just over an hour.

## What to see / what to do
- Chambord with the double-helix staircase and the terrace.
- Chenonceau, with the story of the demarcation line in the gallery.
- A troglodyte cellar dug into the tuffeau.
- A whole day by bicycle along the river.

## Possible extensions
- **Bordeaux (+4-6 days):** continuing toward the southwest.
- **Paris (+2-3 days):** an hour and a half by train.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
Amboise for the central position and the town life; Blois as an alternative.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
Rillettes, beurre blanc, appellation goat cheeses, tarte Tatin, white wines from Chenin and reds from Cabernet Franc.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't see more than six** in a week.
- **Don't do only the big names**: the lesser ones have a tenth of the crowd.
- **Villandry off-season has bare gardens**: check the period.

## Chiusura personale
The Loire works if you choose one château a day and spend the rest of the time along the river: it's the only great European river left largely uncanalized, and the châteaux are there because of it, not the other way around.
