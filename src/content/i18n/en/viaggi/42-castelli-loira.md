# The Loire Châteaux in 6 Days

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 6 days, 5 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, car plus one day by bicycle
**Categoria:** Europa · Francia · Loira · castelli · giardini

---

## Apertura personale
The golden rule here is not to see too many: five or six châteaux in six days, chosen by type and not by fame, with a whole day left for the bicycle along the river, which is how this UNESCO landscape should really be crossed.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** April-June for the gardens in bloom, September-October for the colors and fewer crowds.
- **Base:** Amboise, Tours or Blois.
- **One château a day**, not two: after the third masterpiece in two days you stop telling them apart.
- **Bicycle:** the Loire à Vélo cycling route links the châteaux along the embankment, on the flat.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 — Arrival and Blois
We started from **Blois**, and it's a good way to get into the theme: the château sums up four centuries of architectural styles in a single courtyard, a kind of index of what you see in the following days. For sleeping Blois is fine, but the best base for the week is Amboise, more central and with real town life.

### Day 2 — Chambord
**Chambord** took our whole day. Inside is the double-helix staircase, where two people climb without crossing paths, then the apartments and the terrace at the top, a forest of chimneys and chimney pots. Outside is the enclosed park, to be explored by bike or on foot: without it, the visit remains half done.

### Day 3 — Chenonceau and Amboise
In the morning we were at **Chenonceau**, the château built on the river Cher, with the gallery that crosses it from one bank to the other and the story of the demarcation line that ran right there. In the afternoon at **Amboise**, at the **Clos Lucé**, the house where Leonardo da Vinci lived. Two visits in a day, but different from each other. It's the right limit, because after the third masterpiece in two days you stop telling them apart.

### Day 4 — Cycling along the Loire
It's the day without châteaux, and perhaps the most important. We rented a bike and followed the **Loire à Vélo**, the cycle route that runs along the embankment, all flat, from Amboise toward Chaumont-sur-Loire or toward Tours. The stops are in villages that don't have a château, and maybe in a troglodyte cellar dug into the tuffeau. It's the right way to see this landscape: the Loire is one of the few great European rivers left largely uncanalized, and the châteaux are here for her.

### Day 5 — Villandry and Azay-le-Rideau
**Villandry** is for the Renaissance gardens, laid out in geometric patterns. Off-season they are bare, so it's best to check the period before going. Then **Azay-le-Rideau**, the château on the island that is mirrored in the water.

### Day 6 — Cheverny or Fontevraud, and departure
The last visit is a choice. **Cheverny** is still lived in by the same family; **Fontevraud** holds the tombs of the Plantagenets. Then we headed home in the afternoon, with rillettes and a bottle of Chenin or Cabernet Franc in the suitcase.

## What to see / what to do
- Chambord with the double-helix staircase and the terrace.
- Chenonceau, with the story of the demarcation line in the gallery.
- A troglodyte cellar dug into the tuffeau.
- A whole day by bicycle along the river.

## Possible extensions
- **Bordeaux (+4-6 days):** continuing toward the southwest.
- **Paris (+2-3 days):** an hour and a half by train.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
Amboise for the central position and the town life; Blois as an alternative.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
Rillettes, beurre blanc, appellation goat cheeses, tarte Tatin, white wines from Chenin and reds from Cabernet Franc.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't see more than six** in a week.
- **Don't do only the big names**: the lesser ones have a tenth of the crowd.
- **Villandry off-season has bare gardens**: check the period.

## Chiusura personale
The Loire works if you choose one château a day and spend the rest of the time along the river: it's the only great European river left largely uncanalized, and the châteaux are there because of it, not the other way around.
