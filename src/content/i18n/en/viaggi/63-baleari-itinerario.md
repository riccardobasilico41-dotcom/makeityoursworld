# Balearic Islands in 8 Days: Mallorca, Ibiza and Menorca

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (trip taken by Rick; itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:** **May-June or September**
**Durata:** 8 days, 7 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, ferry between the islands, rental car on each
**Categoria:** Europa · Spagna · Baleari · isole · mare

---

## Apertura personale
The Balearics are three different islands held together only by geography and the Mediterranean summer, not by a shared character. Mallorca is almost a small continent: a real capital, Palma, and a UNESCO-listed mountain range, the Serra de Tramuntana. Ibiza is two islands in one: the south of world-famous nightlife and the rural, almost hippie north that mainstream guides tend to skip altogether. Menorca is the deliberate choice of silence: much of its territory is a UNESCO biosphere reserve, and tourist development has stayed contained by political choice since the '80s.

Eight days, linked by ferry, are enough to see all three without reducing them to stopovers of a few hours: three to four days in Mallorca, the largest, and two to three each for Ibiza and Menorca. With less time, it's better to pick just one rather than squeeze them all in: further down there is a comparison of the three for anyone who has to decide.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **May-June** and **September**, the best compromise between pleasant weather and lower prices. **July-August** is the peak: flights, ferries and lodging have some of the highest prices in Europe, and beaches and old towns are very crowded.
- **Ferries between the islands:** Baleària and Trasmed cover the main routes. **Palma-Ibiza**: 2h15-4h15 depending on the type of ship. **Alcúdia-Ciutadella** (Mallorca-Menorca): 1h-2h30, the fastest route; **Palma-Mahón**: about 6h. In high season it's best to book ahead, because prices go up and seats run out.
- **Ibiza, beyond the nightlife:** the north of the island (Sant Joan de Labritja, Santa Gertrudis) is rural and quiet, with secluded coves and craft markets (see the dedicated experience page). To get there comfortably you need a car or a scooter.
- **Menorca:** the smallest and least developed of the three islands, with the Camí de Cavalls, the old coastal path of about 185 km that goes all the way around it.
- **Rental car:** recommended on all three islands; on Menorca it is almost essential, because buses don't reach the most beautiful coves.
- **Electricity and currency:** euro, type C/F sockets identical to Italy.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Palma de Mallorca
We landed in Palma and headed to the center. We spent the afternoon at the **Cathedral of Palma (La Seu)**, Gothic, facing the sea, with Antoni Gaudí's interventions inside. The evening in the **Barrio Gótico**, among the alleys of the old town and the Almudaina Palace.

### Day 2 — The Serra de Tramuntana: Valldemossa and Sóller
A day for the **Serra de Tramuntana**, the UNESCO World Heritage mountain range that runs for 90 km along the northwest coast: **Valldemossa**, the stone village where Chopin and George Sand stayed, and **Sóller**, which you reach on the historic 1912 train (or by car). The afternoon at Port de Sóller, on the jagged coast below the Serra. Anyone wanting to add a stretch of hiking on the GR221 will find the details on the dedicated experience page.

### Day 3 — From Mallorca to Ibiza
The morning is free, for Palma or one last tour of the Serra. In the afternoon we took the ferry to **Ibiza** (about 2h15-4h15 depending on the type of ship). The evening in the center of Ibiza Town.

### Day 4 — Ibiza Town and Dalt Vila
The day was for **Dalt Vila**, the fortified upper town of Ibiza Town, a UNESCO World Heritage site, with its Renaissance walls and medieval alleys. The afternoon is free between the port and the nearby beaches; for those looking for nightlife, a first evening at one of the historic clubs, to be booked ahead in high season.

### Day 5 — Northern Ibiza: coves and markets
A day in the **rural north of the island**: the coves of Cala Xarraca and Cala Sant Vicent, the village of **Santa Gertrudis de Fruitera** with its cafés and, if the calendar lines up, the Saturday market in Sant Joan or the hippie-craft market at Las Dalias. If the trip falls on a Sunday, the evening goes to **Benirràs** for the sunset with drums (see the dedicated experience page).

### Day 6 — From Ibiza to Menorca
The ferry to **Menorca**, often with a stop or change in Mallorca depending on the available route. The afternoon in **Ciutadella**, the former capital, with its old harbor and the Gothic Cathedral inside the walls.

### Day 7 — Ciutadella and the Camí de Cavalls
We devoted the morning to a stretch of the **Camí de Cavalls**, the coastal path around the island: some sections near Ciutadella are fine even for those who aren't fit. The afternoon at **Cala Macarelleta** or **Cala Turqueta**, among the least built-up coves in the Balearics.

### Day 8 — Mahón and end of the trip
We moved to **Mahón (Maó)**, the current capital of Menorca, with one of the deepest natural harbors in the Mediterranean. One of the Talayotic megalithic sites in the area is worth a visit (for example Torre d'en Galmés), remains of a prehistoric civilization that exists only here. The trip ends with the flight home from Mahón airport.

## What to see / what to do
- **The Cathedral of Palma** and the Barrio Gótico in Mallorca.
- **The Serra de Tramuntana**, a UNESCO World Heritage site, between Valldemossa and Sóller.
- **Dalt Vila**, the fortified upper town of Ibiza Town.
- **Northern Ibiza**, coves and markets beyond the nightlife.
- **The Camí de Cavalls** in Menorca, the coastal path around the island.
- **The Talayotic sites** of Menorca, a UNESCO World Heritage site since 2023.

## Possible extensions
- **Formentera (+1-2 days):** by ferry from Ibiza (about 30 minutes), the smallest of the inhabited Balearics, with some of the most beautiful beaches in the archipelago.
- **The full GR221 (+5-6 days):** the complete crossing of the Serra de Tramuntana on foot, sleeping in mountain huts, for those who want to turn the Mallorca stop into a real trek.
- **The coves of southern Mallorca (+1-2 days):** Cala Mondragó and the coves around Santanyí, less trodden than the northwest coast.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
In Mallorca, **Palma** for city life or **Sóller/Port de Sóller** as a base in the Serra de Tramuntana. In Ibiza, **Ibiza Town/Dalt Vila** to stay close to the clubs of the south or **Santa Gertrudis/Sant Joan** for the quiet version of the island. In Menorca, **Ciutadella** for the historic atmosphere or **Mahón** for the connections.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
In Mallorca, **sobrasada** (spreadable cured sausage with paprika) and **ensaïmadas** (spiral pastry). In Ibiza, **bullit de peix** (fish stew with the rice served separately), best in the small country restaurants of the north rather than in the harbor places. In Menorca, **caldereta de langosta** (lobster stew, not cheap) and **Mahón-Menorca DOP** cheese.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book flights and ferries well ahead for July-August**: it is the period of peak demand on all three islands, and prices rise accordingly.
- **Don't reduce Ibiza to just the nightlife**: the north is the part that surprises most people who arrive with a ready-made idea of the club island.
- **Allow more time than expected on the Serra de Tramuntana**: winding roads, scenic but slow.
- **Rent a car in Menorca too**, not just on the other two islands: without one, a good part of the best coves stays out of reach.
- **Check seasonal ferry schedules**: outside the summer months, departures drop considerably.

## Chiusura personale
What stays with me is how much the three islands disprove the idea of a "single archipelago": Mallorca is almost a small continent, Ibiza lives two parallel lives that rarely meet, Menorca deliberately chose to stay smaller and quieter than the other two. Seeing them in sequence, one after the other, is the most honest way to understand what really sets them apart.
