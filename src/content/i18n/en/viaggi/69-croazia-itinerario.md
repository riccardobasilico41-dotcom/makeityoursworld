# Croatia in Two Weeks: From Zagreb to Dubrovnik, Through Lakes, Roman Towns and Islands

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (itinerary and practical information verified through research in October 2026)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 14 days, 13 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, rental car from Zagreb to Split, then catamarans between the islands as far as Dubrovnik
**Categoria:** Europa · Croazia · Dalmazia · isole · mare · laghi · città storiche

---

## Apertura personale
Croatia has 1,800 kilometers of mainland coastline and more than a thousand islands, and it's the Adriatic country that, from Italy, feels like one you already know. The trip works best when it steps beyond the postcard of the sea. This itinerary starts inland, from Zagreb and the sixteen cascading lakes of Plitvice, descends to the Dalmatian coast by way of Zadar, the Krka waterfalls and Šibenik, stops in Diocletian's Palace in Split, then leaves the car behind and continues by catamaran between Hvar and Korčula as far as the walls of Dubrovnik.

It's a convenient trip for anyone leaving from Italy: euro, Schengen area, electronic ID card, efficient roads and ferries, short distances. And it's a country that in high season fills up to the brim, with prices that in Dubrovnik and Hvar reach the level of the priciest Mediterranean destinations. That's why the itinerary is designed for late May-June or September, and it suits anyone who wants to combine nature, Roman and Venetian towns and islands in two weeks without rushing.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go, month by month:** **April** and **October** are mild, with Plitvice and the towns at their best but a cool sea (16-19 °C) and fewer ferries; **May** has the greenery and the Plitvice waterfalls at their fullest, sea around 19-20 °C; **June** and **September** are the best months, with the sea at 22-25 °C, summer connections running and fewer crowds; **July-August** are the peak, with 30-35 °C, maximum prices and queues, Plitvice and Dubrovnik crowded and ferries to be booked in advance; **from November to March** many businesses on the islands close and connections shrink, while Zagreb in December has one of the busiest Christmas markets in Europe.
- **Documents:** Croatia is in the European Union and since 2023 in the Schengen area: the electronic ID card (CIE) or the passport is enough, valid for the whole stay, with no systematic checks at the border with Slovenia. From August 3, 2026 the old paper ID card is no longer valid for travel abroad, even if it hasn't expired. For a trip to Montenegro or Bosnia you still need a document valid for travel abroad and, if you cross the border with a rental car, the car's green card (ask the rental company for authorization).
- **Currency and payments:** euro since 2023. Cards accepted almost everywhere; cash is useful for small bars on the islands, markets and some guesthouses (sobe).
- **Getting around:** the **car** is convenient from Zagreb to Split, for Plitvice, Krka and stops along the coast; on the islands and in Dubrovnik it's more a problem than a help (expensive parking, ferry queues). The simplest solution is to return it in Split, with a one-way surcharge, and continue with the fast **catamarans** (Jadrolinija, TP Line, Krilo). Buses (Arriva, FlixBus) connect the coastal cities well.
- **Tolls:** motorways are toll roads with traditional toll booths, card or cash in euros; Zagreb-Split costs roughly €25-30 for a car. The Croatian motorways (HAC) have set March 1, 2027 for the switch to a system without toll booths (free flow, "Crolibertas"), with no announced increases.
- **Driving:** an Italian license is enough. Dipped headlights on even in daytime, reflective vest, warning triangle and first-aid kit on board; blood-alcohol limit 0.5 per mille, zero under 24. Fines are high and the police are strict on the coast and on the Zagreb-Split road.
- **The Pelješac bridge:** since 2022 the **Pelješac bridge** links southern Dalmatia to the rest of the country without passing through the short stretch of Bosnian coast at Neum: those driving to Dubrovnik no longer have two borders to cross.
- **Safety:** it's a very safe country; Viaggiare Sicuri reports no public-order concerns. The risks are heat, forest fires in the hottest summers, sea urchins (water shoes), narrow coastal roads crowded in summer, and the bora, the north-easterly wind that can stop catamarans and close the Krk bridge. The Farnesina also reports disputed bills for food and drinks: ask the price first.
- **Local rules:** Split, Hvar and Dubrovnik fine people who walk around the old town in swimwear or shirtless, who drink in the street and who sit on monuments. In Split the fine for swimwear or alcohol in the street is €150; in Hvar it goes up to €500-700, and €400 for sleeping in the street or on the beach; the cap on on-the-spot fines is €700. In Dubrovnik you also get fined for disorderly behavior in the center.
- **Tourist tax:** paid per night and per person, set by each municipality: in Dubrovnik €2.65 from April to September, in Zagreb €1.86, less in low season. It's often excluded from the online price.
- **Time zone and electricity:** same as Italy, type C and F sockets.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Zagreb
We arrived in Zagreb, reached by plane or by car from Trieste in about two and a half hours. The city is divided in two: the medieval **Upper Town** (Gornji Grad), with St. Mark's Church and its roof of colored tiles with coats of arms, the Stone Gate and the Lotrščak Tower, from which a cannon fires every day at noon; and the Austro-Hungarian **Lower Town** (Donji Grad), with horseshoe-shaped squares and parks. It's worth getting up to the Upper Town before 12, on foot from Jelačić Square or on the Tomićeva Street **funicular**, one of the shortest in the world, to hear the cannon shot from the Strossmayer promenade.

In the afternoon we went to the **Dolac market**, with its red umbrellas, and to the **Museum of Broken Relationships**, which collects objects left by couples who split up. *The market is at its best in the morning: after lunch many stalls close, so if you arrive late you can swap the order and do the museum first.* In the evening, the cafés of Tkalčićeva Street. Before dinner you can stop by the **cathedral**, with its two neo-Gothic spires visible from all over the city, and the Stone Gate, where people from Zagreb light candles in front of the image of the Virgin that survived the fire of 1731.

**In practice:** Franjo Tuđman airport about 17 km from the center, linked by shuttle bus and taxi · funicular ticket of a few euros, departures every few minutes · Museum of Broken Relationships about €7, open daily until the evening · to sleep, the Lower Town or around Jelačić Square, handy for leaving by car tomorrow.

### Day 2 — Toward Plitvice: the lower lakes
We drove about two hours south to **Plitvice Lakes National Park**, a UNESCO site since 1979: sixteen terraced lakes linked by waterfalls and travertine barriers, crossed by wooden walkways. You sleep near the park, at Mukinje, Rastovača or Grabovac, to get in at opening the next day.

In the afternoon, once the morning groups had left, we did the **lower lakes** from Entrance 1, as far as **Veliki Slap**, the park's tallest waterfall (78 meters). The ticket is valid for one day and must be bought online with an entry time slot; for two days the dedicated ticket is worth it.

**In practice**
- Day ticket for an adult: €40 from June to September (€25 entering in the afternoon, after 4 pm in June-August and after 3 pm in September), €23 in April, May and October.
- In high season the number of visitors is capped: book online.
- In the park swimming, feeding animals and leaving the walkways are forbidden.

### Day 3 — Plitvice at opening and the road to Zadar
We entered at opening to the **upper lakes**, from Entrance 2: we crossed Lake Kozjak on the electric boat and climbed among the walkways to Lake Prošćansko. Before 9 the walkways are almost empty; after 10, in summer, you move in a line. It takes 3-4 hours. Part of the uphill route can be done on the park's **panoramic train**, which links the stations along the upper lakes and is included in the ticket: it's worth riding it up and walking down, so the waterfalls are always in front of you.

In the afternoon we headed down to the coast: about two hours to **Zadar**, through Lika and the Velebit tunnel, where the sky often changes suddenly from inland to sea. *With a strong bora the A1 motorway on the Velebit stretch can be closed to high vehicles or slowed down: before leaving it's worth checking the traffic status on the Croatian motorways website.* You reach Zadar in time to leave the car and take a first evening stroll along the waterfront.

**In practice:** Plitvice-Zadar about 2 hours, partly on the A1 toll motorway · Kozjak boat and panoramic train included in the park ticket · water and some food in your backpack, refreshment points are few · Zadar's old town is pedestrian: better a place with parking or the paid car parks just outside the walls.

### Day 4 — Zadar: the Sea Organ and the Greeting to the Sun
**Zadar** sits on a peninsula and has a Roman and Venetian center: the forum, the pre-Romanesque church of **St. Donatus**, the cathedral of St. Anastasia with a bell tower to climb for the view. On the waterfront are the two installations by the architect Nikola Bašić: the **Sea Organ**, stone steps with pipes below the surface that play with the waves, and the **Greeting to the Sun**, a circle of solar panels that lights up in colors in the evening. Morning is the best time for the old town and the bell tower, before the heat; then you can pass through the sixteenth-century **Land Gate**, with the lion of St. Mark, and the Square of the Five Wells.

Sunset here is a city ritual. Anyone who wants to add a day by the sea can go to the island of **Ugljan** by ferry (about 25 minutes) or to **Kornati National Park** by boat. *For sunset it's best to reach the Sea Organ steps at least half an hour early: the best spots fill up fast, and the Greeting to the Sun only lights up after the sun has gone down.* To cross the harbor there's also the **barkajol**, the small rowing boat that for centuries has linked the two shores in a few minutes.

**In practice:** St. Anastasia bell tower a few euros, often cash only · St. Donatus with a paid ticket, a few euros · Sea Organ and Greeting to the Sun free, always accessible · Ugljan with Jadrolinija ferries from Zadar harbor · the Kornati trip is a full day on a boat, to book the day before.

### Day 5 — The Krka waterfalls and Šibenik
**Krka National Park** is about an hour south of Zadar. From **Skradin** we took the boat up the river to **Skradinski Buk**, a large waterfall of travertine steps crossed by walkways; from there you can continue to the monastery on the island of Visovac. Since 2021 swimming under the waterfalls has been forbidden. From June to September the ticket for all sites costs €40 (€30 after 3 pm), in April, May and October €20; it includes the Skradin-Skradinski Buk boat, running from April to October.

In the afternoon we went to **Šibenik**, with the **Cathedral of St. James**, built entirely of stone, a UNESCO site, with a frieze of seventy-one carved heads on the apses, and the fortress of St. Michael above the center. Šibenik is easy to explore on foot in three or four hours, climbing the stone alleys from the waterfront to the cathedral and then to **St. Michael's Fortress**, which looks over the roofs, the St. Anthony Channel and the islands. *At Krka it's best to be on the first morning boat: after 11 the Skradinski Buk walkways clog up, especially when the tour buses arrive.*

**In practice:** Zadar-Skradin about 1 hour by car, paid parking near the marina · Skradinski Buk walkways about 2 hours on foot · Skradin-Šibenik about 20 minutes · St. Michael's Fortress about €11.50, combined with the town's other fortresses about €15 · cathedral with a ticket of a few euros · you can sleep in Šibenik or go back to Zadar.

### Day 6 — Trogir and arrival in Split
Along the coastal road toward Split we stopped at **Trogir**, a little island linked by a bridge, with an intact medieval center, a UNESCO site, and the Romanesque portal of the Cathedral of St. Lawrence signed by master Radovan. You visit it in a couple of hours. It's best to arrive in the morning, enter through the land gate and wander the alleys to the cathedral square, then go out to the waterfront toward the **Kamerlengo Fortress**, the Venetian tower at the tip of the island, which looks over the roofs and the bridge to Čiovo. The nearby beaches, on the island of Čiovo, are a possible stop for a swim before moving on.

We reached **Split** in the afternoon and returned the car: from here on we moved on foot and by catamaran. In the evening, the **Riva**, the waterfront below the palace, full of people until late. *Before returning the car, check the drop-off time and place carefully: some rental companies are at the airport, which is right next to Trogir, and from there to central Split you need a bus or taxi.* The catamaran port is a few minutes' walk from the Riva, handy for the departures of the following days.

**In practice:** Šibenik-Trogir about 1 hour on the coastal road or the motorway · Split airport a few kilometers from Trogir · Trogir-Split about 30-40 minutes, with heavy traffic in summer · Trogir center pedestrian, paid parking before the bridge · in Split, better to stay near the palace or the port.

### Day 7 — Split: Diocletian's Palace and Marjan
Split's old town is inside **Diocletian's Palace**, built by the Roman emperor around 300 AD to retire to private life. In the Middle Ages the inhabitants moved in and turned it into a town: houses, shops and churches occupy the Roman rooms. Sights include the **Peristyle**, the Cathedral of St. Domnius in the emperor's mausoleum, the bell tower to climb, the palace **cellars** (about €10, €5 for youths and students). It's best to start early, ahead of the cruise ships, entering through the **Bronze Gate** on the waterfront, which leads straight into the cellars, and leaving through the Golden Gate, where the large statue of Bishop Gregory of Nin stands.

The afternoon is for **Marjan** hill, a wooded park at the tip of the peninsula, with trails, viewpoints and coves for swimming, or for the city beach of **Bačvice**, where they play picigin, the shallow-water ball game born here. You walk up Marjan from the Varoš district, on a stairway that reaches the first viewpoint above the harbor in a quarter of an hour. *In summer bring water and go up in the late afternoon, when the woods give shade; the coves on the south side can also be reached by bike, which you can rent in town.*

**In practice:** the palace can be seen on foot in half a day · cellars about €10, bell tower and cathedral with separate or combined tickets · in the old town no swimwear and no drinking in the street, with fines · Marjan free entry · Bačvice 10 minutes' walk from the Riva · for tomorrow, catamarans to Hvar to book ahead in summer.

### Day 8 — By catamaran to Hvar
We took the catamaran from Split harbor to **Hvar** town, about an hour (roughly €10-25 in summer depending on the company). Hvar is the sunniest island in the Adriatic, with lavender fields inland and a Venetian town overlooking a bay full of boats: St. Stephen's Square, the arsenal, the 1612 theater among the oldest in Europe, and the **Spanish Fortress** above the town, from which you see the archipelago at sunset.

*Good to know: Hvar has a famous nightlife and strict rules to contain it, with fines for drinking in the street, walking around in swimwear or sleeping on the beach. Those looking for quiet can sleep in **Stari Grad** or **Jelsa**, on the other side of the island.*

Arriving in the morning, you drop your bags and walk around town in the cooler hours: **St. Stephen's Cathedral** at the end of the square, the Franciscan monastery at the tip of the bay, with its cloister and cypresses. The climb to the fortress is for the late afternoon, to see the Pakleni Islands in low light; the path through the pines starts from the center and takes about twenty minutes.

**In practice:** Split-Hvar catamarans by Jadrolinija, TP Line and Krilo, about 1 hour · car ferries arrive in Stari Grad, not Hvar town · Spanish Fortress with a ticket of a few euros, or combined with the theater, arsenal and other sites about €15 · in August book accommodation well ahead.

### Day 9 — Hvar: the Pakleni Islands
In front of Hvar harbor are the **Pakleni Islands**, an archipelago of pine groves and coves reached by water taxi every half hour in summer (roughly €10-15 round trip). The bays of **Palmižana** and **Ždrilca** on Sveti Klement have restaurants and clear water; other coves are reachable only on foot. It's best to take one of the first boats in the morning, when the bays are still quiet, and walk the trails through the pines to the less frequented coves on the south side. *The beaches are almost all rock or pebbles: you need water shoes, water and sunscreen, because there's little shade.*

Alternatively you can take a scooter or car ride in the interior of the island: abandoned villages like **Malo Grablje**, the lavender fields (they bloom between June and July) and the **Stari Grad** plain, a UNESCO site for the division of fields dating to the Greek colonists. The tour ends well in **Stari Grad**, one of the oldest towns in Europe, with its long, narrow harbor and Tvrdalj, the Renaissance fortified house of the poet Petar Hektorović. You head back to Hvar town for the last evening before Korčula.

**In practice:** water taxis to the Pakleni from Hvar harbor, roughly €10-15 round trip, check the last return boat on the spot · scooters for rent in town, inland roads narrow and winding · Hvar-Stari Grad about 20-30 minutes by car or bus · Palmižana restaurants are pricey, better to book or bring your own lunch.

### Day 10 — Korčula, Marco Polo's town
From Hvar we took the catamaran to **Korčula**, about an hour and a half. The old town is built on a herringbone-shaped peninsula: the side streets are curved to break the wind and let air circulate. It's the town that claims to be the birthplace of Marco Polo, with a house-museum dedicated to him. Then the walls, the Cathedral of St. Mark and sunset from the tower. The old town takes a couple of hours: you enter through the **Land Gate**, with its stairway and the Revelin tower, and climb the main street to the cathedral, built of the island's pale stone.

On summer evenings you can see the **moreška**, a traditional sword dance that depicts the battle between two kings. The island produces **grk**, a white wine that grows only in the Lumbarda area. Lumbarda is a few kilometers from town, reachable by bus, bike or water taxi, with small wineries and sandy beaches, rare in Dalmatia. *The Hvar-Korčula catamaran has few departures a day in summer: book it as soon as your dates are fixed.*

**In practice:** Hvar-Korčula catamaran about 1 hour 30 minutes, roughly €20-30 · moreška usually on Monday and Thursday evenings in July and August, with tickets around €20 for an adult, dates to check on the Korčula tourist website · Lumbarda about 15 minutes by car or bus · to sleep, the old town or Lumbarda.

### Day 11 — From Korčula to Dubrovnik and the walls
From Korčula to **Dubrovnik** the catamaran takes about two hours (roughly €25-35). You arrive at the port of Gruž and reach the old town by bus or taxi. We devoted the afternoon to the **walls**: about two kilometers of walkway around the center, with towers and bastions above the red roofs and the sea. The ticket is about €40 and includes the **Lovrijenac fortress**; it's also valid on the following days. You usually enter at the **Pile Gate**, but there are other entrances near the old port and St. John's Fort; the full circuit runs in one direction only and takes a couple of hours with stops.

It's best to go up in the late afternoon, when the sun is lower and the cruise ships have left the port. Today Dubrovnik limits the number of cruise ships per day, but in the middle hours the center remains very crowded. *On the walls there is almost no shade: bring water, a hat and comfortable shoes, and check the time of last entry, which changes with the season.* From the **Minčeta** tower, the highest point, you see the whole city.

**In practice:** Korčula-Dubrovnik catamaran about 2 hours, roughly €25-35 · from Gruž to the Pile Gate on Libertas buses 1A and 1B, ticket about €2 · walls about €40, Lovrijenac included within a few days · alternatively the one-day Dubrovnik Pass, about €40, with walls, museums and buses · book accommodation ahead from June to September.

### Day 12 — Dubrovnik: Stradun, Lokrum and the cable car
Early in the morning we walked the **Stradun**, the main street paved with polished stone, the Rector's Palace, the Franciscan monastery with one of the oldest pharmacies in Europe still operating, Orlando's column. Then we took a boat to **Lokrum**, the island reserve 15 minutes from the old port, with peacocks, a botanical garden, the Benedictine monastery and the "Dead Sea," a small salt lake connected to the sea (the reserve ticket, €30 for an adult and €5 for ages 7 to 18, includes the boat).

At sunset we went up by **cable car** to Mount Srđ, from which you see the city and the Elaphiti archipelago. If you prefer, you can swim at **Buža**, a bar carved into the rocks outside the walls. Lokrum makes a good half day among trails and rocks; *overnight stays on the island are not allowed and the last return boat leaves in the late afternoon, so note its time as soon as you arrive.* The cable car leaves from above the old town, a few minutes' walk from the Ploče Gate; at the top there's also Fort Imperial, with a small museum on the war of the 1990s. If you have the legs, you can walk down along the zigzag path.

**In practice:** Stradun and churches at opening, before the cruise ships · boats to Lokrum from the old port, roughly every half hour in season · cable car about €30 return for an adult, not included in the Dubrovnik Pass · in strong wind the cable car may stop · for dinner, better away from the Stradun, in the side streets or in Lapad.

### Day 13 — Ston and the Pelješac peninsula
We took a day trip to **Ston**, an hour to the north-west, on the **Pelješac** peninsula. Ston has the longest defensive walls in Europe after the Great Wall, over five kilometers climbing the hill, and the oldest salt pans still in use in the Mediterranean. In the bay of **Mali Ston** oysters are farmed, to be eaten raw with a glass of white wine. The walls can be walked from Ston to Mali Ston in under an hour, with steep climbs; *do them early in the morning or in the late afternoon, because in summer there's no shade on the hill.*

The peninsula continues with the vineyards of **Dingač** and **Postup**, where plavac mali is produced, Croatia's best-known red, with wineries offering tastings. Alternatively there's a boat trip to the **Elaphiti** islands (Koločep, Lopud and Šipan), or to Montenegro, to the **Bay of Kotor**, two hours' drive away with a border crossing. For Ston and Pelješac you need a rental car for the day or an organized tour: there are buses from Dubrovnik, but with few departures. *If you drive and taste wine, remember the Croatian alcohol limit and the frequent checks.*

**In practice:** Dubrovnik-Ston about 1 hour by car · Ston walls with a paid ticket, much cheaper than Dubrovnik's, to check at the entrance · oysters in the places on the little harbor of Mali Ston · Pelješac wineries best booked · Elaphiti on Jadrolinija scheduled boats or organized trips from Gruž port · for Montenegro you need a document valid for travel abroad and the rental company's authorization.

### Day 14 — Return from Dubrovnik
Dubrovnik airport (Čilipi) is about 20 km south of the city, with direct flights to several Italian cities in season. Alternatively there's the overnight ferry to Bari with Jadrolinija at certain times of the year. With an afternoon flight you can use the morning for a last walk in the old town at opening, when the Stradun is still empty, or for a swim off the rocks outside the walls.

The airport is reached by the shuttle bus that leaves from the Gruž bus station and passes near the Pile Gate, timed with the flights, or by taxi. *In high season the coastal road south gets congested: leave with plenty of margin, especially on rental changeover days.* If you go back by ferry, you leave from Gruž port in the evening and reach Bari the next morning, with the option of taking your car.

**In practice:** Dubrovnik-airport about 30-40 minutes · airport shuttle with a ticket of a few euros, taxi considerably more expensive · ferry to Bari with cabin or seat, to book ahead and check for operating dates · tourist tax often to be paid in cash at check-out.

## Variations
**One week.** Split (2 days with Trogir), Hvar (2), Korčula (1) and Dubrovnik (2). You arrive by plane in Split and leave from Dubrovnik, with no car.

**With Istria (+3-4 days at the start).** From Italy you enter **Istria** by car: **Rovinj**, the Venetian town on the sea; **Pula**, with the Roman Arena among the best preserved in the world; **Poreč**, with the Euphrasian Basilica and its Byzantine mosaics; inland villages such as **Motovun** and **Grožnjan**, truffles and olive oil. Then you head down toward Plitvice by way of Rijeka or Opatija.

**With Vis and the Blue Cave.** From Split, the island of **Vis**, the farthest and least built-up, a military base closed to foreigners until 1989, with Stiniva beach in a cove between two cliffs and the **Blue Cave** on the islet of Biševo, visited by boat in the middle hours of a sunny day.

**By boat.** A week on a gulet or sailboat from Split among Brač, Hvar, Vis and Korčula is one of the most popular formulas on the Dalmatian coast.

**With Montenegro.** From Dubrovnik the Bay of Kotor is about two hours' drive: those with a few more days can continue toward Kotor, Perast and the Montenegrin coast, with an external Schengen border to cross.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
Approximate ranges per night for a double room in June or September; in July-August add another 40-60%.
- **Zagreb:** Lower Town or near Jelačić Square. Budget: hostels and rooms €50-80. Mid-range: hotels €90-150. High-end: historic hotels over €200.
- **Plitvice:** guesthouses and rooms in the villages around the entrances (Mukinje, Rastovača, Grabovac), €60-120; the few hotels inside the park are convenient for the dawn entry.
- **Zadar and Šibenik:** apartments in the old town €80-150; seaside hotels at Borik (Zadar) for the mid and high range.
- **Split:** inside or next to Diocletian's Palace to be in the heart of the city (rooms €100-180), Bačvice or Meje for a bit of quiet. Hostels from €30-40 a bed.
- **Hvar:** Hvar town for evening life (€100-220 and more in August), Stari Grad and Jelsa for tranquility and lower prices.
- **Korčula:** the old town or Lumbarda, €90-160.
- **Dubrovnik:** inside the walls for the atmosphere, with plenty of stairs to climb with luggage (rooms from €150, often over €250 in high season); Lapad and Gruž for lower prices and bus connections. The sobe, rooms for rent in private homes, remain the cheapest option everywhere.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
- **Zagreb and the interior:** štrukli (pasta filled with cheese, boiled or baked), continental cooking with meat and mushrooms, the desserts of the historic pastry shops.
- **Lika (Plitvice):** spit-roasted lamb and suckling pig, cheeses and potatoes under the peka.
- **Dalmatia:** grilled fish with blitva (chard and potatoes), fish brudet, **pašticada** (beef stewed in wine, with gnocchi), Dalmatian pršut, olives. The **peka**, meat or octopus cooked under an iron bell covered in embers, must be ordered a few hours ahead.
- **Islands:** Pag cheese, the grk wine of Korčula, the islands' olive oil.
- **Ston:** Mali Ston oysters and mussels, with the white wine of Pelješac.
- **Where:** the **konobe**, the traditional trattorias, are the best choice; outside the most touristy old towns you eat better and spend less.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book Plitvice online with the time slot**, and enter at opening.
- **Return the car in Split** instead of taking it to the islands: faster catamarans, no ferry queues or parking at €30-50 a day.
- **Book the catamarans in advance** in July and August: the runs fill up.
- **Respect the rules of the old towns**: no swimwear, no alcohol in the street, no monuments as seats.
- **Water shoes**: many beaches are pebbles or rock, with sea urchins.
- **Climb the walls of Dubrovnik in the late afternoon**, when the city empties out.
- **Check the tourist tax** and parking costs before booking accommodation.
- **Don't leave the visit to Plitvice or Krka for July-August if you can avoid it**: the walkways become queues.

## Scams and common annoyances
- **Currency exchange with hidden fees** and independent ATMs (often Euronet) with high fees: withdraw from bank machines and decline conversion into your home currency.
- **Taxis without a meter** in Split and Dubrovnik: use apps or agree the price beforehand.
- **Cover charge and bread brought to the table and added to the bill**: ask for the menu with prices, especially for fish, which is paid by the kilo.
- **Boat trips sold on the waterfront** with stops and services different from what was promised: read carefully what's included.
- **Unofficial parking** near the most famous beaches: park only in marked areas.

## Chiusura personale
Croatia, seen in two weeks, is a country of passages: from the inland lakes to the Roman and Venetian towns, from the coast to the islands, from central Dalmatia to the walls of Dubrovnik. This itinerary holds up because it uses the car where needed and leaves it where it becomes a burden, and because it avoids the months when Plitvice, Hvar and Dubrovnik are at their limit. It suits anyone leaving from Italy who wants a trip close to home, simple to organize, with real beach days and something to see every day.
