# Croatia in 14 Days: From Zagreb to Dubrovnik, Through Lakes, Roman Towns and Islands

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (itinerary and practical information verified through research in October 2026)
**Periodo del viaggio:** late May-June or September, with the sea already warm, frequent ferries and fewer crowds than in July-August
**Durata:** 14 days, 13 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, rental car from Zagreb to Split, then catamarans between the islands as far as Dubrovnik
**Categoria:** Europa · Croazia · Dalmazia · isole · mare · laghi · città storiche

---

## Apertura personale
Croatia has 1,800 kilometers of mainland coastline and more than a thousand islands, and it's the Adriatic country that, from Italy, feels like one you already know. The trip works best when it steps beyond the postcard of the sea. This itinerary starts inland, from Zagreb and the sixteen cascading lakes of Plitvice, descends to the Dalmatian coast by way of Zadar, the Krka waterfalls and Šibenik, stops in Diocletian's Palace in Split, then leaves the car behind and continues by catamaran between Hvar and Korčula as far as the walls of Dubrovnik.

It's a convenient trip for anyone leaving from Italy: euro, Schengen area, electronic ID card, efficient roads and ferries, short distances. And it's a country that in high season fills up to the brim, with prices that in Dubrovnik and Hvar reach the level of the priciest Mediterranean destinations. That's why the itinerary is designed for late May-June or September, and it suits anyone who wants to combine nature, Roman and Venetian towns and islands in two weeks without rushing.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go, month by month:** **April** and **October** are mild, with Plitvice and the towns at their best but a cool sea (16-19 °C) and fewer ferries; **May** has the greenery and the Plitvice waterfalls at their fullest, sea around 19-20 °C; **June** and **September** are the best months, with the sea at 22-25 °C, summer connections running and fewer crowds; **July-August** are the peak, with 30-35 °C, maximum prices and queues, Plitvice and Dubrovnik crowded and ferries to be booked in advance; **from November to March** many businesses on the islands close and connections shrink, while Zagreb in December has one of the busiest Christmas markets in Europe.
- **Documents:** Croatia is in the European Union and since 2023 in the Schengen area: the electronic ID card (CIE) or the passport is enough, valid for the whole stay, with no systematic checks at the border with Slovenia. From August 3, 2026 the old paper ID card is no longer valid for travel abroad, even if it hasn't expired. For a trip to Montenegro or Bosnia you still need a document valid for travel abroad and, if you cross the border with a rental car, the car's green card (ask the rental company for authorization).
- **Currency and payments:** euro since 2023. Cards accepted almost everywhere; cash is useful for small bars on the islands, markets and some guesthouses (sobe).
- **Getting around:** the **car** is convenient from Zagreb to Split, for Plitvice, Krka and stops along the coast; on the islands and in Dubrovnik it's more a problem than a help (expensive parking, ferry queues). The simplest solution is to return it in Split, with a one-way surcharge, and continue with the fast **catamarans** (Jadrolinija, TP Line, Krilo). Buses (Arriva, FlixBus) connect the coastal cities well.
- **Tolls:** motorways are toll roads with traditional toll booths, card or cash in euros; Zagreb-Split costs roughly €25-30 for a car. The Croatian motorways (HAC) have set March 1, 2027 for the switch to a system without toll booths (free flow, "Crolibertas"), with no announced increases.
- **Driving:** an Italian license is enough. Dipped headlights on even in daytime, reflective vest, warning triangle and first-aid kit on board; blood-alcohol limit 0.5 per mille, zero under 24. Fines are high and the police are strict on the coast and on the Zagreb-Split road.
- **The Pelješac bridge:** since 2022 the **Pelješac bridge** links southern Dalmatia to the rest of the country without passing through the short stretch of Bosnian coast at Neum: those driving to Dubrovnik no longer have two borders to cross.
- **Safety:** it's a very safe country; Viaggiare Sicuri reports no public-order concerns. The risks are heat, forest fires in the hottest summers, sea urchins (water shoes), narrow coastal roads crowded in summer, and the bora, the north-easterly wind that can stop catamarans and close the Krk bridge. The Farnesina also reports disputed bills for food and drinks: ask the price first.
- **Local rules:** Split, Hvar and Dubrovnik fine people who walk around the old town in swimwear or shirtless, who drink in the street and who sit on monuments. In Split the fine for swimwear or alcohol in the street is €150; in Hvar it goes up to €500-700, and €400 for sleeping in the street or on the beach; the cap on on-the-spot fines is €700. In Dubrovnik you also get fined for disorderly behavior in the center.
- **Tourist tax:** paid per night and per person, set by each municipality: in Dubrovnik €2.65 from April to September, in Zagreb €1.86, less in low season. It's often excluded from the online price.
- **Time zone and electricity:** same as Italy, type C and F sockets.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Zagreb
We arrived in Zagreb, reached by plane or by car from Trieste in about two and a half hours. The city is divided in two: the medieval **Upper Town** (Gornji Grad), with St. Mark's Church and its roof of colored tiles with coats of arms, the Stone Gate and the Lotrščak Tower, from which a cannon fires every day at noon; and the Austro-Hungarian **Lower Town** (Donji Grad), with horseshoe-shaped squares and parks.

In the afternoon we went to the **Dolac market**, with its red umbrellas, and to the **Museum of Broken Relationships**, which collects objects left by couples who split up. In the evening, the cafés of Tkalčićeva Street.

### Day 2 — Toward Plitvice: the lower lakes
We drove about two hours south to **Plitvice Lakes National Park**, a UNESCO site since 1979: sixteen terraced lakes linked by waterfalls and travertine barriers, crossed by wooden walkways. You sleep near the park, at Mukinje, Rastovača or Grabovac, to get in at opening the next day.

In the afternoon, once the morning groups had left, we did the **lower lakes** from Entrance 1, as far as **Veliki Slap**, the park's tallest waterfall (78 meters). The ticket is valid for one day and must be bought online with an entry time slot; for two days the dedicated ticket is worth it.

**In practice**
- Day ticket for an adult: €40 from June to September (€25 entering in the afternoon, after 4 pm in June-August and after 3 pm in September), €23 in April, May and October.
- In high season the number of visitors is capped: book online.
- In the park swimming, feeding animals and leaving the walkways are forbidden.

### Day 3 — Plitvice at opening and the road to Zadar
We entered at opening to the **upper lakes**, from Entrance 2: we crossed Lake Kozjak on the electric boat and climbed among the walkways to Lake Prošćansko. Before 9 the walkways are almost empty; after 10, in summer, you move in a line. It takes 3-4 hours.

In the afternoon we headed down to the coast: about two hours to **Zadar**, through Lika and the Velebit tunnel, where the sky often changes suddenly from inland to sea.

### Day 4 — Zadar: the Sea Organ and the Greeting to the Sun
**Zadar** sits on a peninsula and has a Roman and Venetian center: the forum, the pre-Romanesque church of **St. Donatus**, the cathedral of St. Anastasia with a bell tower to climb for the view. On the waterfront are the two installations by the architect Nikola Bašić: the **Sea Organ**, stone steps with pipes below the surface that play with the waves, and the **Greeting to the Sun**, a circle of solar panels that lights up in colors in the evening.

Sunset here is a city ritual. Anyone who wants to add a day by the sea can go to the island of **Ugljan** by ferry (about 25 minutes) or to **Kornati National Park** by boat.

### Day 5 — The Krka waterfalls and Šibenik
**Krka National Park** is about an hour south of Zadar. From **Skradin** we took the boat up the river to **Skradinski Buk**, a large waterfall of travertine steps crossed by walkways; from there you can continue to the monastery on the island of Visovac. Since 2021 swimming under the waterfalls has been forbidden. From June to September the ticket for all sites costs €40 (€30 after 3 pm), in April, May and October €20; it includes the Skradin-Skradinski Buk boat, running from April to October.

In the afternoon we went to **Šibenik**, with the **Cathedral of St. James**, built entirely of stone, a UNESCO site, with a frieze of seventy-one carved heads on the apses, and the fortress of St. Michael above the center.

### Day 6 — Trogir and arrival in Split
Along the coastal road toward Split we stopped at **Trogir**, a little island linked by a bridge, with an intact medieval center, a UNESCO site, and the Romanesque portal of the Cathedral of St. Lawrence signed by master Radovan. You visit it in a couple of hours.

We reached **Split** in the afternoon and returned the car: from here on we moved on foot and by catamaran. In the evening, the **Riva**, the waterfront below the palace, full of people until late.

### Day 7 — Split: Diocletian's Palace and Marjan
Split's old town is inside **Diocletian's Palace**, built by the Roman emperor around 300 AD to retire to private life. In the Middle Ages the inhabitants moved in and turned it into a town: houses, shops and churches occupy the Roman rooms. Sights include the **Peristyle**, the Cathedral of St. Domnius in the emperor's mausoleum, the bell tower to climb, the palace **cellars** (about €10, €5 for youths and students).

The afternoon is for **Marjan** hill, a wooded park at the tip of the peninsula, with trails, viewpoints and coves for swimming, or for the city beach of **Bačvice**, where they play picigin, the shallow-water ball game born here.

### Day 8 — By catamaran to Hvar
We took the catamaran from Split harbor to **Hvar** town, about an hour (roughly €10-25 in summer depending on the company). Hvar is the sunniest island in the Adriatic, with lavender fields inland and a Venetian town overlooking a bay full of boats: St. Stephen's Square, the arsenal, the 1612 theater among the oldest in Europe, and the **Spanish Fortress** above the town, from which you see the archipelago at sunset.

*Good to know: Hvar has a famous nightlife and strict rules to contain it, with fines for drinking in the street, walking around in swimwear or sleeping on the beach. Those looking for quiet can sleep in **Stari Grad** or **Jelsa**, on the other side of the island.*

### Day 9 — Hvar: the Pakleni Islands
In front of Hvar harbor are the **Pakleni Islands**, an archipelago of pine groves and coves reached by water taxi every half hour in summer (roughly €10-15 round trip). The bays of **Palmižana** and **Ždrilca** on Sveti Klement have restaurants and clear water; other coves are reachable only on foot.

Alternatively you can take a scooter or car ride in the interior of the island: abandoned villages like **Malo Grablje**, the lavender fields (they bloom between June and July) and the **Stari Grad** plain, a UNESCO site for the division of fields dating to the Greek colonists.

### Day 10 — Korčula, Marco Polo's town
From Hvar we took the catamaran to **Korčula**, about an hour and a half. The old town is built on a herringbone-shaped peninsula: the side streets are curved to break the wind and let air circulate. It's the town that claims to be the birthplace of Marco Polo, with a house-museum dedicated to him. Then the walls, the Cathedral of St. Mark and sunset from the tower.

On summer evenings you can see the **moreška**, a traditional sword dance that depicts the battle between two kings. The island produces **grk**, a white wine that grows only in the Lumbarda area.

### Day 11 — From Korčula to Dubrovnik and the walls
From Korčula to **Dubrovnik** the catamaran takes about two hours (roughly €25-35). You arrive at the port of Gruž and reach the old town by bus or taxi. We devoted the afternoon to the **walls**: about two kilometers of walkway around the center, with towers and bastions above the red roofs and the sea. The ticket is about €40 and includes the **Lovrijenac fortress**; it's also valid on the following days.

It's best to go up in the late afternoon, when the sun is lower and the cruise ships have left the port. Today Dubrovnik limits the number of cruise ships per day, but in the middle hours the center remains very crowded.

### Day 12 — Dubrovnik: Stradun, Lokrum and the cable car
Early in the morning we walked the **Stradun**, the main street paved with polished stone, the Rector's Palace, the Franciscan monastery with one of the oldest pharmacies in Europe still operating, Orlando's column. Then we took a boat to **Lokrum**, the island reserve 15 minutes from the old port, with peacocks, a botanical garden, the Benedictine monastery and the "Dead Sea," a small salt lake connected to the sea (the reserve ticket, €30 for an adult and €5 for ages 7 to 18, includes the boat).

At sunset we went up by **cable car** to Mount Srđ, from which you see the city and the Elaphiti archipelago. If you prefer, you can swim at **Buža**, a bar carved into the rocks outside the walls.

### Day 13 — Ston and the Pelješac peninsula
We took a day trip to **Ston**, an hour to the north-west, on the **Pelješac** peninsula. Ston has the longest defensive walls in Europe after the Great Wall, over five kilometers climbing the hill, and the oldest salt pans still in use in the Mediterranean. In the bay of **Mali Ston** oysters are farmed, to be eaten raw with a glass of white wine.

The peninsula continues with the vineyards of **Dingač** and **Postup**, where plavac mali is produced, Croatia's best-known red, with wineries offering tastings. Alternatively there's a boat trip to the **Elaphiti** islands (Koločep, Lopud and Šipan), or to Montenegro, to the **Bay of Kotor**, two hours' drive away with a border crossing.

### Day 14 — Return from Dubrovnik
Dubrovnik airport (Čilipi) is about 20 km south of the city, with direct flights to several Italian cities in season. Alternatively there's the overnight ferry to Bari with Jadrolinija at certain times of the year.

## Variations
**One week.** Split (2 days with Trogir), Hvar (2), Korčula (1) and Dubrovnik (2). You arrive by plane in Split and leave from Dubrovnik, with no car.

**With Istria (+3-4 days at the start).** From Italy you enter **Istria** by car: **Rovinj**, the Venetian town on the sea; **Pula**, with the Roman Arena among the best preserved in the world; **Poreč**, with the Euphrasian Basilica and its Byzantine mosaics; inland villages such as **Motovun** and **Grožnjan**, truffles and olive oil. Then you head down toward Plitvice by way of Rijeka or Opatija.

**With Vis and the Blue Cave.** From Split, the island of **Vis**, the farthest and least built-up, a military base closed to foreigners until 1989, with Stiniva beach in a cove between two cliffs and the **Blue Cave** on the islet of Biševo, visited by boat in the middle hours of a sunny day.

**By boat.** A week on a gulet or sailboat from Split among Brač, Hvar, Vis and Korčula is one of the most popular formulas on the Dalmatian coast.

**With Montenegro.** From Dubrovnik the Bay of Kotor is about two hours' drive: those with a few more days can continue toward Kotor, Perast and the Montenegrin coast, with an external Schengen border to cross.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
Approximate ranges per night for a double room in June or September; in July-August add another 40-60%.
- **Zagreb:** Lower Town or near Jelačić Square. Budget: hostels and rooms €50-80. Mid-range: hotels €90-150. High-end: historic hotels over €200.
- **Plitvice:** guesthouses and rooms in the villages around the entrances (Mukinje, Rastovača, Grabovac), €60-120; the few hotels inside the park are convenient for the dawn entry.
- **Zadar and Šibenik:** apartments in the old town €80-150; seaside hotels at Borik (Zadar) for the mid and high range.
- **Split:** inside or next to Diocletian's Palace to be in the heart of the city (rooms €100-180), Bačvice or Meje for a bit of quiet. Hostels from €30-40 a bed.
- **Hvar:** Hvar town for evening life (€100-220 and more in August), Stari Grad and Jelsa for tranquility and lower prices.
- **Korčula:** the old town or Lumbarda, €90-160.
- **Dubrovnik:** inside the walls for the atmosphere, with plenty of stairs to climb with luggage (rooms from €150, often over €250 in high season); Lapad and Gruž for lower prices and bus connections. The sobe, rooms for rent in private homes, remain the cheapest option everywhere.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
- **Zagreb and the interior:** štrukli (pasta filled with cheese, boiled or baked), continental cooking with meat and mushrooms, the desserts of the historic pastry shops.
- **Lika (Plitvice):** spit-roasted lamb and suckling pig, cheeses and potatoes under the peka.
- **Dalmatia:** grilled fish with blitva (chard and potatoes), fish brudet, **pašticada** (beef stewed in wine, with gnocchi), Dalmatian pršut, olives. The **peka**, meat or octopus cooked under an iron bell covered in embers, must be ordered a few hours ahead.
- **Islands:** Pag cheese, the grk wine of Korčula, the islands' olive oil.
- **Ston:** Mali Ston oysters and mussels, with the white wine of Pelješac.
- **Where:** the **konobe**, the traditional trattorias, are the best choice; outside the most touristy old towns you eat better and spend less.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book Plitvice online with the time slot**, and enter at opening.
- **Return the car in Split** instead of taking it to the islands: faster catamarans, no ferry queues or parking at €30-50 a day.
- **Book the catamarans in advance** in July and August: the runs fill up.
- **Respect the rules of the old towns**: no swimwear, no alcohol in the street, no monuments as seats.
- **Water shoes**: many beaches are pebbles or rock, with sea urchins.
- **Climb the walls of Dubrovnik in the late afternoon**, when the city empties out.
- **Check the tourist tax** and parking costs before booking accommodation.
- **Don't leave the visit to Plitvice or Krka for July-August if you can avoid it**: the walkways become queues.

## Scams and common annoyances
- **Currency exchange with hidden fees** and independent ATMs (often Euronet) with high fees: withdraw from bank machines and decline conversion into your home currency.
- **Taxis without a meter** in Split and Dubrovnik: use apps or agree the price beforehand.
- **Cover charge and bread brought to the table and added to the bill**: ask for the menu with prices, especially for fish, which is paid by the kilo.
- **Boat trips sold on the waterfront** with stops and services different from what was promised: read carefully what's included.
- **Unofficial parking** near the most famous beaches: park only in marked areas.

## Approximate budget 2026
Per person, sharing a double room, in June or September; in July-August add 30-50%.
- **Budget:** €70-100 a day, with hostels or sobe, cooking in an apartment and buses.
- **Mid-range:** €130-200 a day, with central hotels or apartments, restaurants and a few excursions.
- **High-end:** over €300 a day, with top-end hotels in Dubrovnik and Hvar and private boats.
- **Individual items:** Plitvice €40 from June to September (€23 in spring and October); Krka €40 from June to September (€20 in spring and October); Dubrovnik walls about €40; Diocletian's Palace cellars about €10; Lokrum with boat €30; catamaran Split-Hvar €10-25, Hvar-Korčula €20-30, Korčula-Dubrovnik €25-35; rental car €40-90 a day in summer plus the one-way surcharge; Zagreb-Split toll €25-30.

## Chiusura personale
Croatia, seen in two weeks, is a country of passages: from the inland lakes to the Roman and Venetian towns, from the coast to the islands, from central Dalmatia to the walls of Dubrovnik. This itinerary holds up because it uses the car where needed and leaves it where it becomes a burden, and because it avoids the months when Plitvice, Hvar and Dubrovnik are at their limit. It suits anyone leaving from Italy who wants a trip close to home, simple to organize, with real beach days and something to see every day.
