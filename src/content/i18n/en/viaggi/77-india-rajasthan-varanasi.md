# India: Varanasi and Rajasthan in 17 Days, from the Ganges to the Thar Desert

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (itinerary and practical information complete and verified as of October 2026)
**Periodo del viaggio:** designed for October to March, with November and February the best months
**Durata:** 17 days, 16 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independent, with a domestic flight Delhi-Varanasi, an overnight train Varanasi-Agra, daytime trains in Rajasthan, a car with driver for the stretches without a railway and a flight home from Udaipur
**Categoria:** Asia · India · Varanasi · Gange · Rajasthan · deserto del Thar · treni notturni · spiritualità

## Apertura personale
Varanasi is the city that changes a trip to India. Rajasthan shows the India of the maharajas, forts and palaces; Varanasi shows the India that lives, prays and dies on the banks of the Ganges, in full view of everyone, every day for three thousand years. Put together in seventeen days, the two halves complete each other.

This itinerary starts in Delhi, flies straight to Varanasi for three nights on the river and then heads west on an overnight train to Agra, the most Indian way of getting around. From there it follows Rajasthan in full: Jaipur, the holy town of Pushkar, Jodhpur, the Thar Desert with a night among the dunes near Jaisalmer, the Ranakpur temple and the lakes of Udaipur, from where you fly to Delhi. It's designed for anyone with a good two weeks, who can handle a few long transfers and wants to see the most intense part of northern India without rushing.

The order is not random. Varanasi first, while you're fresh, because it's the most emotionally powerful and most tiring stop; Rajasthan afterward, with a rhythm that slows down toward Udaipur. Anyone who prefers the reverse order can do it: the overnight train runs in both directions.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** October to March. Varanasi in December and January has cold mornings (5-8 °C) and thick fog on the river at dawn, which sometimes cancels the boats and delays trains and flights; November and February are the most balanced months for the whole itinerary. From April to June the heat tops 40-45 °C both on the Ganges and in the Thar; with the monsoon (July to September) the Ganges rises, floods the lower ghats and boats may be suspended.
- **Documents:** passport valid for at least 6 months from the date of the visa application and two blank pages, e-Tourist Visa on the official portal indianvisaonline.gov.in, to be requested at least 4 days ahead ($25 for 30 days, $40 for one year, plus a 2.5-3% bank fee; fees unchanged in 2026), and the free online e-Arrival Card within the 72 hours before arrival, mandatory since April 1, 2026. Banned at entry: satellite phones, drones without a permit and e-cigarettes.
- **Currency and payments:** Indian rupee, roughly 95-105 to the euro. Cards in hotels and good restaurants; cash for boats, tuk-tuks, temple offerings, markets and tips.
- **Trains:** they are the logistical heart of this itinerary. Book on irctc.co.in with a foreigner account or with authorized apps, 4-6 weeks ahead for the Varanasi-Agra overnight. Recommended class on the overnight: 2A (two-tier berths with curtains) or 3A.
- **Temples:** shoulders and legs covered, shoes off, often no leather. At Varanasi's Kashi Vishwanath you can't enter with phones, bags and pens: you leave them in the lockers outside.
- **Health:** bottled water only; bathing in the Ganges is not recommended, the water is heavily polluted. Hepatitis A and B and typhoid vaccines recommended, plus an up-to-date tetanus shot; for a night in the desert you need warm clothes.
- **Clothing and women traveling:** loose clothes covering shoulders and legs, a scarf always in your bag. In Varanasi the alleys are narrow and crowded: better not to walk them alone after sunset.
- **Connectivity:** Airtel or Jio SIM at Delhi airport; good signal almost everywhere, weak among the dunes.
- **Time zone:** UTC+5:30, 3.5 hours ahead of Italy during daylight saving time, 4.5 in winter.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Delhi and Old Delhi
You almost always reach Indira Gandhi airport at night or early in the morning. For the center there's the prepaid taxi from the official counter, Uber or the hotel transfer: *anyone who tells you at the exit that your hotel is closed or full is just after a commission from another one*. After a few hours of rest, we spent the afternoon in **Old Delhi**, reached by metro to Chandni Chowk: the **Jama Masjid**, the alleys by cycle rickshaw, the Khari Baoli spice market and the fried parathas of Paranthe Wali Gali. We got back before dark.

**In practice:** Airport Express metro to New Delhi in about 20 minutes · Jama Masjid closed to tourists during prayer · cycle rickshaw 300-500 rupees a ride, agree the price beforehand.

### Day 2 — Delhi: Humayun, Qutub Minar and flight to Varanasi
Early in the morning we went to **Humayun's Tomb**, the Mughal mausoleum that inspired the Taj Mahal, when the gardens are still almost empty, then to the **Qutub Minar**, south of the city. If you have time, stop by the **Gurudwara Bangla Sahib**, the Sikh temple where you eat for free at the community kitchen. In the late afternoon we took the flight to **Varanasi**: about an hour and a half, many flights a day with IndiGo and Air India, twelve hours saved compared to the train.

From Varanasi airport to the center takes a good hour. The car stops at the edge of the old city, and the last stretch to the hotel on the ghats is done on foot through the alleys, with your luggage. *It's a good idea to ask the hotel to send someone to pick guests up where the car drops them off: in the evening, the first time, the alleys are a maze.*

**In practice:** Humayun and Qutub Minar about 600 rupees each · Delhi-Varanasi flight 3,500-7,000 rupees if booked early · choose a hotel on the ghats or near them, not along the busy roads.

### Day 3 — Varanasi: the ghats at dawn and the aarti at Dashashwamedh
The day began before the sun, on a boat. A rowboat from **Assi Ghat** or Dashashwamedh goes up the Ganges while pilgrims step down for their ritual bath, women let little flower lamps float away and priests recite the morning prayers. At **Assi Ghat**, at dawn, there's also the Subah-e-Banaras, a ceremony with chanting, yoga and a small aarti. Then we walked north along the 84 ghats, one after another, among washermen, wrestlers, cows and chai sellers.

The afternoon is for resting, because Varanasi wears you out. At sunset we went back to **Dashashwamedh Ghat** for the **Ganga Aarti**, the evening ceremony: seven priests swing great fire lamps to the rhythm of bells and chanting, in front of thousands of people on the steps and on the boats. To find a spot on the steps you have to arrive at least an hour early; otherwise you watch from the water, from a boat.

**In practice:** private rowboat one hour at dawn roughly 800-1,500 rupees, agree the price beforehand · Ganga Aarti every evening, starting around 6 p.m. in winter and 7 p.m. in summer · frequent pickpocketing in the aarti crowd: bag in front.

### Day 4 — Varanasi: Manikarnika, the Kashi Vishwanath and the alleys of the old city
**Manikarnika Ghat** is the main cremation ghat, where the pyres burn day and night. For Hindus, dying and being cremated in Varanasi frees you from the cycle of rebirths. You can watch in silence, from a distance, but without taking photos and without letting yourself be accompanied by anyone who offers to explain: *the spontaneous "guide" who takes you to a terrace to watch, and then asks for an offering for the wood of the poor, is the best-known scam in the city*.

Behind the ghats opens the old city, a maze of alleys a meter and a half wide, full of little shrines, silk shops and lassi stalls. The **Kashi Vishwanath**, the golden temple dedicated to Shiva and one of the holiest places of Hinduism, now has a large corridor connecting it to the river; the lines can be long, and inside you don't bring phones, bags and pens. In the afternoon a **lassi** at Blue Lassi, Varanasi silk in the weavers' workshops and, for those who want it, a second sunset on the ghats, quieter, to the north.

**In practice:** Manikarnika no photos, respect for grieving families · Kashi Vishwanath with lockers for belongings outside, covered clothing · "handmade" silks aren't always handmade: buy in recommended shops.

### Day 5 — Sarnath and the overnight train to Agra
**Sarnath**, half an hour from Varanasi, is the place where the Buddha gave his first sermon after enlightenment. The Dhamek stupa, the ruins of the monastery, the temples built by Sri Lanka, Tibet, Thailand and Japan and the archaeological museum, with the Ashoka lion capital that became a symbol of India, made for a quiet morning after the intensity of the ghats. *The museum is closed on Fridays: if the day coincides, it's best to swap it with the previous one.*

In the late afternoon we left on the **overnight train to Agra**: about fourteen hours in a berth, arriving the next morning. It's an experience in itself, with the chai seller passing at dawn and families sharing food in the compartment. Luggage should be chained to the berth with a lock; documents and phone stay on you.

**In practice:** Sarnath about 300 rupees for foreigners (UNESCO site since July 2026), museum separate, closed on Fridays · the Marudhar Express leaves from Varanasi City (not the Junction) around 4:30-4:40 p.m. and arrives at Agra Fort around 6:35 a.m., in 2A or 3A, to be booked 4-6 weeks ahead · alternatively fly Varanasi-Delhi and take a fast train to Agra.

### Day 6 — Agra: the Fort, the "baby Taj" and sunset from Mehtab Bagh
We reached Agra in the morning: shower and breakfast at the hotel. In the late morning we visited **Agra Fort**, the red sandstone Mughal citadel, then the **Itimad-ud-Daulah**, the inlaid mausoleum that anticipates the Taj and is almost always quiet. At sunset we went to **Mehtab Bagh**, the garden on the opposite bank of the Yamuna, from where you look straight at the Taj Mahal, in the pink evening light. Early dinner and to bed, because the next day you enter at dawn.

**In practice:** Agra Fort about 650 rupees · sleep in Taj Ganj, near the east gate · check that the next day isn't Friday, the Taj's closing day.

### Day 7 — The Taj Mahal at dawn and the road to Jaipur
We were at the gates of the **Taj Mahal** half an hour before dawn, with the online ticket on our phone and no large bags, food, tripods or drones. The first hour is the right one: the marble goes from gray to pink to white, the pool reflects the dome and you climb onto the platform without a crush. Then breakfast and departure with the driver toward Jaipur, stopping at **Fatehpur Sikri**, Akbar's abandoned capital, and, if there's time, at the **Chand Baori stepwell at Abhaneri**. We reached Jaipur in the evening.

**In practice:** Taj Mahal about 1,100 rupees plus 200 for the mausoleum, online ticket · Fatehpur Sikri about 600 rupees · Agra-Jaipur 5-6 hours with stops.

### Day 8 — Jaipur, the Pink City
A full day for the capital of Rajasthan. At **Amber Fort** we arrived at opening, climbing on foot along the ramp, for the Hall of Mirrors and the terraces over the lake; then a stop at the **Jal Mahal** and the **Panna Meena** stepwell. In the afternoon the old city: the **Hawa Mahal** with its honeycomb facade, the **City Palace** and the **Jantar Mantar** observatory. We watched the sunset from **Nahargarh Fort**, above the city, and dinner was a Rajasthani thali.

**In practice:** Amber 1,000 rupees for foreigners from 2026, Hawa Mahal, Jantar Mantar and Nahargarh 600 each, City Palace about 700 · the composite ticket is now a 10-day pass for all state monuments of Rajasthan at 5,500 rupees: only worth it for those visiting many · *nobody sells gems to resell in Italy at a profit: it's a scam*.

### Day 9 — Jaipur: the bazaars and the road to Pushkar
In the morning we went to the bazaars, when the shops open and the city is still cool: Johari Bazaar for jewelry and textiles, Bapu Bazaar for embroidered shoes, a block-printing workshop. After lunch we left for **Pushkar**, about three hours by road, and arrived in time for sunset on the **ghats of the holy lake**, when the evening chants fill the water with sounds and bells. Pushkar is traditionally vegetarian: in town, no meat, eggs or alcohol.

**In practice:** Jaipur-Pushkar about 3 hours by car, or train to Ajmer · *the flower "as a gift" for the puja on the ghats is followed by a request for a donation: politely decline* · photograph the bathers only if you ask first.

### Day 10 — Pushkar at dawn and the road to Jodhpur
To the **Savitri temple**, on the hill above the town, you climb on foot (or by cable car) to see the sunrise over the lake and the desert. Then the **Brahma temple**, one of the very few in India dedicated to the creator god, and a last walk in the bazaar. In the early afternoon we left for **Jodhpur**, about four and a half hours, and arrived in time for sunset on the terrace of a haveli below the fort, with the blue houses of the old city stretching toward the desert.

**In practice:** climb to the Savitri temple about 45 minutes on foot · Pushkar-Jodhpur about 4-5 hours by car · sleep in the Ghanta Ghar or Navchokiya area.

### Day 11 — Jodhpur: Mehrangarh and the blue city
We visited **Mehrangarh Fort** in the morning, with the audio guide included in the ticket: the palaces of carved sandstone and the museum of weapons, palanquins and miniatures. We walked down to the **Jaswant Thada** cenotaph, then spent the afternoon among the blue alleys of Navchokiya and Brahmpuri, as far as the **Toorji ka Jhalra** stepwell. In the evening the **Ghanta Ghar**, the clock tower with the market around it, and a makhaniya lassi.

**In practice:** Mehrangarh about 600 rupees with audio guide · the zipline on the walls is paid separately · stepped alleys: no wheeled suitcases.

### Day 12 — Toward Jaisalmer and the night in the Thar Desert
The transfer to **Jaisalmer** is by train or car, about five hours in an increasingly dry landscape. The big luggage stays at the hotel in Jaisalmer, and in the afternoon you head for the dunes of **Sam** or **Khuri**, 40-50 kilometers away, where we slept in a tented camp. Camel ride at sunset, dinner around the fire with the music of the Manganiyar, the musicians of the desert, and a night under a sky with very little artificial light.

*The Sam dunes are the most spectacular but also the most crowded, with dozens of camps and jeeps; Khuri is smaller and quieter.* The quality of the camps varies enormously: it's best to choose places with recent reviews and a stated policy on how animals are treated.

**In practice:** Jodhpur-Jaisalmer about 5 hours by train or car · tented camp with dinner, breakfast and camel roughly 3,000-8,000 rupees per person depending on category · in the desert at night in December-January it drops below 5 °C.

### Day 13 — Jaisalmer, the golden city
In the morning we came back from the dunes and spent the day in town. **Jaisalmer Fort**, in yellow sandstone, is one of the few in the world still inhabited: a few thousand people live within the walls, among houses, shops, carved Jain temples and hotels. Below the fort are the **Patwon** and Nathmal **havelis**, merchants' palaces decorated like stone lace. At sunset, the fort's bastions or Lake **Gadisar** with its pavilions on the water.

**In practice:** free entry to the fort, paid for the royal palace and the Jain temples (open to non-Jains only in the morning) · Patwon ki Haveli 100-200 rupees for foreigners depending on the house visited · sleeping inside the fort contributes to its deterioration through water use: better a hotel below the walls.

### Day 14 — From Jaisalmer to Jodhpur
A return day heading east: morning train or car from Jaisalmer to **Jodhpur**, about five hours, and a free afternoon in the blue city. A stroll through the Sardar spice market, Lake Ranisar at the foot of the fort, dinner on a terrace with Mehrangarh lit up. It's a buffer day, useful even just for washing clothes before the last part of the trip.

**In practice:** anyone with fewer days can fly from Jaisalmer: from late October to late March Air India has daily flights to Delhi and, from October 25, 2026, to Mumbai; in summer the airport closes, or sleep in Jodhpur and leave early for Udaipur · desert sand gets everywhere: a bag for electronics is a must.

### Day 15 — Ranakpur and arrival in Udaipur
By car heading south, through the Aravalli hills, we stopped at the fifteenth-century **Jain temple of Ranakpur**, with 1,444 marble columns, each carved differently. Non-Jains enter only in the window between noon and the afternoon: it's best to leave Jodhpur in the morning to arrive at opening. We reached **Udaipur** in the late afternoon, and the first dinner was on a terrace over **Lake Pichola**.

**In practice:** Jodhpur-Ranakpur about 3 hours, Ranakpur-Udaipur about 2 hours · at Ranakpur no leather items and no food · you can add Kumbhalgarh fort, two hours more.

### Day 16 — Udaipur, the city of lakes
The **City Palace**, built over four centuries on the Pichola shore, with the peacock courtyards and balconies over the lake; the Jagdish temple; the Gangaur and Ambrai ghats in the morning. In the afternoon we took a boat ride on **Lake Pichola**, stopping at Jag Mandir, and the sunset is watched from the Monsoon Palace at **Sajjangarh** or from a terrace. In the evening, for those who want it, there's the traditional dance show at Bagore ki Haveli.

**In practice:** City Palace from 400 rupees (museum only) to about 600-700 for foreigners · boat at sunset costs more than by day · Bagore ki Haveli evening show, paid.

### Day 17 — Flight from Udaipur to Delhi and departure
One last quiet morning in Udaipur, maybe with breakfast on the lake, then the flight to **Delhi**, about an hour and a half, and the international connection, often overnight. Anyone with a flight to Europe in the early hours of the next day takes a room near the airport, at Aerocity, for a few hours of rest.

**In practice:** Udaipur-Delhi flight 4,000-8,000 rupees if booked early · leave at least 4-5 hours between the arrival of the domestic flight and the intercontinental flight, because of frequent delays in winter.

## Where to stay
- **Delhi:** Karol Bagh or Connaught Place in the mid-range, South Delhi for quiet, Aerocity near the airport for the last night or for late-night arrivals.
- **Varanasi:** on the **ghats** or in the alleys just behind, between Assi and Dashashwamedh: budget guesthouses from 1,500 rupees, heritage hotels in the havelis on the river in the mid and high range. Chain hotels in the new city (Cantonment) are comfortable but far from the river, and the river is the reason to be here.
- **Agra:** Taj Ganj and Fatehabad Road, on foot from the east gate.
- **Jaipur:** havelis and heritage hotels in the old city or Bani Park.
- **Pushkar:** guesthouses with a lake terrace; during the camel fair prices multiply and you need to book months ahead.
- **Jodhpur:** havelis below the fort, Ghanta Ghar and Navchokiya.
- **Jaisalmer:** hotels in the havelis below the fort; tented camp at Sam or Khuri for the night in the desert.
- **Udaipur:** Lal Ghat and Gangaur Ghat in the budget and mid-range, Ambrai and Hanuman Ghat for the view; palace hotels on the lake in the luxury range.

## Where to eat
- **Varanasi:** thick lassi with fruit, kachori sabzi in the morning, tamatar chaat (tomato chaat, a specialty of the city), malaiyo (saffron milk foam) in winter, Banaras paan after dinner. Many restaurants on the ghats are vegetarian; near the Kashi Vishwanath you won't find meat.
- **Delhi and Agra:** parathas, kebabs, chaat, Punjabi and Mughlai cooking, petha in Agra.
- **Jaipur and Jodhpur:** dal baati churma, laal maas, ker sangri, mirchi bada, pyaaz kachori and makhaniya lassi.
- **Pushkar:** vegetarian thalis and malpua, with no meat or alcohol.
- **Jaisalmer and the desert:** Thar cooking with dried legumes, desert berries, millet bread (bajra roti); in the tented camps, buffet dinner.
- **Udaipur:** lakeside terraces for the view, the thali houses of the old city for the food.
- **Golden rules:** food cooked in front of you, bottled water, no bathing in the Ganges and no river water, not even "blessed."

## Getting around
- **Delhi-Varanasi flight:** about 1 hour 30 minutes, 3,500-7,000 rupees if booked early. By train it's 8 hours on the daytime Vande Bharat (CC seats, 1,800-2,200 rupees) or 12-13 hours on the overnight trains.
- **Varanasi-Agra overnight train:** the Marudhar Express (numbers 14853, 14863 or 14865 depending on the day) leaves Varanasi City around 4:30-4:40 p.m. and arrives at Agra Fort around 6:35 a.m., about 14 hours; it then continues to Jaipur and Jodhpur. 2A (two tiers, curtains) is the best compromise between comfort and price; 3A is fine for those who adapt. Sheets and blankets are provided in all air-conditioned classes. Alternatively fly Varanasi-Delhi and take a fast train to Agra.
- **Daytime trains in Rajasthan:** Jodhpur-Jaisalmer and back (about 5-6 hours), bookable on IRCTC.
- **Flights from Jaisalmer:** winter only (late October-late March), with Air India to Delhi and Mumbai.
- **Car with driver:** Agra-Jaipur, Jaipur-Pushkar-Jodhpur, Jodhpur-Ranakpur-Udaipur and the Jaisalmer dunes. To be booked for the days needed (3,000-4,500 rupees per leg) or for all of Rajasthan (2,800-4,000 rupees a day, tolls and parking to be clarified, tip at the end of the trip).
- **In Varanasi:** you walk along the ghats and in the alleys; boats connect the ghats by river; tuk-tuks and e-rickshaws for the distances to the new city and Sarnath.
- **Flight home:** Udaipur-Delhi, about 1 hour 30 minutes.

## When to go, month by month
- **October:** end of the monsoon, the Ganges is still high and some low ghats are flooded; hot by day. A good month for Rajasthan.
- **November:** the best month. Diwali (November 8 in 2026), Dev Deepawali in Varanasi with all the ghats lit up (November 24 in 2026), the Pushkar camel fair around the Kartik full moon. Cities are full: book early.
- **December-January:** high season, mild days and cold mornings, thick fog in Varanasi and between Delhi and Agra, with train and flight delays of many hours. Nights at or near freezing in the desert.
- **February:** ideal weather everywhere, less fog.
- **March:** the heat begins; Holi (March 22 in 2027) is spectacular in Varanasi and Pushkar, but should be experienced with caution, especially by women.
- **April-June:** extreme heat, 40-45 °C and more in the Thar. Not recommended.
- **July-September:** monsoon. The Ganges rises, floods the ghats and boats may be suspended; the aarti moves to the terraces. Udaipur is green and the lakes are full.

## Common scams and safety
- **Varanasi, wood for the poor:** at Manikarnika a man offers to explain the cremations, takes you to a terrace and then asks for a donation for the wood of the hospice's sick. Watch on your own, from a distance, and say no.
- **Varanasi, boats:** fix the price before getting on, per boat and not per person, and with the duration. The rower can stop halfway and ask for more.
- **Varanasi, "blessings":** the sadhu who lets himself be photographed or the boy who puts a red mark on your forehead ask for money afterward. A small offering is normal if you choose to photograph a sadhu, but ask first.
- **Varanasi, silk:** the "weavers'" shops where tuk-tuks and guides take you often sell synthetic silk at high prices.
- **"Your hotel is closed" and fake tourism offices in Delhi:** as everywhere in India, you answer by calling the hotel and you don't enter agencies that claim to be official.
- **Jaipur gems and Pushkar flowers:** two classics, both to be refused.
- **On the overnight train:** luggage chained with a lock or a chain, valuables on you, be careful with anyone offering food or drinks from strangers.
- **Women traveling:** covered clothing, booked transfers for night arrivals, the upper berth on the train (more private), women's queues at security checks. In the alleys of Varanasi and in the aarti crowd harassment and groping are possible: better to be accompanied after sunset.
- **The road:** no driving yourself, no night transfers by car, seat belt always.
- **Terrorism alert:** Viaggiare Sicuri reports high vigilance in Delhi and Rajasthan in crowded places, such as markets, stations and temples; it's wise to register on Dovesiamonelmondo before leaving.

## Variations
- **12-13 day version:** drop Jaisalmer and the desert (days 12-14) and go directly from Jodhpur to Udaipur. What remains is Varanasi, the Golden Triangle, Pushkar, Jodhpur and Udaipur.
- **21 day version:** add Bundi and Chittorgarh between Pushkar and Udaipur, or a night at Kumbhalgarh, and one more night in Varanasi for Ramnagar and a music or cooking lesson.
- **With Khajuraho:** between Varanasi and Agra, the temples of Khajuraho (UNESCO heritage, famous for their sculptures) can be reached by flight or by a train from Varanasi and then by train toward Agra or Jhansi; it takes one more night.
- **Reverse order:** you can do Rajasthan first and finish in Varanasi, with a flight from Varanasi to Delhi on the last day.
- **Rajasthan only:** the ten-day version is in the guide *India: Rajasthan in 10 Days*.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book the overnight train well in advance**, at least 4-6 weeks ahead, and have a plan B (Varanasi-Delhi flight) if it stays on the wait list.
- **Check the closing days:** Taj Mahal closed on Fridays, Sarnath museum closed on Fridays.
- **In Varanasi sleep on the river**, not in the new city: the river at dawn is the reason for the trip.
- **Arrive at the aarti an hour early** and keep your bag in front.
- **Never photograph cremations** or grieving families.
- **Leave your phone at the hotel or in the locker** for the Kashi Vishwanath.
- **Don't bathe in the Ganges** and don't drink the river water.
- **Leave margin on flights**: in December-January fog causes delays of hours. No intercontinental flight on the same day as a domestic flight without at least 4-5 hours of margin.
- **Choose the desert camp carefully:** the cheapest is rarely the best, for hygiene and for the animals.

## Chiusura personale
This trip is remembered by contrast. The boats on the Ganges in the morning fog and the fire of the aarti in the evening, then the marble of the Taj at dawn, the walls of Mehrangarh, the silence of the dunes, the lake of Udaipur. In between, a night on a train that explains India better than any guidebook. Anyone with seventeen days and the wish to be inside things, not just look at them, finds here the whole of northern India, with its excesses and its beauty.
