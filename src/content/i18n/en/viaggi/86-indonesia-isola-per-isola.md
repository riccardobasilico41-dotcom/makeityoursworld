## Apertura personale
Indonesia isn't a country to see all in one go. There are more than seventeen thousand islands, and the ones I've seen feel like different countries: the jungle of Sumatra, the volcanoes of Java, the rice terraces of Bali, the dragons of Komodo, the funerals of Toraja, the sea of Raja Ampat.

Two things up front. First: every island deserves at least a week, if not more. Try to squeeze five into fifteen days and you'll spend the trip in airports. Second: the destinations are interchangeable. There's no right order and no must-see island. You choose based on the season, because the seasons change from one island to the next, and Raja Ampat runs opposite to almost everything else.

This guide lines up the main islands: what to do and see, how to get there and get around, when to go and when to avoid them, how many days you need. Each one links to its full page and to the site's itineraries.

This itinerary brings together different trips into one practical route: places I've personally visited at different times, put in order so you can make the most of each destination.

## Scheda pratica
- **Documents:** passport valid for at least 6 months. Apply online for the **e-VOA** at evisa.imigrasi.go.id, at least a week ahead (about 500,000 IDR). You also need the **All Indonesia Arrival Card**, free, filled in at allindonesia.imigrasi.go.id or in the app within 72 hours before every entry: beware of sites that ask for money.
- **Bali tourist levy:** 150,000 IDR per person, paid online on the official Love Bali site (lovebali.baliprov.go.id). Some islands and parks have their own fees: Nusa Penida and Lembongan, Komodo National Park, Raja Ampat, the Mentawai surf tax.
- **Domestic flights:** they're the backbone of the trip. The main hubs are Jakarta, Bali (Denpasar) and Makassar. Routes and airlines change often: check them when you book and don't plan tight connections.
- **Time zones:** three. WIB (UTC+7) for Sumatra, the Mentawai, Java and western and central Borneo; WITA (UTC+8) for Bali, Lombok, Komodo, Flores and Sulawesi; WIT (UTC+9) for Raja Ampat and Papua.
- **Currency and cash:** Indonesian rupiah (IDR): check the exchange rate before you leave. On Bali and in Java's cities cards are accepted almost everywhere. Elsewhere you need cash, and ATMs are rare: on Flores, in the Mentawai and on the Raja Ampat islands, don't count on them.
- **Boats and ferries:** gov.uk points out that the sea changes quickly and that boats can be overloaded or poorly maintained. Check there are life jackets, and avoid crossings after dark if the boat isn't properly equipped.
- **Volcanoes:** several are active and can close airports and trails at short notice. Respect exclusion zones and the authorities' instructions.
- **Health:** no mandatory vaccinations, except yellow fever if you arrive from a country at risk. Before you go, see a travel medicine clinic: for some areas (rural Sumatra, Flores, Papua) malaria prophylaxis is considered.
- **Safety:** the country is calm for travelers. The real risks are scooter accidents, currents, volcanoes and extremely strict drug laws. In the Papua region gov.uk advises caution, especially in Highland Papua and Central Papua. Up-to-date advice is on viaggiaresicuri.it (for Italians) or your own government's site.
- **Nyepi:** the Balinese New Year (March, date varies) shuts Bali down for 24 hours and closes the airport.
- **From the airport to the centre:** at **Bali Ngurah Rai (DPS)** **official taxis** are paid at the arrivals desk, at a fixed price per zone: about 150,000-200,000 IDR to Kuta, Seminyak or Sanur, about 350,000 IDR to Ubud, an hour and a half or more away · **Grab and Gojek** cost less, but the pickup point changes often: check it in the app before walking out · drivers booked through the hotel wait with a sign · ignore anyone in arrivals who presents himself as an "official" driver · at **Jakarta Soekarno-Hatta (CGK)** the Railink train reaches the centre in about 50 minutes, about 70,000 IDR; Bluebird taxis are metered, plus tolls.

## When to go: the island calendar
The islands are interchangeable, and the calendar is the easiest way to choose. From May to September almost the whole archipelago is in the dry season, which is also high season. From November to March it rains more across much of the country, and Raja Ampat is in its best season.

| Island or area | Best time | Avoid or good to know |
|---|---|---|
| Bali and the Nusas | April-October | Nyepi in March; rain from November to March |
| Lombok and the Gilis | dry season, April-October | Rinjani closes in the first months of the year (in 2026 from January 1 to March 31) |
| Java | May-September | clearer skies for the Bromo sunrise |
| Sumatra | June-September | muddy trails in the rains |
| Mentawai | May-September | heavy rain from November to January |
| Komodo | April-December | rougher seas in the rainy months |
| Flores | May-October | muddy roads and trails in the rains |
| Sulawesi | May-October | long roads to Toraja in any season |
| Raja Ampat | October-April | May-September more wind, but fewer people |
| Indonesian Borneo | roughly May-September, the driest season | it rains a lot outside it too |

**In practice:** in July and August Bali, the Nusas, Komodo, Sulawesi, Sumatra and the Mentawai all work. In October you can pair Sulawesi or Komodo with Raja Ampat. Between December and March, if you want the sea, the safest choice is Raja Ampat.

## Itinerario isola per isola

### Bali
**What to do and see:** the rice terraces of Tegalalang, Jatiluwih and Sidemen, Ubud with the Monkey Forest and its temples, sunrise on Mount Batur, the north with the Munduk waterfalls and Lake Beratan, the Uluwatu coast with its cliff temple and the Kecak dance at sunset, the sea at Amed. Bali is two different islands depending on where you sleep. The south between Canggu and Seminyak is concrete, traffic and bars for visitors. Just twenty kilometers inland, the flower offerings reappear in front of the houses. What stuck with me most from Bali is the Kecak dance: seventy men sitting in a circle making all the music with their voices.

**Getting there and around:** international flights to Denpasar (DPS). On the island, a private driver, Grab or Gojek, or a scooter only if you have real experience and an international driving permit.

**When to go:** April-October. Watch out for Nyepi in March.

**How many days:** at least a week, with two bases: one inland (Ubud or Munduk) and one on the coast (Uluwatu).

**In practice:** Bali tourist levy 150,000 IDR per person, paid online on the official Love Bali site · Kecak at Uluwatu at sunset with a separate ticket: arrive an hour early for a seat · for sunrise on Batur you leave around 3-4 a.m. with a local guide · on Nyepi the island shuts down for 24 hours, airport included.

**Read more:** [the Bali page](/destinations/indonesia/bali), [Java, Bali and the Gilis in 14 days](/trips/java-bali-gili-itinerary), [Bali, the Nusas and Komodo in 14 days](/trips/bali-nusa-penida-komodo-itinerary).

### Nusa Penida and Nusa Lembongan
**What to do and see:** on Nusa Penida, Kelingking Beach, Broken Beach, Angel's Billabong, Diamond Beach and snorkeling with manta rays at Manta Point. On Nusa Lembongan, the mangroves, Devil's Tear and the Yellow Bridge to Nusa Ceningan. Nusa Penida is a victim of its own success: by midday Kelingking is a line of people on a steep trail.

**Getting there and around:** fast boats from Sanur, about half an hour, with several departures a day. Local boats run between Lembongan and Penida. On the island the roads are narrow and steep: a driver is better. On arrival you pay a local tax, separate from Bali's.

**When to go:** like Bali, April-October, with calmer seas for the crossings.

**How many days:** a day trip from Bali is possible, but with lines. Three days, sleeping on the islands, let you reach the viewpoints before the boats from Sanur. I spent three nights on Penida and explored all of it at an easy pace: that's the rhythm I recommend.

**In practice:** fast boat from Sanur about 30-45 minutes, buy it at the pier or from a known operator · be at Kelingking by 8 a.m. · the descent to the beach is steep with no continuous railings: closed shoes needed · carry cash in rupiah on the islands.

**Read more:** [the Nusa Penida day trip](/experiences/indonesia/nusa-penida-day-trip), [Bali, the Nusas and Komodo in 14 days](/trips/bali-nusa-penida-komodo-itinerary).

### Lombok and the Gilis
**What to do and see:** the Mount Rinjani trek to the crater rim above Lake Segara Anak, the Senaru waterfalls, the southern beaches of Kuta, Tanjung Aan and Selong Belanak, and the three Gilis with no cars or scooters. Rinjani is the hardest trek I've done in Indonesia: the climb ends on volcanic sand where every three steps you lose one. Waking up above the lake with the clouds below makes up for everything. On the Gilis you see turtles straight from the beach. Trawangan is more of a party island at night than you'd expect, Meno is the opposite.

**Getting there and around:** fly to Lombok (LOP) or take a fast boat from Bali, from Padang Bai or Sanur. On the Gilis, only cidomo, the horse carts, and bicycles. On the rest of Lombok, a car with driver or a scooter.

**When to go:** the dry season. Rinjani closes in the rainy months: in 2026 it stayed closed from January 1 to March 31. Even in the dry season the park can close single trails because of bad weather or accidents. A guide and a licensed operator are mandatory, and tickets are booked on the park's official E-Rinjani app (Android and iOS). Note: the erinjani.id website now redirects to a private agency, not to the park.

**How many days:** a week: three days for Rinjani, the rest between the Gilis and the south.

**In practice:** Rinjani in three days and two nights, with the ticket on the E-Rinjani app and an authorized operator · fast boat Bali-Gilis about two hours, cancelled in rough seas · no motor vehicles on the Gilis: on foot, by bike or by cidomo · cash for Gili Meno and Gili Air, where ATMs are few.

**Read more:** [the Lombok and Gili page](/destinations/indonesia/lombok-gili), [the Rinjani trek](/experiences/indonesia/mount-rinjani-trek), [Java, Bali and the Gilis in 14 days](/trips/java-bali-gili-itinerary).

### Java
**What to do and see:** Borobudur at sunrise and Prambanan at sunset, the kraton and batik workshops of Yogyakarta, sunrise on Bromo and the blue fire of Kawah Ijen. Java is the part of Indonesia that feels least like a vacation and leaves the sharpest memory. At Ijen we climbed in the dark with gas masks on, and next to us passed miners carrying eighty-kilo baskets of sulfur on their shoulders. The Bromo sunrise is spectacular, but you share it with hundreds of jeeps.

**Getting there and around:** fly to Jakarta, Yogyakarta or Surabaya. Trains between the main cities, minivans and 4x4 jeeps for Bromo, a car with driver for Ijen. Tickets to climb Borobudur are capped and sell out early: buy them in advance.

**When to go:** May-September, with the clearest skies for the sunrises.

**How many days:** a week between Yogyakarta, Bromo and Ijen.

**In practice:** buy the ticket to climb Borobudur online days ahead · trains between Yogyakarta, Surabaya and Probolinggo can be booked on the official railway app, Access by KAI · for Ijen you leave at night, around 1 a.m., with a guide and a gas mask; the crater can close because of the gases · at Bromo it's cold before dawn: bring heavy layers.

**Read more:** [the Java page](/destinations/indonesia/java), [Bromo sunrise by jeep](/experiences/indonesia/bromo-sunrise-jeep), [Java, Bali and the Gilis in 14 days](/trips/java-bali-gili-itinerary).

### Sumatra
**What to do and see:** the orangutans in the jungle at Bukit Lawang, Lake Toba and Samosir island with its Batak villages, the volcanoes of Berastagi. Further south, the Bukittinggi highlands, with the Harau Valley and Lake Maninjau. At Bukit Lawang we climbed through mud clinging to roots, and after three hours of seeing nothing an orangutan came down onto a branch a few meters away. Some guides lure the animals with food: refuse it explicitly when you book.

**Getting there and around:** fly to Medan for the north, to Padang for Bukittinggi and the Mentawai. Car with driver or bus. Mountain roads are much slower than they look.

**When to go:** June-September, the driest season for trekking.

**How many days:** at least a week for the north, between Bukit Lawang, Berastagi and Toba.

**In practice:** Medan to Bukit Lawang about three or four hours by road · trekking only with a licensed guide, as a day trip or with one or two nights in the jungle · for Samosir, ferry from Parapat to Tuktuk, under an hour · cash for the villages, ATMs are only in the bigger towns.

**Read more:** [the Sumatra page](/destinations/indonesia/sumatra), [orangutan trekking](/experiences/indonesia/orangutan-trekking-bukit-lawang), [Sumatra and the Mentawai in 14 days](/trips/sumatra-mentawai-itinerary).

### The Mentawai
**What to do and see:** a few days in the Siberut jungle with a Mentawai family, accompanied by Mentawai guides, then the beaches and surf of Sipora. I went with Mentawai Tribe, and these places are incredible. It's not for everyone: mud everywhere, insects, no electricity, you sleep on a mat. And there's an ethical side to face honestly: you're a guest in someone's home, not at a show.

**Getting there and around:** the Mentawai Fast ferry from Padang to Siberut and to Tuapejat, on Sipora. Times and prices change often, and in rough seas the boats don't leave: you need buffer days. On Siberut, motorized canoe and trekking. Surfers pay a local surf tax, amount to be checked.

**When to go:** May-September. From November to January it rains a lot.

**How many days:** a week: three or four days in the jungle, the rest on Sipora.

**In practice:** Mentawai Fast from Padang, a few hours' crossing, with times to reconfirm the day before · on Siberut you need strong repellent, mud shoes and a visit to a travel medicine clinic before leaving · cash for the whole stay · surf tax only for surfers.

**Read more:** [the Mentawai page](/destinations/indonesia/mentawai), [Sumatra and the Mentawai in 14 days](/trips/sumatra-mentawai-itinerary).

### Komodo and Flores
**What to do and see:** the dragons on Rinca and Komodo Island, Padar at sunrise, Pink Beach and snorkeling between Kanawa, Kelor and Manta Point. On Flores, the Trans-Flores highway, the villages of Bena and Wae Rebo, sunrise over the three lakes of Kelimutu. The dragons almost always stay still: what impresses you is their size, and the ranger holding nothing but a forked stick. Rinca is wilder and less crowded than Komodo, and it's worth asking for it specifically. Flores is the part of Indonesia that got left behind, and that's exactly what makes it the most interesting for me. Wae Rebo is the most powerful thing on the island: three hours climbing through the forest, then seven conical houses in a clearing among the mountains.

**Getting there and around:** direct flights from Bali and Jakarta to Labuan Bajo, the departure port for all park tours. In the park, only by boat, as a day trip or with nights on board. On Flores, a car with driver along the Trans-Flores: two hundred kilometers can take a whole day. For the east of the island, fly to Ende or Maumere. Since October 30, 2024, park entry for foreigners costs 250,000 IDR per person, including trekking and snorkeling.

**When to go:** April-December for Komodo, May-October for Flores.

**How many days:** three or four days for Komodo, a week if you add Flores. Together they easily make ten days.

**In practice:** park entry 250,000 IDR per person for foreigners · for sunrise on Padar the boat leaves Labuan Bajo before 5 a.m., or you sleep on board · choose licensed operators with life jackets on board · at Wae Rebo you sleep in the village, walking up from the village of Denge.

**Read more:** [the Komodo page](/destinations/indonesia/komodo), [the Flores page](/destinations/indonesia/flores), [the Komodo dragon trek](/experiences/indonesia/komodo-dragon-trek), [Kelimutu sunrise](/experiences/indonesia/kelimutu-sunrise), [Bali, the Nusas and Komodo in 14 days](/trips/bali-nusa-penida-komodo-itinerary).

### Sulawesi
**What to do and see:** in the south, Tana Toraja, with its tongkonan villages, rock tombs and Toraja funerals; Makassar with Fort Rotterdam. In the north, Manado, the Tangkoko reserve with its tarsiers, the Tomohon highlands and the walls of Bunaken marine park. Tana Toraja is the most different thing I've seen in Indonesia. A Toraja funeral lasts days, gathers hundreds of people and includes the sacrifice of buffalo in front of everyone. Bunaken is the other side: coral walls dropping sheer, where you turn onto your back and there's no bottom below.

**Getting there and around:** fly to Makassar for the south, to Manado for the north. From Makassar to Rantepao it's 8-10 hours by car or bus, including overnight. There are flights to Toraja airport, but they're few and can be canceled. Between Makassar and Manado you fly. To organize the island I recommend Wale Adventure, based in Tomohon: private tours in the north and to Toraja (for transparency, I built their website).

**When to go:** May-October.

**How many days:** a week for Toraja and the north, more if you add other areas. Toraja alone takes three days plus two on the road.

**In practice:** Makassar to Rantepao 8-10 hours, night buses are the most common option · the big Toraja funerals are often concentrated in the summer months: go with a local guide and a small gift for the family · Tangkoko's tarsiers are seen at dusk with a ranger · Bunaken is under an hour by boat from Manado.

**Read more:** [the Sulawesi page](/destinations/indonesia/sulawesi), [diving in Bunaken](/experiences/indonesia/diving-bunaken-national-park), [Bali, Sulawesi and Raja Ampat in 12 days](/trips/bali-sulawesi-raja-ampat-itinerary).

### Raja Ampat
**What to do and see:** snorkeling and diving around Kri, Arborek, Cape Kri and the Fam Islands, the Piaynemo viewpoint, the homestay villages. Further south, Misool, with a liveaboard. It's the most beautiful place I've seen underwater, and also the hardest to reach. If you want to stay on Arborek, you'll find homestays on the local homestay portal, stayrajaampat.com (for example Worisun and Adefiro). Staying in homestays instead of resorts is the choice worth making, because that model keeps the park's protection going.

**Getting there and around:** fly to Sorong from Jakarta, Makassar and Manado, or from Bali on Garuda's direct flight in the months it runs (in 2026-2027 from late October to March). Then the ferry to Waisai, about 2 hours, with an early-afternoon departure and, not every day, a morning one. Between the islands, only homestay boats, private boats or liveaboards. The park permit (PIN) is mandatory and is paid in Sorong or at Waisai port: the amount for foreigners changed in 2026 and needs to be checked at the time. There are no ATMs on the islands.

**When to go:** October-April, with calmer seas, manta rays and big pelagics. From May to September the southeast winds blow: transfers take longer and outings to exposed areas can be canceled. We went in August: the sea was spectacular anyway, ten days without rain, but the wind made itself felt. And in summer you see fewer big pelagics.

**How many days:** a week at the very least. With less, you spend more time traveling than in the water.

**In practice:** Sorong-Waisai ferry about 2 hours · park permit paid in Sorong or Waisai, 2026 amount to be checked · withdraw all the cash for your stay in Sorong, there are no ATMs on the islands · book homestays ahead, usually full board, with boat transfers paid separately.

**Read more:** [the Raja Ampat page](/destinations/indonesia/raja-ampat), [the Raja Ampat liveaboard](/experiences/indonesia/raja-ampat-liveaboard-diving), [Bali, Sulawesi and Raja Ampat in 12 days](/trips/bali-sulawesi-raja-ampat-itinerary).

### Indonesian Borneo
**What to do and see:** Kalimantan is the Indonesian part of Borneo. The best-known place is **Tanjung Puting** National Park, in Central Kalimantan: you travel upriver on a **klotok**, the wooden boat where you eat and sleep, to Camp Leakey, the research centre opened in 1971 where rescued and orphaned orangutans were studied and returned to the forest. Here you'll find the basics.

**Getting there and around:** fly to Pangkalan Bun, with connections from Jakarta or Surabaya, then by road to the port of Kumai, where the klotoks leave from. Tours are organized with local operators.

**When to go:** roughly the driest season, from May to September.

**How many days:** three or four days on the river, a week including travel.

**In practice:** klotok trips usually last three days and two nights, with meals and guide included · book with a local operator and ask whether the park permit is included · go to the orangutan feeding points at the times set by the rangers · repellent and long sleeves for evenings on the river.

**Read more:** [Malaysian Borneo](/destinations/borneo), with the orangutans of Sepilok and the Kinabatangan River, for those who want Borneo with more infrastructure.

## How to combine them: the islands are interchangeable
There's no set route. You start from the month you're traveling and the time you have, and you choose. With two weeks, two islands is the maximum if you don't want to rush. With three or four weeks you can add one or two. The air hubs help: from Bali you fly to Labuan Bajo and Lombok, and to Manado and Sorong direct or with a stopover depending on the season; from Makassar to Toraja, Manado and Sorong; from Jakarta to Yogyakarta, Medan and Padang.

**Ready-made examples on the site:**
- **Two weeks between May and September, classic Indonesia:** [Java, Bali and the Gilis](/trips/java-bali-gili-itinerary).
- **Two weeks between April and October, sea and dragons:** [Bali, Nusa Penida, Nusa Lembongan and Komodo](/trips/bali-nusa-penida-komodo-itinerary).
- **Twelve days, sea and forest away from the crowds:** [Bali, Sulawesi and Raja Ampat](/trips/bali-sulawesi-raja-ampat-itinerary), with Sulawesi-only (May-October) and Raja Ampat-only (October-April) versions.
- **Two weeks between May and September, jungle and surf:** [Sumatra and the Mentawai](/trips/sumatra-mentawai-itinerary).
- **Five weeks, from Sumatra to Sulawesi:** [Indonesia in 35 days](/trips/indonesia-itinerary).

**Combinations that work less well:** Raja Ampat with the western islands in the middle of winter, when it rains on Bali and Java. Toraja and Raja Ampat together in a few days, because the road to Toraja eats up two days. Rinjani between January and March, when it's closed.

- **Bali:** the rice terraces, Ubud and the Kecak dance at Uluwatu.
- **Nusa Penida:** Kelingking at sunrise and the manta rays.
- **Lombok and the Gilis:** Rinjani and turtles from the beach.
- **Java:** Borobudur, Bromo and the blue fire of Ijen.
- **Sumatra:** the orangutans of Bukit Lawang and Lake Toba.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't cram too many islands into one trip:** each deserves at least a week.
- **Choose the island by the month:** Raja Ampat runs opposite to the rest of the archipelago.
- **Leave a margin between flights and boats:** routes and times change, and the sea decides.
- **Certified guides, always:** for the orangutans, for Rinjani and for the dragons.
- **Refuse orangutan baiting** with food when you book in Bukit Lawang.
- **Buy your Borobudur ticket right away:** the climb is capped.
- **Gas mask at Ijen**, no exceptions.
- **Scooter only if you really know how to ride**, with an international driving permit.
- **Cash outside Bali and Java:** ATMs are rare.
- **Respect the people who host you:** in the Mentawai, in Toraja, in Wae Rebo you ask before taking photos.
- **Fill in the e-VOA and All Indonesia Arrival Card** on the official portals before every entry.

## Chiusura personale
The islands I've seen feel like different countries. Rinjani is the hardest trek I've done in Indonesia; in Bukit Lawang, after three hours in the mud without seeing anything, an orangutan came down onto a branch a few metres away; Tana Toraja is the most different thing I've seen; Raja Ampat the most beautiful place I've seen underwater, and also the most tiring to reach.

My one piece of advice: pick one or two islands based on the month and give each of them at least a week. I went back above all for the people: the smiles, like in Thailand.
