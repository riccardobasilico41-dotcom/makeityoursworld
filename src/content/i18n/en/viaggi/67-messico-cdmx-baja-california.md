# Mexico City and Baja California Sur: From the Capital to the Whale Sea in 16 Days

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (trip done by Rick; itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 16 days, 15 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independent, using the metro and apps in Mexico City, a domestic flight to La Paz and a rental car in Baja California Sur
**Categoria:** Nord America · Messico · Città del Messico · Baja California · balene · mare · natura · roadtrip

---

## Apertura personale
It's two different Mexicos, one after the other. The first is Mexico City: twenty million people at 7,349 feet (2,240 meters), the pyramids of Teotihuacán an hour away by bus, the most important archaeological museum in the Americas, and street food that on its own justifies the trip. The second is Baja California Sur, the long, narrow peninsula between the Pacific and the Sea of Cortez: giant-cactus desert running right down to the water, uninhabited islands, fishing villages and, in winter, one of the largest gatherings of cetaceans on the planet.

The reason to go to Baja in February and March is the animals. **Gray whales** come down from the Arctic to give birth in the Pacific lagoons and swim up to the fishermen's boats; in La Paz Bay you swim alongside the **whale shark**; in Loreto's national park the **blue whale** passes through, the largest animal that ever lived; at Los Cabos the humpbacks breach. This itinerary joins the two halves with a two-hour flight: five days in the capital, then a road trip from La Paz to Cabo San Lucas via the whale lagoons and Loreto. It's for people who love nature and the sea, don't mind driving long, empty roads, and also want to see the big city.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go, animal by animal:**
  - **Gray whales** (Pacific lagoons): the official season runs from mid-December to the end of April at San Ignacio and Ojo de Liebre, and from January to the end of April at Bahía Magdalena. The best period, with mothers and calves in the lagoons, is **mid-February to the end of March**.
  - **Whale shark** (La Paz Bay): from October-November to April. The season only opens once enough animals are in the refuge zone; in 2025-2026 it started in late November. In recent seasons there have been temporary suspensions because of scarce sightings.
  - **Blue whale** (Loreto): mid-January to the end of March.
  - **Humpback whales** (Los Cabos): December to April, more numerous between January and March.
  - **Sea lions** at Los Islotes (Espíritu Santo): all year, but in the summer breeding season the swimming rules are stricter.
- **Month by month, in brief:** December and January have whale sharks and humpbacks but few gray whales and cool water (68-72 °F / 20-22 °C); **February and March** are the full months; April has warmer water but the whales are leaving; May to October is very hot, with a tropical sea and hurricane season (peak August-October), and no whales. In Mexico City, February and March are dry and sunny, with cool nights.
- **Documents:** passport valid for at least 6 months on arrival, no visa for up to 180 days. At Mexico City airport you go through the e-gates, which issue the entry permit automatically; border police may ask for your accommodation booking and your onward ticket, so keep them handy. E-cigarettes and refill liquids are banned in Mexico. To rent a car, the Italian foreign ministry (Farnesina) recommends an **international driving permit** in addition to your Italian license, and notes that foreign car insurance policies aren't valid in Mexico. It's worth registering with **Dove Siamo nel Mondo** (the Italian travelers' registry).
- **Currency and payments:** Mexican peso (MXN), roughly **€1 = 20 MXN** in 2026. In Baja California Sur many tourist prices are in US dollars; paying in pesos is almost always cheaper. You need cash for the lagoon pangas, fishermen's cooperatives, gas stations and small towns.
- **Getting around:** in Mexico City, metro (5 MXN ticket), Metrobús and ride apps; for Teotihuacán, the bus from the Terminal del Norte. **Mexico City-La Paz** flight (about 2 hours 15 minutes, Aeroméxico, Volaris, Viva). In Baja a **rental car** is almost essential: Águila buses link the towns but not the lagoons or the beaches. Fly home from **San José del Cabo airport (SJD)**, with flights to Mexico City and direct to the United States.
- **Driving in Baja:** Mexican liability insurance is mandatory and often isn't included in online booking prices, so the final bill at the counter is higher. Don't drive at night: livestock on the road, no lighting and roads without shoulders. There are military checkpoints along the Transpeninsular, routine and quick. Fill up whenever you can on the long stretches (Ciudad Constitución-Loreto, Loreto-La Paz).
- **Safety:** Mexico City needs the care of any big city: no street-hailed taxis (Viaggiare Sicuri recommends taxis de sitio booked through the hotel, or Uber), watch for pickpockets in the metro, bus terminals and markets, and avoid the Tepito and Iztapalapa neighborhoods and, outside the city, Ecatepec, Naucalpan and Ciudad Nezahualcóyotl. Baja California Sur is not among the areas of particular caution for the Farnesina, which instead asks for maximum caution in the state of **Baja California**, to the north (Tijuana, Ensenada); in Baja California Sur the main risks are the road at night, currents on the Pacific beaches and the sun. **Michoacán** and Guerrero, outside this itinerary, are among the states to avoid.
- **Health:** no mandatory vaccinations; hepatitis A and typhoid are recommended. Drink bottled water. Public hospitals don't treat foreigners and private ones are very expensive: health insurance with repatriation is essential. In Mexico City, in the dry season (November-May), air pollution reaches high peaks. In the city you feel the altitude for the first two days. In Baja bring sunscreen, a hat and plenty of water even in winter; for boat trips, take a seasickness pill before setting out.
- **Time zone:** Mexico City is UTC-6 all year (7 hours behind Italy in winter). Baja California Sur is on **UTC-7**, one hour behind the capital: keep it in mind for the flight.
- **Electricity:** 127 V, type A and B plugs, you need an adapter.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Mexico City
We landed at Benito Juárez airport (AICM) and got into the city by prepaid authorized taxi or by app. The base for the days in the city is **Roma Norte** or **Condesa**, two early-20th-century neighborhoods with tree-lined avenues, parks and plenty of places to eat and drink, convenient and safe. We kept the first afternoon light: between jet lag and altitude, your body needs a day.

In the evening we strolled through Parque México and had our first tacos al pastor, sliced off the vertical spit with a piece of pineapple on top.

### Day 2 — The Centro Histórico
The heart of the city is best explored on foot. The **Zócalo**, one of the largest squares in the world; the **Metropolitan Cathedral**, which visibly leans because it's built on the bed of a drained lake; the **Templo Mayor**, the main temple of Tenochtitlán, rediscovered in 1978 during metro works; the **Palacio Nacional**, with Diego Rivera's murals on the country's history. Then we walked the pedestrian Madero street, as far as the Casa de los Azulejos and the **Palacio de Bellas Artes**, with murals by Orozco and Siqueiros inside.

Sunset is best seen from above, from the viewing deck of the **Torre Latinoamericana** or from the café terraces above Bellas Artes. In the evening the center empties out: for dinner it's better to head back to Roma or Condesa.

**In practice**
- The Templo Mayor and the Anthropology Museum are closed on Mondays.
- Palacio Nacional: you enter with your passport, hours vary and there are lines.
- Metro: single fare 5 MXN; at rush hour the cars at the front of the train are reserved for women.

### Day 3 — Teotihuacán
**Teotihuacán**, an hour by bus from the Terminal del Norte, was the largest city in the Americas around 500 AD. We walked along the **Calzada de los Muertos**, between the Pyramid of the Sun and the Pyramid of the Moon, with the temple of the Feathered Serpent at the end of the Ciudadela. It's best to be at the gate when it opens at 9, with water and a hat: the site is huge and there's no shade.

From 2026 admission for foreigners costs about **210 MXN**. If you like, you can go up in a hot-air balloon at dawn (roughly 2,500-4,000 MXN). On the way back you can stop at the **Basilica of Guadalupe**, the most visited Catholic shrine in the Americas.

### Day 4 — Chapultepec and the Museo Nacional de Antropología
The **Museo Nacional de Antropología** is worth a whole morning on its own: the Aztec Sun Stone, the Olmec heads, the Maya halls with the reconstruction of Pakal's tomb. From 2026 the ticket for foreigners costs about **210 MXN**. The museum is inside **Chapultepec Forest**, the city's largest park, where on the hill stands the castle that was Emperor Maximilian's residence, now a history museum.

We spent the afternoon among cafés and galleries, in Polanco or back in Roma. If it's a Tuesday, Friday or Sunday, in the evening you can go to the **Arena México** for **lucha libre**, Mexican masked wrestling: tickets are bought at the arena box office or online, and you go and come back by taxi or app.

### Day 5 — Coyoacán and Xochimilco
**Coyoacán** is a colonial village swallowed up by the city: squares, churches, the market with seafood tostadas and Frida Kahlo's **Casa Azul**, to be booked online a few days ahead. A few blocks away is Trotsky's house-museum.

In the afternoon we went to **Xochimilco**, to the canals left over from the ancient lake, which you travel on **trajineras**, the colorful flat-bottomed boats, with mariachis and food vendors pulling alongside in their own boats. The boat price is set by the hour (roughly 600-700 MXN an hour for the whole boat) and should be agreed at the dock before you get on.

### Day 6 — Flight to La Paz and the Malecón
On the morning flight we reached **La Paz** (about 2 hours 15 minutes), gaining an hour with the time difference. We picked up the car at the airport, which you need right away to get into town, and settled in near the **Malecón**, the three-mile (five-kilometer) waterfront promenade where families, cyclists and ice cream vendors come out to walk in the evening.

La Paz is the state capital, but it has the pace of a provincial town. The daily appointment is sunset over the Sea of Cortez, with the promenade sculptures in silhouette.

### Day 7 — The whale shark and Playa Balandra
We dedicated the morning to the **whale shark**, the largest fish in the world, which in winter feeds on plankton in a refuge zone of the bay, near the sandspit of El Mogote. You head out by boat with an operator authorized by CONANP and swim at the surface next to the animal, in small groups and in turns of a few minutes. The rules are strict: no touching, minimum distance, no flash. The season only opens when the sharks are there, so check before booking.

In the afternoon we went to **Playa Balandra**, a bay of very shallow turquoise water among red hills, 25 minutes by car. It's a protected area with limited access in two shifts (8-12 and 13-17, extended to 19 at Easter, in summer and in December; 450 people per shift), to be booked on the CONANP protected areas portal. Entry costs 120 MXN per person per day, half for students.

**In practice**
- Whale shark tour: roughly 1,500-2,500 MXN per person, plus the sanctuary wristband (215 MXN for foreigners in the 2025-2026 season).
- Check that the operator is on CONANP's official list for the current season.
- Water at 68-72 °F (20-22 °C) in February: a short wetsuit is useful.
- If Balandra is full, nearby Playa El Tecolote and Pichilingue have no shifts.

### Day 8 — Isla Espíritu Santo and the sea lions of Los Islotes
We spent the whole day on a boat heading to the **Parque Nacional Archipiélago Espíritu Santo**, a UNESCO World Heritage site: an uninhabited volcanic island with red cliffs, white-sand bays and clear water. The stop everyone is waiting for is **Los Islotes**, the rocks at the northern tip where a sea lion colony lives: you snorkel while the young ones come up curious and circle around the swimmers.

The day usually includes lunch on the beach in the bay of Ensenada Grande and a few stops along the coast, sometimes with dolphins or mantas. You leave early from the Malecón or from Pichilingue and return in the afternoon.

*Good to know: the tour costs roughly 2,500-3,500 MXN per person, plus the park fee (brazalete) if it isn't included. The sea can be rough: with strong north wind the trips are canceled, so it's best to keep a spare day in La Paz.*

### Day 9 — To Bahía Magdalena: the gray whales
We headed for the Pacific: about three hours by car through the cardón desert, to **Puerto Adolfo López Mateos** or **Puerto San Carlos**, the two fishing villages on **Bahía Magdalena**. Here every winter the gray whales give birth and nurse their calves in the lagoon, after a journey of over six thousand miles (ten thousand kilometers) from the Arctic.

Outings are by panga, the fishermen's boats, with local cooperatives. In the lagoon the mothers often come up to the boats with their calf, and sometimes they let themselves be touched: it's the whales' own behavior, not something to go looking for. The indicative price is around **1,500-2,000 MXN an hour for the whole boat** (up to 6-8 people), and it's worth joining other travelers at the dock. You sleep in one of the two villages, in simple lodgings.

*Good to know: the best outings are in the morning, when the wind is lighter. If you arrive in the afternoon, your first outing is the next day.*

### Day 10 — A second lagoon outing and the road to Loreto
We went back to the lagoon at dawn, when it's flat. Then we crossed the peninsula again toward the Sea of Cortez: from Ciudad Constitución to **Loreto** the road climbs into the Sierra de la Giganta and drops to the sea, about two and a half to three hours in all.

Loreto is a **Pueblo Mágico** founded in 1697 by the Jesuits. The **Misión de Nuestra Señora de Loreto** was the first mission in the Californias, and from here the colonization set out that reached all the way to San Francisco. The center is small and neat, with a quiet malecón and seafood restaurants.

### Day 11 — Loreto: the blue whale and Isla Coronado
The **Parque Nacional Bahía de Loreto** is one of the spots in the Sea of Cortez where the **blue whale** feeds in winter, along with fin whales and dolphins. Trips leave from the malecón with authorized lancheros and last about five hours; the indicative price starts at 3,000 MXN per person. The season runs from mid-January to the end of March.

If you'd rather have a quieter day, or have already seen the whales, you can go to **Isla Coronado**, with a white beach and snorkeling water, or head up to the **Misión de San Javier**, an hour and a half away in the sierra, one of the best-preserved missions on the peninsula.

### Day 12 — From Loreto to Todos Santos
This is the long leg of the road trip: about five and a half hours of driving south, first on the Transpeninsular as far as Ciudad Insurgentes, then past La Paz to **Todos Santos**, on the Pacific. We left early to arrive in daylight. After La Paz the road crosses the desert and reaches the ocean right on the Tropic of Cancer.

Todos Santos is another Pueblo Mágico, with colonial houses, art galleries, cafés and a more relaxed feel than Cabo. In the evening we had dinner in the center.

### Day 13 — Todos Santos, Playa Cerritos and the turtles
In the morning we were at the beach at **Playa Cerritos**, 15 minutes south, the only one in the area where you can swim with reasonable safety and where you can take surf lessons. The other Pacific beaches here have strong currents and breaking waves.

At sunset, in hatching seasons, Todos Santos's conservation projects release into the sea the turtles born in their nurseries (in winter mostly leatherback and olive ridley turtles). You take part with a small donation, following the volunteers' instructions. Dates depend on the hatchings and have to be asked about in town.

### Day 14 — Cabo San Lucas: El Arco and the humpbacks
An hour's drive took us to **Cabo San Lucas**, the tip of the peninsula. By boat, from the marina, you go to **El Arco**, the rock arch where the Sea of Cortez meets the Pacific, to the sea lion colony and to **Playa del Amor**, the beach squeezed between two seas.

From December to April the waters off Cabo are full of **humpback whales**. Whale-watching trips last two to three hours and on shared boats cost roughly 80-130 dollars per person (about 1,400-2,200 MXN), less on the big boats and more on the small ones; a private boat for eight people comes to about 700 dollars. Cabo is the most touristy and expensive part of the trip, with the nightlife of the Marina; to sleep, many people prefer San José del Cabo.

### Day 15 — San José del Cabo and Cabo Pulmo
For the last full day there are two options. **San José del Cabo** is the colonial, quiet part of Los Cabos, with the plaza, the mission and the gallery district; on Thursday evenings, from November to June, there's the **Art Walk**, with galleries open and music in the street.

Anyone with energy goes to **Cabo Pulmo**, about two hours north-east (the last few miles on a dirt road): a national park with the only hard-coral reef in the Gulf of California, protected since 1995 and full of fish again. Snorkeling and diving are only done with local operators and in authorized zones.

### Day 16 — Return from San José del Cabo
We returned the car at **San José del Cabo** airport, from where you fly to Mexico City, connecting to Europe, or via the United States. Better to leave a wide margin: between the car check, check-in and a connection in Mexico City, you should allow at least four hours between flights.

## Variations
**12 days, without Loreto.** Four days in Mexico City, four in La Paz (whale shark, Balandra, Espíritu Santo and a day trip to Bahía Magdalena leaving at dawn), one in Todos Santos and two in Los Cabos. You miss the blue whale, and you drive a lot less.

**The more "sociable" gray whales: San Ignacio.** **Laguna San Ignacio**, further north, is the lagoon where close encounters with the whales are most frequent, inside the El Vizcaíno Biosphere Reserve. From Loreto it's about four hours by car north, so it should be added as a 2-3 day extension, with tent camps on the lagoon or lodgings in San Ignacio. Further north still, **Ojo de Liebre**, near Guerrero Negro, is the lagoon with the highest number of whales.

**The north of Baja by car.** With three weeks you can do the whole Transpeninsular from Tijuana or Ensenada (Valle de Guadalupe wines) down to Cabo, about 1,050 miles (1,700 km), bearing in mind that the Farnesina asks for maximum caution in the state of Baja California because of the presence of criminal groups; or cross the Sea of Cortez on the **Baja Ferries** ferry from La Paz (Pichilingue) to Mazatlán or Topolobampo and continue on the mainland.

**Outside whale season.** From May to October Baja remains a trip of sea, desert and snorkeling, but without cetaceans, with strong heat and possible hurricanes between August and October. The whale shark returns between October and November.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
Indicative ranges per night for a double room in high season (February-March).
- **Mexico City:** Roma Norte or Condesa. Budget: hostels and private rooms 600-1,200 MXN. Mid-range: boutique hotels 1,800-3,500 MXN. High-end: design hotels or in Polanco over 5,000 MXN.
- **La Paz:** on the Malecón or in the center just behind the waterfront, everything on foot. Budget: hostels and small hotels 700-1,200 MXN. Mid-range: waterfront hotels 1,800-3,000 MXN. High-end: resorts toward Pichilingue and Costa Baja over 4,500 MXN.
- **Bahía Magdalena:** Puerto Adolfo López Mateos or Puerto San Carlos, simple lodgings and small motels 700-1,500 MXN; the organized tent camps on the bay's islands are the high end, sold as packages.
- **Loreto:** the historic center near the mission and the malecón, 1,200-2,500 MXN for mid-range; resorts in the Nopoló area for high-end.
- **Todos Santos:** historic center, with small charming mid- and high-range hotels (2,000-5,000 MXN) and a few budget guesthouses; sleeping here costs less than in Cabo.
- **Los Cabos:** San José del Cabo for mid-range and a quiet atmosphere (1,800-3,500 MXN); the "Corridor" between the two towns for high-end resorts, often over 7,000 MXN; Cabo San Lucas for those who want nightlife.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
- **Mexico City:** tacos al pastor, de suadero and de canasta; tamales and atole in the morning; quesadillas (which here may come without cheese, you have to ask); churros with chocolate; the markets of Coyoacán and San Juan; mezcalerías in Roma. The chef-driven restaurants of Polanco and Roma need to be booked weeks ahead.
- **La Paz and the Sea of Cortez:** fish and shrimp tacos fried in batter, the most typical breakfast in Baja; ceviche, aguachile, almejas chocolatas (big clams cooked on the embers), pescado zarandeado.
- **Bahía Magdalena and Loreto:** whatever the fishermen bring in, grilled or as ceviche, in family restaurants; in Loreto the clams and the catch of the day on the malecón.
- **Todos Santos and Los Cabos:** more international and pricier cooking, with organic farms from the Todos Santos area and high-end restaurants in the Corridor; the street tacos of San José del Cabo remain the simplest option.
- **To drink:** damiana, the herbal liqueur of Baja; wines from the Valle de Guadalupe, in the north of the peninsula; local craft beers.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book the right window**: gray whales are at their best from mid-February to the end of March. Before paying for a trip just for the animals, check when the whale shark season opens.
- **Read the car quote carefully**: Mexican liability insurance is mandatory and is often paid at the counter.
- **Never drive at night in Baja**: livestock, no lights and no shoulders.
- **Cash for the lagoons**: the fishermen's cooperatives and village gas stations often don't take cards.
- **Authorized operators** for the whale shark and the blue whale: CONANP publishes the lists every season.
- **A spare day in La Paz**: the north wind often cancels the Espíritu Santo trips.
- **Don't swim on the open Pacific beaches** between Todos Santos and Cabo, except Cerritos and the coves marked as safe: strong currents.
- **Book the Casa Azul and the Balandra shift** a few days ahead.

## Scams and Common Annoyances
- **Street-hailed taxis in Mexico City**: only prepaid taxis at the airport, taxis de sitio or apps.
- **Gas stations**: check that the pump starts at zero, pay in cash counting your change out loud, or by card without letting it out of your sight.
- **"Timeshare" in Los Cabos**: at San José del Cabo airport and in the Cabo San Lucas Marina, salespeople in uniform offer free tours or discounts in exchange for a half-day timeshare presentation. Walk past without stopping.
- **"Surprise" car insurance** at the rental counter: always ask for the final price with the mandatory insurance before booking.
- **Unauthorized tours** for whale sharks or with whales, cheaper but outside the distance and time rules: besides the harm to the animals, they risk having the boat seized.
- **Pickpockets** in the Mexico City metro and in the markets: backpack in front, phone in a closed pocket.

- **Mid-range:** 3,000-4,500 MXN a day, about €150-225.
- **High-end:** over 7,000 MXN a day, from €350 up, with resorts in Los Cabos and private trips.
- **Individual items:** Teotihuacán and the Anthropology Museum about 210 MXN each for foreigners; Mexico City-La Paz flight roughly 1,500-3,500 MXN; rental car with mandatory insurance 900-1,800 MXN a day; whale shark 1,500-2,500 MXN plus a 215 MXN wristband; Espíritu Santo 2,500-3,500 MXN; panga for the gray whales about 1,500-2,000 MXN an hour per boat; blue whale from 3,000 MXN; humpbacks at Cabo 1,400-2,200 MXN (80-130 dollars); Balandra 120 MXN.

## Chiusura personale
The two halves of this trip balance each other. Mexico City is dense, loud and full of history, and demands attention for every day you give it; Baja California Sur is empty and quiet, with few roads and many miles, and gives its best when you let it move at the pace of the wind and the animals. The timing is fixed: outside February and March you can still make the trip, but it becomes a different trip. It suits people who want to put together a great capital and one of the most life-filled seas on the planet, and who accept driving and booking the boat trips with days to spare.
