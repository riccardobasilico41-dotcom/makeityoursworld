# Montenegro in 10 Days: From the Bay of Kotor to Durmitor and the Tara Canyon

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (complete itinerary and practical information, verified in October 2026)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 10 days, 9 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independent, with a rental car, the Kamenari ferry and a few boat and inflatable-raft excursions
**Categoria:** Europa · Balcani · Montenegro · Bocche di Cattaro · mare · montagna · roadtrip · monasteri

## Apertura personale
Montenegro is a small country, about the size of Abruzzo, that you can cross from the sea to the mountains in ten days without ever driving more than three hours at a stretch. In half an hour by car you go from a Mediterranean fjord with Venetian towns to a plateau of pastures at 4,900 feet (1,500 meters), and in another hour to one of the deepest canyons in Europe. That's its strength, and the reason it's best seen by car.

This itinerary holds together the two souls of the country. The coast, with the **Bay of Kotor**, Kotor and Perast, Budva and the Riviera; the interior, with **Mount Lovćen** and the old capital Cetinje, **Lake Skadar**, the **Ostrog** monastery wedged into the rock, **Durmitor** and the **Tara canyon**. It's meant for people who want a sea-and-mountain trip in one, two hours' flight from Italy, and for those who want to combine it with Dubrovnik, Mostar or northern Albania: the variations at the end explain how.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** from late May to late June and in September, with the sea already warm, the mountains open and fewer crowds. July and August are the months of heat (over 95 °F / 35 °C on the coast and in Podgorica), traffic on the coastal road, border lines and cruise ships at Kotor. Durmitor is snowed in from November to April and becomes a ski destination.
- **Documents:** Montenegro is not part of the European Union or the Schengen area, but for Italian citizens a **passport** is enough, for tourist stays of up to 90 days, or the **electronic ID card valid for travel abroad**, which however is only good for up to 30 days. Viaggiare Sicuri reports border problems with renewed paper cards bearing the stamp and with electronic ones extended with a certificate from the town hall: the passport is better. At the borders with Croatia, which is in Schengen, you go through the European EES biometric check. Check the Montenegro card on Viaggiare Sicuri before leaving.
- **Currency:** the **euro** is used, adopted unilaterally: no exchange and no fees on euro withdrawals (some local ATMs still charge a fee).
- **Payments:** cards accepted in hotels, restaurants and supermarkets in the towns; cash for parking, the Kotor walls, the Perast boats, the national parks and the mountain konobe.
- **Tourist tax:** about €1 per person per night, with amounts that change from municipality to municipality; children up to 12 don't pay, from 12 to 18 you pay half. The accommodation adds it, and also registers guests with the authorities. Anyone staying in private homes without registration may have problems at checks.
- **Connectivity:** Montenegro is not in the European Union, so "roam like at home" **doesn't apply**: with many Italian carriers data costs a lot. Tourist SIMs from One, Crnogorski Telekom and m:tel in kiosks and at the airport, or an eSIM.
- **Health:** European health insurance card not valid. Viaggiare Sicuri recommends a policy with medical expenses and medical repatriation: public facilities are poorly equipped and services are often paid in cash. Bring your regular medications from home. Tap water is generally drinkable. Ticks in the meadows of Durmitor and Lovćen.
- **Electricity:** 230V, type C and F sockets, as in Italy.
- **Time zone:** the same as Italy, with daylight saving time.
- **Language:** Montenegrin (very close to Serbian, Croatian and Bosnian), written in Latin and Cyrillic alphabets. English is spoken in tourism; Italian is often understood on the coast.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival at Tivat and Kotor at sunset
From Rome and Milan you fly to **Tivat**, the coast's airport, a quarter of an hour from Kotor, or to **Podgorica**, the capital, an hour and a half away. With the rental car picked up at the airport we reached **Kotor**, at the end of the innermost arm of the Bay, where the mountains drop straight into water that looks like a lake. We spent the first evening in the old town, a maze of little squares, churches and cats enclosed by Venetian walls, when the cruise ships have left again and the alleys go quiet.

**In practice:** sleeping in the old town means no car: better Dobrota or Muo, a few minutes away, with parking · parking around the walls is expensive and full in summer · check the cruise ship calendar on the port's website and plan visits for the days with fewer landings.

### Day 2 — Kotor: the old town and the walls up to the fortress
We climbed the **walls** and the **fortress of St. John** early in the morning, before the heat: about 1,350 steps and 850 feet (260 meters) of elevation gain, a good hour's climb at an easy pace, with the view opening over the Bay at every switchback. We came down in late morning and wandered the old town: the 12th-century **Cathedral of Saint Tryphon**, the churches of St. Luke and St. Nicholas, the Arms and Flour squares, the Maritime Museum.

In the afternoon we went to the sea at **Dobrota**, on the shore toward the north, where the 18th-century ship captains' palaces face small beaches and piers. In the evening we had dinner at a konoba with Adriatic fish and red vranac wine.

**In practice:** admission to the walls about €15 in season, cash · walking shoes and water: there's no shade on the climb · Cathedral of Saint Tryphon with a small ticket.

### Day 3 — Perast, Our Lady of the Rocks and the loop around the Bay
Seven and a half miles (twelve kilometers) along the shore took us to **Perast**, a baroque town of a single street, with sixteen palaces and seventeen churches built by the families of captains in the service of Venice. In front of the town are two islets: **St. George**, with the monastery among the cypresses, and **Our Lady of the Rocks** (Gospa od Škrpjela), an artificial island built by throwing stones and sunken hulls into the sea, with a church full of sailors' ex-votos. We reached them on the boats that leave from the waterfront.

In the afternoon we continued around the bay by car: **Risan**, with the Roman mosaics of a 2nd-century villa, then the narrow **Verige** passage and the **Kamenari-Lepetane ferry**, a five-minute crossing that avoids the full loop of the bay, as far as the Luštica peninsula and **Tivat**, with the Porto Montenegro marina. In the evening we went back to Kotor along the other shore.

**In practice:** boat to Our Lady of the Rocks a few euros per person round trip, small donation for the church museum · in Perast you leave the car in the lots at the town entrance · Kamenari ferry about €5 for a car, pedestrians free, open day and night, long lines on summer afternoons.

### Day 4 — The Lovćen switchbacks, Njeguši and Cetinje
The road that climbs from Kotor toward **Lovćen** is one of the most spectacular in the Balkans: twenty-five tight hairpins on the mountain wall, with the Bay getting smaller beneath you. At the top is **Njeguši**, the village where smoked cured ham (pršut) and mountain cheese are made, to be tasted in one of the trattorias along the road. We continued into the national park to the **Njegoš mausoleum**, on the summit of Jezerski vrh at 5,436 feet (1,657 meters), reached by a tunnel and 461 steps: from the terrace, on clear days, you can see the sea, Lake Skadar and Albania.

Then we dropped down to **Cetinje**, the old royal capital: a quiet little town, with 19th-century embassies turned into museums, King Nikola's palace and the Cetinje monastery, still active. You sleep here or head down to the coast.

**In practice:** the switchback road is narrow, with tricky meetings with buses and campers: drive slowly and honk on blind curves · national park admission €3, mausoleum about €8 extra · Kotor-Njeguši about 1 hour, Njeguši-Cetinje half an hour.

### Day 5 — Budva, the coast and Sveti Stefan
From Cetinje we came down in forty minutes to **Budva**, the country's beach capital. The old town, on a fortified peninsula, is small and pleasant in the morning; the rest of the town is a waterfront of big hotels, clubs and crowded beaches. The nicest part of the coast is further south: **Sveti Stefan**, the islet-fishing village turned into a resort, is best seen from the viewpoint on the road or from the public beach next to it. The afternoon is for the sea in one of the bays between Budva and Petrovac, like **Drobni pijesak** or Petrovac's beach with its quiet promenade.

**In practice:** the island of Sveti Stefan is accessible only to resort guests, when it's open · sun loungers and umbrellas on the equipped beaches €15-30 a day in high season · sleep in Petrovac or in Budva.

### Day 6 — Lake Skadar: Virpazar and Rijeka Crnojevića
**Lake Skadar** (Skadarsko jezero), shared with Albania, is the largest lake in the Balkans and a national park with over 280 bird species, including the rare Dalmatian pelican. From **Virpazar** leave the boats that wind among water-lily channels, islets with monasteries and fishing villages. At lunch, the lake's carp and eel and the wines of the Crmnica area, one of the oldest in the country.

In the afternoon we went up to the **Pavlova Strana** viewpoint, the bend of the Crnojević river seen from above, the lake's most famous image, and then down to the village of **Rijeka Crnojevića** with its stone bridge. You sleep on the lake or in Podgorica.

**In practice:** national park admission about €5 · boat ride from Virpazar €15-30 per person for 2-3 hours, pricier on a private boat · narrow, scenic road between Virpazar and Rijeka Crnojevića.

### Day 7 — The Ostrog monastery and the road to Žabljak
The **Ostrog monastery** is Montenegro's most important pilgrimage site, and one of the most visited in the Balkans by Orthodox, Catholics and Muslims. The upper monastery is white, wedged into a vertical rock face at 2,950 feet (900 meters), with the relics of Saint Basil in the cave chapel. We drove up a steep road to the parking lot and did the last stretch on foot. You visit in silence, with shoulders and legs covered (women are asked to wear a skirt, lent at the entrance).

From Ostrog we continued north to **Nikšić** and across the plateau to **Žabljak**, the base for Durmitor, at 4,760 feet (1,450 meters). We arrived in the afternoon, with time for a first walk to the **Black Lake** (Crno jezero), at the foot of the fir forest.

**In practice:** free entry to the monastery, donations welcome · no photos inside the chapels · Ostrog-Žabljak about 2.5 hours · in Žabljak the evenings are cool even in summer: bring a sweatshirt.

### Day 8 — Durmitor: the Black Lake, the panoramic road and Bobotov Kuk
**Durmitor**, a UNESCO site, is a limestone massif with 48 peaks above 6,500 feet (2,000 meters) and eighteen glacial lakes, called "the eyes of the mountain." In the morning we walked the loop around the **Black Lake**, an hour and a half on foot through the forest. Then two options. Hikers climb **Bobotov Kuk**, the highest peak, at 8,278 feet (2,523 meters), from the Sedlo pass: about 6-7 hours round trip, with no technical difficulty but with exposed stretches and a significant elevation gain. Those who prefer the car drive the **Durmitor panoramic road** (about 50 miles / 80 km as a loop, P14), through canyons, passes and pastures with shepherds, stopping at the Sedlo pass and at the viewpoint over the Sušica canyon.

**In practice:** national park admission about €5 for the Black Lake, parking extra · panoramic road open roughly June to October, narrow and without guardrails in many stretches · for Bobotov Kuk start early: afternoon thunderstorms are frequent in summer.

### Day 9 — The Tara bridge, rafting and the descent south
The **Tara canyon** is up to 4,300 feet (1,300 meters) deep, among the deepest in Europe. The **Đurđevića Tara bridge**, a 1940 concrete arch bridge 490 feet (150 meters) above the river, is reached in half an hour from Žabljak; the ziplines over the gorge also start from here. **Rafting** on the Tara is done on the most beautiful stretch, from Brštanovica to Šćepan Polje, near the Bosnian border: 11 miles (18 kilometers) of moderate rapids and emerald water, in half a day with lunch at the camps along the river.

In the afternoon we headed south through the **Morača canyon**, stopping at the 13th-century Morača monastery, to **Podgorica** or back to the coast for the last night, depending on the airport of the return flight.

**In practice:** rafting on the classic stretch roughly €50-80 per person with lunch, book with reputable operators with life jackets and helmets · ziplines on the bridge €20-30 · Žabljak-Podgorica about 3 hours via Morača.

### Day 10 — Podgorica and departure
The last day is for heading home. **Podgorica** is a young, not very monumental capital, rebuilt after the war: if you have a few hours you can see the clock tower in the Ottoman quarter of Stara Varoš, the bridge over the Ribnica river and the Orthodox Cathedral of the Resurrection, with its golden mosaics. Then we returned the car at the airport. If you're flying from Tivat, you go back to the coast the evening before and spend the last morning on the Bay.

**In practice:** Podgorica airport about 15 minutes from the center · return the car with a full tank, there are few gas stations near the airport · Tivat is 1.5 hours from Podgorica.

## Where to Stay
- **Kotor and the Bay:** in the **old town** for atmosphere, without a car and with the noise of the bars in summer; in **Dobrota**, **Muo** or **Prčanj** for quiet and parking, in apartments and small hotels on the sea; in **Perast** for a night in a captain's palace. Budget €40-70 a night for a double, mid-range €80-150, high-end over €200 at Porto Montenegro and in the Luštica resorts.
- **Cetinje:** guesthouses and small hotels, low prices.
- **Budva and the Riviera:** Budva for those who want nightlife, **Petrovac** and **Pržno** for a quiet sea. In July and August prices double.
- **Lake Skadar:** guesthouses and farm stays in **Virpazar** and the surrounding villages, often with their own cellar.
- **Žabljak:** guesthouses, wooden chalets and small mountain hotels, €40-90; campgrounds around the Black Lake.
- **Tara canyon:** rafting camps with bungalows along the river near Šćepan Polje, for those who want to sleep there.
- **Podgorica:** convenient city hotels for the last night before the flight.

## Where to Eat
- **On the coast:** grilled fish, black risotto, Bay mussels buzara-style, octopus under the bell (ispod sača), in konobe by the sea. Fish is often paid by the kilo: ask the price first.
- **Njeguši and Lovćen:** **pršut**, the smoked ham, with njeguški cheese and homemade bread, and the njeguški steak, a steak stuffed with ham and cheese.
- **Lake Skadar:** smoked carp and eel, the wines of Crmnica (vranac and kratošija) and the families' grappa (rakija).
- **Mountains (Durmitor, Tara):** **kačamak** (polenta with cheese and sour cream), **cicvara**, lamb cooked under the bell, mountain-pasture cheeses and kajmak.
- **Everywhere:** burek in the morning, ćevapi, pljeskavica and the rakija offered at the end of a meal. Portions are enormous: order less than usual.

## Getting Around
- **Rental car:** the best solution. Pick up at Tivat or Podgorica, roughly €30-60 a day in summer, less in low season. To leave the country (Croatia, Bosnia, Albania) you need written authorization from the rental company and the **green card**, with an indicative surcharge of €30-60 per rental, up to €100 with some companies for Albania. Some local rental companies don't allow Albania. In the car you need the original vehicle registration document, even for rental cars: with a photocopy you're turned back.
- **Driving rules:** headlights on in daytime all year, alcohol limit 0.03% (0.3 g/l) (zero for new drivers), seat belt mandatory, phone banned while driving. Carry a warning triangle, first-aid kit, reflective vest and spare bulbs; from November 15 to March 31 chains or snow tires. Speed limits are low and checks frequent, with fines to be paid on the spot or at a bank.
- **Roads:** the coastal road between Herceg Novi and Ulcinj is slow and busy in summer. The Sozina tunnel (between Virpazar and Bar) and the Smokovac-Mateševo motorway stretch, toward the north, are tolled, a few euros. Mountain roads are narrow, with hairpins and few guardrails: travel only in daylight.
- **Kamenari-Lepetane ferry:** crosses the narrowest point of the Bay in five minutes, about €5 for a car, day and night. It avoids the loop around the bay for those arriving from Herceg Novi or Croatia and heading toward Tivat and Budva.
- **Without a car:** frequent buses between Kotor, Budva, Podgorica and Herceg Novi; buses and a train for Podgorica-Bar and for the north (Kolašin). For Durmitor, Ostrog and the lake you need organized tours or a driver.
- **From Italy by sea:** the overnight **Bari-Bar** ferry has run only in summer in recent years, and according to Viaggiare Sicuri in summer 2026 there are no direct connections between Italy and Montenegro. Anyone traveling with their own car goes via Durrës, in Albania, or via Croatia.

## Borders and Combined Variations
- **Croatia (Dubrovnik):** **Debeli Brijeg** crossing between Herceg Novi and Dubrovnik, about an hour's drive from Kotor. Croatia is in the Schengen area: document check and EES biometric registration. In summer lines of one to three hours, worse on Saturdays and in the afternoon; Viaggiare Sicuri reports waits of 4 hours and, in extreme cases, up to 7: water and food in the car. Dubrovnik has flights from Italy and allows an "open-jaw" itinerary.
- **Bosnia and Herzegovina (Trebinje, Mostar):** **Trebinje** is half an hour from Herceg Novi or Dubrovnik, with its Ottoman bridge and vineyards. **Mostar** and its Old Bridge are 3 hours from Kotor via Trebinje. The Šćepan Polje crossing, near the Tara rafting, also links Durmitor with Foča and Sarajevo, on a narrow mountain road.
- **Albania (Shkodër, Theth):** **Hani i Hotit** crossing after Podgorica or **Sukobin** after Ulcinj. **Shkodër** is an hour and a half from Podgorica; from there you climb to the valleys of the **Albanian Alps** (Theth, Valbona), on mountain roads. Check that the rental allows entry into Albania.

**Ready-made variations:**
- **7 days, coast and lake only:** Kotor (2 nights), Perast and Lovćen, Budva, Lake Skadar, Ostrog as a day trip. Without Durmitor.
- **14 days, Montenegro and Dubrovnik:** fly into Dubrovnik, two nights in the city, then the itinerary from Kotor, returning to Dubrovnik through Trebinje.
- **14 days, Montenegro and Bosnia:** after Durmitor you cross at Šćepan Polje toward **Sarajevo** (2 nights) and **Mostar** (1 night), returning to the coast via Trebinje.
- **14 days, Montenegro and northern Albania:** after Lake Skadar you enter Albania for Shkodër and Theth (3-4 nights), then return to Podgorica for Durmitor.
- **More sea:** add **Ulcinj**, with its old town and Velika Plaža, a seven-and-a-half-mile (twelve-kilometer) beach, and the Luštica peninsula.

## When to Go Month by Month
- **April-May:** spring, mild temperatures, sea still cold until late May, mountains with snow at altitude. Good for towns and the lake.
- **June:** the best month along with September. Warm sea, mountains open, mid-range prices.
- **July-August:** high season. Strong heat, traffic on the coast, border lines and cruise ships at Kotor; Durmitor is the cool refuge. Peak prices, reservations needed.
- **September:** sea still warm, fewer crowds, late-summer colors in the mountains, grape harvest on the lake.
- **October:** quiet coast, first rains, Durmitor panoramic road heading toward closure.
- **November-March:** rainy season on the coast (coastal Montenegro is among the rainiest areas in Europe in winter), snow on Durmitor with skiing at Žabljak and Kolašin. Many beach facilities closed.

## Indicative Budget 2026
Prices per person in a double room, flights excluded (from Italy to Tivat or Podgorica roughly €80-250 round trip).
- **Budget (€50-70 a day):** guesthouses and apartments, local cooking, shared car.
- **Mid-range (€90-140 a day):** small hotels by the sea and in the mountains, dinners in konobe, boat excursions and rafting.
- **High-end (from €200 a day):** resorts at Luštica and Porto Montenegro, design hotels in Kotor and Budva.
- **Main items:** car €30-60 a day; Kotor walls about €15; Kamenari ferry about €5 for the car; national parks €3-5 each, Njegoš mausoleum about €8; boat to Our Lady of the Rocks a few euros; lake boat ride €15-30; rafting €50-80; a meal in a konoba €15-30.

## Common Scams and Safety
- **The country is safe** for tourists, according to Viaggiare Sicuri, despite the presence of local criminal groups who settle scores among themselves. The most common problems are bag-snatching and petty theft in the crowded coastal resorts in summer, especially on the beach, and unlocked cars left with items in view. The country is at seismic risk and in summer there are fires.
- **Taxis without a meter** in Kotor and Budva: ask the price first, or use local apps.
- **Fish by the kilo:** in some tourist konobe the bill is a surprise; ask for the weight and price before ordering.
- **Unofficial parking:** sometimes "parking attendants" offer their services in free areas; park only in marked areas, paying at the meters or by SMS.
- **Road:** reckless overtaking and high speed on mountain roads by locals; drive slowly, never at night on passes and switchbacks.
- **Mountains:** on Durmitor, quick afternoon thunderstorms and fog; start early, check the weather, tell someone your route.
- **Rafting:** choose licensed operators, with life jackets and helmets and trained guides.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Check the cruise ship calendar** before setting the day for Kotor and Perast.
- **Climb the Kotor walls at dawn**, with cash and water.
- **Activate a data plan or buy a local SIM:** European roaming doesn't apply in Montenegro.
- **Ask the rental company for the authorization and the green card** if you want to cross into Croatia, Bosnia or Albania, before booking.
- **Allow for lines at the Croatian border** in summer, and cross early in the morning.
- **Don't underestimate driving times:** 60 miles (100 kilometers) of mountain road can take two and a half hours.
- **Cover up at Ostrog and in the monasteries**, and bring a sweatshirt for Durmitor even in August.
- **Carry cash** for parks, boats and mountain trattorias.

## Chiusura personale
Montenegro is a trip of contrasts at close range: the Venetian stones of Kotor and the silence of a glacial lake, the calm water of the Bay and the rapids of the Tara, the coastal crowds in August and the shepherds on the Durmitor plateau. Ten days are enough to see both halves, and to understand why people who visit often come back, perhaps crossing one more border.
