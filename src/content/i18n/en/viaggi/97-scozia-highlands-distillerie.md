# Scotland by Car: Edinburgh, the Highlands and Distilleries in Two Weeks

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (itinerary built on Rick's trip, done by car in July, and completed with research: practical information, distillery hours and prices checked in November 2026; stock photos from Wikimedia Commons)

**Durata:** 12 days, 11 nights, plus 4-5 days for the North Coast 500 variant
**Compagni di viaggio:** itinerary designed for a couple or a small group with a rental car, and at least one person who drives and doesn't drink

## Apertura personale
Scotland is done by car. Rick went in July and drove around it. The first thing to factor in is driving "the other way round": you keep left, the gearstick is on the other side, and at roundabouts you go clockwise. In the Highlands many roads also have a single lane, with passing places to let oncoming traffic through. You get used to it quickly, but take the first miles calmly.

The loop starts in **Edinburgh**, goes through **Glasgow** and **Loch Lomond**, comes down to the sea at **Oban** and climbs through the mountains of **Glencoe** to **Fort William** and the **Glenfinnan** viaduct. Then the **Isle of Skye**, **Loch Ness** and **Inverness**, the distilleries of **Speyside** and the **Cairngorms**, before returning to Edinburgh. Rick did this whole loop: Glasgow only in passing, the Hebrides from Oban, the Glenfinnan viaduct for the Harry Potter train. For him Glencoe is beautiful, Loch Ness a bit meh. In Oban he ate very good fish at **Ee-Usk**, on the harbour.

The weather is unpredictable: sun, rain and wind can all come in the same hour. Keep a waterproof jacket in the car even in August, and don't build your days around a single view.

If you have a few more days, you can go up to the far north, on the **North Coast 500**, the road that loops around the northern tip of Scotland. Rick drove it, and it's breathtaking. It's explained further down, as a 4-5 day variant.

And one rule applies to the whole trip, distilleries included. In Scotland the drink-driving limit is lower than in England, but Rick's advice is even simpler: **zero tolerance**. Whoever drives doesn't drink, not even a taste.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** The itinerary works from May to September, with the longest days, and for Rick autumn, with the foliage, is beautiful too; the weather stays unpredictable in every season
- **Documents:** Scotland is part of the United Kingdom, outside the European Union. Since April 2, 2025, Italian citizens need an **ETA**, to apply for on gov.uk or with the official UK ETA app: it costs £20 (2026) and is valid for 2 years, or until your passport expires. You need a **passport**: the ID card is accepted only in special cases.
- **Car:** rent it in Edinburgh. The Italian driving licence is valid with no international permit. A car that isn't too big is better, for the single-track roads.
- **Driving on the left:** on single-track roads you pull into the passing place on your left, or wait opposite the one on your right, and thank the other driver with a wave. You don't park in passing places. Sheep and deer cross suddenly.
- **Drink-driving:** in Scotland the limit is 50 mg per 100 ml of blood (22 micrograms per 100 ml of breath), against 80 mg in England. Rick's advice is zero tolerance: whoever drives doesn't drink.
- **Distances:** they look short on the map, but times get longer with narrow roads, stops and weather. Fill up whenever you can.
- **Ferries:** they are run by CalMac, and with a car you book ahead.
- **Distilleries:** tours are booked online, in summer even weeks ahead. Many don't admit children under 8.
- **Edinburgh:** since July 24, 2026 there is a 5% visitor levy on the price of accommodation, for the first 5 nights.
- **Health:** the EHIC covers necessary treatment in the public health service; travel insurance is still recommended. In summer the Highlands have midges, harmless but annoying little flies: you need a repellent.
- **Phone:** EU roaming doesn't apply in the United Kingdom. There are stretches with no signal: offline maps.
- **Sockets:** type G. You need an adapter.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Edinburgh: the Old Town and the Royal Mile
From the airport you reach the centre by tram or bus. You don't need the car yet. The afternoon is for the **Royal Mile**, the Old Town street that runs down from the castle towards Holyrood, with **St Giles'** Cathedral halfway and the **closes**, the narrow alleys that open off both sides. In the evening climb **Calton Hill** to watch the sunset over the Old Town.

### Day 2 — Edinburgh: the castle and Arthur's Seat
**Edinburgh Castle** opens at 9:30. Buy the ticket online, with a date and time: £23.50, against £26 at the gate (2026). Inside are the Scottish Crown Jewels and St Margaret's Chapel. In the afternoon walk around the **New Town** and **Princes Street Gardens**, below the castle rock. If your legs are up to it, climb **Arthur's Seat**, the hill of an ancient volcano inside Holyrood Park.

### Day 3 — Glasgow, Loch Lomond and the road to Oban
Pick up the car and head west. **Glasgow** is about an hour away: a morning is enough for the medieval **cathedral** and the **Necropolis**, the hill of Victorian monuments next to it. It's better to park outside the centre. Rick only passed through too. Then drive up along **Loch Lomond**, with a stop at **Luss**, on the shore. From there the road goes through the mountains and reaches the sea at **Oban** in the late afternoon.

### Day 4 — Oban: the distillery, McCaig's Tower and dinner at Ee-Usk
**Oban** is a harbour town, where the ferries leave for the islands. In the centre is **Oban distillery**, with the classic tour from £24 (2026), to book on malts.com. Above the harbour is **McCaig's Tower**, the stone colosseum with a view over the bay. If you want an island, take the CalMac ferry to **Mull** and spend the day in **Tobermory**, with its colourful houses on the harbour and another distillery. From Oban Rick went to the Hebrides.

In the evening have dinner at **Ee-Usk**, on the North Pier: fish and seafood on the water, with the ferries passing in front. For Rick it's very good. Book ahead.

### Day 5 — Glencoe and Fort William
Head back north and enter **Glencoe**: the narrow valley between the walls of the **Three Sisters** and, at the entrance, the pyramid of **Buachaille Etive Mòr**. Take photos only from the car parks along the A82, never from the roadside. In good weather there's a short walk in the valley. For Rick Glencoe is beautiful. In the evening you reach **Fort William**, at the foot of **Ben Nevis**, the highest mountain in the United Kingdom.

### Day 6 — Glenfinnan and the road to Skye
West of Fort William is **Glenfinnan**, with the arched railway viaduct from the Harry Potter films and the monument on Loch Shiel. In summer the **Jacobite** crosses it, the steam train from Fort William to Mallaig: from the paths above the visitor centre you can watch it go over. To ride it, book on the West Coast Railways website; seats are few. Rick came here just for the Harry Potter train.

From Mallaig you can take the CalMac ferry to **Armadale**, on Skye, booking a place for the car. Or you go back and reach the island by the **Skye Bridge**, passing **Eilean Donan** castle. Night in **Portree** or nearby.

### Day 7 — Skye: the Old Man of Storr and the Quiraing
Leave at dawn for the **Old Man of Storr**, the rock pinnacle above the road north of Portree: about two hours up and down. Then drive around the **Trotternish** peninsula and go up to the **Quiraing**, the huge landslip with pinnacles and sheer green walls. The road over the ridge is single-track. In fog you see nothing: in that case swap the order with the next day.

### Day 8 — Skye: the Fairy Pools and Talisker
Early morning at the **Fairy Pools**, the clear pools at the foot of the Cuillin. In the afternoon go to **Carbost**, on the shore of Loch Harport, for **Talisker distillery**: tour with tasting from £22 (2026), to book on malts.com, especially in summer. Whoever drives should ask whether there's a sample to take away.

### Day 9 — From Skye to Inverness along Loch Ness
A day on the road. You leave the island by the bridge, pass **Eilean Donan** and reach **Loch Ness**. On the shore is **Urquhart** Castle, in ruins on a headland: tickets are bought online, with a date and time, on the Historic Environment Scotland website. In the evening you reach **Inverness**, the capital of the Highlands.

Rick did Inverness and Loch Ness. For him the loch is a bit meh: worth a stop on the way, not the trip on its own.

If you've chosen the North Coast 500 variant, you start from here, or directly from Skye towards Applecross.

### Day 10 — Speyside: the distillery trail
**Speyside**, the valley of the River Spey, is the area with the most distilleries in Scotland. Two tours a day are enough. The famous names are **Glenfiddich** in Dufftown, **The Macallan** near Craigellachie and **The Glenlivet**; in Knockando there's **Cardhu**, with tours from £18 (2026). Everything by booking. Whoever drives doesn't drink: many distilleries give the "driver's dram", a sample in a small sealed bottle to take away. Night in Speyside, in Aberlour or Dufftown.

### Day 11 — The Cairngorms, Dalwhinnie and the return to Edinburgh
You cross **Cairngorms** National Park, the largest in the United Kingdom, with a stop at **Loch Morlich**, among the pines below the mountains. Heading down the A9 there's **Dalwhinnie**, one of the highest distilleries in Scotland, at 355 metres: 45-minute classic tour from £21 (2026). Then you continue down the A9 towards Perth and reach **Edinburgh**, where you return the car.

### Day 12 — Departure from Edinburgh
The last morning is for an unhurried walk on the **Royal Mile** or a visit to the National Museum of Scotland, then the flight home.

## The distillery trail
Rick did the distilleries, and they're a part of the trip not to skip. There are distilleries almost everywhere in Scotland, but the itinerary passes the most famous ones and a couple of lesser-known ones. Prices and hours are those on the official websites in November 2026.

**The famous ones**
- **Oban** (Oban, day 4): 1-hour classic tour from £24. Open every day: April 10-18, May to September 10-19, October 10-18, November to March 10-17. The tour programme is under review: recheck it.
- **Talisker** (Carbost, Skye, day 8): 1-hour tour with tasting from £22. March to October 10-17, November to February 10-16:30. Maintenance from October 11 to November 1, 2026, with some experiences suspended.
- **Glenfiddich**, **The Macallan** and **The Glenlivet** (Speyside, day 10): visits by booking on the official websites, where you should check prices and hours.
- **Cardhu** (Knockando, Speyside): tours from £18 for "A Nip Round", 1 hour, and from £23 for the "Flavour Journey", 1 hour and a half. May to September 10-18; January and February open Thursday to Monday. It can't be reached by public transport.

**Two hidden gems**
- **Dalwhinnie** (day 11): founded in 1897, at 355 metres it is one of the highest distilleries in Scotland, inside Cairngorms National Park. 45-minute classic tour from £21, 30-minute guided tasting from £14. Open every day 10-16.
- **Glen Ord** (Muir of Ord, on the Black Isle): the distillery of The Singleton, northwest of Inverness. The North Coast 500 website names it as the ideal start or end of the route. Classic tour of 1 hour and a quarter from £24. May to August 10-18, September to April 10-17. Dates are bookable about 90 days ahead.

**Whoever drives doesn't drink**
In Scotland the drink-driving limit is lower than in England. Rick's advice is zero tolerance. Many distilleries give drivers the **driver's dram**, a sample in a small sealed bottle to take away. Not all of them offer it: ask when you book. If everyone wants to taste, you can take an organised tour or a taxi for Speyside.

**Common rules:** book online, ahead in summer; groups are at most 16 people; children under 8 usually can't join the tours. In winter distilleries may close at the last minute because of the weather.

## Islay variant: the peat distilleries
If you love peated whisky, add 2-3 days after Oban. Drive down to the port of **Kennacraig**, on the Kintyre peninsula, and take the CalMac ferry to **Port Askaig**, in about 1 hour and 55 minutes. The port of Port Ellen is closed for works from June 2, 2026 until 2029. Book the car ahead.

On the south coast of the island there are three distilleries close to one another: **Laphroaig**, **Lagavulin** and **Ardbeg**.
- **Lagavulin:** 1 hour and a half classic tour from £22 (2026). March to October every day 10-17; November to February Monday to Saturday only. In the summer months and during the Fèis Ìle festival tours sell out early.
- **Laphroaig:** March to October every day 9:45-17 (2026). Tours and prices on the official website.

## North Coast 500 variant: the far north
The **North Coast 500** is a 516-mile loop, about 830 km, that starts and ends in Inverness and goes around the northern tip of Scotland. Driven in one go it would take 11-13 hours, but most people who do it take 5-7 days. Rick drove it: for him it's a breathtaking road.

With 4-5 extra days you enter from the west side, leaving Skye after day 8, and come out at Inverness, where you pick up the itinerary again from Speyside. The stages below are a suggestion:
- **Day A:** from Skye to **Applecross** over the **Bealach na Bà**, the pass with tight hairpins and a view of Skye, then the mountains of **Torridon**.
- **Day B:** from Torridon to **Ullapool**, the harbour village known for its fish, then **Assynt** and **Ardvreck** Castle on the loch.
- **Day C:** the **Kylesku** bridge and the Sutherland coast to **Durness**, with **Smoo Cave**.
- **Day D:** from Durness to **John o' Groats** and the **Duncansby** sea stacks, then down the east coast.
- **Day E:** **Glen Ord** on the Black Isle and arrival in **Inverness**. If you only have four days, combine D and E.

Most of the road is single-track. The Bealach na Bà is not suitable for large motorhomes and should be avoided in fog or ice. Petrol stations and shops are rare: fill up whenever you can. With a motorhome you only sleep at campsites and in permitted areas.

## The weather
The weather in Scotland is unpredictable, and Rick remembers it well. In summer highs are usually between 12 and 19 degrees, in winter between 2 and 6. The west coast and the islands are the windiest and wettest areas. In practice:
- waterproof jacket and layers always in the car, even in August;
- put the most famous views (Storr, Quiraing, Glencoe) on days with some margin, so you can move them;
- summer days are long, and the best weather often comes in the evening;
- in summer, when there's no wind, the midges come out: repellent.

Rick went in July, but he recommends autumn too: with the foliage Scotland is beautiful.

- **Glasgow:** the cathedral and the Necropolis.
- **On the road:** Loch Lomond, Glencoe, the Glenfinnan viaduct, Eilean Donan, Loch Ness with Urquhart.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Zero tolerance at the wheel.** The Scottish limit is lower than the English one, and Rick's advice is not to drink at all. Taste the driver's dram in the evening, at the hotel.
- **Take the first miles on the left calmly.** It's better to pick up the car outside central Edinburgh, where the roads are quieter.
- **Passing places are only for letting cars through.** Don't park there for photos.
- **Book accommodation and distillery tours in advance.** In summer Skye and Speyside fill up.
- **Edinburgh Castle and Urquhart with an online ticket.** They have timed entry.
- **Fill up whenever you can.** In the Highlands and on the North Coast 500 petrol stations are far apart.
- **Offline maps.** There's no signal on many stretches.
- **Don't build a day around a single view.** In fog you can't see the Quiraing: you need a plan B.
