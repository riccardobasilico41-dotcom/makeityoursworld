# Scotland by Car: Edinburgh, the Highlands and Distilleries in Two Weeks

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (itinerary built on Rick's trip, done by car in July, and completed with research: practical information, distillery hours and prices checked in November 2026; stock photos from Wikimedia Commons)
**Periodo del viaggio:** July
**Durata:** 12 days, 11 nights, plus 4-5 days for the North Coast 500 variant
**Compagni di viaggio:** itinerary designed for a couple or a small group with a rental car, and at least one person who drives and doesn't drink
**Categoria:** Europa · Scozia · Edimburgo · Highlands · Skye · Oban · Loch Ness · Speyside · distillerie · whisky · on the road · North Coast 500

## Apertura personale
Scotland is done by car. Rick went in July and drove around it. The first thing to factor in is driving "the other way round": you keep left, the gearstick is on the other side, and at roundabouts you go clockwise. In the Highlands many roads also have a single lane, with passing places to let oncoming traffic through. You get used to it quickly, but take the first miles calmly.

The loop starts in **Edinburgh**, goes through **Glasgow** and **Loch Lomond**, comes down to the sea at **Oban** and climbs through the mountains of **Glencoe** to **Fort William** and the **Glenfinnan** viaduct. Then the **Isle of Skye**, **Loch Ness** and **Inverness**, the distilleries of **Speyside** and the **Cairngorms**, before returning to Edinburgh. Rick did this whole loop: Glasgow only in passing, the Hebrides from Oban, the Glenfinnan viaduct for the Harry Potter train. For him Glencoe is beautiful, Loch Ness a bit meh. In Oban he ate very good fish at **Ee-Usk**, on the harbour.

The weather is unpredictable: sun, rain and wind can all come in the same hour. Keep a waterproof jacket in the car even in August, and don't build your days around a single view.

If you have a few more days, you can go up to the far north, on the **North Coast 500**, the road that loops around the northern tip of Scotland. Rick drove it, and it's breathtaking. It's explained further down, as a 4-5 day variant.

And one rule applies to the whole trip, distilleries included. In Scotland the drink-driving limit is lower than in England, but Rick's advice is even simpler: **zero tolerance**. Whoever drives doesn't drink, not even a taste.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** The itinerary works from May to September, with the longest days, and for Rick autumn, with the foliage, is beautiful too; the weather stays unpredictable in every season
- **Documents:** Scotland is part of the United Kingdom, outside the European Union. Since April 2, 2025, Italian citizens need an **ETA**, to apply for on gov.uk or with the official UK ETA app: it costs £20 (2026) and is valid for 2 years, or until your passport expires. You need a **passport**: the ID card is accepted only in special cases.
- **Car:** rent it in Edinburgh. The Italian driving licence is valid with no international permit. A car that isn't too big is better, for the single-track roads.
- **Driving on the left:** on single-track roads you pull into the passing place on your left, or wait opposite the one on your right, and thank the other driver with a wave. You don't park in passing places. Sheep and deer cross suddenly.
- **Drink-driving:** in Scotland the limit is 50 mg per 100 ml of blood (22 micrograms per 100 ml of breath), against 80 mg in England. Rick's advice is zero tolerance: whoever drives doesn't drink.
- **Distances:** they look short on the map, but times get longer with narrow roads, stops and weather. Fill up whenever you can.
- **Ferries:** they are run by CalMac, and with a car you book ahead.
- **Distilleries:** tours are booked online, in summer even weeks ahead. Many don't admit children under 8.
- **Edinburgh:** since July 24, 2026 there is a 5% visitor levy on the price of accommodation, for the first 5 nights.
- **Health:** the EHIC covers necessary treatment in the public health service; travel insurance is still recommended. In summer the Highlands have midges, harmless but annoying little flies: you need a repellent.
- **Phone:** EU roaming doesn't apply in the United Kingdom. There are stretches with no signal: offline maps.
- **Sockets:** type G. You need an adapter.
- **From the airport to the centre:** you land at Edinburgh (EDI) · the tram reaches Princes Street and St Andrew Square in about 30-35 minutes, every 7-10 minutes by day, from about 5:00 to midnight, about £7.90 a ride, ticket from the machine at the stop before boarding · Lothian's Airlink 100 goes to Waverley in about half an hour, about £6, payable on board with a contactless card · taxi from the official rank, about £25-30 to the centre · Uber works from the signed pick-up area · the typical mistake is boarding the tram without a ticket: there are inspections and the fine is steep.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Edinburgh: the Old Town and the Royal Mile
From the airport you reach the centre by tram or bus. You don't need the car yet: Edinburgh is hard to park in and easy to walk, so it's better to pick the car up only on day 3. The tram stops at Princes Street and St Andrew Square, a short walk from the Old Town; the Airlink 100 covers roughly the same route and costs a little less. Once the bags are dropped, you walk down into the historic centre. *The slopes and stairways between the New Town and the Old Town are steep: comfortable shoes from day one.*

The afternoon is for the **Royal Mile**, the Old Town street running down from the castle towards Holyrood, with **St Giles' Cathedral** halfway along and the **closes**, the narrow alleys opening off both sides. It's worth stepping into them: many come out onto courtyards or views over the lower town that you can't see from the main street. Inside St Giles there are the stained-glass windows and the Thistle Chapel. In the evening you climb **Calton Hill** to watch the sunset over the Old Town. From up there you see the castle, the spire of the Scott Monument and, on the other side, the sea of the Firth of Forth. In July the sun sets late, around ten, and the light lasts a long time.

**In practice:** airport tram about £8 per ride, 30-35 minutes, ticket from the machine or by contactless card before boarding · Airlink 100 a little cheaper, about half an hour · St Giles and Calton Hill free · in August, with the festivals, accommodation must be booked months ahead.

### Day 2 — Edinburgh: the castle and Arthur's Seat
**Edinburgh Castle** opens at 9:30. Tickets are bought online, with a date and time slot: £23.50, against £26 at the gate (2026). The first morning slot is the quietest, and it's worth heading straight for the jewels before the queue builds. Inside are the Scottish Crown Jewels and St Margaret's Chapel, the oldest building in the city. Allow at least a couple of hours for the visit. Monday to Saturday, at one o'clock sharp, the **One O'Clock Gun** is fired from the ramparts.

The afternoon is for walking in the **New Town** and **Princes Street Gardens**, below the castle rock. The New Town is the Georgian city, laid out on a grid, with squares and pale stone houses; a few minutes away is **Dean Village**, the old mill hamlet on the Water of Leith. Those with the legs for it climb **Arthur's Seat**, the hill of an ancient volcano inside Holyrood Park. You start from the Holyrood side, at the bottom of the Royal Mile: the summit is at 251 metres and it's always windy on top. *In the rain the rocks on the last stretch get slippery.*

**In practice:** castle £23.50 online, £26 at the gate (2026), opens at 9:30 · Arthur's Seat about an hour and a half to two hours up and down, free · for the next day it's best to pick the car up at the airport or outside the centre, where the roads are quieter.

### Day 3 — Glasgow, Loch Lomond and the road to Oban
You pick up the car and head west. **Glasgow** is about an hour away: one morning is enough for the medieval **cathedral** and the **Necropolis**, the hill of Victorian monuments next to it. The cathedral is one of the few Scottish medieval churches to survive the Reformation intact, and St Mungo's crypt is the oldest part. From the Necropolis, climbing among the obelisks, you see the whole city. It's best to park outside the centre. Rick, too, only passed through.

Then you drive up along **Loch Lomond**, with a stop at **Luss**, on the shore. It's a village of stone cottages with a pier on the loch, handy for stretching your legs and getting something to eat. From there the road winds through the mountains and reaches the sea at **Oban** in the late afternoon. You leave the A82 at Crianlarich, take the A85, and along **Loch Awe** you see the ruins of **Kilchurn Castle** on the tip of the loch. *It's the first day of driving on the left: better not to cover too much ground and to stop often.*

**In practice:** Edinburgh-Glasgow about an hour and a quarter · Glasgow-Luss about 45 minutes, Luss-Oban about an hour and three quarters without stops · cathedral and Necropolis free, the cathedral is a working church and may close for services · paid parking in Luss · two nights in Oban.

### Day 4 — Oban: the distillery, McCaig's Tower and dinner at Ee-Usk
**Oban** is a small harbour town where the ferries to the islands leave from. In the centre is the **Oban distillery**, with the classic tour at £24 (2026), to book on malts.com. It's small, squeezed between the seafront road and the hill, and the tour is one of the most compact of the whole trip. Above the harbour stands **McCaig's Tower**, the stone colosseum with the view over the bay: you walk up from the centre in about ten minutes, on a short steep climb, and the light over the islands is best at sunset.

Those who want an island take the CalMac ferry to **Mull** and spend the day in **Tobermory**, with its colourful houses on the harbour and another distillery. You land at Craignure, and from there Tobermory is about an hour on a single-track road. From Oban Rick went to the Hebrides. *With a car the ferry must be booked ahead; on foot there's almost always room.*

In the evening you have dinner at **Ee-Usk**, on the North Pier: fish and seafood on the water, with the ferries passing in front. For Rick it's delicious. Book ahead.

**In practice:** Oban distillery £24 (2026), open every day, until 7pm from May to September · McCaig's Tower free · Oban-Craignure ferry about 45-50 minutes, timetable on calmac.co.uk · Ee-Usk to be booked, in summer even a few days ahead.

### Day 5 — Glencoe and Fort William
You head back north and enter **Glencoe**: the narrow valley between the walls of the **Three Sisters** and, at its entrance, the pyramid of **Buachaille Etive Mòr**. From Oban it takes about an hour, over the Connel bridge and along the shore of Loch Linnhe; for the most striking first view you enter from the south instead, on the A82 coming down from Rannoch Moor. Photos are taken only from the car parks along the A82, never from the roadside. For Rick Glencoe is beautiful.

In good weather there's a short walk in the valley. The loop around **Glencoe Lochan**, the little loch among the pines above the village, is easy and takes less than an hour; those with the legs and mountain boots can climb to the **Lost Valley**, the hidden valley behind the Three Sisters, on a rocky path of about three hours return. The National Trust for Scotland visitor centre, just before the village, has maps and the day's weather. In the evening you reach **Fort William**, at the foot of **Ben Nevis**, the highest mountain in the United Kingdom. *Climbing Ben Nevis is a full day's walk: it doesn't fit this itinerary.*

**In practice:** Oban-Glencoe about an hour, Glencoe-Fort William about half an hour · car parks along the A82 are small and full by midday: early morning is better · avoid the Lost Valley in heavy rain, there's a stream to ford · night in Fort William, to be booked well ahead in summer.

### Day 6 — Glenfinnan and the road to Skye
West of Fort William is **Glenfinnan**, with the arched railway viaduct from the Harry Potter films and the monument on Loch Shiel. In summer the **Jacobite**, the steam train from Fort William to Mallaig, crosses it: from the paths above the visitor centre you can watch it go over. The morning train crosses the viaduct at around 11; the expected times are posted at the visitor centre every day. The path to the viewpoint climbs in about ten minutes. *The car park fills up well before the train passes: arrive at least an hour early.*

To ride it you book on the West Coast Railways website; seats are few. Rick came here precisely for the Harry Potter train. From Mallaig you can take the CalMac ferry to **Armadale**, on Skye, booking the car. Or you drive back and reach the island over the **Skye Bridge**, passing **Eilean Donan Castle**, on its islet linked to the shore by a stone bridge. Night in **Portree** or nearby.

**In practice:** Fort William-Glenfinnan about half an hour, Glenfinnan-Mallaig about 45 minutes · paid parking at Glenfinnan, a few pounds · book the Jacobite as soon as sales open, usually early in the year; the season changes every year · Mallaig-Armadale ferry about 30 minutes · Eilean Donan paid, a little over £10.

### Day 7 — Skye: the Old Man of Storr and the Quiraing
You set off at dawn for the **Old Man of Storr**, the rock pinnacle above the road north of Portree: about two hours up and down. The car park is a quarter of an hour from Portree, on the A855, and the path climbs through the felled forest up to the pinnacles. The morning light comes from the east, from the sea, and at that hour the car park is still half empty. *At the top the wind is strong even in summer: jacket and layers.*

Then you drive around the **Trotternish** peninsula and up to the **Quiraing**, the huge landslip with pinnacles and sheer green walls. Along the coast you stop at **Kilt Rock** and **Mealt Falls**, which drop straight into the sea, a few steps from the car park. The road over the ridge is single-track. From the car park at the top of the pass the Quiraing path starts: even the first stretch is enough for the view, the full loop takes a good couple of hours. In fog you see nothing: in that case you swap the order with the next day.

**In practice:** Storr car park paid, about £5-6 for a few hours, free in the evening and at night · Portree-Quiraing around Trotternish about an hour's drive without stops · on the Quiraing road stop only in car parks, never in passing places · night in Portree again.

### Day 8 — Skye: the Fairy Pools and Talisker
Early morning at the **Fairy Pools**, the crystal-clear pools at the foot of the Cuillin. From Portree it's about three quarters of an hour, the last stretch on a narrow road into Glen Brittle. From the car park a gently climbing path follows the stream past small waterfalls and blue pools; the crossings are on stepping stones and in the rain you get wet feet. *The later you arrive, the fuller the car park: cars queuing on the single-track road are the area's main problem.*

The afternoon is in **Carbost**, on the shore of Loch Harport, for the **Talisker distillery**: tour with tasting at £22 (2026), to book on malts.com, especially in summer. Whoever is driving asks whether there's a tasting to take away. Those with time and light left drive out to **Neist Point** lighthouse, on the western tip of the island, mostly for the sunset, or stop at **Dunvegan Castle**, the seat of the MacLeods.

**In practice:** Portree-Fairy Pools about 45 minutes, Fairy Pools-Carbost about 20 · Fairy Pools car park paid, run by a trust that uses it to maintain the paths · Talisker £22 (2026), 10am-5pm from March to October · Neist Point about an hour from Carbost, the path to the lighthouse is steep on the way back.

### Day 9 — From Skye to Inverness along Loch Ness
A day on the road. You leave the island by the bridge, pass **Eilean Donan** and reach **Loch Ness**. If you didn't stop at the castle on day 6, this is your chance, ideally in the morning before the coaches. From Eilean Donan the A87 crosses the mountains of Glen Shiel to Invergarry; then the A82 runs along the whole north shore of the loch, through **Fort Augustus**, where you can see the locks of the Caledonian Canal.

On the shore stands **Urquhart Castle**, ruined on a headland: tickets are bought online, with a date and time slot, on the Historic Environment Scotland website. In the evening you reach **Inverness**, the capital of the Highlands, with the River Ness and a small centre, handy for dinner and a stroll. Rick did Inverness and Loch Ness. The loch, for him, is a bit meh: worth the stop along the way, not the trip on its own.

Those who chose the North Coast 500 variant set off from here, or straight from Skye towards Applecross.

**In practice:** Portree-Inverness about three hours without stops · Urquhart about £14 online and £16 at the gate, open until evening from April to August, last entry one hour before closing · in summer Urquhart tickets sell out: buy them a few days ahead · night in Inverness.

### Day 10 — Speyside: the distillery trail
**Speyside**, the valley of the River Spey, is the area with the most distilleries in Scotland. Two tours a day are enough. The famous names are **Glenfiddich** in Dufftown, **The Macallan** near Craigellachie and **The Glenlivet**; in Knockando there's **Cardhu**, with tours from £18 (2026). Everything by booking. From Inverness to Dufftown it's about an hour and a quarter; the distilleries are a few kilometres apart, and it's best to put one in the morning and one in the afternoon, with a break in between.

Whoever drives doesn't drink: many distilleries offer the "driver's dram", the tasting in a sealed miniature bottle to take away. Between tours you can walk a stretch of the **Speyside Way**, the path along the river, which passes Aberlour and Craigellachie, with Thomas Telford's old iron bridge over the Spey. Night in Speyside, in Aberlour or Dufftown. *The driver's dram is tasted in the evening, at the hotel, not in the car.*

**In practice:** Inverness-Dufftown about an hour and a quarter · Dufftown, Craigellachie and Aberlour 10 minutes from each other · prices and hours for Glenfiddich, The Macallan and The Glenlivet on the official websites, in summer to be booked weeks ahead · Cardhu can't be reached by public transport · children under 8 are usually not admitted on tours.

### Day 11 — The Cairngorms, Dalwhinnie and the return to Edinburgh
You cross the **Cairngorms** National Park, the largest in the United Kingdom, with a stop at **Loch Morlich**, among the pines below the mountains. The loch is about ten kilometres from **Aviemore**, the park's main town, and has a sandy beach, rare in the Highlands; around it are the woods of Glenmore Forest, with short marked trails. It's the right moment for a walk, because the rest of the day is driving.

Heading down the A9 there's **Dalwhinnie**, one of the highest distilleries in Scotland, at 355 metres: classic 45-minute tour at £21 (2026). Then you continue down the A9 towards Perth and reach **Edinburgh**, where you return the car. Those with time stop at **Pitlochry**, a Victorian town halfway, for a break. *The A9 alternates dual-carriageway stretches with single-lane-each-way sections: overtake only where it's allowed.*

**In practice:** Dufftown-Aviemore about an hour, Aviemore-Dalwhinnie about 40 minutes, Dalwhinnie-Edinburgh about two and a quarter hours · Dalwhinnie open every day 10am-4pm, tour £21 (2026) · returning the car at the airport avoids city-centre traffic · night in Edinburgh, with the 5% visitor levy.

### Day 12 — Departure from Edinburgh
The last morning is for an unhurried walk on the **Royal Mile** or a visit to the National Museum of Scotland, then the flight home. The museum, on Chambers Street, a few minutes from the Royal Mile, is free and has a roof terrace with a view of the castle; a quick visit takes a couple of hours. Those who prefer the open air take one more stroll in **Princes Street Gardens** or the New Town.

For the airport you take the tram or the Airlink 100 again, both leaving the centre frequently. Allow a margin: at rush hour traffic can lengthen the ride. Whisky bought at the distilleries goes in the hold luggage, well wrapped, because liquids are limited in hand luggage. *If you have a connection at another airport, check its rules too.*

**In practice:** tram to the airport about 30-35 minutes from the centre, every 7-10 minutes during the day · National Museum of Scotland free · whisky bottles in the hold, wrapped in clothes · check the airport's liquids rules before leaving.

## The distillery trail
Rick did the distilleries, and they're a part of the trip not to skip. There are distilleries almost everywhere in Scotland, but the itinerary passes the most famous ones and a couple of lesser-known ones. Prices and hours are those on the official websites in November 2026.

**The famous ones**
- **Oban** (Oban, day 4): 1-hour classic tour from £24. Open every day: April 10-18, May to September 10-19, October 10-18, November to March 10-17. The tour programme is under review: recheck it.
- **Talisker** (Carbost, Skye, day 8): 1-hour tour with tasting from £22. March to October 10-17, November to February 10-16:30. Maintenance from October 11 to November 1, 2026, with some experiences suspended.
- **Glenfiddich**, **The Macallan** and **The Glenlivet** (Speyside, day 10): visits by booking on the official websites, where you should check prices and hours.
- **Cardhu** (Knockando, Speyside): tours from £18 for "A Nip Round", 1 hour, and from £23 for the "Flavour Journey", 1 hour and a half. May to September 10-18; January and February open Thursday to Monday. It can't be reached by public transport.

**Two hidden gems**
- **Dalwhinnie** (day 11): founded in 1897, at 355 metres it is one of the highest distilleries in Scotland, inside Cairngorms National Park. 45-minute classic tour from £21, 30-minute guided tasting from £14. Open every day 10-16.
- **Glen Ord** (Muir of Ord, on the Black Isle): the distillery of The Singleton, northwest of Inverness. The North Coast 500 website names it as the ideal start or end of the route. Classic tour of 1 hour and a quarter from £24. May to August 10-18, September to April 10-17. Dates are bookable about 90 days ahead.

**Whoever drives doesn't drink**
In Scotland the drink-driving limit is lower than in England. Rick's advice is zero tolerance. Many distilleries give drivers the **driver's dram**, a sample in a small sealed bottle to take away. Not all of them offer it: ask when you book. If everyone wants to taste, you can take an organised tour or a taxi for Speyside.

**Common rules:** book online, ahead in summer; groups are at most 16 people; children under 8 usually can't join the tours. In winter distilleries may close at the last minute because of the weather.

## Islay variant: the peat distilleries
If you love peated whisky, add 2-3 days after Oban. Drive down to the port of **Kennacraig**, on the Kintyre peninsula, and take the CalMac ferry to **Port Askaig**, in about 1 hour and 55 minutes. The port of Port Ellen is closed for works from June 2, 2026 until 2029. Book the car ahead.

On the south coast of the island there are three distilleries close to one another: **Laphroaig**, **Lagavulin** and **Ardbeg**.
- **Lagavulin:** 1 hour and a half classic tour from £22 (2026). March to October every day 10-17; November to February Monday to Saturday only. In the summer months and during the Fèis Ìle festival tours sell out early.
- **Laphroaig:** March to October every day 9:45-17 (2026). Tours and prices on the official website.

## North Coast 500 variant: the far north
The **North Coast 500** is a 516-mile loop, about 830 km, that starts and ends in Inverness and goes around the northern tip of Scotland. Driven in one go it would take 11-13 hours, but most people who do it take 5-7 days. Rick drove it: for him it's a breathtaking road.

With 4-5 extra days you enter from the west side, leaving Skye after day 8, and come out at Inverness, where you pick up the itinerary again from Speyside. The stages below are a suggestion:
- **Day A:** from Skye to **Applecross** over the **Bealach na Bà**, the pass with tight hairpins and a view of Skye, then the mountains of **Torridon**.
- **Day B:** from Torridon to **Ullapool**, the harbour village known for its fish, then **Assynt** and **Ardvreck** Castle on the loch.
- **Day C:** the **Kylesku** bridge and the Sutherland coast to **Durness**, with **Smoo Cave**.
- **Day D:** from Durness to **John o' Groats** and the **Duncansby** sea stacks, then down the east coast.
- **Day E:** **Glen Ord** on the Black Isle and arrival in **Inverness**. If you only have four days, combine D and E.

Most of the road is single-track. The Bealach na Bà is not suitable for large motorhomes and should be avoided in fog or ice. Petrol stations and shops are rare: fill up whenever you can. With a motorhome you only sleep at campsites and in permitted areas.

## The weather
The weather in Scotland is unpredictable, and Rick remembers it well. In summer highs are usually between 12 and 19 degrees, in winter between 2 and 6. The west coast and the islands are the windiest and wettest areas. In practice:
- waterproof jacket and layers always in the car, even in August;
- put the most famous views (Storr, Quiraing, Glencoe) on days with some margin, so you can move them;
- summer days are long, and the best weather often comes in the evening;
- in summer, when there's no wind, the midges come out: repellent.

Rick went in July, but he recommends autumn too: with the foliage Scotland is beautiful.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Zero tolerance at the wheel.** The Scottish limit is lower than the English one, and Rick's advice is not to drink at all. Taste the driver's dram in the evening, at the hotel.
- **Take the first miles on the left calmly.** It's better to pick up the car outside central Edinburgh, where the roads are quieter. *My memory: driving on the left is fine for me, but it takes some practice, let's say. And in the countryside the roads can get narrow now and then.*
- **Passing places are only for letting cars through.** Don't park there for photos.
- **Book accommodation and distillery tours in advance.** In summer Skye and Speyside fill up.
- **Edinburgh Castle and Urquhart with an online ticket.** They have timed entry.
- **Fill up whenever you can.** In the Highlands and on the North Coast 500 petrol stations are far apart.
- **Offline maps.** There's no signal on many stretches.
- **Don't build a day around a single view.** In fog you can't see the Quiraing: you need a plan B.
