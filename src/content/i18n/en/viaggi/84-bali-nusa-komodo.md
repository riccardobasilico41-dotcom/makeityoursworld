# Bali, Nusa Penida, Nusa Lembongan and Komodo in Two Weeks: Rice Terraces, Cliffs and Dragons

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (Rick has seen Bali, Nusa Penida and Komodo; routes, times and prices checked on November 5, 2026 against official and industry sources, to be rechecked before you go. Boat times to the Nusa islands and the amount of the Nusa local tax are not confirmed)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 14 days, 13 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** independently, with a day driver on Bali, fast boats to the Nusa islands, a round-trip domestic flight to Labuan Bajo and a boat with nights on board in Komodo National Park

## Apertura personale
Bali, the two Nusa islands and Komodo go well together. The Nusas are about half an hour by fast boat from Sanur, and there's a short direct flight from Bali to Labuan Bajo. In two weeks you string together rice terraces, cliffs and dragons without spending more time traveling than staying put.

What stuck with me most from Bali was the Kecak dance at Uluwatu at sunset, more than any rice field or beach. That's why the trip ends there. Nusa Penida, on the other hand, is a victim of its own success: by midday Kelingking is a line of people on a steep trail. Here the Nusas get three days, and you sleep on Penida precisely so you reach the viewpoints before the boats from Bali. In Komodo the dragons almost always stay still. What impresses you is their size, and the fact that the ranger is holding nothing but a forked stick.

Why 14 days: three nights in Ubud, three on the Nusas, three in Labuan Bajo and on the boat, a buffer day before the flight back to Bali and two nights in Uluwatu, close to the airport. With fewer days you rush. With more, each of these islands deserves at least a week.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** April to October, the dry season on Bali and the Nusas. In Komodo the dry season runs from April to December. For boat crossings the sea matters more than the rain: in rough seas boats run late or don't leave.
- **Documents:** passport valid for at least 6 months, **e-VOA** online at evisa.imigrasi.go.id and the **All Indonesia Arrival Card** before entry.
- **Bali tourist levy:** 150,000 IDR per person, paid online on the official Love Bali site (lovebali.baliprov.go.id).
- **Nusa tax:** on arrival at Nusa Penida and Nusa Lembongan you pay a local tax, separate from Bali's. The amount has changed several times: ask your boat operator.
- **Fast boats to the Nusas:** they leave from Sanur, with several departures a day from different companies. The crossing takes about half an hour, longer in rough seas. Times and prices vary between companies and over time and the last boats fill up: book the day before.
- **Flight to Labuan Bajo:** there are direct flights from Denpasar to Labuan Bajo (Komodo Airport, LBJ). Airlines change: check the routes when you book.
- **From the airport to the centre:** at **Denpasar Ngurah Rai (DPS)** there are no trains or metro. You leave by fixed zone-fare taxi from the official desk in arrivals (to Ubud about 300,000-450,000 IDR and 1-2 hours; Uluwatu is about 45 minutes away), by Grab or Gojek from the pickup points shown in the app (about 200,000-350,000 IDR to Ubud) or with a driver booked beforehand. Anyone offering a "taxi" in the arrivals hall away from the desk charges more: say no. At **Labuan Bajo (LBJ)** the airport is a few minutes from the harbour and the hotels, and almost all hotels and boat tours arrange pickup.
- **Komodo National Park:** since October 30, 2024, entry for foreigners costs 250,000 IDR per person, including trekking, wildlife watching and snorkeling. Diving is paid separately, 25,000 IDR per activity. The boat tour usually handles the tickets: ask what's included before you pay.
- **Currency and payments:** Indonesian rupiah (IDR): check the exchange rate before you leave. On Bali cards are accepted almost everywhere. On the Nusas and in Labuan Bajo, carry cash.
- **Time zone:** Bali, the Nusas and Flores are all on WITA (UTC+8).
- **Scooter or driver:** on the Nusas the roads are narrow and steep, and on Bali traffic is heavy. Only ride a scooter if you have real experience and an international driving permit. For everyone else, there's a driver.
- **At sea:** the currents between the Komodo islands and around Penida are strong. Swim only where the crew says, never alone. Before getting on a boat, check there are life jackets.
- **Nyepi:** the Balinese New Year (March, date varies) shuts Bali down for 24 hours and closes the airport.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Bali and the road to Ubud
You land in Denpasar and head up to **Ubud**, the base for the first three days. Best to have paid the tourist levy online already, together with the e-VOA and the All Indonesia Arrival Card, so you get through the checks faster. From the airport to Ubud it's about forty kilometers, but with south Bali traffic it takes one to two hours. The simplest option is a transfer from your accommodation or a pre-booked driver; otherwise ride-hailing apps, checking the pickup point carefully. *Lots of drivers come forward outside arrivals: if you go with one of them, agree on the price before you get in.*

In Ubud I stayed at **Villa Jacques**, and it was beautiful: you book it on villa rental platforms. If you want an Italian-speaking guide, you can message **Pande Wirta**, good and professional. The first day is just for arriving. If you still have energy, a late-afternoon walk in the center is enough, between **Jalan Monkey Forest** and **Ubud Palace** (Puri Saren), where Balinese dance performances are often held in the evening, with tickets sold on the spot. For dinner a warung is fine, with a nasi campur. Go to bed early: the time difference with Italy is seven hours in winter and six in summer, and tomorrow the alarm goes off at dawn.

**In practice:** airport to Ubud about 1-2 hours by car, depending on traffic · Love Bali levy of 150,000 IDR, e-VOA and arrival card done before you leave · rupiah from ATMs at the airport or in Ubud · book your driver and guide today for days 2 and 3.

### Day 2 — Ubud and the Tegalalang rice terraces
The **Tegalalang rice terraces** are the most famous and also the busiest: they're best early in the morning. They're about half an hour's drive north of Ubud, and before eight it's cool, the light is low over the terraces and the groups haven't arrived yet. You walk down dirt paths and small bamboo bridges; at some points farmers ask for a small offering to let you through, and that's normal. *Swings and photo nests are private paid attractions, separate from the terrace ticket.* If you want a workout, climb **Mount Batur** for sunrise, leaving in the night: in that case you see Tegalalang on the way back, in the late morning.

Then the **Monkey Forest** and the temples in town. The sacred monkey forest is a park with three temples among huge trees, home to hundreds of macaques: keep glasses, hats and food in a closed bag, and don't touch or feed the monkeys. Afterwards, on foot, **Pura Taman Saraswati** with its lotus pond in front of the temple, and Ubud market. Here, away from the concrete of the south, the flower offerings in front of the houses are renewed every morning. The hot afternoon is the time for a massage or a break at the villa. For a scooter in Ubud there's **Feby Yastika**, but only if you really know how to ride.

**In practice:** Tegalalang about 25,000 IDR entry, varying by access point, swings extra · Monkey Forest about 130,000 IDR for foreign adults since April 2026, open roughly 9 am to 6 pm · Batur: hotel pickup between 2 and 3 am, about two hours' climb, book through an agency or your accommodation · in temples cover shoulders and legs, sarongs are lent or rented at the entrance.

### Day 3 — Sidemen and the Mount Agung valley
A day trip to **Sidemen**, at the foot of Mount Agung. The terraces are less touristy than Tegalalang and tour buses barely get there. The road from Ubud drops toward Klungkung and then climbs back up the valley with many bends: allow one to one and a half hours. Leave early, because the summit of Agung, Bali's sacred volcano, is clearest in the morning and often clouds over later. You walk through the rice fields and stop at a warung with a view of the volcano. *The paths through the fields aren't marked: a village guide is easy to find and keeps you from ending up in people's courtyards.*

On the way it's worth stopping in **Klungkung** (Semarapura), home to **Kerta Gosa**, the kingdom's old hall of justice, with ceilings painted in the traditional Kamasan style. The valley also has weaving workshops where songket is made, the cloth woven with gold and silver threads. Head back in the afternoon, before dark, because the valley roads are narrow and poorly lit. From Ubud it's a day trip with a driver: agree on the price before you set off, and ask whether fuel and parking are included.

**In practice:** Ubud to Sidemen about 1-1.5 hours by car · Kerta Gosa with a modest entrance ticket · few ATMs in the valley, better to carry cash · if you want to slow down, there are guesthouses among the rice fields for a night in Sidemen.

### Day 4 — Fast boat from Sanur to Nusa Lembongan
You go down to **Sanur** harbor with a driver, leaving early to beat the traffic. From Ubud it's about twenty kilometers, but in the morning the traffic heading to the coast converges right on Sanur, and ride-hailing apps can't pick up inside the harbor: a pre-booked driver is best, and arrive at least half an hour early to check in at the company's desk. In about half an hour by boat you reach **Nusa Lembongan**, the quietest of the Nusas. *On Lembongan you often land on the beach with your feet in the water: shoes in hand and a small backpack, the crew unloads the big bags.*

The afternoon is for the **mangroves**, which you tour in a small boat, and **Devil's Tear**, the cliff where waves explode against the rock. The mangroves are on the northeast coast and the tour takes about an hour: boatmen run it when the tide is high enough to get through the channels. At Devil's Tear stay behind the higher rocks, because big waves arrive without warning. At sunset you cross the **Yellow Bridge**, which links Lembongan to **Nusa Ceningan**, and look over the **Blue Lagoon**. Only jump from the cliff when the sea is calm, and never alone.

**In practice:** Ubud to Sanur about 45 minutes to 1 hour in the morning, plus a buffer · fast boat Sanur to Lembongan about 30 minutes, book the day before · local Nusa levy paid on arrival, ask the operator for the amount · on the island you get around by scooter or pick-up with a driver · mangrove tour about an hour, price agreed on the spot.

### Day 5 — From Lembongan to Nusa Penida: snorkeling with manta rays
In the morning a local boat takes you from Lembongan to **Nusa Penida** in a few minutes: arrange it on the spot with your accommodation. Today is a sea day. The snorkeling trip to **Manta Point** and the nearby bays can be booked on Penida or already on Lembongan. Trips usually leave in the morning, when the sea is calmer, and besides Manta Point they normally stop at **Crystal Bay**, **Gamat Bay** and the Toyapakeh wall. The mantas are often there, but not always, and the sea here can be rough: the crew decides whether you go. *At Manta Point there's often a swell and the water is cooler than you'd expect: if you're not confident in the sea, ask for a life jacket and stay close to the boat.* Don't touch the mantas, and watch them from a few meters away.

The afternoon is for settling in and resting. To stay, the north coast between Toyapakeh and Ped is handy, where the boats arrive and there are warungs and rentals, or the west side, closer to Kelingking. At sunset Crystal Bay is close by. In the evening, book the driver for tomorrow, leaving before dawn. You sleep on Penida, and that's the trick of the three days: tomorrow morning you reach the viewpoints before the boats from Bali. I spent three nights on Penida and explored all of it at an easy pace, and I recommend doing the same: here the island gets two nights so everything fits into 14 days, but if you have an extra day, put it here.

**In practice:** local boat Lembongan to Penida a few minutes, arranged with your accommodation · half-day snorkeling trip, ask beforehand whether mask, fins and life jacket are included · few ATMs, mostly on the north coast: better to arrive with cash · book tomorrow's driver in the evening.

### Day 6 — Nusa Penida: Kelingking, Broken Beach and Angel's Billabong
Leave at first light for **Kelingking Beach**, the T-Rex-shaped bay. By midday it's a line of people on a steep trail, so get there very early. The island's roads are slow and even a few kilometers can take a long time: leaving around six means having the viewpoint almost to yourself. The descent to the beach is steep and tiring: you need shoes with a good grip. It takes about thirty to forty-five minutes down, over steps, rocks and bamboo handrails, and the climb back is harder. *In rain or on wet ground, skip the descent, and at the beach swimming is dangerous because of the currents: just get your feet wet.* Bring water, as there's no shade on the trail.

Then **Broken Beach**, the rock arch over the sea, and **Angel's Billabong**, the natural pool on the cliff, where you swim only at low tide with a calm sea. The two are close together and you visit them on foot one after the other. At Angel's Billabong the real danger is the waves that wash over the edge of the pool: if the sea is breaking, just look. If you prefer the east coast, go to **Diamond Beach** and **Atuh**, with fewer people. Both coasts in one day is too much unless you leave at dawn: better pick one and save the other for a possible third night. Penida's roads are narrow and full of potholes: a driver with a car takes a lot of worry off your hands.

**In practice:** leave around 6 am to arrive before the boats from Bali · Kelingking descent about 30-45 minutes, longer climb back, closed shoes and water · small entry or parking fees at the viewpoints, in cash · driver for the day with the list of stops agreed beforehand.

### Day 7 — Back to Sanur and flight to Labuan Bajo
Morning boat from Penida to Sanur, then straight to Denpasar airport for the flight to **Labuan Bajo**. An early-afternoon flight is best, because boats run late in rough seas. From Sanur to the airport allow half an hour to an hour, depending on traffic, and for domestic flights it's best to arrive an hour and a half to two hours early. *On domestic flights the checked baggage allowance can be lower than on international ones: check it when booking, especially if you carry a mask, fins and gear.* The direct flight takes just over an hour.

Labuan Bajo airport is a few minutes from the center, so you arrive in time to settle in and stop by the boat operator to confirm tomorrow's departure time and what's included. In the evening go to the **night market** on the waterfront: the catch of the day is laid out and grilled on the spot, at prices far lower than the restaurants overlooking the harbor. It's the **Kampung Ujung** market, which comes alive at sunset: you pick the fish at the stall and pay by weight, and ask the price before it goes on the grill.

**In practice:** boat Penida to Sanur about 30-45 minutes · Sanur to the airport about 30-60 minutes · flight Denpasar to Labuan Bajo about 1 hour 15, direct with several airlines · at the night market agree on the fish price first · night in town, near the harbor.

### Day 8 — By boat in the park: Kelor, Rinca and sunset at Kalong
You board a boat for three days and two nights. Sleeping on board is the only way to be on Padar at sunrise, before the groups. I did the liveaboard with **Kanha Loka**, and I recommend it. You leave Labuan Bajo harbor early in the morning. Boats range from wooden phinisi with cabins and private bathrooms to simple boats with mattresses on deck: before paying, ask what the cabin is like, whether snorkeling gear is included and who pays the park tickets. *Since 1 April 2026 the park has a cap of about 1,000 visitors a day across Komodo, Rinca and Padar, and Padar is visited in pre-booked time slots: your operator must have them booked already.*

First stop is **Kelor**, for a short climb and a swim. Then **Rinca**, the dragon island that's wilder and less crowded than Komodo: ask the operator for it specifically. You walk with a ranger, never alone, because the dragons are dangerous predators. The walk takes about an hour and is best done in the cooler hours; the ranger decides the route and the distance from the animals, and anyone with open wounds should tell them beforehand. At sunset the boat stops off **Kalong**, the island where flocks of flying foxes take off at dusk. The boat often anchors nearby for the night.

**In practice:** departure from Labuan Bajo harbor in the morning · park entry about 250,000 IDR for foreigners, usually handled by the operator · since 2026 a daily cap and booked slots for Padar, to confirm with the operator · hat, sunscreen and cash for tipping the crew.

### Day 9 — Padar at sunrise, Pink Beach and Komodo Island
The best moment is **Padar** at sunrise: a short, steep climb up a ridge, and below you three bays with three different sands all at once. Do it very early, because later on there's a steady line on the trail and the heat kicks in. The boat moves overnight or before dawn to the bay below the island, and you go ashore while it's still dark. The climb is a staircase that takes about half an hour, with a few stops at viewpoints along the way. *Headlamp, closed shoes and water: there's no shade at the top and after seven the sun is already strong.* You come down before the day boats from Labuan Bajo arrive.

Then **Pink Beach**, with its pinkish sand: the snorkeling is better than the beach. The color comes from tiny red fragments of marine organisms mixed into the white sand, and it shows best in the morning light. In the afternoon, **Komodo Island**, with trails starting from Loh Liang. At the ranger station you choose a short, medium or long route; in the afternoon heat the short one is enough, because the dragons often stay right near the park buildings. Walk in line behind the ranger and don't get closer for photos. The second night is spent on board again.

**In practice:** Padar with a booked morning slot · climb about 30 minutes, steep · on Komodo routes of different lengths with the ranger · at Pink Beach get in the water where the crew says.

### Day 10 — Manta rays, Taka Makassar, Kanawa and back to Labuan Bajo
Last morning at sea. If the season and currents allow, the boat stops at **Manta Point**, then at **Taka Makassar**, one of the classic stops on boat trips in the park. At Manta Point you snorkel in groups with a guide in the water, drifting with the current while the boat follows: if you're not comfortable, stay on board, as the mantas can also be seen from above. Taka Makassar is a white sandbank that emerges at low tide in the middle of a turquoise sea, and it's at its best when the tide is low. *Here you don't chase or touch the mantas, and with fins you always stay above the coral, never standing on it.*

The last snorkel is at **Kanawa**, before heading back to Labuan Bajo in the afternoon. Kanawa is a small island with the reef very close to shore, easy even for weak swimmers. The currents here are strong: get in the water when the crew says so. After landing you go back to town: it's best to have booked your accommodation for the next two nights before the boat trip, as well as any dive for tomorrow. In the evening, the waterfront again or the Kampung Ujung market.

**In practice:** spots and times depend on the tides and the crew decides · back at Labuan Bajo harbor in the afternoon · town accommodation and dive center booked ahead in high season · leave the crew tip before going ashore.

### Day 11 — Labuan Bajo, buffer day
A day kept free on purpose. If the weather canceled an outing, you make it up here. If everything went smoothly, it's a day for a dive with a Labuan Bajo dive center or for doing nothing at all. Day trips into the park leave early in the morning and usually include two or three dives, returning in the afternoon; you need your certification and logbook. *The most widely used guidelines ask for at least 18 hours between the last of several dives and a flight: with tomorrow's flight, dive only in the morning and only if the flight is in the afternoon.*

Non-divers have options close to town. **Batu Cermin** cave is a few kilometers from the center, with a narrow passage where at certain hours the light comes in and reflects off the rock. Further away, about an hour's drive into the Flores hinterland, is **Cunca Wulang**, a canyon with pools for swimming in the dry season. Or stay in town, between a coffee by the harbor and the sunset from one of the hills above the bay. With Komodo's seas, it's better not to have your flight back on the same day you come ashore.

**In practice:** dives with a separate park fee, 25,000 IDR per activity · at least 18 hours between the last dive and the flight · Batu Cermin a few kilometers from the center, Cunca Wulang about an hour's drive, with a guide or driver · dinner at the night market or on the waterfront.

### Day 12 — Flight to Bali and arrival in Uluwatu
Flight from Labuan Bajo to Denpasar and the road to **Uluwatu**, at Bali's southern tip. It's close to the airport, so it's the right base for the last days. The flight takes just over an hour, and from Denpasar to Uluwatu allow forty-five minutes to an hour by car, more at rush hour. A midday flight works best, so you arrive with time to settle in and watch the first sunset over the cliffs. *In Uluwatu the distances between beaches, temple and accommodation aren't walkable: you need a driver, a scooter if you're experienced, or ride-hailing apps.*

The south around Seminyak and Canggu is concrete, traffic and bars for visitors: Uluwatu has the same coastline with more character. To stay, the area between Bingin, Padang Padang and Suluban puts the beaches a few minutes away. The afternoon is for resting after the days at sea; in the evening, dinner at one of the warungs on the Bingin cliffs, watching the sun go down over the ocean. If you want tomorrow's Kecak dance without a rush, check times and tickets today.

**In practice:** flight Labuan Bajo to Denpasar about 1 hour 15 · airport to Uluwatu about 45 minutes to 1 hour · stay between Bingin, Padang Padang and Suluban · arrange a driver or scooter for the two days.

### Day 13 — Uluwatu: beaches and the Kecak dance at sunset
Uluwatu's beaches lie at the bottom of stairways cut into the rock: **Padang Padang**, **Bingin**, **Suluban** with its bar inside the cave. Visit them in the morning, when it's cooler and the stairs are in shade, and at low tide, because at Suluban and Bingin the sand almost disappears at high tide. The waves are reef breaks for people who already surf. If you want a lesson, message **Hendra**, who teaches surfing in Kuta. *The reef is sharp and just below the surface: for a swim, wait for the right tide and get in where others do.*

In the late afternoon you go up to the cliff temple, and at sunset there's the **Kecak dance**: dozens of men sitting in a circle make all the music with their voices. The temple, **Pura Luhur Uluwatu**, stands on the edge of a sheer cliff above the ocean: you enter wearing the sarong handed out at the entrance and walk the clifftop path before the show. The temple monkeys are famous for stealing glasses, phones and hats, so hold on to them. For the Kecak arrive an hour early, because the best seats in the amphitheater fill up fast.

**In practice:** Kecak about 150,000 IDR, show around 6 pm, buy your ticket early · temple entry separate, sarong provided at the entrance · Padang Padang with a small ticket · beaches at low tide, suitable shoes for the stairs.

### Day 14 — Departure from Denpasar
From Uluwatu to the airport is a short drive, but traffic in south Bali is unpredictable: leave a margin. Allow half an hour to an hour, and for international flights be at the airport at least three hours ahead. If your flight leaves in the evening, you still have a morning by the sea. *For evening flights, ask for a late check-out or keep the room until the afternoon, so you can shower after the last swim.*

With a free afternoon you can stop in **Jimbaran**, on the way to the airport, where you dine on the beach with grilled fish at sunset. Spend leftover rupiah before security, since they're hard to change outside Indonesia. A few days before the flight, check the airline's exit requirements and do the online check-in.

**In practice:** Uluwatu to the airport about 30 minutes to 1 hour · at the airport at least 3 hours before international flights · ask your accommodation for a late check-out · Jimbaran a few minutes from the airport for a last dinner.

## What to see / what to do
- **Bali's rice terraces:** Tegalalang early in the morning, Sidemen for peace and quiet.
- **Nusa Lembongan and Ceningan:** mangroves, Devil's Tear, the Yellow Bridge and the Blue Lagoon.
- **Nusa Penida:** Kelingking at sunrise, Broken Beach, Angel's Billabong, and snorkeling with manta rays.
- **The Komodo dragons** on Rinca and Komodo Island, always with a ranger.
- **Padar at sunrise**, before the groups arrive.
- **Pink Beach, Taka Makassar and Kanawa** for snorkeling.
- **The Kecak dance at Uluwatu** at sunset.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Sleep on Nusa Penida** instead of doing a day trip from Bali: it's the only way to have Kelingking without the line. I spent three nights there and recommend it that way, at an easy pace.
- **Book Nusa boats the day before** in high season: the last departures fill up.
- **Don't ride a scooter on the Nusas** unless you have real experience: narrow, steep roads full of potholes.
- **Early-afternoon flight to Labuan Bajo:** the boat from Penida can run late.
- **Sleep on the boat in Komodo** to be on Padar at sunrise.
- **Ask for Rinca** on the tour, and only walk among the dragons with a ranger.
- **Ask what's included** in the boat price: park tickets, snorkeling, diving.
- **Keep a buffer day in Labuan Bajo** before the flight to Bali.
- **Pay the Bali levy online** before you leave, on the Love Bali site.
- **Skip generic south Bali**: Uluwatu has the same coast with more character.

## Chiusura personale
This loop combines Indonesia's most famous side with a piece of its wildest, with short transfers. Bali is better seen from Ubud and Uluwatu. The Nusas should be taken slowly and with early alarms. Komodo remains the place where an animal lying still in the shade manages to impress you more than any view. If you have a few extra days, stretch it out: each of these islands deserves at least a week.
