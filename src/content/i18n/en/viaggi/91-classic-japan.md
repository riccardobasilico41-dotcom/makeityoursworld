# Classic Japan in 17 Days: Tokyo, Hakone, the Japanese Alps, Kyoto, Kōya-san and Miyajima

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (places Rick visited, put together into one practical route; practical information checked in October-November 2026, to recheck before departure; first-person memories only those given by Rick, none invented)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 17 days, 16 nights; 14- and 21-day variants at the end
**Compagni di viaggio:** independently, by train with a 14-day JR Pass plus local buses (Nakasendō, Kamikochi, Shirakawa-go) and the private Nankai line for Kōya-san

## Apertura personale
The classic trip to Japan runs almost entirely along the Pacific coast: Tokyo, Fuji, Kyoto, Osaka, Hiroshima. This itinerary keeps that backbone and adds the interior, the part many people skip on a first trip. The Japanese Alps, the old Nakasendō road between Magome and Tsumago, Takayama with its morning markets, the thatched-roof houses of Shirakawa-go, Kanazawa and the sacred mountain of Kōya-san.

The site already has another trip to Japan, [the 12 April days among sakura, Fuji and Kyoto](/viaggi/giappone-itinerario). This one is something else. It has more days, fewer big cities and more nights in ryokan, shukubō and mountain villages. Of Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka it keeps the minimum: if you want the detail of those days, you'll find it there.

Like every trip on this site, it's rooted in places I've personally visited and experiences I've actually lived, put together into one practical route.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** late April to early June and October to mid-November. Kamikochi opens in mid-April and closes in mid-November (in 2026 from April 17 to November 15). June-July is the rainy season over most of Honshu, August is hot and humid. In winter Shirakawa-go and Takayama are under snow: beautiful, but Kamikochi is closed and the Nakasendō can have snow on its stone steps.
- **Documents:** Italian citizens need no tourist visa for up to 90 days, with a passport valid for the whole stay.
- **Currency:** yen. Cards work almost everywhere in the cities; in mountain villages, in the Kamikochi lodges and in some shukubō you need cash.
- **JR Pass:** in 2026 the ordinary 14-day pass costs 80,000 yen bought online (84,000 from an agency abroad), the 7-day one 50,000. It covers the Hikari and Kodama Shinkansen, the Hokuriku Shinkansen, and the Hida and Thunderbird limited expresses. Nozomi trains need a separate special ticket (4,960 yen between Tokyo and Kyoto). It doesn't cover the buses to Magome, Kamikochi and Shirakawa-go, the Odakyu lines to Hakone or the Nankai to Kōya-san: add up the legs before you buy it.
- **Buses to book:** some Nohi bus runs between Takayama, Shirakawa-go and Kanazawa are reservation only. The direct Matsumoto-Takayama bus is reservation only; times and rules for the Kamikochi buses are on the operators' websites. Better to check as soon as you have your dates.
- **Luggage:** a trolley on the Nakasendō and in Kamikochi is a problem. Between Magome and Tsumago there's the tourist offices' luggage service; for the rest you use takkyubin, hotel-to-hotel shipping, arranged the day before.
- **Where to sleep:** at least one night in a ryokan with onsen (Hakone), one in a Nakasendō village, one in a shukubō at Kōya-san. The gasshō houses in Shirakawa-go that offer lodging are few and need booking well ahead.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Tokyo
Landing at Narita or Haneda, an IC card (Suica or PASMO) topped up for metro and buses, and an easy first evening. Don't activate the JR Pass today: for the first three days you get around by metro, and the 14-day pass has to cover the long part of the trip. To sleep, an area handy for the trains on day 3: Shinjuku, or Tokyo Station.

### Day 2 — Tokyo, a single day
One full day, picking one side of the city. The old side: Asakusa and Senso-ji early, Ueno, Yanaka with its neighborhood streets. Or the new side: Meiji-Jingu, Harajuku, Shibuya and Shinjuku in the evening. Tokyo gets short shrift, on purpose: the real trip starts tomorrow. If you have two extra days, put them here; the addresses are in the [sakura trip](/viaggi/giappone-itinerario).

### Day 3 — Hakone, the first ryokan
From Shinjuku you reach Hakone on the Odakyu line, or by Shinkansen to Odawara. The classic route is a loop: mountain train, funicular, ropeway over the fumaroles of Owakudani, boat on Lake Ashi, with Fuji visible only on clear days. Odakyu's Hakone Freepass covers almost everything. The evening is the reason for the stop: a ryokan with onsen, kaiseki dinner and futon. *Fuji from Hakone is often hidden: if you want it at all costs, you can take the trip to Kawaguchiko described in the sakura trip.*

### Day 4 — Toward the Nakasendō: Magome
From Odawara the Shinkansen takes you to Nagoya, then the Shinano limited express to Nakatsugawa and the bus to **Magome**. This is where you activate the JR Pass, if you chose one. Magome is an Edo-period post town, built uphill along the old road that linked Kyoto and Edo through the mountains. You arrive in the afternoon, when the day-trip groups leave. You sleep in a minshuku in the village or in Tsumago.

### Day 5 — On foot from Magome to Tsumago, then Matsumoto
The most famous stretch of the Nakasendō: about 8 km, 2-3 hours of walking through woods, waterfalls and a teahouse halfway, with the Magome pass at about 790 meters. From late March to late November the tourist offices of the two villages ship luggage: you drop it off between 8:30 and 11:30 and pick it up on arrival after 13:00, at 1,000 yen per bag. **Tsumago** is the village its residents have restored and protected since 1968. From Nagiso station the Chuo line train takes you to **Matsumoto**. If you arrive in time, you can see the castle today.

### Day 6 — Kamikochi and the road to Takayama
**Kamikochi** is a valley at about 1,500 meters inside Chubu Sangaku National Park, closed to private cars: you get there only by bus or taxi from the Sawando and Hirayu parking areas. The basic walk goes from Taisho Pond to Kappa Bridge, flat, between the Azusa river and the Hotaka peaks. If you have the legs, carry on to Myojin. In the afternoon the bus goes down to Hirayu Onsen and from there to **Takayama**. Best to ship your luggage straight to Takayama the day before. *Off season, from mid-November to mid-April, Kamikochi is closed: take the direct Matsumoto-Takayama bus and gain half a day for Matsumoto Castle.*

### Day 7 — Takayama
Start early with the **morning markets**, along the Miyagawa river and in front of Takayama Jinya, the old seat of local government. Then **Sanmachi Suji**, the streets of dark wooden houses with sake shops, recognizable by the cedar balls hanging at the entrance. In the afternoon the Hida Folk Village, an open-air museum of mountain houses, useful even if you don't then sleep in Shirakawa-go. For dinner, Hida beef, as a steak or as street skewers.

### Day 8 — Shirakawa-go and Kanazawa
The Nohi bus takes you from Takayama to **Shirakawa-go** and continues to Kanazawa: just over two hours for the whole route. Some runs are reservation only; fares and times on the Nohi website. The village of Ogimachi has been a UNESCO World Heritage site since 1995 for its gasshō-zukuri houses, with steep thatched roofs shaped like hands joined in prayer. The Shiroyama viewpoint gives the overall view. Walk through it discreetly: these are lived-in houses, and many can't be visited. In the afternoon another bus continues to **Kanazawa**. If you booked in time, you sleep in a gasshō house and continue tomorrow.

### Day 9 — Kanazawa
Kanazawa was spared by wartime bombing and has kept much of the city of samurai and merchants. **Kenroku-en**, one of the most famous gardens in Japan, should be seen at opening. Then the castle, the **Higashi Chaya** teahouse district, the Nagamachi samurai district and the **21st Century Museum** of contemporary art. For lunch, **Omicho** market, for fish from the Sea of Japan.

### Day 10 — From Kanazawa to Kyoto
Hokuriku Shinkansen to Tsuruga and the Thunderbird limited express to Kyoto, with one change at Tsuruga, all covered by the JR Pass. Arrival by lunch. The afternoon is for Higashiyama, on foot from Kiyomizu-dera toward Gion. Kyoto needs more days than this trip gives it: here you get the two you need to see its center, the rest is in the sakura trip.

### Day 11 — Kyoto
**Fushimi Inari** at dawn, before the groups, climbing past the part everyone photographs. Then the northwest of the city: Kinkaku-ji, the sand garden of Ryoan-ji, Arashiyama in the afternoon. The day is packed. If you prefer a slower pace, swap Arashiyama for the zen temple district of Daitoku-ji.

### Day 12 — Nara
Half a day, or a whole one at a calm pace. From Kyoto you get there on the JR Nara Line. Todaiji with the Great Buddha, Kasuga Taisha with its lanterns, the sika deer roaming free in the park. You go back to Kyoto for the night, or continue toward Osaka to be closer to tomorrow's Nankai.

### Day 13 — Kōya-san, the night at the temple
From Osaka Namba the Nankai line climbs to Gokurakubashi, then a funicular and a bus take you up to the plateau of **Kōya-san**, the center of Shingon Buddhism founded by Kūkai in the 9th century. There are more than a hundred temples on the plateau, and many offer lodging: the **shukubō**. You sleep on tatami and have dinner with the monks' vegetarian cuisine, shojin ryori. Before dinner, Kongobu-ji and the Danjo Garan complex. *Check the status of the funicular and the connections on the Nankai website before you leave.*

### Day 14 — Okunoin at dawn and back to Osaka
In the morning you join the monks' prayer, then walk through **Okunoin**, the largest cemetery in Japan: about two kilometers among centuries-old cedars and over two hundred thousand tombs and monuments, up to Kūkai's mausoleum. In the afternoon you go down to **Osaka**. The evening is for Dotonbori and Shinsekai, with okonomiyaki and takoyaki.

### Day 15 — Hiroshima and Miyajima
The Shinkansen gets you to Hiroshima from Shin-Osaka. In the morning the **Peace Park and Museum**, with the time they ask for. Then local train and JR ferry to **Miyajima**, both covered by the pass. It's worth sleeping on the island: in the evening, when the day-trippers leave, Itsukushima Shrine and the torii in the water are a different place.

### Day 16 — Miyajima and the way back
In the morning Mount Misen, on foot or by ropeway, with the view over the Seto Inland Sea. Then ferry and Shinkansen toward Osaka, if you fly from Kansai, or toward Tokyo, if you leave from there: a long ride, the last full day of the JR Pass.

### Day 17 — Return home
Departure from Kansai or Tokyo. From Kansai, the Haruka reaches the airport from Kyoto and Osaka.

## What to see / what to do
- **The Nakasendō between Magome and Tsumago:** eight kilometers on foot on a road from three hundred years ago, with your luggage traveling on its own.
- **Kamikochi:** the simplest way to see the Japanese Alps up close, on flat trails. Only from mid-April to mid-November.
- **Takayama at dawn:** the morning markets and the Sanmachi streets before ten.
- **Shirakawa-go:** the gasshō houses, best seen also in the evening or early morning if you sleep there.
- **Kenroku-en and Higashi Chaya in Kanazawa.**
- **A night in a shukubō at Kōya-san**, with the morning prayer and Okunoin.
- **Miyajima in the evening**, after the last wave of day trips.

## Variants
**14 days.** Drop Hakone (go from Tokyo straight to Nagoya) and Nara becomes a half day from Kyoto. Skip Kōya-san or Hiroshima: keeping both in 14 days means rushing.

**21 days.** Two more days in Tokyo, with the trip to Nikko or Kawaguchiko. One more day in Kyoto. A night in Hirayu or Okuhida Onsen between Kamikochi and Takayama. Himeji Castle between Osaka and Hiroshima.

**In winter (December-March).** Kamikochi is closed. Shirakawa-go under snow is the show of the season, and in January there are a few evenings of illumination (in 2027 on January 11, 17, 24 and 31). On those dates access is regulated and lodging is assigned by lottery, months ahead. If you want to ski, add a few days in Hakuba, described in the [Hokkaido and skiing trip](/viaggi/hokkaido-sci-giappone).

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Do the JR Pass math.** This route uses many buses and private lines the pass doesn't cover. The 14-day pass pays off if you activate it on day 4 and finish with the return to Tokyo; without the return, single tickets may cost less.
- **Book the buses as soon as you have your dates:** Nohi for Shirakawa-go, the Matsumoto-Takayama bus, and the gasshō lodgings.
- **Ship your luggage** between Matsumoto and Takayama and between Takayama and Kanazawa. In the villages and in Kamikochi you get around with a small backpack.
- **Cash** for minshuku, the morning markets, the shukubō.
- **Respect in lived-in villages:** in Shirakawa-go and on the Nakasendō, don't enter private courtyards and don't leave trash: take your rubbish with you.
- **Onsen and tattoos:** many ryokan still ban them in the shared baths. Rooms with a private bath or private rental baths, the kashikiri, solve it.

## Chiusura personale
The classic trip shows the Japan of the big cities and the most famous temples. This version adds the mountain villages, the roads on foot and the nights in temples, and asks in return for more days and a bit of organizing around buses. If you have seventeen days and a first trip behind you, or want to make just one trip but a complete one, here you'll find the Japan that lies between the two coasts.
