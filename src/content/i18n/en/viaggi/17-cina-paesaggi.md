# The Other China in 18 Days: Zhangjiajie, Sichuan, Yunnan and Yangshuo

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 18 days, 17 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independent, with domestic flights and high-speed rail booked in advance
**Categoria:** Asia · Cina · paesaggi · trekking · montagna · natura

---

## Apertura personale
This is the trip you take the second time, when the Great Wall and the Terracotta Army are already behind you and the real question remains: what is China like when it stops being the China of monuments? The answer lies in four provinces that foreign travelers barely touch (Hunan, Sichuan, Yunnan, Guangxi) and in a sequence of landscapes with few equals: quartzite pillars drifting in and out of the fog, a town built over a waterfall, a vertical megalopolis where the subway runs through an inhabited building, a 5,600-meter massif serving as the backdrop to an open-air show, a gorge three thousand meters deep to cross on foot, and the karst towers on the Li River. You walk a lot, you climb to altitude, and almost everywhere you're the only foreigner in sight.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** April-May or September-October. Golden Week (October 1-7) and Chinese New Year are still to be avoided, and here the reason is even more concrete than on the classic axis: at Zhangjiajie, in those days, cable car lines exceed three hours. From June to August the rains close stretches of Tiger Leaping Gorge, and Chongqing becomes one of the country's "furnace cities," with weeks above 40 degrees (104°F).
- **Documents, payments, connectivity, trains:** the same rules as the classic itinerary apply: 30-day visa exemption through the end of 2026, Alipay or WeChat Pay set up **before** leaving, foreign eSIM or VPN installed **before**, train tickets on sale 15 days ahead, at a time that varies from station to station. Here, though, the problem gets worse: outside the big cities English disappears entirely, and the camera translator goes from convenience to necessity.
- **Domestic flights:** on this itinerary they're unavoidable and need to go in the budget. Zhangjiajie-Chengdu, Chengdu-Lijiang and Lijiang-Guilin by train would cost a whole day each. Chinese domestic flights are cheap and frequent, but delays are common: better not to plan tight connections.
- **Altitude:** this is the factor this itinerary adds compared to the other one. Lijiang sits at 2,400 meters, Shangri-La tops 3,200, the Impression Lijiang amphitheater is at 3,100 and the Yulong Xueshan cable car reaches 4,506. The oxygen canisters sold at the park entrance are not a souvenir. If you fly in from sea level you need to sleep at least one night in Lijiang before going up.
- **Park admissions, the real expense:** Zhangjiajie National Park costs about 236 yuan (valid 4 days, internal shuttles included), but the elevator and cable cars are paid separately; Tianmen Shan is a separate ticket of around 250-280 yuan; for Yulong Xueshan you need admission, shuttle and cable car; Impression Lijiang costs 190-280 yuan. Added up, these items exceed everything else in the trip.
- **Mandatory reservations:** the Chengdu panda base has named entry tied to your passport and physical ticket offices closed; the Tianmen Shan cable car has limited time slots; the big Yulong Xueshan cable car has daily quotas that sell out; Impression Lijiang has to be booked by choosing the performance.
- **Approximate budget:** lower than the classic itinerary on lodging and food (in provincial cities a new, clean room can be found for 40€, a meal costs less than 10), higher on domestic transport and park admissions.
- **Food:** three different cuisines in eighteen days. Hunan's is the spiciest in China in the strict sense: it burns, period. Sichuan adds the pepper that numbs the tongue, the famous "málà." Yunnan is the exact opposite: mushrooms, flowers, herbs and even cheeses, very rare in the rest of the country.
- **Luggage:** real hiking shoes, not sneakers, because you walk at Zhangjiajie, on the Tianmen staircase and for two days along Tiger Leaping Gorge. Layers for altitude, a light rain shell, a headlamp for the trek, and cash, because along the gorge trail the connection comes and goes.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Chongqing
We landed at Chongqing Jiangbei (CKG), connected to the center by subway. We settled on the Yuzhong peninsula, the historic and financial core, the right base for everything you do in the city.

The first evening was for getting oriented, which here means something specific: realizing that the city is built on multiple levels and that what looks like one block on the map can be separated by a hundred meters of elevation. For dinner, **xiaomian**, the spicy noodles that in Chongqing are eaten on the street, sitting on plastic stools.

Before you even leave the airport it's worth sorting out two things: payments and connectivity. In China almost everything is paid with **Alipay** or **WeChat Pay**, which now also accept foreign credit cards, and without an eSIM or a VPN many Western apps don't work. The metro from the airport to the center takes under an hour. The first afternoon a stroll around **Jiefangbei**, the pedestrian area of the Liberation Monument, is enough, plus a walk to the tip of the peninsula where the Yangtze and the Jialing meet. *Google Maps is unreliable here: use Amap (Gaode), also in English.*

**In practice:** airport to Yuzhong by metro, about an hour with one change, a ticket of a few yuan · taxis and Didi are cheap, but at rush hour the bridges jam · sleep between Jiefangbei and Hongya Dong to do everything on foot

### Day 2 — Vertical Chongqing
We devoted the day to the strangest city in China. At **Liziba** the monorail enters and exits the sixth floor of an inhabited apartment building; then the old district of **Ciqikou**, preserved inside the megalopolis.

In the late afternoon we took the **cable car over the Yangtze**: a few yuan, five minutes suspended above the river, with the city lighting up on both sides.

After dark, **Hongya Dong**, but from the opposite bank of the Jialing, not from inside. Inside it's a themed shopping mall and you wonder why everyone talks about it; from outside it's eleven stories of illuminated buildings reflected in the water, with the skyscrapers behind.

For dinner, **Chongqing hot pot**, the original, which makes no concessions: red broth of beef tallow and chili, no softened versions.

**In practice:** Liziba is on monorail line 2, the viewing platform is below the station · Yangtze cable car: long queues in the evening, better to go before sunset · in hot pot you can ask for mild spice (wei la), which is still spicy

### Day 3 — Furong Zhen, the town on the waterfall
**High-speed train Chongqing-Furong Zhen, about 3.5 to 4.5 hours with a change at Zhangjiajie West.** The train has made easy a leg that used to take a whole day on the road.

**Furong Zhen** is a Tujia village of wooden stilt houses leaning against the rock, with a waterfall passing beneath and through it. We arrived in the afternoon, when the tour buses were still there, and the good part began around six: the groups all leave together, the lights come on over the waterfall and the town goes back to being four streets of wet stone.

Don't miss the **walkway carved into the rock that passes behind the waterfall**, from which you emerge drenched in spray.

**We slept in the town**, in a guesthouse overlooking the river: it's the only reason not to do it as a day trip.

**In practice:** train tickets on 12306 or Trip.com with your passport, best bought as soon as sales open · Furong Zhen has an entry ticket for the village, check at the time · the waterfall is lit up until late in the evening

### Day 4 — To Zhangjiajie
We transferred to **Wulingyuan**, the town just outside the main entrance of the national park (about 2 hours by car or bus from Furong Zhen).

The afternoon was for getting organized, which matters more here than elsewhere: the park is huge, the ticket is valid for four days and the map needs to be studied the night before, not at the entrance. If you improvise, you spend the next day on shuttles and in lines.

*Sleeping in Wulingyuan and not in the city is the choice that saves two hours a day of transfers.*

The park has four entrances: **Wulingyuan**, the national forest one near Zhangjiajie city, **Yangjiajie** and **Tianzi Shan**. You buy the ticket once, it's valid for several consecutive days and includes the eco-buses linking the areas; the Bailong elevator, the cable cars and the Ten-Mile Gallery mini-train are paid separately. At the first entry the ticket is registered with your passport and face recognition. In the evening, in town, buy water and snacks for the next day, a light raincoat and some supplies: inside the park there are kiosks, but they cost more. *It's worth fixing tomorrow's route now, area by area, and leaving on the first bus.*

**In practice:** Furong Zhen to Wulingyuan by bus via Zhangjiajie or car with driver, about 2 hours · park ticket with eco-buses around 230-240 yuan, valid 4 days · in Wulingyuan simple restaurants and hotels of every level near the entrance

### Day 5 — Yuanjiajie and the Bailong Elevator
We were **at the entrance at opening**, not a minute later: it's the difference between five minutes of line for the elevator and an hour and a half.

The **Bailong Elevator** climbs 326 meters glued to the rock wall, inside a glass cage, and in the last hundred meters the rock disappears. At the top are the **Yuanjiajie** viewpoints over the Hallelujah Pillar (the column that inspired the floating mountains in Avatar) and the First Bridge Under Heaven.

The tip that's worth the day: walk to the farthest platforms. On the famous one there are three hundred people; five hundred meters away, on the same walkways, there are ten.

*On fog, flip the reasoning: it's what makes the pillars look suspended in the void. With a clear sky they look like ordinary mountains.*

**In practice:** Bailong elevator about 65 yuan going up, separate from the park ticket · the park opens around 7, go straight to the elevator · bring water and a packed lunch, there are toilets up top but not many

### Day 6 — Tianzi Shan and the valley floor
Second day in the park, with the ticket already in hand. **Tianzi Shan** in the morning, for the widest panorama over the whole massif, and in the afternoon the descent along the **Golden Whip Stream trail (Jinbian Xi)**, at the bottom of the valley.

It's the part almost nobody does and it's worth half a day: looking at those pillars from below, neck craned and the stream beside you, is more impressive than seeing them from above. And the tour buses don't reach the valley floor.

To get up to Tianzi Shan you can use the cable car or the eco-bus to the **Helong** area, where the lookouts face the **Imperial Brush Peaks**, spires that look like upturned brushes. The Golden Whip Stream trail is about seven and a half kilometers long, almost all flat, and takes two to three hours to the national forest entrance. Macaques used to tourists live along the stream: *no food in your hands or visible in your bags, because they snatch it*. If your legs still have it, add the **Ten-Mile Gallery**, the valley of pillars with the mini-train that runs through it in a few minutes.

**In practice:** Tianzi Shan cable car about 70 yuan one way · the eco-buses are included in the ticket, at peak times you wait · from the bottom of the stream you exit at the national forest entrance, buses to Wulingyuan from there

### Day 7 — Tianmen Shan and flight to Chengdu
We went into the city for a day at **Tianmen Shan**, with the cable car time slot booked in advance. The cable car, almost seven and a half kilometers long, is among the longest in the world: it leaves from the city center, takes almost half an hour and near the end flies over the road of 99 hairpin turns.

At the top is the **Heaven's Gate**, a natural arch cave 131 meters high, reached by climbing 999 steps (or by escalators inside the mountain, for those who don't feel up to it), and there are the **cliffside walkways with glass floors**: safe and wide, but if you're afraid of heights you stop after three steps.

**Evening flight Zhangjiajie-Chengdu.**

**In practice:** Tianmen Shan ticket with cable car in time slots, book ahead on the official site or Trip.com · on the glass walkways you wear shoe covers, for a fee · Zhangjiajie to Chengdu flight under two hours, the airport is about twenty minutes from town

### Day 8 — Chengdu: pandas, tea and hot pot
We went to the **giant panda base at the 7:30 slot**, with the named reservation made days earlier. Everything depends on the time: at seven thirty the animals are awake, eating bamboo and climbing; at eleven they're asleep, and whoever arrives then leaves convinced it's an overrated place.

Don't skip the **red panda** area, which almost everyone rushes past to get to the giants, and it's the most fun.

The afternoon at the **Wenshu Monastery** and then in **People's Park**, at the teahouse: it's the quintessential city activity, and seeing hundreds of people sitting drinking tea unhurried says more about Chengdu than any monument.

In the evening, **Sichuan hot pot** with the divided broth. Sichuan pepper doesn't burn, it numbs the tongue, and the first time the sensation is almost alarming, before becoming an addiction.

**In practice:** panda base about 55 yuan, name-based booking with your passport up to 14 days ahead · by taxi or Didi it's half an hour from the center, the metro takes longer · Wenshu is free to enter, in People's Park you order tea at the table and pay little

### Day 9 — Leshan, then flight to Lijiang
Day trip to the **Leshan Giant Buddha**, 71 meters carved into the rock at the confluence of three rivers, about an hour by high-speed train. Alternatively **Dujiangyan**, the irrigation system in uninterrupted operation since the 3rd century BC, but they're in opposite directions and in a single day you only do one.

**Evening flight Chengdu-Lijiang.** You arrive at 2,400 meters, which is why the real altitude is only tackled from the next day.

From Leshan station to the site it's about thirty minutes by taxi or bus. The Buddha can be seen in two ways: from above, beside the head, and then down the **Nine-Turn Plank Road**, the walkway carved into the cliff that leads to the statue's feet; or from the river, by boat, which passes in front in a few minutes and shows the whole statue together with the two guardian figures. *On holidays the queue for the stairway can take more than an hour: arrive at opening or choose the boat.* For lunch, in Leshan you eat **bobo ji**, cold skewers in spicy broth, paid by the stick.

**In practice:** Chengdu to Leshan high-speed train about an hour · site ticket around 80 yuan, boat separate for a few dozen yuan · for the evening flight leave a good margin: getting back to Chengdu and out to the airport takes a couple of hours

### Day 10 — Lijiang and Baisha
We entered the **old town of Dayan at six in the morning**, before any shop opens: wet alleys, flowing canals, the Naxi women sweeping in front of their houses. It's a completely different place from seven in the evening, when the same street is a corridor of souvenirs with loud music.

In the afternoon we went to **Baisha**, ten kilometers north. It's the original Naxi village, almost nobody goes, and what you look for in Dayan without finding it is still there. You get there by bike or taxi.

In the evening, the **cross-the-bridge noodles** and grilled goat cheese, which in Yunnan are normal and in the rest of China don't exist.

**In practice:** Lijiang old town maintenance fee, about fifty yuan, only sometimes asked at the entrances · Baisha is reached by bike or taxi in about twenty minutes · dinner in Dayan or Shuhe, the other old village nearby, which is quieter

### Day 11 — Yulong Xueshan and Impression Lijiang
A day at altitude, with everything booked in advance, because the daily quotas sell out.

In the morning **Impression Lijiang**, the open-air show directed by Zhang Yimou, with over five hundred performers (almost all inhabitants of villages in the area, not actors) in an amphitheater at 3,100 meters whose only backdrop is the Jade Dragon massif. You need heavy layers even in August: you sit still for over an hour, outdoors, in the wind.

In the afternoon the **glacier cable car to 4,506 meters**, but only if the sky is clear: with clouds you go up into the white and the ticket is spent anyway. On the walkways at altitude you take twenty steps and stop, and that's normal.

*If the sky is overcast, the sensible alternative is the Spruce Meadow cable car at 3,200 meters, which costs half and has much less of a line.*

**In practice:** tickets for the park, shuttle, cable car and show to be booked days ahead on the official channels, quotas sell out · at altitude oxygen canisters are sold, useful if you're short of breath · heavy layers, gloves and sunglasses even in summer

### Day 12 — Tiger Leaping Gorge, day one
We transferred to **Qiaotou** (about two hours from Lijiang) and set off on foot along the **high trail**, on the north side of the gorge, the one facing Haba Xueshan. Big bags stay in storage in Lijiang or are carried to the arrival guesthouse.

The hard part is the climb of the **28 bends**, a solid hour of switchbacks out of breath (you're already above 2,500 meters). Then the trail eases into a traverse and the gorge opens up: you hear the Yangtze before you see it, a constant roar rising from almost three thousand meters below.

Five or six hours of walking, and the night in a guesthouse on the trail. There's nothing to do but have dinner all at the same table and look at the mountain on the other side, and that is exactly the point.

**In practice:** bus or car Lijiang to Qiaotou about 2 hours, gorge entry ticket at the start · along the 28 bends locals offer horses for hire for the climb · guesthouses on the high trail to book in summer, cash useful for small expenses

### Day 13 — Second day of trek and Shangri-La
Three or four hours of walking to **Walnut Garden** and the descent to the valley floor. If you want, you can go down to the Tiger Leaping Stone, on the river, with a steep staircase to do there and back.

In the afternoon we transferred to **Shangri-La** (about 2 hours). You climb above 3,200 meters and the air changes: the landscape becomes Tibetan plateau, nights are cold in every season and you run out of breath just climbing a flight of stairs.

The last stretch of the high trail drops down to the gorge road, where there are guesthouses and small restaurants. The descent to the river from the Walnut Garden area crosses private paths: *residents charge a toll of a few yuan at several points, and that's normal.* The final climb back includes an almost vertical metal ladder, not advisable for anyone who suffers from vertigo. In the afternoon the road climbs north and leaves the Naxi part of Yunnan for Tibetan country: once in Shangri-La, take the first evening slowly, walking gently, drinking plenty and no alcohol. *Altitude sickness usually shows up as a headache and restless sleep: if it gets worse, go down.*

**In practice:** transfer Walnut Garden to Shangri-La by car or minibus, about 2 hours · in Shangri-La it's best to sleep in Dukezong old town · nights are cold all year, you need fleece and a jacket

### Day 14 — Shangri-La and return to Lijiang
The morning we spent at **Songzanlin Monastery**, the largest Tibetan Buddhist complex in Yunnan, and in the old town of **Dukezong**, with its great rotating prayer wheel.

First taste of highland cooking: yak butter tea (which throws you off at the first sip), dried yak meat, steamed momo.

We returned to **Lijiang** in a little over an hour by train, far more comfortable than the four hours by road of the past.

Songzanlin is a complex of temples on a hill, about twenty minutes from town, built in the seventeenth century and also called the little Potala: it takes a couple of hours, climbing the central stairway to the main hall. It's best in the morning, when the monks are in the halls and there are fewer people. Dukezong was largely destroyed by a fire in 2014 and rebuilt; the golden **prayer wheel** on Guishan hill is among the largest in the world and is turned clockwise, as a group. The train to Lijiang, open only a few years, has changed the times of this leg. *Above 3,000 meters the sun burns even in the cold: sunscreen and hat.*

**In practice:** Songzanlin with entry ticket and internal shuttle · Shangri-La to Lijiang train just over an hour, tickets on 12306 · in Lijiang sleep near the station or in the old town, depending on your flight time

### Day 15 — Flight to Guilin and transfer to Yangshuo
**Flight Lijiang-Guilin**, then about an hour by bus to **Yangshuo**.

The choice of lodging matters more than it seems: the town is convenient but noisy, especially around West Street, while the countryside guesthouses along the **Yulong River** cost less and are the real reason to come here.

The first evening, **beer fish**, pan-cooked with peppers and tomato, along the river.

The flight takes a couple of hours and it's best to take it in the morning, so you reach Yangshuo in the early afternoon. Direct buses to Yangshuo leave from Guilin airport; alternatively there's the high-speed train, but Yangshuo station is outside town and you need a taxi anyway. The afternoon is spent between the town and the Li River: **West Street** is the oldest pedestrian street, now full of bars and shops, worth seeing once and then leaving behind. *To sleep along the Yulong choose places with transfers or bikes included: the country roads aren't lit at night.*

**In practice:** Lijiang to Guilin flight about 2 hours · Guilin airport to Yangshuo bus about an hour and a half · guesthouses on the Yulong a few kilometers from town, low prices even in high season

### Day 16 — Xingping by raft and sunrise at Xianggong
**Wake up before five** and climb to the viewpoint on **Xianggong Hill**, steep and in the dark, for sunrise over the bend of the Li River with the fog lifting. It's the photograph we had in mind when we left.

In the morning we took the **bamboo raft on the Xingping-Yangdi stretch**: an hour of sailing at water level among the karst towers, including the spot reproduced on the 20-yuan note.

*On the choice between the raft and the long cruise from Guilin, it's best to be clear: the cruise lasts four hours, costs quite a lot and is done on a two-deck boat with two hundred other people. The raft covers only the best stretch, lasts an hour, costs a fraction and you sit half a meter from the water.*

**In practice:** the climb up Xianggong takes about twenty minutes on steps, with an entry ticket; you need a headlamp · Xingping-Yangdi rafts to book the day before through your guesthouse · Yangshuo to Xingping about an hour by bus or taxi

### Day 17 — Yangshuo by bicycle
It's the day with no plan, which on a trip to China is worth more than it seems: an **electric bike** and the road along the **Yulong River**, five hours stopping wherever you happen to be among rice paddies, water buffalo and stone bridges.

For those with energy left, Yangshuo is one of the world capitals of **limestone climbing**, with schools and guides in town.

In the evening, the **Impression Liu Sanjie** show, by the same director as Impression Lijiang, staged on the river with the karst towers lit up as scenery.

The road along the Yulong runs up the valley to **Dragon Bridge**, a Ming-dynasty stone arch bridge and the starting point for the pole-pushed bamboo rafts, slower than the Xingping ones. Along the way there are small country restaurants where you eat outdoors with a view of the peaks. If you want a view from above, climb **Moon Hill**, with its big natural arch, reached by a steep stairway of about half an hour. *E-bikes have limited range: check the battery before setting off and don't go too far.*

**In practice:** e-bike rental in town or at your guesthouse, a few dozen yuan a day · Impression Liu Sanjie every evening in season, tickets in several price bands · you need cash or Alipay for the small country restaurants

### Day 18 — Guilin and departure
Transfer to **Guilin** and departure. If your flight is in the afternoon, you can spend the morning at the **Longji rice terraces** only if you have a full day: they're two and a half hours to the north and can't be done in a rush.

Without time for Longji, the morning in Guilin is spent in the city, between **Elephant Trunk Hill**, the rock on the river that is the city's symbol, and the lakes in the center, which you walk around. **Reed Flute Cave**, a few kilometers away, is lit with colored lights and takes under an hour. If you do go to Longji, leave at dawn and climb to the villages of **Ping'an** or **Dazhai**, where the terraces are seen from paths between the wooden houses. *The best season for the terraces is between May, when they're flooded, and October, before the harvest.*

**In practice:** Yangshuo to Guilin airport about an hour and a half by car or bus · Longji about two and a half hours from Guilin, area entry ticket and shuttle up to the villages · for an international flight get to the airport three hours early

## What to see / what to do
- **Be at the Zhangjiajie entrance at opening.** It's the decision that changes the quality of the day the most.
- **The Golden Whip trail on the valley floor**, which almost nobody does.
- **Furong Zhen after six in the evening**, when the buses have left.
- **Hongya Dong from the opposite bank**, never from inside.
- **The panda base at 7:30**, never at 11.
- **Impression Lijiang**, for the backdrop more than the staging.
- **The glacier cable car only with a clear sky.** Otherwise Spruce Meadow and done.
- **Dayan at six in the morning** and **Baisha in the afternoon**: they're the two Lijiangs that almost nobody puts together.
- **The high trail of Tiger Leaping Gorge**, not the valley-floor viewpoint reachable by car.
- **The raft at Xingping**, not the long cruise from Guilin.
- **Sunrise at Xianggong**, if the weather allows.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
- **Chongqing:** Yuzhong peninsula, ideally with a river view: here the view is the city.
- **Furong Zhen:** inside the town, in a guesthouse overlooking the river or the waterfall. Simple standards, small rooms, no English.
- **Zhangjiajie:** two different bases with opposite logic. **Wulingyuan** for the days in the park, to be at the entrance at opening; **Zhangjiajie city** for Tianmen Shan, whose cable car leaves from the center. Doing everything from one base means losing hours.
- **Chengdu:** around Wenshu Monastery or Kuanzhai Xiangzi, both central and well served by the subway.
- **Lijiang:** courtyard guesthouse inside the old town, knowing that cars don't enter Dayan: you arrive at the edge and continue on foot, with your bags on the cobbles. If you prefer comfort, sleep in Shuhe.
- **Tiger Leaping Gorge:** guesthouses along the high trail. No heating: you need layers for the night.
- **Shangri-La:** Dukezong old town.
- **Yangshuo:** in the countryside along the Yulong River, or in Xingping, not on West Street.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
- **Chongqing:** original hot pot, xiaomian for breakfast sitting on the street, laziji, the fried chicken buried under a mountain of dried chilies, from which it has to be fished out.
- **Furong Zhen and Hunan:** Tujia cuisine, that is stewed river fish, pork smoked over the fire, spicy tofu. Hunan cooking burns without numbing: it's different from Sichuan's, and more direct.
- **Chengdu:** mapo tofu, kung pao chicken (which has nothing to do with the version served in Europe), dan dan noodles, rabbit. And hot pot with the divided broth, without playing the hero.
- **Lijiang and Yunnan:** cross-the-bridge noodles, the Bai grilled goat cheese, sautéed mushrooms in season, Naxi smoked pork. Inside the old town prices are tourist prices: two alleys out is enough to halve them.
- **Shangri-La:** yak butter tea, momo, thick soups. Highland cooking, very far from the rest of the trip.
- **Yangshuo:** beer fish along the river, stuffed river snails, Guilin rice noodles for breakfast with pickles and peanuts. West Street is the street of restaurants for foreigners: just step out of it to eat better and spend half.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book everything in advance, even what seems unlikely:** pandas, cable cars, shows. On this itinerary every main attraction has daily quotas and named entry tied to your passport.
- **At Zhangjiajie, arrive at opening.** It's the concrete difference between five minutes and an hour and a half of line for the Bailong Elevator.
- **Don't sleep in the city thinking you'll do the park in a day.** Wulingyuan exists for this.
- **Respect the altitude.** At least one night in Lijiang before going up to 4,506 meters, and no surprises at Shangri-La, where you sleep above 3,200.
- **Don't do Tiger Leaping Gorge from the valley-floor road.** Buses drop groups at the official viewpoint for twenty minutes: whoever does it that way thinks they've seen the gorge and hasn't.
- **Don't plan the trek between June and August**, when landslides close stretches of trail.
- **Bring cash for the trek:** along the trail the connection comes and goes and not all guesthouses have a working QR code.
- **Don't take tight connections on domestic flights.** Delays are common, and sometimes long.
- **Avoid Chongqing in July and August.** It's one of China's "furnace cities" and no daytime program holds up.
- **Factor in the cost of park admissions.** Added up, they exceed everything else in the trip: it's the most frequent budget surprise on this itinerary.

## Chiusura personale
If the first trip to China is for ticking off the things you already knew, this one is for realizing how little you knew. None of these stops appears in the brochures and in almost all of them you're the only foreigner in sight: in Zhangjiajie park, on the gorge trail, in the alleys of Baisha at dawn. What remains, in the end, isn't a monument but a sequence: the elevator climbing along the wall and the void that opens in the last hundred meters; the roar of the Yangtze rising from three thousand meters beneath your feet; five hundred farmers in costume performing with a glacier behind them; and an hour on a raft on the Li River in the morning, with the towers gliding by half a meter from the water and no hurry to arrive.
