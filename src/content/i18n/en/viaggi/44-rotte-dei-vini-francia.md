# The Wine Routes of France: Alsace and Champagne in 8 Days

**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 8 days, 7 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independent, by car and by bike in Alsace
**Categoria:** Europa · Francia · vino · Alsazia · Champagne

---

## Apertura personale
Among all the French wine routes, this itinerary combines the two best suited to a first time: Alsace, the most beautiful and the most accessible, and Champagne, the easiest to reach from Paris. Burgundy, harder and more expensive, is left for a later trip.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **May-June or September-October**
- **Alsace**: wineries often welcome visitors without an appointment, with free or almost free tastings.
- **Champagne**: an appointment is recommended for the big houses, free or paid depending on the house.
- **Designated driver, or spit when tasting**, always.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 — Arrival in Colmar
We started in Alsace, the most accessible of the wine routes. We spent the first day in the old town of **Colmar**, with the Unterlinden Museum and the Isenheim Altarpiece. In the evening, tarte flambée.

### Day 2 — Riquewihr and Eguisheim by bike
We rented bikes and rode between the villages of the **Route des Vins**: Riquewihr, Eguisheim and Kaysersberg. Here the wineries often welcome you without an appointment and tastings are free or almost free: you walk in and chat.

### Day 3 — Route des Vins to the south
We followed the wine road south, past Colmar, with a tasting at a producer who receives visitors without formality. The designated driver rule always applies, or you spit when tasting.

### Day 4 — Strasbourg
We spent a day in **Strasbourg**, between the cathedral, the Petite France quarter and Alsatian cooking, choucroute included.

### Day 5 — Transfer to Reims
We moved on to Champagne, and here everything changes: the big houses have to be booked in advance, and sometimes the visit is free, sometimes paid. We spent the afternoon in **Reims**, in the cathedral of the coronations, with Chagall's stained glass.

### Day 6 — A Champagne crayère
At one house we went down into the **crayères**, the chalk galleries dug by the Romans, and the tasting came together with an explanation of riddling. In Alsace wine is told through conversation; here you watch a technical process. For a snack, jambon de Reims and pink biscuits.

### Day 7 — Épernay and the Avenue de Champagne
We went to **Épernay**, where the galleries run under the street itself. Here you can add another house of your choice.

### Day 8 — Return
From Reims to Paris it's less than an hour by train. Burgundy is left for the next trip: it's harder and more expensive, with a welcome opposite to the Alsatian one.

## What to see / what to do
- The Route des Vins d'Alsace by bike.
- A Champagne crayère dug by the Romans.
- The cathedral of Reims.

## Possible extensions
- **Burgundy (+3-4 days):** with appointments made weeks ahead.
- **Paris (+2-3 days):** a natural connection from Reims.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
Colmar or a village on the Route des Vins for Alsace; Reims or Épernay for Champagne.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
Tarte flambée and choucroute in Alsace, jambon de Reims and pink biscuits in Champagne, Alsatian whites and Champagne itself.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't drive after tastings.**
- **Don't treat Burgundy like Alsace**: the welcome works the opposite way.
- **Book the big houses** in Champagne in advance.

## Chiusura personale
The difference between Alsace and Champagne is all in the welcome: in Alsace you walk in and chat, in Champagne you go down into a Roman gallery and watch a technical process. They are two opposite ways of telling the story of French wine, and put in sequence they complete each other.
