# Northern Thailand in Two Weeks: Chiang Mai, Pai, Chiang Rai and the Golden Triangle

**Durata:** 13 days, 12 nights, plus 3-4 optional days for the Mae Hong Son loop or Sukhothai
**Compagni di viaggio:** independently, with a night train, buses, minivans, a car with driver and one domestic flight

## Apertura personale
The classic Thailand trip is the beach one, and it's already on the site: [from Bangkok to the southern sea](/viaggi/thailandia). This is the other one, the north. Mountains, wooden temples, evening markets and a slower pace. I went to Chiang Mai on a separate trip, and you feel the contrast with Bangkok and the south from the first day. The morning with the elephants at Elephant Nature Park, without ever riding them, is still one of my best memories of Thailand.

For Pai, Chiang Rai and the Golden Triangle you'll find mostly practical information here, checked against official sources.

The route climbs from Bangkok on the night train, stops for almost a week in Chiang Mai, goes through Pai and ends in Chiang Rai and at the border on the Mekong. There's also a long section on elephants: how to see them without doing harm, and how to recognize a serious place.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** November to February, in the cool, dry season. At certain times of year the north, Chiang Mai included, is covered by seasonal smoke from burning crops and forest: according to the UK government, pollution sometimes reaches dangerous levels and can worsen asthma and breathing problems. Before you leave, check the air quality index on aqicn.org.
- **Documents:** valid passport. Visa-exemption rules changed on 15 September 2026 (for British nationals, the visa-free tourist stay is now 30 days): Italian and other nationals should check their own government's advice before booking. Since 1 May 2025 all foreigners must fill in the **Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC)** online, within the 3 days before arrival.
- **Currency:** Thai baht (THB). Evening markets, songthaews and mountain villages need cash.
- **Transport:** night train Bangkok-Chiang Mai, minivan to Pai, bus Chiang Mai-Chiang Rai, car with driver or tour for the Golden Triangle, flight Chiang Rai-Bangkok to get back.
- **Roads and motorbikes:** according to the UK government, Thailand has a very high number of road accidents, especially on motorbikes. Helmets are compulsory by law. You need an international driving permit together with your national licence, and without a valid licence for that bike your travel insurance may not cover you. Many rental scooters in tourist areas aren't even registered. The mountain roads to Pai and Mae Hong Son are narrow and full of bends: if you aren't already a good rider, take the minivan.
- **Borders:** the Golden Triangle sits on the border with Laos and Myanmar. The situation in Myanmar is unpredictable and can change without notice; not all crossings into Laos are open to foreigners. On this trip you look at the border from the Thai bank, you don't cross it.
- **Temples:** shoulders and knees covered, shoes off in the halls. A sarong in your backpack solves almost everything.
- **Elephants:** no rides, no shows, no bathing with tourists. The dedicated section is further down.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Bangkok
You land in Bangkok, and the first day is for getting over the flight. A walk along the river, dinner in Chinatown, early to bed. Bangkok has its [full guide page](/destinazioni/thailandia/bangkok): here we keep only the essentials, because this trip is about the north.

### Day 2 — Bangkok's temples and the night train to Chiang Mai
Early morning at the **Grand Palace** with Wat Phra Kaew, then **Wat Pho** with the great reclining Buddha and the ferry to **Wat Arun**. All three require covered shoulders and knees. Grand Palace tickets and hours are on the official website. In the evening you go to **Krung Thep Aphiwat**, the new central station where long-distance trains now leave from, no longer old Hua Lamphong. Night trains to Chiang Mai take between 12 and 14 hours. Berths are booked on the official railway website, dticket.railway.co.th, and around Thai holidays they can sell out weeks ahead. *If you're short on time, a domestic flight to Chiang Mai saves half a day, but you lose the night on the train.*

### Day 3 — Chiang Mai: arrival and the old city
You arrive in the morning. The **old city** sits inside a square moat and is easy on foot or by bicycle. Three temples not to miss: **Wat Phra Singh**, **Wat Chedi Luang** with its great ruined stupa, and **Wat Sri Suphan**, the silver temple, in the silversmiths' quarter. Women may not enter the ordination hall of Wat Sri Suphan. The afternoon is slow: a coffee, a massage, dinner at a market.

### Day 4 — Doi Suthep, Wat Pha Lat and the evening market
**Wat Phra That Doi Suthep** is the temple on the mountain above the city, with its golden stupa and a staircase of more than three hundred steps lined with nagas. There's also a funicular. Go early in the morning, before the groups. If your legs are up to it, walk up the **Monk's Trail**, which passes **Wat Pha Lat**, a stone and wood temple hidden in the forest, with a stream running down the rocks. Since 1 October 2025 Doi Suthep-Pui National Park charges an entrance fee on some trails too: check the amount locally. In the evening, if it's Saturday, the **Wualai** walking market; if it's Sunday, the **Sunday Walking Street** on Ratchadamnoen Road. On other days there's the Night Bazaar, more touristy.

### Day 5 — Cooking class or Doi Inthanon
Two options. The first is a **Thai cooking class**, which in Chiang Mai usually starts with shopping at the market and ends at the table with what you've cooked. It's the right place to learn **khao soi**, the northern coconut-curry noodle soup. The second is **Doi Inthanon**, Thailand's highest mountain, as a day trip with a driver or a tour: waterfalls and highland forest. At the top, 2,565 metres up, it's cool even when the city is T-shirt weather.

### Day 6 — A day with elephants, the right way
The elephant day, at a place chosen with the criteria in the dedicated section below. Book a few days ahead; almost every center picks you up from your hotel in Chiang Mai. Read the program carefully before paying: if it includes riding them, washing them or bathing with them, it's not the right place. You come back to town tired in the evening, and dinner near the hotel is enough.

### Day 7 — From Chiang Mai to Pai
By minivan to **Pai**, on a narrow mountain road that's all bends. If you get carsick, take something before leaving and sit up front. Pai is a village in a green valley that over the years has become a backpacker favorite: guesthouses, cafés, an evening market on the main street. The afternoon is for settling in and a walk around.

### Day 8 — Pai: canyon, countryside and hot springs
A day around the village. **Pai Canyon** at sunset, with trails along ridges of red earth: they have no railings and they're narrow, so no stunts for photos. The valley has waterfalls, rice fields and hot springs. Many people rent a scooter here. If you do, you need the right licence, a fastened helmet and insurance that actually covers you. If you're not confident, take a songthaew or hire a driver for the day.

### Day 9 — Back to Chiang Mai and Nimman
Minivan back to Chiang Mai, on the same winding road. The afternoon in **Nimman**, the modern neighborhood of specialty coffee shops and restaurants, or one last temple in the old city. It's the breather before the last part of the trip.

### Day 10 — From Chiang Mai to Chiang Rai and the White Temple
Bus from Chiang Mai's **Arcade** station to **Chiang Rai**: about three and a half hours on official timetables. There's no train. The afternoon is for **Wat Rong Khun**, the **White Temple**, just outside town. Thai artist Chalermchai Kositpipat designed it and opened it to visitors in 1997: it's white, covered in mirrors, with a bridge that crosses a sea of outstretched hands. In 2026 the ticket for foreigners costs 200 baht; Thai nationals enter free. Go toward the end of the day, when the tour buses have left.

### Day 11 — Chiang Rai: the Blue Temple and Baan Dam
In the morning **Wat Rong Suea Ten**, the **Blue Temple**, all blue and gold inside and out, with a large white Buddha in the hall. It's close to the center. Then **Baan Dam**, the **Black House**, north of the city: a complex of dark buildings designed by the artist Thawan Duchanee, filled with his work. It's a private art museum, not a temple, and it's the strangest place on the trip. The afternoon downtown, at the clock tower, and the evening at the night market.

### Day 12 — The Golden Triangle: Sop Ruak, the Hall of Opium and Chiang Saen
A day with a driver or a tour to the **Golden Triangle**, northeast of Chiang Rai. At **Sop Ruak** the Ruak river flows into the Mekong, and from the bank you can see Laos and Myanmar. The viewpoint is touristy, but the place matters. Not far away is the **Hall of Opium**, a museum owned by a Thai royal family foundation, on how opium was grown and produced in this region: it's the most serious part of the day and worth more than the viewpoint. Check opening days and ticket price beforehand. On the way back, **Chiang Saen**, a small town on the Mekong with walls and the ruins of ancient temples. You look at the border, you don't cross it.

### Day 13 — Flight from Chiang Rai to Bangkok and home
Flight from Chiang Rai airport to Bangkok and connection to your intercontinental flight, with a few hours of margin between the two. If you're continuing to the sea, fly south instead of home: it's explained further down.

## Elephants: how to see them without doing harm
This is the part of the trip that needs the most preparation, because in Chiang Mai there are a huge number of offers and many look alike. The word "sanctuary" guarantees nothing: places that make the animals work use it too.

**Why to avoid rides, shows and bathing.** For an elephant to accept a tourist on its back, a bath with strangers or a circus act, the animal has to be trained. According to World Animal Protection, close contact suggests the elephants have been cruelly trained. In December 2019 ABTA, the UK's largest association of travel agents and tour operators, also listed rides, shows, bathing and any tourist contact without a barrier as unacceptable in its guidelines. There's a safety issue too: the UK government notes that tourists have been seriously injured, and some killed, when handlers lost control of elephants.

**Why "no riding" isn't enough.** Many centers have taken the saddles off and moved to washing, river bathing, photos and hand-feeding. World Animal Protection writes that these experiences too can hide the same exploitation: punishment-based training and animals regularly restrained. Removing the saddle is a step, not the solution.

**How to recognize a serious place.** The questions are the ones World Animal Protection suggests asking yourself, plus a few common-sense ones.
- **Do you watch or touch?** At the best places you observe, from a platform or at a distance. No bathing, no washing, no hugging selfies.
- **Are the elephants being elephants?** They walk, graze, forage, stay in groups, roll in the mud. They aren't chained, and they aren't standing around waiting for tourists.
- **Are there babies?** A baby you can touch, especially without its mother, is a bad sign. Genuine orphanages that rescue babies from the wild are the exception.
- **Hooks.** The hooked stick shouldn't be in sight, except in real emergencies.
- **How many of them, and how many of us.** World Animal Protection doesn't give a right number of elephants or visitors, and we won't invent one. But it's worth asking how many animals live at the center, how much land they have, how many people come in each day and where each elephant comes from. A serious place answers without fuss, and tells the story of each animal on its website.
- **The program.** If the day is packed with activities with the elephants, the elephants are working. If it's mostly walking, watching and listening, that's a good sign.

**Elephant Nature Park and Save Elephant Foundation.** It's the best-known center in the area, and it's the one I went to. It was founded by Saengduean "Lek" Chailert who, according to Save Elephant Foundation, began rescuing injured, neglected and elderly elephants in the 1990s and opened the sanctuary in the Mae Taeng valley, near Chiang Mai, in 2003. The foundation writes that elephants there are not ridden and don't have to work or perform tricks. The park states that its elephants come from street begging, riding and circuses. On its website, the elephants' bath is something you watch, and it isn't guaranteed, because the animals follow their own rhythms. In November 2026 the park lists half-day visits at 2,500 baht, a full day, a night in the park and a weekly volunteer program.

There's one thing you should know, though. World Animal Protection keeps a public list of centers that follow its guidelines, updated in December 2025. Elephant Nature Park is marked as a high-welfare venue that still offers hand-feeding over a barrier, and it has been temporarily removed from the "elephant-friendly" list due to a legal investigation. We don't know more and won't add more: check the updated list before booking.

**World Animal Protection's list.** For Chiang Mai, in December 2025, the venues following the guidelines included ChangChill (View Doi and Hillside), Burm and Emily's Elephant Sanctuary, Kindred Spirit Elephant Sanctuary and the Mahouts Elephant Foundation's LIFE Project. It isn't our ranking: it's their list, and it changes. Reread it on the [World Animal Protection website](https://www.worldanimalprotection.org/elephant-friendly-tourist-guide) before choosing.

## What to see / what to do
- **Bangkok:** Grand Palace, Wat Pho and Wat Arun in the same morning. The rest is on the [Bangkok page](/destinazioni/thailandia/bangkok).
- **Chiang Mai:** the old city temples, Doi Suthep via the Monk's Trail, Wat Pha Lat, the Saturday and Sunday walking markets, a cooking class, Doi Inthanon. All on the [Chiang Mai page](/destinazioni/thailandia/chiang-mai).
- **Pai:** the canyon at sunset, the hot springs, the evening market. On the [Pai page](/destinazioni/thailandia/pai-mae-hong-son).
- **Chiang Rai:** the White Temple in the late afternoon, the Blue Temple in the morning, Baan Dam. On the [Chiang Rai page](/destinazioni/thailandia/chiang-rai).
- **Golden Triangle:** the Hall of Opium before the viewpoint, then Chiang Saen. On the [Golden Triangle page](/destinazioni/thailandia/triangolo-d-oro).
- **Elephants:** one center, chosen well, for a full day instead of two half days at different places.

## Variations
**The Mae Hong Son loop (3-4 extra days).** From Pai you carry on west to **Mae Hong Son**, near the Myanmar border, and return to Chiang Mai from the south, via Mae Sariang, closing a mountain loop. Many do it by motorbike, and that's exactly where the risks are: constant bends, steep climbs and descents, trucks. If you aren't already a good rider, with a valid licence and insurance that covers you, do it by car with a driver. For nights in Mae Hong Son and along the road we have no checked addresses to recommend.

**Sukhothai (2 extra days).** Sukhothai was the capital of the Sukhothai Kingdom in the 13th and 14th centuries, and its historical park has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1991. It fits between Bangkok and Chiang Mai, breaking up the journey north. Connections between Bangkok, Sukhothai and Chiang Mai need checking at the time, because we haven't verified them.

## How to combine it with the sea
The north and the sea work well in the same trip, and the best season coincides: from November to February the north is cool and dry, and the Andaman Sea is in its dry season too. There are two ways to do it.
- **North first, then the sea.** Follow this route up to Day 12, then on Day 13 fly south instead of home, and carry on with the [trip to the southern sea](/viaggi/thailandia): Khao Sok, Krabi, Koh Phi Phi. From north to south you often go through Bangkok: direct routes change, so check them.
- **A short version of both.** With three weeks in total, you can keep Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai and the Golden Triangle (skipping Pai) and give about ten days to the sea.

The Gulf of Thailand, with Koh Samui, Koh Phangan and Koh Tao, has different seasons from the Andaman side: it's explained on the [Gulf page](/destinazioni/thailandia/golfo-thailandia).

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Choose the elephant center before you leave**, calmly, and not from a flyer at the hotel.
- **Book the night train early**, because berths can sell out weeks ahead around Thai holidays. Only on the official site or through well-known resellers.
- **Don't rent a scooter for the first time on the road to Pai.** Helmet, international driving permit, insurance that covers you: without them, take the minivan.
- **Check the air** before and during the trip.
- **Dress for the temples**: shoulders and knees covered, always.
- **Go to the White Temple late**, once the tours have left.
- **At the Golden Triangle, the museum before the viewpoint.** And you look at the border from the Thai bank.
- **Bring a sweater.** On Doi Inthanon and in Pai, early in the morning, it gets properly cold.

## Chiusura personale
What stayed with me from Chiang Mai is the pace, slower than the rest of the country, and the morning with the elephants without ever riding them. If you're going now, you have one more job: choose the center by looking at what happens to the animals once the tourists leave.
