# Northern Thailand in Two Weeks: Chiang Mai, Pai, Chiang Rai and the Golden Triangle

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (places Rick visited, put together into one practical route; practical information checked in October-November 2026, to recheck before departure; first-person memories only those given by Rick, none invented)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 13 days, 12 nights, plus 3-4 optional days for the Mae Hong Son loop or Sukhothai
**Compagni di viaggio:** independently, with a night train, buses, minivans, a car with driver and one domestic flight
**Categoria:** Asia · Thailandia · nord · montagna · templi · cultura · cibo · elefanti etici

## Apertura personale
The classic Thailand trip is the beach one, and it's already on the site: [from Bangkok to the southern sea](/viaggi/thailandia). This is the other one, the north. Mountains, wooden temples, evening markets and a slower pace. I went to Chiang Mai on a separate trip, and you feel the contrast with Bangkok and the south from the first day. The morning with the elephants at Elephant Nature Park, without ever riding them, is still one of my best memories of Thailand.

The route climbs from Bangkok on the night train, stops for almost a week in Chiang Mai, goes through Pai and ends in Chiang Rai and at the border on the Mekong. There's also a long section on elephants: how to see them without doing harm, and how to recognize a serious place.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** November to February, in the cool, dry season. At certain times of year the north, Chiang Mai included, is covered by seasonal smoke from burning crops and forest: according to the UK government, pollution sometimes reaches dangerous levels and can worsen asthma and breathing problems. Before you leave, check the air quality index on aqicn.org.
- **Documents:** valid passport. Visa-exemption rules changed on 15 September 2026 (for British nationals, the visa-free tourist stay is now 30 days): Italian and other nationals should check their own government's advice before booking. Since 1 May 2025 all foreigners must fill in the **Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC)** online, within the 3 days before arrival.
- **Currency:** Thai baht (THB). Evening markets, songthaews and mountain villages need cash.
- **Transport:** night train Bangkok-Chiang Mai, minivan to Pai, bus Chiang Mai-Chiang Rai, car with driver or tour for the Golden Triangle, flight Chiang Rai-Bangkok to get back.
- **Roads and motorbikes:** according to the UK government, Thailand has a very high number of road accidents, especially on motorbikes. Helmets are compulsory by law. You need an international driving permit together with your national licence, and without a valid licence for that bike your travel insurance may not cover you. Many rental scooters in tourist areas aren't even registered. The mountain roads to Pai and Mae Hong Son are narrow and full of bends: if you aren't already a good rider, take the minivan.
- **Borders:** the Golden Triangle sits on the border with Laos and Myanmar. The situation in Myanmar is unpredictable and can change without notice; not all crossings into Laos are open to foreigners. On this trip you look at the border from the Thai bank, you don't cross it.
- **Temples:** shoulders and knees covered, shoes off in the halls. A sarong in your backpack solves almost everything.
- **Elephants:** no rides, no shows, no bathing with tourists. The dedicated section is further down.
- **From the airport to the centre:** from **Bangkok Suvarnabhumi (BKK)** the Airport Rail Link reaches Phaya Thai in about 26 minutes (up to 45 THB, token from the machines on the basement level, from about 6am to midnight) · public taxis from the official counter on level 1, around the clock: meter plus a 50 THB airport surcharge and tolls, about 300-450 THB to the centre · Grab about 380-550 THB, pickup on level 4 · whoever offers a fixed fare without the meter in arrivals usually asks double · at **Chiang Rai (CEI)**, for the departure, the centre is about a quarter of an hour away by Grab or taxi.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Bangkok
You land in Bangkok, and the first day is for getting over the flight. From **Suvarnabhumi** airport the simplest way into the city is the Airport Rail Link to Phaya Thai, or a taxi from the official desk on the arrivals floor, with the meter running. Pick a hotel near the river or a metro stop: tomorrow you start early for the temples, and in the evening you leave for the north with your backpack.

The afternoon is light: a walk along the **Chao Phraya** river, perhaps on one of the public boats that shuttle between the piers, dinner in **Chinatown** along Yaowarat Road, where the street food stalls light up at sunset, and early to bed. *The humid heat of the first day is more tiring than you expect: drink a lot and don't overfill the plan.* Bangkok has its [full guide page](/destinazioni/thailandia/bangkok): here we keep only the essentials, because this trip is about the north.

**In practice:** Airport Rail Link from Suvarnabhumi to the centre in under half an hour · taxis only from the official desk and on the meter · Yaowarat is at its best after sunset · fill in the TDAC in the 3 days before arrival.

### Day 2 — Bangkok's temples and the night train to Chiang Mai
Early morning at the **Grand Palace** with Wat Phra Kaew, the temple of the Emerald Buddha: go in at opening time, before the groups and before the worst of the heat. Then, ten minutes away on foot, **Wat Pho** with the great reclining Buddha, 46 metres long and covered in gold, and from there the ferry across the river to **Wat Arun**, the Temple of Dawn, with its porcelain-decorated tower. All three require covered shoulders and knees. Grand Palace tickets and hours are on the official website.

In the evening you go to **Krung Thep Aphiwat**, the new central station where long-distance trains now leave from, no longer old Hua Lamphong. Night trains to Chiang Mai take between 12 and 14 hours. Berths are booked on the official railway website, dticket.railway.co.th, and around Thai holidays they can sell out weeks ahead. Second-class air-conditioned sleepers are the most common compromise: the staff make up the beds after departure. *If you're short on time, a domestic flight to Chiang Mai saves half a day, but you lose the night on the train.*

**In practice:** Grand Palace about 500 baht, open from 8.30 with last entry in the early afternoon, sometimes closed for royal ceremonies · Wat Pho about 300 baht and Wat Arun about 200, separate tickets, cash only · river ferry a few baht · bring dinner and water for the train.

### Day 3 — Chiang Mai: arrival and the old city
You arrive in the morning. From Chiang Mai station to the **old city** you take a red songthaew, the pick-up truck with benches that works as a shared taxi, or Grab. The old city sits inside a square moat and is easy on foot or by bicycle. Drop your bags at the hotel, have breakfast and start the temples in the cooler hours.

Three temples not to miss: **Wat Phra Singh**, the most revered in the city, **Wat Chedi Luang** with its great ruined stupa, partly brought down by an earthquake in the sixteenth century, and **Wat Sri Suphan**, the silver temple, in the silversmiths' quarter just south of the walls. Women may not enter the ordination hall of Wat Sri Suphan. The afternoon is slow: a coffee, a massage, dinner at a market. *After a night on the train, don't overdo it: Chiang Mai still has several days ahead.*

**In practice:** red songthaews, agree the fare before getting in, or Grab · many old city temples by free donation, Wat Chedi Luang about 40-50 baht for foreigners · shoulders and knees covered · to sleep, the old city and the area around Tha Phae Gate are handy for getting around on foot.

### Day 4 — Doi Suthep, Wat Pha Lat and the evening market
**Wat Phra That Doi Suthep** is the temple on the mountain above the city, with its golden stupa and a staircase of more than three hundred steps lined with nagas. There's also a funicular. Go early in the morning, before the groups. If your legs are up to it, walk up the **Monk's Trail**, which passes **Wat Pha Lat**, a stone and wood temple hidden in the forest, with a stream running down the rocks. Since 1 October 2025 Doi Suthep-Pui National Park charges an entrance fee on some trails too: check the amount locally.

The trail starts at the end of Suthep Road, near the university, and is marked by orange strips tied to the trees; the stretch from Wat Pha Lat to Doi Suthep is steeper. *Monks live and meditate along the trail and at Pha Lat: walk quietly and dress for a temple.* To get down there are songthaews leaving from the car park. In the evening, if it's Saturday, the **Wualai** walking market; if it's Sunday, the **Sunday Walking Street** on Ratchadamnoen Road. On other days there's the Night Bazaar, more touristy.

**In practice:** Doi Suthep about half an hour by road from the old city, shared songthaew from the end of Suthep Road · temple ticket about 30 baht for foreigners, funicular about 20-50 baht · shoes with good grip, the Monk's Trail is slippery after rain · the walking markets open in the late afternoon and fill up after sunset.

### Day 5 — Cooking class or Doi Inthanon
Two options. The first is a **Thai cooking class**, which in Chiang Mai usually starts with shopping at the market and ends at the table with what you've cooked. It's the right place to learn **khao soi**, the northern coconut curry noodle soup. Many classes are held on farms outside the city, with a garden where you pick the herbs, and almost all pick you up at the hotel; there's a morning and an afternoon version, so the rest of the day stays free.

The second is **Doi Inthanon**, Thailand's highest mountain, as a day trip with a driver or a tour: waterfalls and highland forest. At the top, at 2,565 metres, it's cool even when the city is T-shirt weather. The classic stops are **Wachirathan** waterfall, the two royal pagodas built for the king and queen, the short trail through the mossy forest near the summit and, if there's time, a hill-village market along the road. *Leave early: from Chiang Mai to the summit is a couple of hours by car, and the clouds rise during the morning.*

**In practice:** cooking class to book the day before, half a day · Doi Inthanon about 300 baht park entrance for foreigners, royal pagodas on a separate ticket · car with driver for the day, agree the price beforehand · a sweater in your backpack.

### Day 6 — A day with elephants, the right way
The elephant day, at a place chosen using the criteria in the dedicated section below. Book days in advance; almost every centre picks you up at your hotel in Chiang Mai. Before paying, read the programme carefully: if it includes riding, washing or bathing with them, it's not the right place. The serious centres are almost all outside the city, in the valleys towards Mae Taeng or Mae Wang, and the transfer out usually takes over an hour.

A good day is mostly walking behind the animals at a distance, watching them eat, graze or roll in the mud, and listening to the staff tell you where each elephant comes from. *Clothes you don't mind getting dirty, closed shoes, sunscreen and repellent: you're outdoors most of the time.* If something in the programme doesn't match what the website says, it's worth asking about it on the spot. In the evening you come back to town tired, and dinner near the hotel is all you need.

**In practice:** book directly on the centre's website, not from leaflets at the hotel · back in Chiang Mai in the late afternoon · prices from a few thousand baht for the day, lunch often included · check the up-to-date World Animal Protection list before choosing.

### Day 7 — From Chiang Mai to Pai
By minivan to **Pai**, on a narrow mountain road that's all bends. If you get car sick, take something before leaving and sit at the front. Minivans leave several times a day from Arcade station or pick you up at your hotel, and usually make a single stop halfway. *The trip takes around three and a half to four hours: leave in the morning so you arrive in daylight and find your room calmly.*

Pai is a town in a green valley that over the years has become a backpacker destination: guesthouses, cafés, an evening market on the main street. The afternoon is for settling in and a walk: the bamboo bridge over the Pai river, the lanes of the centre and, towards sunset, **Wat Phra That Mae Yen**, the temple with the great white Buddha on the hill east of town, with a view over the whole valley. In the evening the Walking Street market, with food and craft stalls, is the natural place for dinner.

**In practice:** Chiang Mai-Pai minivan to book a day ahead, a few hundred baht · about 130 km of bends · the evening market opens around sunset · to sleep, guesthouses in the centre or bungalows just outside, quieter but you need transport.

### Day 8 — Pai: canyon, countryside and hot springs
A day around town. **Pai Canyon** at sunset, with paths along ridges of red earth: they have no railings and are narrow, so no acrobatics for photos. The valley has waterfalls, rice fields and hot springs. Many people rent a scooter here. If you do, you need the right licence, a fastened helmet and insurance that actually covers you. If you're not confident, take a songthaew or a driver for the day.

The easiest order is to leave the canyon for last. In the morning the **hot springs**, nicer in the cool of the early hours, then **Mo Paeng** waterfall, the rice fields and the Second World War memorial bridge on the road to Chiang Mai. In the afternoon, if you don't mind bends, you can drive up to the **Yun Lai** viewpoint, famous at dawn for the sea of clouds over the valley. *Get to the canyon at least an hour before sunset, and bring a torch for the walk back to the car park.*

**In practice:** canyon a few kilometres from town, free access · hot springs with a paid entrance, about 300 baht for foreigners at Tha Pai, elsewhere check prices locally · scooter only with an international licence valid for motorbikes · alternatively, a driver for the day arranged through your guesthouse.

### Day 9 — Back to Chiang Mai and Nimman
Minivan back to Chiang Mai, on the same winding road. If you can, choose a morning departure: you arrive in time for lunch and keep the whole afternoon. The afternoon in **Nimman**, the modern neighbourhood of specialty coffee shops and restaurants, or one last temple in the old city. It's the breathing day before the last part of the trip.

Nimmanhaemin Road and its side streets, called «soi» and numbered, are where many young Chiang Mai residents live and where you find local roasters using coffee grown in the surrounding mountains. If you'd rather stay in the old city, go back to a temple you rushed or book a Thai massage. *It's worth buying the bus ticket to Chiang Rai today: around holidays the morning departures fill up.*

**In practice:** minivan from Pai about three and a half hours · Nimman a few minutes by Grab from the old city · Chiang Rai tickets at Arcade station or on the company's website · last evening in Chiang Mai: a good chance for a market you haven't seen yet.

### Day 10 — From Chiang Mai to Chiang Rai and the White Temple
Bus from Chiang Mai's **Arcade** station to **Chiang Rai**: about three and a half hours according to the official timetable. There's no train. The bus crosses the hills to the north-east and arrives at the terminal in central Chiang Rai, a short walk from the clock tower. Drop your bags at the hotel and eat something before heading out again.

The afternoon is for **Wat Rong Khun**, the **White Temple**, just outside town. It was conceived by Thai artist Chalermchai Kositpipat, who opened it to visitors in 1997: it's white, covered in mirrors, with a bridge that crosses a sea of outstretched hands. In 2026 the ticket for foreigners is 200 baht; Thais enter free. It's best to go towards the end of the day, when the tour coaches have left. *Photography isn't allowed inside the main hall, and the murals mix Buddhist imagery with characters from films and comics: take your time with them.* Next to it is the golden toilet building, the most photographed structure after the temple.

**In practice:** Chiang Mai-Chiang Rai bus several departures a day, book your seat · White Temple about 13 km south of the centre, Grab or songthaew · closes in the late afternoon, check the time · temple dress.

### Day 11 — Chiang Rai: the Blue Temple and Baan Dam
In the morning **Wat Rong Suea Ten**, the **Blue Temple**, all blue and gold inside and out, with a large white Buddha in the hall. It's close to the centre. The temple is recent and was decorated by a pupil of Chalermchai, the White Temple's artist: the first hour has the best light for photos and almost nobody around.

Then **Baan Dam**, the **Black House**, north of the city: a complex of dark buildings designed by artist Thawan Duchanee, with his works. It's a private art museum, not a temple, and the strangest place on the trip. Animal skins, horns and bones are part of the installations: some people find it disturbing, so better to know beforehand. *Baan Dam closes for lunch: arrive in the morning or after one o'clock.* The afternoon in the centre, at the clock tower, and in the evening the night market.

**In practice:** Blue Temple free entry · Baan Dam about 80 baht, open 9 to 17 with a midday break · about twenty minutes by Grab between the two · in the evening the clock tower lights up with a light show.

### Day 12 — The Golden Triangle: Sop Ruak, the Hall of Opium and Chiang Saen
A day with a driver or a tour towards the **Golden Triangle**, north-east of Chiang Rai. At **Sop Ruak** the Ruak river flows into the Mekong, and from the bank you can see Laos and Myanmar. The viewpoint is touristy, but the place matters. Nearby is the **Hall of Opium**, the museum of a Thai royal family foundation about the cultivation and production of opium in this region: it's the most serious part of the day and worth more than the viewpoint. Check opening days and tickets beforehand.

On the way back, **Chiang Saen**, a small Mekong town with walls and the ruins of ancient temples. The border is for looking at, not crossing. The best order is to leave early, do the museum first, which takes a couple of hours, then the viewpoint and lunch by the river, and finish at Chiang Saen in the afternoon, when the light on the temple bricks is warmer. *Along the river you'll be offered boat trips and visits to the Laotian market island: with the borders in their current state, it's better to stay on the Thai bank.*

**In practice:** about an hour and a half by car from Chiang Rai to the Golden Triangle · Hall of Opium closed on Mondays, ticket about 200 baht for foreigners · driver for the day to book through the hotel · carry your passport, there are checkpoints on the border roads.

### Day 13 — Flight from Chiang Rai to Bangkok and home
Flight from Chiang Rai airport to Bangkok and connection with the long-haul flight, with a few hours' margin between the two. Chiang Rai airport is a few kilometres from the centre, a quarter of an hour by Grab or taxi; the flight to Bangkok takes just over an hour. *Check on your ticket whether you land at Suvarnabhumi (BKK) or Don Mueang (DMK): they are two different airports, far apart, and transferring between them takes at least an hour.*

If the flight is in the afternoon, the morning is enough for a last coffee in the centre or a walk to the clock tower. If you're carrying on to the sea, fly south instead of home: it's explained below. Domestic flights often have a lower baggage allowance than long-haul ones, so weigh your bag, especially after two weeks of markets.

**In practice:** Chiang Rai airport about a quarter of an hour from the centre · check in for domestic flights at least an hour before · leave at least three or four hours between the domestic and long-haul flights · check the baggage included in your fare.

## Elephants: how to see them without doing harm
This is the part of the trip that needs the most preparation, because in Chiang Mai there are a huge number of offers and many look alike. The word "sanctuary" guarantees nothing: places that make the animals work use it too.

**Why to avoid rides, shows and bathing.** For an elephant to accept a tourist on its back, a bath with strangers or a circus act, the animal has to be trained. According to World Animal Protection, close contact suggests the elephants have been cruelly trained. In December 2019 ABTA, the UK's largest association of travel agents and tour operators, also listed rides, shows, bathing and any tourist contact without a barrier as unacceptable in its guidelines. There's a safety issue too: the UK government notes that tourists have been seriously injured, and some killed, when handlers lost control of elephants.

**Why "no riding" isn't enough.** Many centers have taken the saddles off and moved to washing, river bathing, photos and hand-feeding. World Animal Protection writes that these experiences too can hide the same exploitation: punishment-based training and animals regularly restrained. Removing the saddle is a step, not the solution.

**How to recognize a serious place.** The questions are the ones World Animal Protection suggests asking yourself, plus a few common-sense ones.
- **Do you watch or touch?** At the best places you observe, from a platform or at a distance. No bathing, no washing, no hugging selfies.
- **Are the elephants being elephants?** They walk, graze, forage, stay in groups, roll in the mud. They aren't chained, and they aren't standing around waiting for tourists.
- **Are there babies?** A baby you can touch, especially without its mother, is a bad sign. Genuine orphanages that rescue babies from the wild are the exception.
- **Hooks.** The hooked stick shouldn't be in sight, except in real emergencies.
- **How many of them, and how many of us.** World Animal Protection doesn't give a right number of elephants or visitors, and we won't invent one. But it's worth asking how many animals live at the center, how much land they have, how many people come in each day and where each elephant comes from. A serious place answers without fuss, and tells the story of each animal on its website.
- **The program.** If the day is packed with activities with the elephants, the elephants are working. If it's mostly walking, watching and listening, that's a good sign.

**Elephant Nature Park and Save Elephant Foundation.** It's the best-known center in the area, and it's the one I went to. It was founded by Saengduean "Lek" Chailert who, according to Save Elephant Foundation, began rescuing injured, neglected and elderly elephants in the 1990s and opened the sanctuary in the Mae Taeng valley, near Chiang Mai, in 2003. The foundation writes that elephants there are not ridden and don't have to work or perform tricks. The park states that its elephants come from street begging, riding and circuses. On its website, the elephants' bath is something you watch, and it isn't guaranteed, because the animals follow their own rhythms. In November 2026 the park lists half-day visits at 2,500 baht, a full day, a night in the park and a weekly volunteer program.

There's one thing you should know, though. World Animal Protection keeps a public list of centers that follow its guidelines, updated in December 2025. Elephant Nature Park is marked as a high-welfare venue that still offers hand-feeding over a barrier, and it has been temporarily removed from the "elephant-friendly" list due to a legal investigation. We don't know more and won't add more: check the updated list before booking.

**World Animal Protection's list.** For Chiang Mai, in December 2025, the venues following the guidelines included ChangChill (View Doi and Hillside), Burm and Emily's Elephant Sanctuary, Kindred Spirit Elephant Sanctuary and the Mahouts Elephant Foundation's LIFE Project. It isn't our ranking: it's their list, and it changes. Reread it on the [World Animal Protection website](https://www.worldanimalprotection.org/elephant-friendly-tourist-guide) before choosing.

## What to see / what to do
- **Bangkok:** Grand Palace, Wat Pho and Wat Arun in the same morning. The rest is on the [Bangkok page](/destinazioni/thailandia/bangkok).
- **Chiang Mai:** the old city temples, Doi Suthep via the Monk's Trail, Wat Pha Lat, the Saturday and Sunday walking markets, a cooking class, Doi Inthanon. All on the [Chiang Mai page](/destinazioni/thailandia/chiang-mai).
- **Pai:** the canyon at sunset, the hot springs, the evening market. On the [Pai page](/destinazioni/thailandia/pai-mae-hong-son).

## Variations
**The Mae Hong Son loop (3-4 extra days).** From Pai you carry on west to **Mae Hong Son**, near the Myanmar border, and return to Chiang Mai from the south, via Mae Sariang, closing a mountain loop. Many do it by motorbike, and that's exactly where the risks are: constant bends, steep climbs and descents, trucks. If you aren't already a good rider, with a valid licence and insurance that covers you, do it by car with a driver. For nights in Mae Hong Son and along the road we have no checked addresses to recommend.

**Sukhothai (2 extra days).** Sukhothai was the capital of the Sukhothai Kingdom in the 13th and 14th centuries, and its historical park has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1991. It fits between Bangkok and Chiang Mai, breaking up the journey north. Connections between Bangkok, Sukhothai and Chiang Mai need checking at the time, because we haven't verified them.

## How to combine it with the sea
The north and the sea work well in the same trip, and the best season coincides: from November to February the north is cool and dry, and the Andaman Sea is in its dry season too. There are two ways to do it.
- **North first, then the sea.** Follow this route up to Day 12, then on Day 13 fly south instead of home, and carry on with the [trip to the southern sea](/viaggi/thailandia): Khao Sok, Krabi, Koh Phi Phi. From north to south you often go through Bangkok: direct routes change, so check them.
- **A short version of both.** With three weeks in total, you can keep Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai and the Golden Triangle (skipping Pai) and give about ten days to the sea.

The Gulf of Thailand, with Koh Samui, Koh Phangan and Koh Tao, has different seasons from the Andaman side: it's explained on the [Gulf page](/destinazioni/thailandia/golfo-thailandia).

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Choose the elephant center before you leave**, calmly, and not from a flyer at the hotel.
- **Book the night train early**, because berths can sell out weeks ahead around Thai holidays. Only on the official site or through well-known resellers.
- **Don't rent a scooter for the first time on the road to Pai.** Helmet, international driving permit, insurance that covers you: without them, take the minivan.
- **Check the air** before and during the trip.
- **Dress for the temples**: shoulders and knees covered, always.
- **Go to the White Temple late**, once the tours have left.
- **At the Golden Triangle, the museum before the viewpoint.** And you look at the border from the Thai bank.
- **Bring a sweater.** On Doi Inthanon and in Pai, early in the morning, it gets properly cold.

## Chiusura personale
What stayed with me from Chiang Mai is the pace, slower than the rest of the country, and the morning with the elephants at Elephant Nature Park, without ever riding them: one of my best memories of Thailand.
