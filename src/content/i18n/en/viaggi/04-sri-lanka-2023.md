# Sri Lanka in 12 Days: From Ancient Temples to the Tea of Nuwara Eliya, Down to the Beaches of the South

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress
**Periodo del viaggio:** November 11-22, 2023
**Durata:** 12 days, 11 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, private van with driver for the whole route, with a local DMC (WanderlustAsia) organizing hotels and activities
**Categoria:** Asia · Sri Lanka · on the road · natura · cultura · mare

---

## Apertura personale
What I remember most about the night on Adam's Peak is the shared effort: setting off in the dead of night to climb 2,243 meters on a fully lit pilgrimage staircase, in single file with hundreds of local pilgrims, the cold making itself felt only at the top after hours of sweating on the way up. Sri Lanka in 12 days was a crazy concentrate of different landscapes (from the Buddhist sites of Anuradhapura to the beaches of the south), but if I have to pick a single moment to open the story, it's that one: not so much the temple at the summit as the physical challenge lived alongside people who have been climbing that staircase all their lives.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** November is already low season because of the rains on the west/south coast, but it is high season for Adam's Peak (lit path full of pilgrims until May) and for the safaris in the north-center.
- **Documents/visa:** electronic ETA visa mandatory, applied for online at eta.gov.lk, issued in about 24h. *Update: at the time of this trip it cost about €70 per person, but since May 25, 2026 the ETA is free for holders of an Italian passport. Conditions change: always check before leaving.*
- **Budget:** recommended starting common fund €250 a head (enough even with extra activities), plus an average of another €200 a head during the trip for food, drinks, water, souvenirs and massages.
- **How I got around:** private van with an English-speaking driver for the whole trip (included in the package), one stretch on the scenic train from Nuwara Eliya to Ella.
- **SIM/data:** better to buy the local SIM (Dialog or SLT Mobitel) right after passport control at the airport: it costs much less than at the desk before the checks.
- **What to pack without getting it wrong:** long pants or a pareo (to cover knees in the temples), socks (in the temples you walk barefoot and the floor can burn), a sweatshirt for the freezing top of Adam's Peak (3-6°C and wind), a BS546-type adapter (three round pins, not the UK/Schuko standard), high-protection mosquito repellent.

### How I did it vs. the "on your own" alternatives
I toured Sri Lanka in a **private van with driver**: comfortable and carefree, but also the most "guided" way to see the country. For those who organize the trip themselves or as a couple and want more freedom, there are two very popular alternatives:

- **Rented tuk tuk, self-drive**: you drive yourself all around the island. You need a temporary local driving permit (about $30-40, arranged by the rental company) plus an international driving permit from your own country. The most reliable companies (e.g. **Holiday Tuk Tuk**, holidaytuktuk.com, based in Kandy) give a full driving lesson before departure and 24/7 assistance along the route. It is the most authentic and fun way to see the country, but you need to be able to handle the chaotic traffic of the cities and the mountain roads (some companies also offer a dedicated lesson for the hairpin bends toward Ella).
- **Local buses, the so-called "chicken buses"**: in Sri Lanka there are no long-distance coaches as elsewhere in Asia, only local public buses: the red ones of the state company **SLTB** and the blue ones of private companies. The nickname "chicken bus" (borrowed from Central America, but often used by travelers here too) describes the experience well: crowded, colorful vehicles, sometimes with animals on board, destination signs that the drivers forget to change. It is the cheapest and most lived-in way to get around, but you must always ask the driver or passengers to confirm the destination. Up-to-date information on schedules and stations is best obtained by asking directly at the bus stations (the biggest is the Central Bus Stand in Pettah, Colombo) or at the hotel; online, sites like Rome2Rio give an idea of the routes, but real times must always be checked on the spot. For trains, on the other hand (like the scenic Nanu Oya-Ella route), tickets must be booked well in advance: they sell out fast and in high season there is a real black market for seats.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 — Negombo
From Colombo airport to Negombo I was driven by Michael, a trusted driver, for about 6,000 LKR per car. Anyone arriving in time for lunch will find at **SeaJoy** a cheap place with good value for money; in the late afternoon we met up at the hotel before going out.

The welcome dinner, not included, is chosen among **Salt Restaurant**, **Tuk Tuk Wine & Dine** and **Jude Restaurant**: there the fish and seafood are good, about €12 a head, but the service is slow and there is Italian music in the background. Afterward, **Rodeo** or the **Sky Lounge**, or you ask guides and locals where there is a beach party. *Tip for the next day: the Negombo fish market, early in the morning, is a sight.*

### Day 2 — Negombo-Anuradhapura
After breakfast, five hours on the road to **Anuradhapura**, the first of the sacred cities of the Sinhalese cultural triangle: immense dagobas, ruins that tell more than two thousand years of Buddhist history and the Sri Maha Bodhi, a direct descendant of the tree under which the Buddha reached enlightenment.

The visit to the site lasts about three hours, with a guide: Anil, who goes by bicycle, or Lalith, who speaks excellent English, for about 6,500 LKR (€19) plus tip. On paper you are back at the hotel in the late afternoon; in reality, between a flat tire and the rain, I arrived much later. Buffet dinner at the hotel, about 2,200 LKR plus drinks.

### Day 3 — Anuradhapura-Dambulla: Mihintale and safari
Wake-up before dawn and half an hour on the road to **Mihintale**, where according to tradition Buddhism arrived in Sri Lanka in the 3rd century BC. The visit lasts a couple of hours: entrance 500 LKR, a sarong for rent at 50 LKR and socks for walking in the temples.

An hour and a half later we were on the **safari** jeep. I did it at **Minneriya**; depending on the season and where the elephants have moved, the local DMC may suggest Hurulu or Kaudulla. You spend about 12,000 LKR per jeep, for six or seven people, plus entrance. Along the way we had an excellent typical lunch, around 2,000 LKR.

In the late afternoon we climbed **Pidurangala Rock** for sunset: half an hour up with a demanding last stretch, so real shoes and not flip-flops. In front is Sigiriya Rock, and it is one of the most beautiful views of the trip. At the top you pay 1,000 LKR; you need the sarong and a headlamp to come down in the dark. Dinner in Dambulla, at **Mom's Kitchen** or the **Golden Palace**.

### Day 4 — Dambulla-Polonnaruwa-Dambulla
Early, half an hour to **Sigiriya**, Lion Rock. The climb takes an hour and a half or two; entrance ranges from 10,000 LKR to 30 USD per person depending on the rate applied, and you need a sarong, water and something to eat. At the top are the foundations of the 5th-century royal palace, and along the mirror wall the frescoes of the celestial maidens: one of the strongest moments of the trip. *Tip: start early, before the heat.*

An hour and ten minutes later we arrived at **Polonnaruwa**, the island's second medieval capital. We toured it by bicycle, rented on the spot for 1,000 LKR (about €2.50), among temples and Buddha statues: two and a half or three hours, lighter and more direct than Sigiriya. Recommended guide: Indu, 6,000 LKR plus tip. Lunch, before or after depending on timing, is at **Priyamali gedara**, a country restaurant over the rice paddies, very good.

On the way back to Dambulla there is the **Ayurvedic massage**, to be booked the day before through the driver. You have dinner in Dambulla or Sigiriya, at the same places as the evening before.

### Day 5 — Dambulla-Kandy
The morning was for the **Golden Temple of Dambulla**: five caves carved into the rock, full of Buddha statues and frescoes, one of the most beautiful and least touristy places of the trip (the ticket office closes at 5 pm, the temple at 7 pm). Then the **Dambulla Wholesale Market**, the biggest wholesale market in Sri Lanka, where you sample street food among the stalls.

Around noon we left for **Kandy**, two hours, with lunch at the **Ranweli Spice Garden** and a visit to the spice plantation. The afternoon is free: the **Kandy Fish Market**, the best place of the whole trip for spices and textiles to bring home, the Bahiravokanda Buddha statue, the lake.

In the evening there is the **Temple of the Tooth**. The ceremony begins as the light fades, with the drums just before, but the line forms from late afternoon: I got in just in time for the drums. Entrance 2,000 LKR, sarong mandatory. For dinner, a South Indian restaurant found by chance: dirt cheap and excellent, even if they get a bit confused with the orders.

### Day 6 — Kandy-Nallathanniya: rafting at Kitulgala
We left Kandy in the morning; if the guide agrees, there is an unscheduled stop at a local orphanage. Two hours and we arrived at **Kitulgala**, for a day on the water: **rafting on the Kelani River**, the same one where *The Bridge on the River Kwai* was filmed, organized by the DMC. With the operator you can add canyoning, waterfall jumps and a jungle walk with lunch.

In the evening we arrived at **Hatton/Nallathanniya**, the base for the climb of **Adam's Peak**, that same night or the next. Dinner at the hotel.

### Night Adam's Peak — the physical challenge of the trip
We set off in the dead of night to reach the top, at 2,243 meters, at dawn. It is two and a half or three hours of pilgrimage staircase, fully lit and crowded: from December to May it is pilgrim season, and the last stretch is done in single file, at a snail's pace. At the top it is cold, between 3 and 6 degrees with the wind, and a sweatshirt is indispensable. Coming down takes another two and a half or three hours. *The real attraction is the effort shared with hundreds of pilgrims, more than the temple at the top: an experience that is worth more for the path than for the destination.*

### Day 7 — Hatton-Nuwara Eliya: tea plantations
After breakfast we went to the **Pedro Tea Plantation**, with a visit to the plantation and the tea factory. The transfer varies a lot depending on the base: for me it was about twenty minutes instead of the two hours planned.

Then **Nuwara Eliya**, Sri Lanka's "Little England", at 1,868 meters. After lunch the afternoon can go in two directions. Relaxed: a massage, the tea ceremony at the Grand Hotel, Victoria Park, Ravana Falls or Lover's Leap Falls, all rather pricey, around 5,000-8,000 LKR. Adventurous: an hour to **Horton Plains National Park** and three hours of walking to **World's End**, the edge of a plateau that drops 870 meters, among the most spectacular views on the island.

You dine at **De Silva Food Center**, **Grand Indian**, **Grand Thai** or, the most local, **Themparadu**. The nightcap is at the **Lakeview Pub**.

### Day 8 — Nuwara Eliya-Ella: the scenic train
After breakfast we went to the station for the **train from Nanu Oya to Ella**. On paper it's three hours; I left in the early afternoon and arrived late in the evening, with nothing to eat on board. *It is the most photographed route in Sri Lanka, among tea valleys, bridges and tunnels, but it must be taken with the right spirit: delays are the norm.*

At **Ella**, before or after the train, if time allows: the **Nine Arches Bridge**, symbol of the country (trains pass several times a day, between morning and late afternoon, but the times are not reliable), the **Lipton's Seat** viewpoint, about an hour and a quarter away, Lover's Leap Falls, the Flying Ravana zip line fifteen minutes away (about €20) and a massage at Spa Ceylon.

The evening was the cooking class with dinner from the DMC, then **One Love Ella**: nice place, but watch out for the drinks offered at the table, which often end up on the bill.

### Day 9 — Ella-Yala-Hikkaduwa: safari and first beach
Very early wake-up and two hours on the road to **Yala National Park**, the park with the highest density of leopards in the world, along with elephants, crocodiles, water buffalo and a great many birds. The safari lasts about four hours and costs 12,000-16,000 LKR per jeep, plus entrance (€27-30 per person) and tip. Breakfast, often prepared by the resort, is eaten mid-morning.

Then we headed toward the coast, two hours and a quarter or two forty. Along the road are the famous stilt fishermen: *today they are almost always a staged show for tourists, no longer a real way of fishing.* In Mirissa, in the early afternoon, a good pizza from a kiosk (about 4,000 LKR), and in the late afternoon we arrived in **Hikkaduwa**. Dinner at a seaside restaurant: slow service, excellent red snapper.

### Day 10 — Hikkaduwa: day at the sea
A full day of beach, to fill as you like. The best spot for **swimming with turtles** is in front of the Hikka Tranz by Cinnamon Hotel. The **Tsunami Museum** tells what the 2004 tsunami did to this coast. Then there are the Big Buddha, surfing, snorkeling, diving and the boat safari on the Madu River; if the weather is bad, you can go to Colombo.

You have lunch on the beach at **Steffi's Mandala**, vegetarian, or at the **Thambili Cafe**. In the evening, the beach club **Sea Salt Society** or **Canal View**.

### Day 11 — Hikkaduwa-Negombo
Last free morning in Hikkaduwa. On the road to Negombo, 45 minutes away, is the **Sea Turtle Conservation Project in Bentota**; if there is time left, in the afternoon you stop by Colombo.

The farewell dinner, not included, goes back to the same places as the first night: Salt Restaurant, Tuk Tuk Wine & Dine, Jude Restaurant. And afterward, maybe, one last beach party.

### Day 12 — Negombo-Airport
Check-out, and Michael took me back to the airport, about 5,000 LKR per car. Nothing is organized: time is free until the flight.

## What to see / what to do
- **Sigiriya:** Lion Rock, among the most spectacular royal ruins in Asia.
- **Adam's Peak at night:** the real physical challenge of the trip, among local pilgrims and sunrise at the top.
- **Safari at Minneriya and Yala:** elephants in the first case, leopards (with a bit of luck) in the second.
- **The Temple of the Tooth in Kandy:** evening ceremony, drums and a very spiritual atmosphere.
- **The Nuwara Eliya-Ella scenic train:** worth doing despite the delays, among tea plantations and bright green valleys.
- **Pidurangala Rock at sunset:** the view of Sigiriya that everyone misses by rushing to do the Rock itself.
- **The beaches of the south:** Mirissa and Hikkaduwa, the relaxed counterpoint after days of temples and hiking.

**Negombo — Regal Réseau Hotel & Spa (arrival) and Goldi Sands Hotel (return)**
The Regal Réseau (4/5 on Tripadvisor, about 400 reviews) is liked above all for the rooftop with infinity pool and view, though according to several guests the bar music carries to the rooms. The Goldi Sands (4/5, over 1,700 reviews) has a buffet breakfast above expectations and a convenient location, walking distance from restaurants and bars, but several reviewers describe it as dated in its furnishings, almost 2-star in style.
*Alternatives in Negombo:* budget: Icebear Guesthouse, Ocean Beauty Guest House · mid-range: Jetwing Sea, Jetwing Lagoon (the two hotels chosen for this trip already fall more or less in this range) · luxury (modest): Jetwing Blue, Sentido Heritance Negombo.

**Anuradhapura — Miridiya Lake Resort**
Average rating 3/5 on Tripadvisor (about 400 reviews), with a useful detail: under the new management the resort seems to have improved a lot (entrance area, food, rooms). The view of Nuwara Wewa lake is much liked; some complaints about showers and humidity in the bathrooms.
*Alternatives in Anuradhapura:* budget: Saubagya Inn, Why Not Palace · mid-range: Kings Town Hotel, Monaara Leisure (the Miridiya Lake Resort is more or less at this level) · luxury: Ulagalla by Uga Escapes, Divulgaha Gedara Boutique Hotel.

**Dambulla — Tropical Life Resort**
The best of the nine, on paper: #1 among the specialty properties in Dambulla and 4/5 on Tripadvisor. Clean, well-equipped rooms, a generous buffet, lots of greenery, with birds and butterflies around the pool. A few reviews mention there is no WiFi in the room.
*Alternatives in Dambulla:* budget: Relax Guesthouse, Lake Island Homestay · mid-range: Lake Lodge Boutique Hotel, Kalundewa Retreat (the Tropical Life Resort is already at this level, in fact above) · luxury: Heritance Kandalama (the famous hotel designed by Geoffrey Bawa), Aliya Resort & Spa.

**Kandy — Rivendell Hotel**
4/5 on Tripadvisor but with mixed opinions: those who like it talk about a quiet location outside the center, views of the hills and very friendly staff; other reviews judge it pricey for what it offers, with a small buffet and, in the morning, the noise of a nearby temple.
*Alternatives in Kandy:* budget: 256 Townhouse Rest, Sharon Inn Kandy · mid-range: Hotel Suisse, OZO Kandy · luxury: Earl's Regency, The Grand Kandyan.

**Nallathanniya — Wathsala Inn**
Base for the climb of Adam's Peak, 3/5 on Tripadvisor: nice view of the valley and the Peak, courteous staff, but quite a few reviews point to maintenance problems (showers, sheets, leaks). Take it for what it is: an essential stopover for setting off at night, not a place for a vacation.
*Alternatives in Nallathanniya:* budget: Slightly Chilled, Sun Mount Adam's Peak · mid-range: White House Adam's Peak, Adams Peak Grand (the Wathsala Inn is more or less at this level, with more variability) · luxury: there are none here: it is a starting village for the trek, not a place to stay.

**Nuwara Eliya — Araliya RED**
One of the most booked in the area, 4/5 with almost 2,000 reviews: much-appreciated heated pool, hospitable staff, view of the town from the rooftop. Some reports of noise from nearby rooms and a smell of diesel at the entrance.
*Alternatives in Nuwara Eliya:* budget: Galaxy Grand Hotel, Star Gate Private Villa · mid-range: Jetwing St. Andrew's, Clifton Inn (the Araliya RED is already an excellent middle ground on this front) · luxury: Heritance Tea Factory, The Grand Hotel Nuwara Eliya.

**Ella — 88th Hotel**
4/5 on Tripadvisor, one of the most recommended places in the area: central location near Ella Rock and the Nine Arches Bridge, warm hospitality. Watch your expectations though: no air conditioning, no elevator, a firm bed with sheets only (no blanket), cleaning only on request.
*Alternatives in Ella:* budget: Ella Alpine Resort, Rock Side Inn (the 88th Hotel is already at this level, basic but well reviewed) · mid-range: Arawe Retreat, Zion View Ella Green Retreat · luxury: 98 Acres Resort & Spa, Nine Skies.

**Hikkaduwa — Sapphire Seas Beachfront Hotel**
Very positive reviews: a boutique hotel right on the sea, spacious rooms, cleanliness among the most praised things and an atmosphere some describe as "an oasis" compared with the busier parts of Hikkaduwa.
*Alternatives in Hikkaduwa:* budget: Hilda Guest House, Amaroo Hikkaduwa · mid-range: Sea Star Boutique Hotel, Hikka Tranz by Cinnamon (the Sapphire Seas is already at this level, boutique) · luxury: Crystal Sands, Aditya Resort.

## Practical tips / what to pack
- **Book the scenic train well in advance**: tickets sell out, especially in high season.
- **Allow for delays**, on trains as on road transfers: in Sri Lanka the schedule doesn't always go to plan.
- **Keep your sarong at hand**: it's needed in almost all temples (Mihintale, Sigiriya, Dambulla, Tooth Temple).
- **Cash in LKR**: many local restaurants and small operators don't accept cards.
- **Be wary of the "stilt fishermen"**: along the south coast they are now almost always a paid staged show for tourists.
- **Ayurvedic massages**: to be booked a day ahead through the driver or the hotel.

## Chiusura personale
From a trip this dense and varied it's hard to choose what to take home first, but if I have to rank the feelings, I put the night on Adam's Peak first, followed by the safari at Minneriya among the elephants and the scenic train to Ella, late as per tradition but beautiful all the same. Among temples, hiking and the beaches of the south, what stays most is precisely the contrast: few countries let you go from a night pilgrimage climb to a beach like Hikkaduwa within three days.
