# Vienna on a Long Weekend

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (trip taken by Rick; itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 3-4 days, 3 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independent, all on foot and by public transport
**Categoria:** Europa · Austria · Vienna · arte · musica · gastronomia

---

## Apertura personale
Vienna is the capital that for six centuries hosted the most powerful court in Central Europe, and you can see it in every corner of the historic center: a palace with over 1,400 rooms just outside the gates, a palace that today holds one of the most reproduced paintings in the world, a Gothic cathedral with a colored-tile roof that has become the very symbol of the city. But what makes Vienna different from Prague or Krakow isn't only imperial grandeur: it's the euro in place of a local currency to convert, and a coffee culture so deeply rooted that it has entered UNESCO's intangible cultural heritage.

Three or four days are enough for the essentials: the Innere Stadt with St. Stephen's Cathedral, the Hofburg, Schönbrunn (which alone is worth half a day), the Belvedere with Klimt and the Prater as a lighter finish. From a practical standpoint it's a simpler weekend than many other capitals (no currency exchange, public transport among the most efficient in Europe), but with a city that still asks you to slow down for at least an hour a day in a historic café, not as a tourist break but as a genuine local custom.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **April-May and September-October**, with mild weather, smaller crowds and shorter lines at Schönbrunn and Stephansplatz. **June-August** is high season: hot and lots of people at the most touristy spots. **November-March** is cold, but the city holds up well in winter too, and in December the Christmas markets, particularly the one in front of City Hall, are among the best known in Europe.
- **Currency:** Austria is in the **eurozone**, unlike Prague and Krakow: no exchange, no risk of penalizing conversions in restaurants. For those arriving from Italy it's a nice practical advantage.
- **Sundays, shops closed:** almost all shops, supermarkets included, **are closed on Sundays by law**. The only exceptions are stores in railway stations and at the airport, and duty pharmacies. Shopping has to be done on weekdays or Saturdays.
- **Beware of pickpockets on the U3 subway line:** especially on the stretch between Stephansplatz and Westbahnhof, and in the most crowded places such as Stephansplatz itself and the Naschmarkt on weekends. Vienna is among the top European cities for number of pickpocketings, while remaining one of the safest in the world for violent crime.
- **Fake police officers:** as in other European capitals, there's the scam of someone presenting themselves as a policeman and asking to check your documents or wallet. A real officer doesn't do that on the street without a clear reason.
- **Getting around the city:** U-Bahn, tram and bus of Wiener Linien, among the most efficient in Europe. Single ticket around **€2.40**, day ticket around **€6**, 48 hours around **€14**. The historic center can be covered largely on foot anyway.
- **From the airport to the centre:** from **Vienna-Schwechat (VIE)** the City Airport Train (CAT) normally reaches Wien Mitte in **16 minutes** (about €14.90 each way), but from late August 2026 to the end of October 2027 it doesn't run into the city because of work on the line and is replaced by buses: check before you leave · the ÖBB regional trains (S7, and the Railjets to the Hauptbahnhof in about 15 minutes) cost less, about €5 with the airport-to-city ticket, from the ÖBB app or the machines · Vienna Airport Lines bus about €11 · fixed-price taxis, Uber and Bolt about €40-50 · the classic mistake is travelling with only a Wiener Linien city ticket, which doesn't cover the stretch out to the airport.
- **Schönbrunn:** the basic tour of the rooms starts from about **€26**; the gardens and park are **free**, the Gloriette on the hill has a separate ticket. It's best to book online with a time slot.
- **State Opera, standing tickets:** from about **€13 to €18** depending on the section, sold only on the day: a first batch online from 10:00 am, a second at the box office from 80 minutes before the show.
- **Historic cafés:** in a Viennese café the table is "rented" for as long as you like. It's not a place to consume quickly, nor a simple break: it's Kaffeehauskultur, UNESCO intangible cultural heritage since 2011.
- **Food:** among savory dishes **Wiener Schnitzel** (breaded veal cutlet) and **Tafelspitz** (Viennese boiled beef); among desserts **Sachertorte** and **Apfelstrudel**. The Naschmarkt remains the best place for an informal, international meal.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Innere Stadt and Stephansdom
We started from the heart of the city, **St. Stephen's Cathedral** (Stephansdom): Gothic, with its roof of 230,000 colored tiles and the south tower (Steffl) 136 meters tall, 343 steps up to a viewpoint over the rooftops of the center. Beneath the cathedral, the **catacombs** hold the tombs of over 10,000 Viennese and the internal organs of the Habsburg emperors, whose bodies instead rest in the Capuchin Crypt, in another part of the city.

We walked along the **Graben** and the **Kärntner Straße**, the pedestrian streets of the historic center, as far as the **Ring**, the 19th-century boulevard that took the place of the medieval walls. In the afternoon, the first contact with **Kaffeehauskultur**: a stop in one of the historic cafés in the center, taking our time, in no hurry to get up.

In the evening we stayed in the Innere Stadt, among the streets of the center. The centre is easy to explore on foot after dinner too: from the Graben head towards **Am Hof** and the **Freyung**, quieter squares than Stephansplatz, or pass through Neuer Markt, home to the **Capuchin Crypt**, which is best kept for another morning. To eat, the side streets around the cathedral have several Beisl, the Viennese taverns, where you can order a schnitzel without the queues of the most famous places. *It is best to climb the south tower early, as soon as it opens: the spiral staircase is narrow and by midday you keep squeezing past people coming down.* During mass the cathedral can only be seen from the back of the nave.

**In practice:** the nave of St Stephen's can be visited freely up to the roped-off area, separate tickets for the tower, the catacombs (guided tour only) and the full visit · U1 and U3 underground, Stephansplatz stop · in the historic cafés you pay at the table, to the waiter · the whole first day is done on foot.

### Day 2 — Hofburg and Naschmarkt
In the morning we went to the **Hofburg**, the Habsburgs' winter palace for over six centuries: the **Sisi Museum**, devoted to the life of Empress Elisabeth, and the **Imperial Apartments**, where Sisi and Franz Joseph lived. Those with time can add a visit to the **Spanish Riding School** (or watch a morning training session), with the Lipizzaner horses and a tradition dating back to the 16th century.

In the afternoon the **Naschmarkt**: over a mile of stalls with fruit, spices and international cuisines, among protected Art Nouveau buildings. On Saturdays there's also the flea market.

The evening is the time for a second historic café, different from the one on the first day: Café Sacher or Café Hawelka. At the Sacher, next to the Opera, the cake is served with whipped cream and at peak times there is almost always a queue at the door; the Hawelka, in a side street off the Graben, is small and dark, with tables close together and the feel of an old drawing room. From the Hofburg to the Naschmarkt it is about twenty minutes on foot, past the Albertina and the Opera. *Go to the market for lunch or in the early afternoon: towards evening many stalls close and mostly the restaurants stay open.* On Saturday mornings the flea market is at the far end of the market, towards the Kettenbrückengasse stop.

**In practice:** the Sisi Museum, the Imperial Apartments and the Silver Collection share a single ticket, best bought online · the Spanish Riding School's morning exercises only take place on some days: check the official calendar · the Naschmarkt is closed on Sundays · U4 underground to Kettenbrückengasse, or U1, U2 and U4 to Karlsplatz.

### Day 3 — Schönbrunn
We devoted almost the whole day to **Schönbrunn**, the summer residence of the imperial family. Of the palace's over 1,400 rooms a selection is visited, with the basic tour or the more extensive Grand Tour. In the afternoon, the free **Baroque gardens**, up to the **Gloriette**, the pavilion on the hill with a view over the whole complex and the city.

Those with time and energy can add the **Schönbrunn Zoo**, in the same park, among the oldest in the world still in operation.

In the evening we went back to the center for dinner. To get there take the **U4** underground to Schönbrunn or Hietzing, about ten minutes from Karlsplatz, and enter the palace at the time printed on your ticket: the **Imperial Tour** covers about twenty rooms, the **Grand Tour** about forty, including Maria Theresa's rooms. After the visit, the park also has the maze, the Palm House and the Crown Prince Garden. *In summer the timed tickets sell out: booking a few days ahead saves hours of waiting in the courtyard.* The walk up to the Gloriette takes about twenty minutes, and the café inside it is the place to stop with the view over the palace.

**In practice:** Imperial Tour from about €26, Grand Tour and combined tickets cost more · gardens and park free, the Gloriette terrace, the maze and the zoo have separate tickets · the zoo alone takes half a day · U4 underground, Schönbrunn stop for the main entrance and Hietzing for the zoo side.

### Day 4 — Belvedere and Prater
In the morning we went to the **Belvedere Palace**, in particular the Upper Belvedere: here is **"The Kiss"** by Gustav Klimt, along with one of the most important collections of Austrian art in the world, from the Baroque to the Secession. It is best to arrive at opening time, because the room in front of The Kiss fills up quickly; from the Upper Belvedere you then walk down to the Lower Belvedere through the **Baroque gardens**, which are free, with the view over the city.

In the afternoon the **Prater**, a public park since 1766, with the **Wiener Riesenrad**: the Ferris wheel built in 1897 for Emperor Franz Joseph's jubilee, for decades the tallest in the world and a survivor of the bombings of the Second World War. For those who want to stay longer there are the attractions of the Wurstelprater. *The Prater is reached by U1 or U2 underground to Praterstern: the wheel is right by the entrance, and late afternoon is the best time to ride it.* Those with time left can walk along the **Hauptallee**, the tree-lined avenue of more than four kilometres that crosses the park.

For the last evening, if planned in advance, a standing ticket at the **State Opera** is the most memorable (and cheapest) way to close the Viennese weekend. Before the performance, have dinner near the Opera, about a quarter of an hour from the Prater by underground; without a ticket you can still walk past the theatre in the evening, when the façade is lit up.

**In practice:** Upper Belvedere with a timed ticket, best booked online, gardens free · tram D from the Ring to the Schloss Belvedere stop · the Prater is free to enter, the Ferris wheel and rides are paid · standing tickets at the Opera from about €13 to €18, same day only.

## What to see / what to do
- **St. Stephen's Cathedral**, with the climb up the south tower.
- **The Hofburg**, between the Sisi Museum and the Spanish Riding School.
- **Schönbrunn**, palace and gardens up to the Gloriette.
- **The Belvedere**, with Klimt's "The Kiss".
- **The Naschmarkt**, for an informal meal among different cuisines.
- **The Prater and the Wiener Riesenrad**, especially at sunset.
- **A tour of the historic cafés**, to understand Kaffeehauskultur first-hand.
- **A standing ticket at the State Opera**, for those who have time to fit it in.

## Possible extensions
- **Salzburg (+1-2 days):** about 2h30 by direct train, for Mozart's hometown, where "The Sound of Music" was filmed. It's a trip in itself.
- **Wachau and the Danube valley (+1 day):** about an hour by train, for the vineyards and medieval villages along the river, between Krems and Melk.
- **Prague:** with a direct train of about 4 hours, it's the natural pairing for this weekend for those with more days. The site has a dedicated itinerary.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
The **Innere Stadt** (the 1st district, inside the Ring) is the most convenient choice, with almost all the attractions within walking distance, but it's also the most expensive area. The nearby districts (2nd, 4th, 7th) cost less, are well connected by U-Bahn and are often a few minutes' walk from the Ring.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
The **Naschmarkt** for an informal meal among different cuisines. The **historic cafés** in the center for traditional pastries (Sachertorte and Apfelstrudel first of all), as well as for coffee. For savory food, Wiener Schnitzel and Tafelspitz remain the most traditional choices in the restaurants of the center.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book Schönbrunn online in advance**, with a time slot, especially in high season.
- **Do your shopping before Sunday**: almost everything is closed by law.
- **Be careful on the U3 subway line** at rush hour.
- **Don't follow anyone who presents themselves as a policeman on the street** asking for documents or money.
- **Don't treat the historic cafés like a quick bar**: the table is "rented" for as long as you like, and it's part of the experience.
- **Get to the Opera's standing-room box office well in advance**, if you're not buying online from 10:00 am.
- **Allow a good half day for Schönbrunn**: it's much bigger than it looks in the photos.

## Chiusura personale
Three or four days are enough for the essentials: the Hofburg, Schönbrunn, Klimt at the Belvedere, the Prater. At least an hour a day, though, belongs to a historic café.
