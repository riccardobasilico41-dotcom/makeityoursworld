# Provence, Camargue and Occitanie in 10 Days

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:** **May-June or September-October**; **late June-early August** for lavender
**Durata:** 10 days, 9 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independent, rental car
**Categoria:** Europa · Francia · Provenza · Camargue · Occitania

---

## Apertura personale
Provence, the Camargue and Occitanie are three different Mediterranean landscapes in one itinerary: villages and Roman ruins to the east, delta and horses in the middle, Cathar castles and canals to the west. It's a trip that combines Roman archaeology, wild nature and a medieval history little known outside France.

## Scheda pratica
- **Lavender**: the window is narrow and changes from plateau to plateau, so it's worth checking the situation a few days before.
- **Calanques**: in summer access changes day by day because of the fire risk, so always check.
- **Crit'Air sticker** for Marseille and Aix.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 — Avignon
We started in the Provence of the popes: in **Avignon**, the Palace of the Popes, the bridge and the old town.

### Day 2 — Pont du Gard and Nîmes
The **Pont du Gard** is best seen from below, and in summer with a swim in the river. Then we went on to **Nîmes**, with the Roman arena and the Maison Carrée.

### Day 3 — Arles and the Camargue
In **Arles** there is the Roman amphitheater. In the afternoon we entered the **Camargue**, and the landscape changed: from monuments to the delta.

### Day 4 — Camargue
A full day in the delta: the flamingos at Pont de Gau, a horseback ride and Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer.

### Day 5 — Aigues-Mortes and the Alpilles
The walls of **Aigues-Mortes** and the pink salt pans, then the Alpilles and **Saint-Rémy-de-Provence**.

### Day 6 — The Luberon and lavender (if in season)
**Gordes**, **Roussillon** with its ochre quarries and, if it's the right time, the lavender fields at Valensole or Sault. The window is narrow and changes from plateau to plateau: check a few days before, and don't walk into private fields for photos.

### Day 7 — The Gorges du Verdon
This is the surprise of the trip: few people plan it, and the **Verdon** remains one of the most spectacular landscapes in France, with water of a green that looks retouched. You can hike along the bottom of the canyon or paddle in by kayak from Lake Sainte-Croix.

### Day 8 — Aix-en-Provence and Marseille
The Cours Mirabeau in **Aix**, then **Marseille**: the Vieux-Port, Le Panier, the MuCEM and bouillabaisse. For Aix and Marseille you need the Crit'Air sticker.

### Day 9 — The Calanques
The **Calanques**, between Marseille and Cassis. In summer access changes day by day because of the fire risk, and it should be checked every morning.

### Day 10 — A Cathar castle or Carcassonne, and departure
If you're continuing west, finish with a Cathar castle or with **Carcassonne** in the evening, before heading home.

## What to see / what to do
- The Pont du Gard, with a swim in the river.
- The flamingos and horses of the Camargue.
- The Gorges du Verdon, on foot along the bottom.
- The Calanques, after checking access.

## Possible extensions
- **Full Occitanie (+3-4 days):** Carcassonne, the Cathar castles, the Canal du Midi.
- **Côte d'Azur (+7-10 days):** continuing east from Marseille.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
Avignon or Saint-Rémy for the western part, Gordes or Apt for the Luberon, Cassis for the Calanques.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
Ratatouille, tapenade, bouillabaisse in Marseille, Cavaillon melons, Provence rosé.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't come for the lavender at the wrong time.**
- **In summer, check access to the Calanques every day.**
- **Don't walk into private lavender fields for photographs.**

## Chiusura personale
The Verdon is the surprise of this itinerary: few people plan it, and it stays with you as one of the most spectacular landscapes in France, with water of a green that looks retouched.
