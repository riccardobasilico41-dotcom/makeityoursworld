# Provence, Camargue and Occitanie in 10 Days

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 10 days, 9 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independent, rental car
**Categoria:** Europa · Francia · Provenza · Camargue · Occitania

---

## Apertura personale
Provence, the Camargue and Occitanie are three different Mediterranean landscapes in one itinerary: villages and Roman ruins to the east, delta and horses in the middle, Cathar castles and canals to the west. It's a trip that combines Roman archaeology, wild nature and a medieval history little known outside France.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **May-June or September-October**; **late June-early August** for lavender
- **Lavender**: the window is narrow and changes from plateau to plateau, so it's worth checking the situation a few days before.
- **Calanques**: in summer access changes day by day because of the fire risk, so always check.
- **Crit'Air sticker** for Marseille and Aix.
- **From the airport to the centre:** the most used airport is **Marseille Provence (MRS)**. · **A1 shuttle** (formerly line 91) to Saint-Charles station: about €15 at 2026 fares, 25-50 minutes, every 10-20 minutes from about 4am until late evening, ticket at the machines or online · **train**: free shuttle to Vitrolles Aéroport Marseille Provence station, then TER to Saint-Charles in about 15-20 minutes or to Avignon, where the itinerary starts, in about an hour · metered **taxi**: about €60 by day and €70 at night to Marseille · it is best to pick up the rental car at the airport and avoid driving into the city.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 — Avignon
We started in the Provence of the popes: in **Avignon**, the Palace of the Popes, the bridge and the old town. The **Palace of the Popes** comes first, at opening time in the morning, before the groups: it is the largest Gothic palace in Europe and was the seat of the papacy for much of the fourteenth century. The visit takes a couple of hours, with the augmented-reality tablet included in the ticket.

Then you walk down to the **Pont Saint-Bénezet**, the bridge of the song, broken off halfway across the Rhône, and climb to the **Rocher des Doms** garden, from where you see the river and the bridge from above. The afternoon is for the old town inside the walls, the Place de l'Horloge and the streets around Les Halles, the covered market. *In July the Festival is on: the city is beautiful and packed, and accommodation prices rise a lot.*

**In practice:** Palace of the Popes about 14.50 euros, combined ticket with the bridge about 17 euros, with the bridge valid for 24 hours from the first entry · the center is walkable, so leave the car in the parking lots outside the walls · to sleep, Avignon or Saint-Rémy as a base for the first days.

### Day 2 — Pont du Gard and Nîmes
The **Pont du Gard** is best seen from below, and in summer with a swim in the river. Then we went on to **Nîmes**, with the Roman arena and the Maison Carrée. Get to the Pont du Gard early, when the light falls on the aqueduct and the parking lots are still empty. You cross the bridge on the lower level, go down to the bank of the Gardon to see it from below, and if you have time follow the paths through the scrub to the remains of the aqueduct and the viewpoints.

The museum explains how the water reached Nîmes, along a route of about fifty kilometers with a minimal gradient. In the afternoon, half an hour away by road, **Nîmes**: the **Arena**, one of the best preserved in the Roman world, the **Maison Carrée**, an almost intact temple, and the **Jardins de la Fontaine** with the Tour Magne at the top of the hill. *On concert and feria days the arena closes to visitors: check the calendar.*

**In practice:** Pont du Gard parking about 9 euros per vehicle, which covers access to the site, open from 8 a.m. to midnight · in Nîmes the pass for the Arena, Maison Carrée and Tour Magne costs about 14.50 euros, the arena alone about 11 · swim in the Gardon only in the marked areas.

### Day 3 — Arles and the Camargue
In **Arles** there is the Roman amphitheater. In the afternoon we entered the **Camargue**, and the landscape changed: from monuments to the delta. The morning in Arles starts at the **Arena**, the Roman amphitheater that still hosts shows, and continues with the ancient theater and the cloister of **Saint-Trophime**, one of the finest in Romanesque Provence.

Arles is also Van Gogh's town: the courtyard of the hospital he painted, the Espace Van Gogh, the Place du Forum with the night café. After lunch you head south and the road crosses rice fields, lagoons and pastures with black bulls and white horses. It is the first contact with the **Camargue**: drive slowly and stop along the side roads, where you see birds without getting out of the car.

**In practice:** Arles has a monuments pass, worth it if you see more than two sites · from Arles to Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer is about forty minutes by car · in the Camargue bring mosquito repellent, especially in the evening and from late spring through summer.

### Day 4 — Camargue
A full day in the delta: the flamingos at Pont de Gau, a horseback ride and Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer. The **Pont de Gau ornithological park** is best in the morning, when the pink flamingos feed in the lagoons close to the paths and the light is softer for photos. You walk marked trails for a couple of hours, among herons, egrets and other migrating birds.

Then the **horseback ride**, on the white horses of the Camargue, through marshes and beaches: many stables along the road to Saintes-Maries offer rides of an hour or two, even for people who have never been in the saddle. The afternoon is for **Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer**, with its fortified church: from the roof you see the whole delta and the sea. Toward evening you go back to the lagoons, when the light drops and the birds are more active.

**In practice:** Pont de Gau is open every day, with hours and prices to check on the park's website · for the ride, choose a stable that goes out in small groups and book the day before · in late May and in October Saintes-Maries hosts large pilgrimages: the village fills up.

### Day 5 — Aigues-Mortes and the Alpilles
The walls of **Aigues-Mortes** and the pink salt pans, then the Alpilles and **Saint-Rémy-de-Provence**. **Aigues-Mortes** is a thirteenth-century fortified town, built by Louis IX to give France a port on the Mediterranean: you climb onto the walls and walk the full circuit of the ramparts, with the **salt pans** stretching toward the sea.

Their pink color comes from a microscopic alga that lives in the salt water; in summer they can be visited by little train or by bike. In the afternoon you drive up to the **Alpilles**, the limestone hills between Arles and Avignon. **Saint-Rémy-de-Provence** is where Van Gogh spent a year in the asylum of Saint-Paul-de-Mausole; just outside town are the Roman ruins of **Glanum**. If there is still time, go up to Les Baux-de-Provence for sunset.

**In practice:** the towers and ramparts of Aigues-Mortes are visited with a Centre des monuments nationaux ticket · from Aigues-Mortes to Saint-Rémy is about an hour by car · the Wednesday morning market in Saint-Rémy is one of the liveliest in the area.

### Day 6 — The Luberon and lavender (if in season)
**Gordes**, **Roussillon** with its ochre quarries and, if it's the right time, the lavender fields at Valensole or Sault. The window is narrow and changes from plateau to plateau: check a few days before, and don't walk into private fields for photos. The **Luberon** looks best early in the morning or toward evening. **Gordes**, perched on its hill, should be seen first from the road coming from Cavaillon, where the viewpoint is, and then on foot through its lanes.

A few minutes away is the **Abbaye de Sénanque**, surrounded by lavender in July. In the afternoon **Roussillon**, the village of red and orange façades, with the **Sentier des Ocres**, a trail through the old quarries where the earth changes color at every bend. If you follow the lavender to **Valensole**, allow more than an hour by car: flowering there usually runs from late June to mid-July, a little later in Sault.

**In practice:** Sentier des Ocres about 3.50 euros, thirty minutes for the short loop and an hour for the long one · closed shoes and clothes that don't mind red dust · to sleep, Gordes or Apt as a base for the Luberon.

### Day 7 — The Gorges du Verdon
This is the surprise of the trip: few people plan it, and the **Verdon** remains one of the most spectacular landscapes in France, with water of a green that looks retouched. You can hike along the bottom of the canyon or paddle in by kayak from Lake Sainte-Croix. The day depends on which you choose. Hikers take the **Sentier Blanc-Martel**, about a dozen kilometers along the floor of the gorge, with ladders and tunnels, starting early: it takes six to seven hours and a torch for the tunnels.

Those who prefer the water rent a kayak or pedal boat at the **Pont du Galetas**, on **Lake Sainte-Croix**, and paddle up into the mouth of the canyon between vertical walls. Either way, in the evening drive the **Route des Crêtes** above La Palud, with its viewpoints over the canyon, and stop at **Moustiers-Sainte-Marie**, the pottery village with the star hanging between two rocks.

**In practice:** for the Blanc-Martel a seasonal shuttle takes you back to the start, with dates to check each year · kayaks and pedal boats are rented by the hour, in summer arrive at opening time · the gorge roads are narrow and slow: allow double the time the map suggests.

### Day 8 — Aix-en-Provence and Marseille
The Cours Mirabeau in **Aix**, then **Marseille**: the Vieux-Port, Le Panier, the MuCEM and bouillabaisse. For Aix and Marseille you need the Crit'Air sticker. The morning in **Aix-en-Provence** is spent on the **Cours Mirabeau**, the avenue of plane trees and fountains, and in the old town, with markets in the squares. Cézanne fans go up to his studio, on the hill north of the center.

Then half an hour by road to **Marseille**. You start at the **Vieux-Port**, climb through the streets of **Le Panier**, the oldest quarter, and reach the **MuCEM**, the museum of Mediterranean civilizations, linked by a footbridge to Fort Saint-Jean. Sunset is watched from **Notre-Dame de la Garde**, the basilica on the hill. Dinner is for **bouillabaisse**. *It is an expensive dish that takes time to prepare: choose a place that serves it the traditional way, often by reservation.*

**In practice:** the MuCEM is closed on Tuesdays, entry to the exhibitions about 11 euros · in Marseille leave the car in a parking garage and get around on foot, by metro or tram · there is also a bus from the Vieux-Port up to Notre-Dame de la Garde.

### Day 9 — The Calanques
The **Calanques**, between Marseille and Cassis. In summer access changes day by day because of the fire risk, and it should be checked every morning. From June to September the prefecture publishes every evening, around 6 p.m., the map of access to the massifs for the next day: at red level the trails are closed to everyone. When they are open, from **Cassis** you walk to **Port-Miou**, **Port-Pin** and **En-Vau**, the last the most beautiful and the most tiring, with steep descents on rock.

Start early, with plenty of water and hiking shoes. On the Marseille side, the Calanque de **Sugiton** can be reached in summer only with a free reservation. When the trails are closed there is still the sea: boats leave the port of Cassis for tours of the calanques. *There is no shade or water in the calanques: in the middle of a July day the heat is a serious problem.*

**In practice:** check the day's access on the prefecture's website or the Mes Calanques app · for Sugiton, booking opens three days ahead and is cancelled automatically if the massif is closed · to sleep, Cassis, handy both for the trails and for the boats.

### Day 10 — A Cathar castle or Carcassonne, and departure
If you're continuing west, finish with a Cathar castle or with **Carcassonne** in the evening, before heading home. From Provence to **Carcassonne** is more than three hours of motorway, so leave in the morning. If you choose a **Cathar castle**, aim for **Peyrepertuse** or **Quéribus**, perched on the ridges of the Corbières, about an hour and a half from Carcassonne: you walk up from the parking lot, often in a strong wind, and the view opens all the way to the Pyrenees.

If you go straight to Carcassonne, enter the **Cité**, the fortified town on the hill, and visit the Château Comtal and the ramparts walk in the afternoon. The inner streets are full of souvenir shops, but you only need to climb onto the wall walk to be back in the Middle Ages, with the double ring of walls and the conical towers. In the evening the Cité empties of groups, and the floodlit walls are the right way to end the trip.

**In practice:** the Cité is open and free at all hours, the Château Comtal and ramparts are paid, from about 13 euros · for the Cathar castles, good shoes, water and a jacket even in summer · to fly home there are the airports of Carcassonne, Toulouse and Montpellier.

## What to see / what to do
- The Pont du Gard, with a swim in the river.
- The flamingos and horses of the Camargue.
- The Gorges du Verdon, on foot along the bottom.
- The Calanques, after checking access.

## Possible extensions
- **Full Occitanie (+3-4 days):** Carcassonne, the Cathar castles, the Canal du Midi.
- **Côte d'Azur (+7-10 days):** continuing east from Marseille.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
Avignon or Saint-Rémy for the western part, Gordes or Apt for the Luberon, Cassis for the Calanques.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
Ratatouille, tapenade, bouillabaisse in Marseille, Cavaillon melons, Provence rosé.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't come for the lavender at the wrong time.**
- **In summer, check access to the Calanques every day.**
- **Don't walk into private lavender fields for photographs.**

## Chiusura personale
The Verdon is the surprise of this itinerary: few people plan it, and it stays with you as one of the most spectacular landscapes in France, with water of a green that looks retouched.
