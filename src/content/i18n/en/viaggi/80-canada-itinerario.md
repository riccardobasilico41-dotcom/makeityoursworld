# Eastern Canada on the road in 16 days: Toronto, Niagara, Ottawa, Montréal, Québec City and Tadoussac

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress
**Periodo del viaggio:** designed for June to September, when the Niagara boat and the whale-watching cruises at Tadoussac are running, or for late September to mid-October, with fall colors in the Laurentians and Charlevoix
**Durata:** 16 days, 15 nights, plus an optional 3-5 day extension to Churchill (Manitoba) for the polar bears
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, on our own, with a pickup truck rented in Toronto and returned in Montréal, about 1,900-2,000 km (1,200 miles) of driving
**Categoria:** Nord America · Canada · Ontario · Québec · road trip · cascate del Niagara · città · balene · orsi polari

---

## Apertura personale
We drove around eastern Canada. We rented a pickup, a big, very nice one, and we found the roads generally good: for a trip like this, driving is the most comfortable way to get around.

At Niagara Falls we also took the helicopter ride. It's beautiful and spectacular, but it's a bit less rewarding than seeing the falls from below, on the boat. If you don't want to get wet, though, it's a good idea.

This guide lays out the classic Ontario and Québec itinerary, the one you do in a little over two weeks with a rental car. You start in **Toronto**, head down to **Niagara Falls**, go back up the St. Lawrence through the **Thousand Islands** and **Kingston**, stop in the capital **Ottawa** and the hills of **Mont-Tremblant**, then the two French-speaking cities, **Montréal** and **Québec City**, and finally the **Charlevoix** coast up to **Tadoussac**, where the Saguenay fjord meets the St. Lawrence and in summer you can see whales. At the end there's a separate extension, much more expensive and far away: **Churchill**, on Hudson Bay, for the polar bears.

## Scheda pratica

### Documents, eTA and driver's license
- **Passport** valid for the whole length of your stay.
- **eTA (Electronic Travel Authorization):** you need it if, like Italian citizens, you're visa-exempt and arrive in Canada **by air**. It costs **CAD 7** per person and is valid for up to **five years**, or until your passport expires if that comes first. You apply only on the official Canadian government website (canada.ca); it usually arrives by email within minutes, but the government recommends having it before you buy your flight. The passport number you enter must match the document you use at check-in. If you enter by land from the United States, you don't need one.
- **Driver's license:** in **Ontario** a valid foreign license is enough for tourists to drive for up to three months; in **Québec** the SAAQ accepts it for up to six consecutive months. An **International Driving Permit** isn't mandatory for a two-week trip, but it's useful and some rental companies ask for it: you get it before leaving, since you can't get one in Canada.
- **Health insurance** with a high coverage limit: care for non-residents is expensive.

### Car rental and driving
- **One-way Toronto-Montréal:** returning the car in another province almost always carries a surcharge. It's worth asking for it in the quote and comparing it with the cost of a return flight to Toronto. Alternatively you can do a loop and go back to Toronto, adding about 550 km (340 miles).
- **Pickup or SUV:** you don't need one for the roads on this itinerary, which are all paved, but a big vehicle is handy with luggage. Rental pickups usually have an open bed: keep suitcases in the cab or covered.
- **Book from Italy** with unlimited mileage and full insurance; you need a credit card in the driver's name for the deposit.
- **Tolls, the 407 ETR:** around Toronto the **407 ETR** expressway is electronic toll only, with no toll booths. From January 1, 2026, rates for cars run from about **CAD 0.50 to 1.19 per kilometer**, depending on zone, time and direction. Without a transponder the plate is photographed and the bill goes to the rental company, which passes it on to you with its fees. Just set the GPS to "avoid tolls": the free **401** follows the same route, usually with more traffic.
- **Rules that surprise you:** speed limits in km/h (usually 100 on highways, lower in many areas), right turn on red allowed in Ontario but **banned on the whole island of Montréal**; stopped school buses with lights flashing must not be passed in either direction. In Québec the road signs are in French only. From **December 1 to March 15** winter tires are mandatory in Québec.
- **Roads:** wide, well-kept highways between cities; heavy traffic in Toronto and Montréal at rush hour and frequent roadworks in summer.

### Money, taxes and tips
- **Currency:** Canadian dollar (CAD). Cards are accepted almost everywhere, even for small amounts.
- **Taxes:** displayed prices almost always **exclude tax**. In Ontario the 13% HST is added, in Québec about 15% (federal plus provincial tax).
- **Tips:** 15-20% at restaurants, often suggested right on the card terminal.

### Weather, time zone and electricity
- **When to go:** June to September to have everything open, with hot, humid summers in Toronto and Montréal; late September to mid-October for the foliage, with cold nights. In winter the cities are under snow, the Niagara boat doesn't run and the whale cruises are shut down.
- **Time zone:** Ontario and Québec are 6 hours behind Italy; Churchill and Winnipeg 7.
- **Electricity:** 120 V, type A and B sockets, you need an adapter.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Toronto
From **Pearson** airport the **UP Express** train takes you to Union Station, downtown, in about 25 minutes. You don't need the car on the first day: in Toronto you get around on foot, by subway and by streetcar, and downtown hotel parking is expensive. In the evening a walk along the waterfront, the **Harbourfront**, is enough to watch the city light up.

**In practice**
- Pearson to Union Station on the UP Express, about 25 minutes
- You pick up the car on the morning of day three

### Day 2 — Toronto: the CN Tower, the lake and the markets
In the morning you go up the **CN Tower**, the 553-meter tower that dominates the skyline: from the observation deck, on clear days, you can see the far shore of Lake Ontario. The basic ticket costs around CAD 43, but there's more than one price tier and it's worth checking the official site.

The rest of the day is for the neighborhoods: the **St. Lawrence Market** for lunch, the red-brick pedestrian streets of the **Distillery District**, the shops and cafés of **Kensington Market**. In the late afternoon the ferry to the **Toronto Islands** takes you in fifteen minutes to the beach where you photograph the skyline at sunset.

**In practice**
- CN Tower: around CAD 43 for basic admission: check cntower.ca
- Ferry to the Toronto Islands from the Jack Layton Ferry Terminal

### Day 3 — Niagara Falls from the boat
With the car picked up, it takes about an hour and a half to get from Toronto to the falls. There are three falls: the horseshoe-shaped **Horseshoe Falls** on the Canadian side, and the **American Falls** with the small **Bridal Veil Falls** on the U.S. side.

The boat is the way of seeing them that we liked best: **Voyage to the Falls** by **Niagara City Cruises** goes up the river to the middle of the horseshoe, right into the water mist. They give you the red poncho on board and you get wet anyway. In 2026 the boat opened on May 1, late because of ice on Lake Erie; the season runs until late fall. In the afternoon you can add **Journey Behind the Falls**, the tunnels cut into the rock that take you behind and beside the falls. In the evening, in summer, the falls are lit up and on many nights there are fireworks.

**In practice**
- Toronto to Niagara Falls: about 130 km (80 miles), an hour and a half without traffic
- Voyage to the Falls: CAD 47.95 adults and CAD 32.95 children 3-12, plus HST (2026 prices reported by several guides; check the official price at niagaracitycruises.com)
- Journey Behind the Falls: around CAD 30 plus HST in high season
- Parking near the falls is paid and expensive: better to leave the car at the hotel and use the WEGO shuttles

### Day 4 — Niagara by helicopter and Niagara-on-the-Lake
The morning is for the helicopter. **Niagara Helicopters** flies every day from 9 a.m. to sunset, weather permitting, and you don't need to book: you show up at the base and wait your turn. The flight lasts about 9 minutes, 12 with boarding, and passes over the river gorges and both fronts of the falls. For us it was beautiful and spectacular, but it's a bit less rewarding than seeing the falls from below, on the boat. It's the right choice for anyone who doesn't want to get wet. I don't remember exactly how much we paid; roughly 200 dollars each. The 2026 rates from resellers start at about CAD 171 plus HST for the basic flight, and you ask for the exact price at the base.

In the afternoon you head up the **Niagara Parkway**, the scenic road along the river, to **Niagara-on-the-Lake**, a nineteenth-century town at the mouth on Lake Ontario. Around it are the wineries of the Niagara Peninsula, famous for **icewine**, the sweet wine made from grapes harvested frozen.

**In practice**
- Niagara Helicopters: daily flights from 9 a.m. to sunset, no booking, only with good visibility
- Niagara Falls to Niagara-on-the-Lake: about 25 km (15 miles) along the Niagara Parkway

### Day 5 — The Thousand Islands
This is the longest transfer day: about four hours east, along Lake Ontario, to **Gananoque**, the gateway to the **Thousand Islands**. Here the St. Lawrence leaves the lake and breaks up into almost two thousand islands and islets, many with a single house on them. Cruises leave from Gananoque and Rockport and last from one to three hours. If you'd rather drive, you can take the **Thousand Islands Parkway**, the scenic road along the river.

**In practice**
- Niagara Falls to Gananoque: about 380 km (235 miles), 4 hours, via the 401 or the QEW depending on traffic
- The longer cruises pass close to the U.S. border: if you want to land on the American side (Boldt Castle) you need the documents to enter the USA

### Day 6 — Kingston and arriving in Ottawa
**Kingston**, half an hour from Gananoque, was the first capital of the Province of Canada. In the morning you visit **Fort Henry**, the nineteenth-century fortress on the hill above the harbor, and the limestone center around City Hall. In the afternoon you drive up to **Ottawa** in a couple of hours, in time for the change of light on Parliament.

**In practice**
- Kingston to Ottawa: about 195 km (120 miles), 2 hours
- Fort Henry is open from May to September: dates and prices on the Parks of the St. Lawrence site

### Day 7 — Ottawa: Parliament, the Rideau Canal and the museums
Ottawa is a small capital and you get around on foot. On **Parliament Hill** the neo-Gothic buildings tower over the river; the **Centre Block** has been closed for years for a long restoration, and guided tours run in the other buildings, with free tickets to book. In summer there's a changing of the guard on the lawn in front.

The **Rideau Canal**, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, runs through the city with its stepped locks right next to Parliament. Then the **ByWard Market**, the covered market and the streets around it, and one of the big museums: the **National Gallery of Canada** or, across the river in Gatineau, the **Canadian Museum of History**.

**In practice**
- Parliament tours are free, book online
- Gatineau is already in Québec: once you cross the bridge, the signs turn French

### Day 8 — Mont-Tremblant and the Laurentians
From Ottawa you enter the **Laurentians**, hills of forests and lakes north of Montréal. **Mont-Tremblant** is a ski resort with a pedestrian village of colorful houses at the foot of the mountain; in summer and fall the gondola takes you to the summit for the view over the lakes. A little farther north is the **Parc national du Mont-Tremblant**, with trails and canoeing, paid admission.

**In practice**
- Ottawa to Mont-Tremblant: about 150 km (90 miles), 2 hours
- The village is expensive to stay in on fall weekends: book early or sleep in the nearby towns

### Day 9 — Montréal: the old city and Notre-Dame Basilica
From Mont-Tremblant to Montréal it's an hour and a half. Once you arrive, the car stays at the hotel: in the city you go on foot and by subway, and on the whole island of Montréal turning right on red is banned.

The afternoon is for **Old Montréal (Vieux-Montréal)**, the cobbled streets between the port and the place d'Armes. **Notre-Dame Basilica**, with its blue and gold interior, can be visited with a ticket. The **Old Port** on the St. Lawrence is the place for an evening stroll.

**In practice**
- Mont-Tremblant to Montréal: about 130 km (80 miles), an hour and a half
- Notre-Dame Basilica: paid admission, hours and prices at basiliquenotredame.ca

### Day 10 — Montréal: Mount Royal, the Plateau and the markets
In the morning you climb **Mount Royal**, the hill the city is named after, up to the **Kondiaronk Belvedere**, from which you see the whole downtown. You come down toward the **Plateau-Mont-Royal**, the neighborhood of outdoor wrought-iron staircases, and **Mile End**, where two historic bakeries, **St-Viateur** and **Fairmount**, bake bagels in a wood-fired oven. In the afternoon the **Marché Jean-Talon**, the big open-air market in Little Italy.

**In practice**
- To get to the Kondiaronk Belvedere you walk up from the park or take the bus
- Montréal is the city of **poutine** and **smoked meat**: try them at least once

### Day 11 — From Montréal to Québec City: the old town
From Montréal to **Québec City** it's under three hours along the St. Lawrence. **Old Québec (Vieux-Québec)**, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is the only fortified city north of Mexico with its walls still standing. You arrive in time for the afternoon on the **Dufferin Terrace**, the wooden boardwalk beneath the **Château Frontenac**, and go down to the lower town by funicular or by the Casse-Cou staircase to the **Petit-Champlain district** and **Place Royale**.

**In practice**
- Montréal to Québec City: about 255 km (160 miles), 2 hours 45 minutes to 3 hours
- Parking inside the walls is awkward: better a hotel with parking or a public lot outside the gate

### Day 12 — Montmorency Falls and the Île d'Orléans
Fifteen minutes from downtown are the **Montmorency Falls**, about 83 meters high, some thirty meters more than Niagara's Horseshoe Falls. You see them from the suspension bridge over the drop, from the staircase that runs down the cliff and from the gondola. Right after, across the bridge, the **Île d'Orléans**: a 67 km (42 mile) loop among farms, country churches, fruit stands and cider and cassis cellars. In the evening you return to the city for the **Plains of Abraham** and the **Citadelle**.

**In practice**
- Parc de la Chute-Montmorency: parking and gondola paid (Sépaq)
- The Île d'Orléans loop takes half a day with stops

### Day 13 — Charlevoix: Baie-Saint-Paul and the road along the river
You head down the north shore of the St. Lawrence toward **Charlevoix**, a region of hills that drop to the river, a UNESCO biosphere reserve. **Baie-Saint-Paul** is a town of art galleries and creameries; from here **Route 362** runs high above the river to **La Malbaie**, with viewpoints over the bends. In the evening you reach **Baie-Sainte-Catherine** and take the ferry to **Tadoussac**.

**In practice**
- Québec City to Baie-Saint-Paul: about 95 km (60 miles), then another 130 km (80 miles) or so to Tadoussac
- The Baie-Sainte-Catherine to Tadoussac ferry, across the Saguenay fjord, is free and runs all day

### Day 14 — Tadoussac and the whales of the St. Lawrence
**Tadoussac** sits at the mouth of the **Saguenay fjord**, where cold, deep water draws whales into the St. Lawrence. Cruises leave from the harbor and last about three hours; you see fin whales, humpbacks in the right weeks and the **belugas**, which live here all year. If you get seasick, you can watch them from land at the **Centre d'interprétation et d'observation de Cap-de-Bon-Désir**, a little farther east. In the afternoon, the trails over the fjord or the dunes above the town.

**In practice**
- Croisières AML, 2026 season from May 9 to November 1, cruise of about 3 hours from CAD 134.99 per adult plus taxes; other operators run shorter Zodiac outings
- Whales are most numerous between July and September

### Day 15 — Back to Montréal
This is the longest driving day of the trip: from Tadoussac to Montréal you retrace the Charlevoix coast and the St. Lawrence, about five and a half hours not counting stops. If you have a flight the evening of the next day, you can break the trip in Québec City. In the evening you return the car, or keep it until the airport.

**In practice**
- Tadoussac to Montréal: about 470 km (290 miles), 5 and a half hours
- Fill up before returning the car, if the contract requires it

### Day 16 — Departure from Montréal
Return flight from **Montréal-Trudeau airport (YUL)**, about twenty kilometers (12 miles) from downtown. With flights to Europe usually leaving in the evening, there's time for one last breakfast in Mile End or a walk up Mount Royal.

**In practice**
- Returning the car at YUL saves the taxi to the airport
- Get to the airport 3 hours early for international flights

## Extension — Churchill (Manitoba) and the polar bears (3-5 days)

> **I didn't do this part myself, friends told me about it: they say that, despite the price, it's beautiful.** The information below is from guides, checked in October 2026, not a travel memory.

**Churchill** is a town of fewer than a thousand people on the shore of **Hudson Bay**, in northern Manitoba. Between **mid-October and mid-November** polar bears gather on the coast waiting for the bay to freeze, so they can go back to hunting seals on the pack ice. It's one of the few places in the world where you can see them up close without an Arctic expedition.

- **How to get there:** no road reaches Churchill. You fly from **Winnipeg** with **Calm Air**, in about two and a half hours, or take the **VIA Rail** train Winnipeg-Churchill, two departures a week and about 45 hours of travel, with frequent delays. You reach Winnipeg by air from Toronto or Montréal.
- **How you see the bears:** in a **tundra buggy**, big vehicles on very tall wheels that roam the Churchill Wildlife Management Area. The bears come up to the vehicles, but you never get out. The daily outing lasts about 8 hours. The main operator is **Frontiers North**; in 2025 the summer day outings cost CAD 279 per adult, taxes included, while the fall ones with the bears should be checked on the site, because the bear season is the most expensive of the year.
- **Price ranges:** the Winnipeg-Churchill round-trip flight runs roughly from CAD 700 to 1,400 depending on how far ahead you book; multi-day packages with flights, lodging and outings cost several thousand dollars per person, and a 6-day program at the Tundra Buggy Lodge for fall 2026 is offered at USD 10,825 per person. Lodging in town is limited and for the bear weeks it's booked many months ahead.
- **Sensible length:** at least three nights in Churchill, to get two days on the tundra buggy even with bad weather.
- **Weather:** in late October and November it's already properly cold, often below freezing and with strong wind: you need a jacket, insulated pants, boots and winter gloves.
- **Safety:** bears also come into town. You don't walk outside the built-up area or after dark, and you follow local guidance.
- **Other seasons:** in July and August there are belugas in the Churchill River estuary, from January to March the northern lights.

## Itineraries by length
- **12 days:** Toronto (1), Niagara (2), Ottawa (1), Montréal (2), Québec City (2), return from Montréal; you skip the Thousand Islands, Mont-Tremblant and Tadoussac.
- **With Tadoussac but without eastern Ontario:** from Niagara you go straight to Montréal (about 6 and a half hours) and gain two days for the Saguenay fjord.
- **Loop from Toronto:** if you don't want to pay the one-way fee, bring the car back to Toronto, with two extra days of driving from Montréal.
- **With Churchill:** in late October or early November, fly from Montréal to Winnipeg at the end of the loop, then the flight to Churchill. At that time the whale season at Tadoussac is ending and the first snowfalls are possible.

## Where to stay
- **Toronto:** the center around Union Station and the waterfront is convenient for arriving and leaving; hotel parking is paid separately.
- **Niagara Falls:** the Fallsview hotels look at the falls from the rooms; those on Lundy's Lane cost less. Niagara-on-the-Lake has B&Bs and quieter small hotels.
- **Thousand Islands and Kingston:** motels and B&Bs in Gananoque, hotels in downtown Kingston.
- **Ottawa:** the ByWard Market and downtown, all within walking distance of Parliament.
- **Mont-Tremblant:** the pedestrian village or the towns around, which are cheaper.
- **Montréal:** Old Montréal for atmosphere, the Plateau for the neighborhoods, downtown for convenience.
- **Québec City:** inside the walls, or just outside with parking included.
- **Charlevoix and Tadoussac:** auberges and motels; in Tadoussac places are few in July and August.

## Where to eat
- **Toronto:** the **peameal bacon sandwich** at the St. Lawrence Market and the food from all over the world in the multicultural neighborhoods.
- **Niagara:** the wineries of the peninsula, with icewine tastings.
- **Montréal:** the wood-fired bagels of St-Viateur and Fairmount, smoked meat, poutine, the Marché Jean-Talon.
- **Québec City and Charlevoix:** the **tourtière** (savory meat pie), Charlevoix cheeses, maple products in the cabanes à sucre and cider from the Île d'Orléans.
- **Tadoussac:** fish and seafood from the St. Lawrence.

## Rough budget
Prices checked in October 2026, per person; taxes are almost always added to the displayed price:
- **eTA:** CAD 7.
- **Voyage to the Falls boat:** CAD 47.95 plus HST.
- **Helicopter flight over Niagara:** roughly 200 dollars each (I don't remember the exact price); in the 2026 price lists the basic flight starts at about CAD 171 plus HST per person.
- **Journey Behind the Falls:** around CAD 30 plus HST.
- **CN Tower:** around CAD 43.
- **Whale cruise at Tadoussac (3 hours):** from CAD 134.99 plus taxes.
- **Rental car, fuel and lodging:** they vary a lot with the season and the one-way fee; budget them for the period you choose.
- **Churchill extension:** flights from Winnipeg roughly CAD 700-1,400 round trip, plus lodging and tundra buggy outings; with a package you easily go over CAD 5,000 per person.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Apply for the eTA only on canada.ca**, at CAD 7, and check the passport number before submitting.
- **Do the boat even if you take the helicopter:** from below, the falls are more rewarding.
- **Set "avoid tolls" around Toronto** so you don't get 407 ETR bills with the rental company's fees.
- **Ask for the one-way fee** before booking the car from Toronto to Montréal.
- **Factor in taxes and tips**, which add a third to many bills.
- **Keep luggage in the cab** if the pickup has an open bed.
- **Remember that in Montréal you can't turn right on red.**
- **Book Tadoussac early in summer and Mont-Tremblant in fall**, when the lodging runs out.
- **For Churchill, book lodging and outings many months ahead:** the bear weeks are few.

## Chiusura personale
Eastern Canada by car is an easy drive: the roads are generally good and the route follows Lake Ontario and then the St. Lawrence up to Tadoussac, with two detours toward Ottawa and the Laurentians. From our experience two tips stay with me: a big pickup is handy for a loop like this, and at the falls, if you can only do one thing, do the boat.
