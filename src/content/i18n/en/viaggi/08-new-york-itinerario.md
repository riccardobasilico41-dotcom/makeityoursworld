# New York: Skyscrapers, Neighborhoods and One Game Each of Baseball and Basketball

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress
**Periodo del viaggio:** September 7-12
**Durata:** 6 days, 5 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, flexible planning, expenses sorted out as we went
**Categoria:** Nord America · USA · New York · città · sport

## Apertura personale
New York moves at a brisk pace, and over six days I crossed it on foot, 9-12 miles (15-20 km) a day, getting back each evening worn out but with my head full of different skylines: from Times Square lit up on the first night to the sunset from Top of the Rock, which New Yorkers themselves prefer to the Empire State Building as a viewpoint. It's a rhythm that leaves little room to breathe, but it's also the only way to fit into a few days a city that deserves far more time.

## Scheda pratica
- **Documenti:** ESTA required (electronic travel authorization, https://esta.cbp.dhs.gov/) for EU citizens with an electronic or biometric passport, for tourist stays of up to 90 days. Anyone who has been to Cuba, North Korea, Syria or Iran can't use the ESTA and has to apply for a proper visa.
- **Moneta:** US dollar. Cards are accepted almost everywhere and many places only take cards, but it's still worth carrying a little cash.
- **Spostamenti:** mostly the subway, Uber/Lyft (better to download the apps before leaving, very handy at night) and walking, averaging 9-12 miles (15-20 km) a day: a serious pace.
- **Budget indicativo:** around €200 (€250-280 to do almost everything).
- **App utili:** offline Google Maps (New York maps downloaded in advance work without a connection, especially useful in the subway), Uber/Lyft for transfers.
- **Consapevolezza sul ritmo:** there are more activities listed than can be done in 6 days. It's normal not to manage everything: better to decide in advance what to leave out, instead of chasing the whole list.
- **From the airport to the centre:** from **JFK** the **AirTrain** (about $8.75, paid on exit at Jamaica or Howard Beach) connects to the E, J/Z or A subway (about $3) or to the LIRR train from Jamaica, faster to Penn Station and Grand Central: 50-75 minutes in all, 24 hours a day · **yellow taxi** from the official rank, flat fare of $70 to Manhattan plus surcharges, tolls and tip, about $90-100 · **Uber/Lyft** from the app zone, often $80-120 · from **Newark (EWR)** AirTrain plus NJ Transit train to Penn Station, about 30 minutes and $16 · never accept rides from people offering them in arrivals.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 (07.09) — Arrival, Times Square
From **JFK** to Manhattan there are three options: a yellow cab at a flat fare, Uber or Lyft, or the **AirTrain** to Jamaica and then the subway or the LIRR train, the cheapest but also the least comfortable with luggage. From **Newark** there's the NJ Transit train to Penn Station. Flights from Italy usually land in the early afternoon, and with the six-hour time difference tiredness comes early: keep the first day light, with nothing booked at a fixed time. *It's worth staying awake until evening, to fix your sleep pattern straight away.*

We arrived, dropped our bags at the Paramount Hotel, at 235 W 46th St, and our first dinner was at **Shake Shack**. Before heading out, it's worth booking **Top of the Rock**, the Rockefeller Center observation deck, for the following days. Then a walk through **Times Square**: at night, with the lights and the billboards, the impact is almost a must on the first evening. We ended the night at Magic Hour Bar.

**In practice:** yellow cab from JFK to Manhattan at a flat fare of about $70, plus tolls, surcharges and tip, 45-75 minutes depending on traffic · AirTrain about $8.75 plus the $3 subway, paid by tapping a contactless card at the turnstiles (OMNY) · the Paramount is a short walk from Times Square, so the first evening is all on foot · Top of the Rock has timed tickets: sunset slots sell out first · in Times Square the costumed characters ask for money for photos.

### Day 2 (08.09) — Harlem, Central Park, Empire State Building
For breakfast you can choose between Le Pain Quotidien, handy for the subway, The Monkey Cup in Harlem, and Junior's, for the cheesecake, which has several locations in the city, including 49th/45th Street and Brooklyn.

We spent the morning in Harlem, at the gospel service at Bethel Gospel Assembly or at **Canaan Baptist Church**: about two hours, no obligation to donate, and you can get up and leave whenever you like. It's a neighborhood experience, far from the organized tours. Mid-morning, the **Harlem walking tour**, about two hours for $275 plus tip, took us through the neighborhood's landmarks, **Apollo Theater** included.

For lunch there are the cheap hot dogs at **Gray's Papaya**, a New York classic, or brunch at Andrew NYC Diner, near Madison Square Garden. In the afternoon we biked around **Central Park**: we took the M1 bus to the front of the Met, for the classic photo on the steps, and from there we rode into the park as far as Strawberry Fields. Dinner at **Kesté**, Neapolitan pizza, and late in the evening we went up the **Empire State Building**.

**In practice:** Harlem gospel services are mostly on Sunday morning and seats for visitors are limited: arrive well ahead, dress modestly and no photos during the service · from Midtown to Harlem by subway about half an hour · bikes in Central Park from hourly rentals or Citi Bike, returned within the time included in the ride · Empire State Building about $45-50 for the 86th floor, more for the 102nd, open until late evening.

### Day 3 (09.09) — Cathedral, MoMA, High Line, The Vessel
Early on we stopped by **Rockefeller Center** on the way to **St. Patrick's Cathedral**. Then **MoMA**, with its main entrance at 11 West 53rd Street: about two hours, giving priority to the fourth and fifth floors, where the most famous works are concentrated. *It's worth downloading the Bloomberg Connects app beforehand, for the map and audio guide, which are only available online.*

For lunch, take your pick of something quick among Shake Shack Midtown East, Five Guys and McDonald's, or **Chelsea Market**. In the early afternoon we walked the **High Line**, about a mile and a half, to **The Vessel**: an hour and a half or two hours in all. Afterward you can go up **Edge** or **Summit**, the Hudson Yards observation decks, or head to Grand Central Station and the Public Library. Dinner was at **Ellen's Stardust Diner**, where the waiters sing.

**In practice:** MoMA about $30 for adults, timed ticket online, best to go in at opening · the High Line is free, open from morning to evening, with entrances all along the route · The Vessel can only be climbed with a timed ticket · Edge and Summit cost about $40-50 and the sunset slot is the most expensive: one observatory at Hudson Yards is enough, since the Empire State and Top of the Rock are already on the plan.

### Day 4 (10.09) — Statue of Liberty, Wall Street, Chinatown, Broadway
In the morning the ferry we'd booked took us to the **Statue of Liberty**, seen from below, and to **Ellis Island**, with its immigration museum. In the early afternoon we came back to Manhattan for lunch. The options: Liberty Bagels, Me La Wa (often with a line) or Uncle Lo in Chinatown, or Prince Street Pizza in SoHo, famous for its American-style pepperoni pizza.

Then **Wall Street**: the Charging Bull, the Stock Exchange, Trinity Church, Zuccotti Park, the Oculus and Ground Zero. We took the subway to **Canal Street** and from there wandered for about an hour through **SoHo**, **Little Italy** and **Chinatown**, passing Pell Street, Doyers Street and Columbus Park, where the Chinese community gathers for board games and tai chi. *Little Italy is by now almost only Mulberry Street, with the famous sign and little else; if you eat there, Lombardi's Pizza is fine but not memorable.*

Sunset is best watched from Time Out Market or Darling Rooftop, with a view of Central Park. In the evening, a **Broadway musical**.

**In practice:** the only official ferry seller is Statue City Cruises, from Battery Park, about $25 with Ellis Island included · pedestal and crown access are limited and must be booked weeks ahead · before boarding there's airport-style security: arrive at least half an hour before your slot · musicals: the most popular shows are booked in advance, for the others there's the TKTS booth in Times Square with same-day discounts · in Chinatown some places take cash only.

### Day 5 (11.09) — Ground Zero, DUMBO, Top of the Rock
On September 11, at **Ground Zero**, there's the commemoration ceremony, if the dates line up; then, if you like, the **9/11 Museum** and **The Oculus**. Lunch with the sandwiches from **Pisillo**.

We spent the afternoon in **DUMBO**, in Brooklyn: the corner of Water Street and Washington Street, the one from the photo with the Manhattan Bridge in the background, the historic **Jane's Carousel**, the **Brooklyn Flea Market** (Sundays only), the bridge arch, **Brooklyn Bridge Park** and the walk back across the **Brooklyn Bridge**. For food there's Vinegar Hill House or the Bluestone Lane coffee shop.

With the time left: the New York Public Library, Fifth Avenue, Macy's, the Plaza, Trump Tower, Rockefeller Center. We watched the sunset from **Top of the Rock**. The last evening started at Virgil's BBQ, continued at 230 Rooftop and ended with a limo ride around Times Square.

**In practice:** the Memorial is free, the 9/11 Museum costs about $36 with a timed ticket · on 11 September the Memorial is reserved for families in the morning for the ceremony and opens to the public later: check that day's hours · for DUMBO take the F train to York Street or the A/C to High Street · walking the Brooklyn Bridge takes 30-40 minutes, best in the direction of Manhattan · book Top of the Rock at sunset days ahead.

### Day 6 (12.09) — Heading home
Check-out and goodbyes.

Flights to Europe from JFK and Newark almost all leave in the late afternoon or evening, so the last day still leaves half a day. Check-out is usually at noon and Midtown hotels keep luggage in storage, so the morning can go to whatever was left off the list: breakfast in a diner, a walk through **Bryant Park** and **Grand Central Terminal**, last purchases on Fifth Avenue.

Leave for the airport with a wide margin: in the afternoon traffic towards JFK can double the time, and with heavy bags a taxi or an app car is easier than the AirTrain. *Security at New York airports can take more than an hour: for an intercontinental flight, better to be at the terminal three hours ahead.* Liquid souvenirs, such as maple syrup or bottles, go in the checked bag.

**In practice:** check-out usually at noon, luggage storage free or for a small tip · from Midtown to JFK by taxi about an hour, more at rush hour, at a flat fare of about $70 plus tolls and tip · alternatively AirTrain plus subway or LIRR · be at the terminal three hours before the flight.

## Live sports: one baseball game and one basketball game
In September you can combine two different sports in the same trip:
- **Baseball**: in September the race to the MLB playoffs is in full swing, with tense games. During this period both the **New York Yankees** (Yankee Stadium, in the Bronx, on the 4 subway line) and the **New York Mets** (Citi Field, in Queens, on the 7 line) play regularly at home. It's a very American experience: hot dogs, beer, the seventh-inning stretch (the sing-along break midway through the game), a ballpark atmosphere unlike a European stadium.
- **Basketball**: the NBA season (Knicks and Nets) only starts in October, so in September the live basketball left is the **New York Liberty**, New York's WNBA team, who play at the **Barclays Center** in Brooklyn (the same arena as the Nets). September is WNBA playoff time. The Liberty, reigning champions since 2024, deliver an electric atmosphere, with tickets usually cheaper than an NBA game and a very involved crowd. A great alternative (or addition) if you don't want to wait for the NBA season.

## What to see / what to do — the highlights
- **Top of the Rock at sunset**: open views over Central Park and the Empire State Building, often preferred by New Yorkers themselves to the Empire State as a viewpoint.
- **Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island**: the story of immigration told up close.
- **DUMBO**: the most photographed view in Brooklyn, looking over the Manhattan Bridge.
- **Broadway**: a live musical, a must.
- **A live game**: baseball (Yankees or Mets) or basketball (New York Liberty), the most genuine way to experience the city as an ordinary New Yorker for an evening.
- **Harlem on Sunday morning**: between the gospel service and the Apollo Theater, the side of New York that's less postcard.
- **High Line and The Vessel**: the former elevated railway turned into a linear park, one of the city's best examples of urban regeneration.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
I slept at the **Paramount Hotel** (now officially "Paramount Times Square – A Generator Hotel"), 235 W 46th St, New York, right in the Theater District, a couple of steps from Times Square.

**What the reviews say:** the strong point is the location, with "location" scores on Booking.com among the highest in the area (around 9-9.3 out of 10). The rooms, however, are small, sometimes tiny even by New York standards (the base "Broadway Petite" rates start at about 140 sq ft with a single bed), with little room for luggage and not-great soundproofing. The lobby and common areas are a plus: lively atmosphere, music, a welcoming bar, staff often described as friendly and helpful. The overall score ranges between 6.6 and 7.1 out of 10 depending on the period. It's a good compromise for anyone who wants to sleep in the heart of the Theater District without spending a fortune, less so for anyone looking for space in the room.

#### Alternatives in the same area (Theater District / Midtown)

**Budget**
- **Row NYC**: 700 8th Ave, a huge hotel with hundreds of simple but functional rooms, often among the lowest prices in the area.
- **Pod Times Square**: 400 W 42nd St, a "pod hotel" formula with compact, basic rooms, good value for the location if you only sleep in the room.

**Mid-range** (the Paramount sits here, toward the lower end of the range precisely because of its small rooms)
- **Novotel New York Times Square**: 226 W 52nd St, rooms bigger than the area's average, a good quality/price compromise.
- **DoubleTree by Hilton New York Times Square West**: 350 W 40th St, a slightly higher standard, convenient if you're arriving at or leaving from Port Authority.

**Classier but not over the top**
- **citizenM New York Times Square**: 218 W 50th St, compact but polished design, very high-tech, lifestyle-hotel prices without straying into luxury.
- **Moxy NYC Times Square**: 485 7th Ave, a Marriott brand with a young spirit, small but well-designed rooms, a very popular rooftop bar.

## Rooftops and evening spots
- **230 Rooftop**: a panoramic terrace with views of the Flatiron Building, the Chrysler Building and the Empire State Building. In winter there are red wool robes and heated transparent igloos. Free entry, no minimum purchase required, open late on Thursdays, cocktails from $9 (non-alcoholic) to $15.
- **Refinery Rooftop**: free entry, but a minimum purchase is required if you book a table; in winter there's a big lit fireplace on the terrace.
- **Lilli' Victoria Establishment** and **Papillon Bistro & Bar**: pubs decorated for the holidays, open late.
- **Blue Note New York**: for a jazz evening in a historic venue.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book Top of the Rock and Broadway musicals** in advance: in high season the best tickets go fast.
- **Download offline Google Maps** before leaving: they come in handy even without a connection for getting around the streets and the subway.
- **Uber/Lyft** are more convenient than taxis for late-night transfers; better to download the apps before departure.
- **Don't try to do everything**: the program is deliberately fuller than what you can manage in 6 days, so it's best to pick your priorities from the start.
- **Games**: book tickets (Yankees/Mets or New York Liberty) a few days ahead, especially for the most in-demand evening games.

## Chiusura personale
What I take home from New York is above all the contrasts between one neighborhood and the next: Sunday morning in Harlem between the gospel service and the Apollo Theater, the Manhattan Bridge seen from DUMBO, a New York Liberty game at the Barclays Center with an electric atmosphere. The most useful piece of advice I gave myself, halfway through the trip, was to stop chasing the whole list: the program was deliberately fuller than what was doable in six days, and that's fine. It means there's a reason to go back.
