# Eastern Sicily in 10 Days: Etna, the Greeks and the Baroque

**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 10 days, 9 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independent, with a rental car
**Categoria:** Europa · Italia · Sicilia · archeologia · vulcani · gastronomia

## Apertura personale
Sicily is systematically undersized at the planning stage: on the map it looks like an island, in reality it's a nation, and the classic "tour of Sicily in a week" turns into six days on interior state roads. The choice that pays off most is the opposite: take **one half** and do it well. This itinerary covers the eastern half (the Greek and Baroque one, dominated by Etna), which is the right choice the first time, with the western half (Palermo, Monreale, Segesta) saved for a second trip. Ten days, two or three bases, and time to stop.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **May-June and September-October**, no debate. July and August are scorching (the interior often exceeds 40 degrees), crowded and expensive. Winter is mild and excellent for art cities and archaeological sites, but the sea is out of the picture and many coastal facilities are closed. April and November are excellent and little visited.
- **Car:** **essential.** The rail network is slow and covers little. Motorways connect Catania and Messina; the rest is state road, and **travel times need to be increased by a third** compared with map estimates.
- **Etna:** the ascent to the summit zone is made by cable car and off-road vehicle, and **above roughly 2,900 meters a guide is mandatory**. INGV publishes bulletins on the activity: **in case of an eruption, access above a certain altitude is closed without notice**. Check before going up, and factor in a plan B.
- **Valley of the Temples:** in summer **it's visited at night or first thing in the morning**. There's no shade, and at noon in July you walk in an oven.
- **Tickets:** Sicilian archaeological sites cost little and there's often a combined ticket that pays off. There are no heavy quotas like at Pompeii, but in high season there's a line at Villa Romana del Casale and the Valley of the Temples.
- **Food:** Sicily has two cuisines, one for each side. In the east fish and pasta prevail: **pasta alla Norma** in Catania, **sea urchins** in Syracuse, swordfish in the Strait. Sweets are a chapter of their own: **cannoli filled to order and never before**, cassata and **granita with a brioche col tuppo for breakfast**, which in summer is the right way to start the day (and it's made with water, sugar and fruit, not with cream).
- **Wine:** Etna Rosso from Nerello Mascalese, Nero d'Avola, Carricante for the whites. Etna wineries are visited **by appointment**.
- **Guidance:** the interior (Enna, Piazza Armerina, the Madonie) is where Sicily is most itself and least touristy, and almost everyone skips it to stay on the coast.
- **From the airport to the centre:** **Catania Fontanarossa (CTA)** is about 5 km from the centre. **Alibus** AMTS to the central station and the centre in 20-30 minutes, €4 (ticket valid 90 minutes, on board, at the airport or in the app), running from about 4:40am to half past midnight · **train** from Catania Aeroporto Fontanarossa station, linked to the terminal by a shuttle, to Catania Centrale in a few minutes · **taxi** at a fixed fare of about €27 to Piazza Duomo or the Central Station (on trial until the end of 2026), best to confirm the price before getting in · Etna Trasporti and Interbus coaches to Taormina and Siracusa · many rental agencies are in outside car parks, reached by their shuttles: the same goes for the return.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Catania
Flight to Catania and car pickup. We settled in the city or on the Etna side. Fontanarossa airport is a few kilometers from the center, and those staying in the city can also leave the car for the next day and take the shuttle bus. In the center, the limited-traffic zones and scarce parking make a car more of a burden than a help: accommodation with parking or a garage is worth it.

The afternoon is for a first walk: **Piazza del Duomo** with the lava-stone elephant fountain, the Liotru, symbol of the city, and **Via Etnea**, which climbs straight toward the volcano. Those with an extra hour can go into the **Benedictine Monastery**, one of the largest monastic complexes in Europe, now home to the university.

The first evening we stopped by the **Pescheria market**, if the hour allows, and had dinner with **pasta alla Norma** (fried eggplant, tomato, ricotta salata): it's the city's dish and takes its name from Bellini's opera, since he was born here.

Catania divides opinion: it's lively and chaotic, built in black lava stone, and less beautiful than Palermo. Take it for what it is, an excellent base, not a destination.

**In practice:** Pescheria open in the morning until lunchtime, closed on Sundays · Benedictine Monastery visited on a paid guided tour · to sleep, agriturismi on the slopes of Etna cost less than the city and are closer to the volcano.

### Day 2 — Etna
It's the day you come east for. **The INGV bulletin must be checked the night before.** Leave Catania early for the **Rifugio Sapienza**, on the south side, at about 1,900 meters: the drive up takes about an hour. Morning is the best time, because clouds tend to rise in the middle of the day and cover the view.

We went up by cable car and off-road vehicle to the summit zone, with the guide mandatory above a certain altitude. You walk on black lapilli that crunch and give way underfoot, with fumaroles coming out of the cracks and the ground warm to the touch where the flows are recent.

**Real layers:** you leave Catania at twenty-five degrees, and at three thousand meters it can be five, with strong wind.

In the afternoon, the **Silvestri craters** at low altitude (free, reachable by car) or an **Etna winery** by appointment: here the vines are often **ungrafted**, because phylloxera never reached this volcanic soil, and there are vines over a hundred years old. The wineries are mostly on the north side, around Castiglione di Sicilia and Randazzo. *When the volcano's activity increases, the summit zone is closed even at a few hours' notice: the plan B of the Silvestri craters and the wineries really matters.*

**In practice:** cable car, off-road vehicle and guide package from about €80-95 per person, higher excursions cost more · boots, windproof jacket and hat even in summer · Rifugio Sapienza to the north-side wineries about an hour's drive.

### Day 3 — Taormina and the Alcantara Gorges
We were in **Taormina** early in the morning, before the tour buses: the Greco-Roman theater overlooking Etna and the sea is one of the most spectacular settings in the Mediterranean. In high season the town is packed and has prices from another region. From Catania it takes about an hour; the car is left in the multi-storey car parks at the edge of the center, from where you go up by shuttle or on foot. After the theater, walk along **Corso Umberto** to Piazza IX Aprile, the terrace over the sea.

In the afternoon we went to the **Alcantara Gorges**, the columnar basalt canyon carved by the river into a lava flow: you go down and walk in the water, icy even in August. The gorges are about half an hour from Taormina. There are several entrances, paid and with different facilities, and at some you can rent waterproof boots. *The water level changes with the season and the rain: in spring the current can be strong, so ask at the entrance before going down into the river.* Those who prefer the sea go down to **Isola Bella** instead, the islet below Taormina linked to the shore by a strip of sand.

**In practice:** Taormina's Ancient Theater about €14, open all day, with evening shows in summer · Alcantara Gorges with paid entrances, water shoes or boots · to eat, in Taormina prices drop as you move away from the Corso.

### Day 4 — Syracuse and Ortigia
We transferred to **Syracuse** and settled in **Ortigia**, the island that is the historic center. From Taormina or Catania it takes an hour or a little more on the motorway. Ortigia is almost entirely a limited-traffic zone: the car is left in the multi-storey car park at the entrance to the island, and from there you go on foot.

We saw the **Neapolis Archaeological Park**, with the Greek theater and the Ear of Dionysius, and then Ortigia on foot: the **Cathedral built inside a Doric temple**, with fifth-century BC columns still built into the walls. You enter a Baroque church and touch Greek columns. The Neapolis is best seen in the morning, because the park is in the open with little shade. From May to July the Greek theater hosts the INDA classical plays, and some areas may be closed while the stage is set up. In Ortigia, besides the Cathedral, you pass the **Fonte Aretusa**, the freshwater spring with papyrus plants, a few meters from the sea.

In the evening, the Ortigia market and dinner with **sea urchins**, which here are the specialty. *The market stalls work mostly in the morning: anyone who wants to see them in full should come back the next day, before leaving.*

**In practice:** Neapolis with a full ticket or a combined one with the Paolo Orsi Museum, prices to be checked on the park's website · Cathedral with a ticket of about €2-3 · Talete car park at the entrance to Ortigia.

### Day 5 — Noto, Modica, Scicli
It's the day of the **Baroque of the Val di Noto**, a UNESCO site: eight cities rebuilt after the earthquake of 1693, all in the same style and at the same moment.

**Noto** with the cathedral staircase and the stone that turns golden at sunset, **Modica** with chocolate worked cold by a technique that came from the Spanish and before them from the Aztecs, **Scicli** with its facades and squares.

The order matters for the light: start with Scicli and Modica in the morning and finish in Noto in the late afternoon, when the stone takes on colour. In Noto, **Corso Vittorio Emanuele** is the backbone of the town, with the cathedral of San Nicolò and the balconies of **Palazzo Nicolaci**, held up by corbels shaped like horses and grotesque figures. In Modica the staircase of the **Cathedral of San Giorgio** towers over the lower town; the chocolate is tasted in the confectioners along the corso. In Scicli, **Via Francesco Mormino Penna** gathers Baroque churches and palaces within a few hundred meters.

It's a day to do by car and without hurry: the cities are twenty kilometers from one another.

**In practice:** Syracuse-Noto about 40 minutes, Noto-Modica about 45, Modica-Scicli about 15 · in the old towns you park at the edges, for a fee · in May Noto hosts the Infiorata, with streets covered in petals and big crowds.

### Day 6 — Ragusa Ibla and Vendicari
In the morning we were at **Ragusa Ibla**, the lower town, the most beautiful piece of Sicilian Baroque: you go down from the upper town along the staircases and arrive at a settlement wedged onto a rock spur. The descent starts from the church of **Santa Maria delle Scale**, with a view over all of Ibla. Down below, the **Cathedral of San Giorgio** rises at the top of a staircase in the main square; at the far end of the town is the **Giardino Ibleo**, with palm trees and a viewpoint over the valley. *The walk back up is long and tiring: those who don't want to repeat it take the city bus or leave the car below, in Ibla.*

The afternoon at the **Vendicari Reserve**, with the salt pans, the abandoned tonnara and the beaches: it's one of the best-preserved areas of the coast. In spring and autumn there are flamingos. From Ragusa it takes about an hour. The reserve has several entrances: from the main one you reach the birdwatching hides and the tonnara, while **Calamosche** beach, one of the finest in the area, is reached from a northern entrance with a fifteen-minute walk. There are no bars and no shade in the reserve: bring water and a hat.

**In practice:** paid parking at the Vendicari entrances in summer · binoculars are useful for the flamingos, which stay in the marshes far from the paths · for the night, the Val di Noto or back to Syracuse.

### Day 7 — Toward the Interior: Piazza Armerina and Enna
It's the day almost everyone skips, and the one that changes your perception of the island.

At Piazza Armerina is the **Villa Romana del Casale**, a UNESCO site: **3,500 square meters of Roman mosaics from the 4th century**, the most extensive and best preserved in the world, protected under a covering and walked on raised walkways.

From the south-eastern coast you climb inland in about an hour and a half to two hours, on main roads through wheat-covered hills. Reach the Villa in the morning: under the covering it gets hot in summer, and groups arrive around midday. The best-known mosaics are the **Great Hunt**, a corridor more than sixty meters long with animals captured in Africa, and the **bikini girls** in the Room of the Ten Maidens. You need a couple of hours.

Then we went up to **Enna**, at the exact geographic center of Sicily, at 900 meters, with a view that on clear days reaches Etna. At the top of the town is the **Castello di Lombardia**, and just beyond it the **Rocca di Cerere**, the spur where the goddess's temple stood. *Enna is often in fog even when the plain is sunny: the view is usually best in the late afternoon.*

**In practice:** Villa Romana del Casale about €14, open all day, with evening openings in summer · Piazza Armerina-Enna about 40 minutes · to sleep, Enna or an agriturismo in the interior.

### Day 8 — Agrigento
We transferred to **Agrigento** for the **Valley of the Temples**. From Enna it's about an hour and a half's drive. The Valley has two main entrances, one to the east near the Temple of Juno and one to the west; those with a car can enter from one side and get back by the internal shuttle or a taxi.

**In summer it's visited at night or first thing in the morning**: over a vast area there is no shade, and at noon in July it's an oven. The **Temple of Concordia** is among the best-preserved Doric temples in the world, and it owes this to the fact that in the 6th century it was converted into a church: that is what saved it.

Not to be skipped: the **Archaeological Museum** with the telamon and the **Kolymbethra Garden**, a citrus garden inside the archaeological area, run by the FAI, where almost no one goes. The museum is outside the Valley, a few minutes' drive away, and holds one of the stone giants, almost eight meters tall, that supported the Temple of Zeus. *The garden has a separate ticket, and you can only enter after paying for the Valley.*

**In practice:** Valley of the Temples open from morning to evening, with evening openings in summer · combined Valley and Kolymbethra ticket around €23 · Archaeological Museum with its own ticket · to sleep, one night in Agrigento is enough.

### Day 9 — Scala dei Turchi and Return Toward Catania
In the morning we went to the **Scala dei Turchi**, the white marl cliff, **with the warning that access has been regulated and in certain periods forbidden** because of erosion: before going it's worth checking what the situation is. It is about twenty minutes from Agrigento, in the municipality of Realmonte. In recent years the path on the cliff has been opened with limited numbers and a fee, with tickets sold on site; when it is closed, the cliff is viewed from the beach below. *The marl is crumbly and slippery when wet: early morning light is the best, and also the least crowded time.*

Then the return toward Catania, with a stop at **Caltagirone** and its staircase of 142 ceramic steps, if time allows. From Agrigento to Caltagirone it's about two hours. Every step of the **Staircase of Santa Maria del Monte** has its riser decorated with different majolica tiles, and ceramicists' workshops line the climb. From Caltagirone to Catania it's another hour and a quarter or so.

**In practice:** Scala dei Turchi with limited, paid access when open (in 2026 about €6, 20-minute slots), rules to be checked on the Comune di Realmonte website · Agrigento-Catania via Caltagirone about 3 and a half hours without stops · for the last night, Catania or the Etna side, near the airport.

### Day 10 — Last Morning and Departure
A last granita with brioche, last purchases (Bronte pistachio, Modica chocolate, Etna wine) and a flight from Catania. Granita is taken at the bar in the morning, almond, pistachio or mulberry depending on the season, and the brioche col tuppo is dipped into it. For shopping, the streets around Via Etnea and the market sell pistachio as cream and chopped, and wine shops ship wine to Italy.

The return to the airport needs a margin: returning the car to the rental car parks, checking the fuel and the shuttle to the terminal take longer than expected. *In hand luggage, spreads and wine follow the liquids rules: better to put them in the hold.* Those with an afternoon flight can take a last walk through the Pescheria, at its best in the morning.

**In practice:** return the car at least two hours before the flight · granita at a morning bar, better if it hasn't been sitting ready for hours · Bronte pistachio PDO to be recognised by the label, beware of prices that are too low.

## What to see / what to do
- **Choose one half.** Doing all of Sicily in ten days means spending the vacation in the car.
- **Etna, checking the INGV bulletin**, with real layers.
- **Taormina before the tour buses**, or not at all.
- **The Cathedral of Syracuse**, where you touch Greek columns inside a Baroque church.
- **The Val di Noto without hurry**: there are twenty kilometers between one city and the next.
- **The interior: Piazza Armerina and Enna**, which almost everyone skips.
- **The Valley of the Temples at night or at dawn.**
- **The Kolymbethra Garden**, inside the Agrigento archaeological area, where almost no one goes.
- **An Etna winery by appointment**, for the ungrafted vines.

## Possible extensions
- **Western Sicily (+7 days):** Palermo, Monreale (six thousand square meters of gold mosaics, Arab craftsmanship and Norman structure), Segesta, Selinunte, the Trapani salt pans and the Egadi Islands. It's the second trip.
- **Aeolian Islands (+4-5 days):** from Milazzo, with Stromboli in permanent eruption and the smaller islands. To be done in June or September.
- **Pantelleria (+5 days):** the anti-island, with no sand beaches, with dammusi and vines grown in pits dug into the ground.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
- **Catania or the Etna side** for the first nights: the agriturismi on the slopes of Etna have better value than the city.
- **Ortigia** in Syracuse: it's the historic center, on an island, and everything is explored on foot.
- **Val di Noto** or back to Syracuse: distances are short.
- **Agrigento** only for the night tied to the Valley of the Temples.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
- **Catania:** pasta alla Norma, the Pescheria market, swordfish.
- **Syracuse:** sea urchins, fish and the Ortigia market in the morning.
- **Val di Noto:** Modica chocolate worked cold, which stays grainy because the sugar doesn't melt.
- **Everywhere:** cannoli **filled to order** (if they're already full in the window, the shell is soggy), cassata, arancini (masculine in Catania, feminine in Palermo: it's a real cultural fault line) and **granita with a brioche col tuppo** for breakfast.
- **To drink:** Etna Rosso, Nero d'Avola, Carricante.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't try to do the whole island.**
- **Increase by a third** the travel times estimated by the maps on the interior state roads.
- **Check the INGV bulletin** before going up Etna, and have a plan B.
- **Don't do Agrigento at noon in summer.**
- **Check the access status of the Scala dei Turchi**, which has been regulated and at times forbidden.
- **Don't skip the interior** to stay only on the coast.
- **Book the Etna wineries**, which are not shops.
- **Don't go in August**, the worst month on every parameter.

## Chiusura personale
My one piece of advice: don't try to do the whole island. Better to take one half, the east with Etna, and do it calmly, without skipping the interior. And not in August.
