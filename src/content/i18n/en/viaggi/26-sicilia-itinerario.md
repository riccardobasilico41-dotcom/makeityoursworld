# Eastern Sicily in 10 Days: Etna, the Greeks and the Baroque

## Apertura personale
Sicily is systematically undersized at the planning stage: on the map it looks like an island, in reality it's a nation, and the classic "tour of Sicily in a week" turns into six days on interior state roads. The choice that pays off most is the opposite: take **one half** and do it well. This itinerary covers the eastern half (the Greek and Baroque one, dominated by Etna), which is the right choice the first time, with the western half (Palermo, Monreale, Segesta) saved for a second trip. Ten days, two or three bases, and time to stop.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **May-June and September-October**, no debate. July and August are scorching (the interior often exceeds 40 degrees), crowded and expensive. Winter is mild and excellent for art cities and archaeological sites, but the sea is out of the picture and many coastal facilities are closed. April and November are excellent and little visited.
- **Car:** **essential.** The rail network is slow and covers little. Motorways connect Catania and Messina; the rest is state road, and **travel times need to be increased by a third** compared with map estimates.
- **Etna:** the ascent to the summit zone is made by cable car and off-road vehicle, and **above roughly 2,900 meters a guide is mandatory**. INGV publishes bulletins on the activity: **in case of an eruption, access above a certain altitude is closed without notice**. Check before going up, and factor in a plan B.
- **Valley of the Temples:** in summer **it's visited at night or first thing in the morning**. There's no shade, and at noon in July you walk in an oven.
- **Tickets:** Sicilian archaeological sites cost little and there's often a combined ticket that pays off. There are no heavy quotas like at Pompeii, but in high season there's a line at Villa Romana del Casale and the Valley of the Temples.
- **Food:** Sicily has two cuisines, one for each side. In the east fish and pasta prevail: **pasta alla Norma** in Catania, **sea urchins** in Syracuse, swordfish in the Strait. Sweets are a chapter of their own: **cannoli filled to order and never before**, cassata and **granita with a brioche col tuppo for breakfast**, which in summer is the right way to start the day (and it's made with water, sugar and fruit, not with cream).
- **Wine:** Etna Rosso from Nerello Mascalese, Nero d'Avola, Carricante for the whites. Etna wineries are visited **by appointment**.
- **Guidance:** the interior (Enna, Piazza Armerina, the Madonie) is where Sicily is most itself and least touristy, and almost everyone skips it to stay on the coast.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Catania
Flight to Catania and car pickup. We settled in the city or on the Etna side.

The first evening we stopped by the **Pescheria market**, if the hour allows, and had dinner with **pasta alla Norma** (fried eggplant, tomato, ricotta salata): it's the city's dish and takes its name from Bellini's opera, since he was born here.

Catania divides opinion: it's lively and chaotic, built in black lava stone, and less beautiful than Palermo. Take it for what it is, an excellent base, not a destination.

### Day 2 — Etna
It's the day you come east for. **The INGV bulletin must be checked the night before.**

We went up by cable car and off-road vehicle to the summit zone, with the guide mandatory above a certain altitude. You walk on black lapilli that crunch and give way underfoot, with fumaroles coming out of the cracks and the ground warm to the touch where the flows are recent.

**Real layers:** you leave Catania at twenty-five degrees, and at three thousand meters it can be five, with strong wind.

In the afternoon, the **Silvestri craters** at low altitude (free, reachable by car) or an **Etna winery** by appointment: here the vines are often **ungrafted**, because phylloxera never reached this volcanic soil, and there are vines over a hundred years old.

### Day 3 — Taormina and the Alcantara Gorges
We were in **Taormina** early in the morning, before the tour buses: the Greco-Roman theater overlooking Etna and the sea is one of the most spectacular settings in the Mediterranean. In high season the town is packed and has prices from another region.

In the afternoon we went to the **Alcantara Gorges**, the columnar basalt canyon carved by the river into a lava flow: you go down and walk in the water, icy even in August.

### Day 4 — Syracuse and Ortigia
We transferred to **Syracuse** and settled in **Ortigia**, the island that is the historic center.

We saw the **Neapolis Archaeological Park**, with the Greek theater and the Ear of Dionysius, and then Ortigia on foot: the **Cathedral built inside a Doric temple**, with fifth-century BC columns still built into the walls. You enter a Baroque church and touch Greek columns.

In the evening, the Ortigia market and dinner with **sea urchins**, which here are the specialty.

### Day 5 — Noto, Modica, Scicli
It's the day of the **Baroque of the Val di Noto**, a UNESCO site: eight cities rebuilt after the earthquake of 1693, all in the same style and at the same moment.

**Noto** with the cathedral staircase and the stone that turns golden at sunset, **Modica** with chocolate worked cold by a technique that came from the Spanish and before them from the Aztecs, **Scicli** with its facades and squares.

It's a day to do by car and without hurry: the cities are twenty kilometers from one another.

### Day 6 — Ragusa Ibla and Vendicari
In the morning we were at **Ragusa Ibla**, the lower town, the most beautiful piece of Sicilian Baroque: you go down from the upper town along the staircases and arrive at a settlement wedged onto a rock spur.

The afternoon at the **Vendicari Reserve**, with the salt pans, the abandoned tonnara and the beaches: it's one of the best-preserved areas of the coast. In spring and autumn there are flamingos.

### Day 7 — Toward the Interior: Piazza Armerina and Enna
It's the day almost everyone skips, and the one that changes your perception of the island.

At Piazza Armerina is the **Villa Romana del Casale**, a UNESCO site: **3,500 square meters of Roman mosaics from the 4th century**, the most extensive and best preserved in the world, protected under a covering and walked on raised walkways.

Then we went up to **Enna**, at the exact geographic center of Sicily, at 900 meters, with a view that on clear days reaches Etna.

### Day 8 — Agrigento
We transferred to **Agrigento** for the **Valley of the Temples**.

**In summer it's visited at night or first thing in the morning**: over a vast area there is no shade, and at noon in July it's an oven. The **Temple of Concordia** is among the best-preserved Doric temples in the world, and it owes this to the fact that in the 6th century it was converted into a church: that is what saved it.

Not to be skipped: the **Archaeological Museum** with the telamon and the **Kolymbethra Garden**, a citrus garden inside the archaeological area, run by the FAI, where almost no one goes.

### Day 9 — Scala dei Turchi and Return Toward Catania
In the morning we went to the **Scala dei Turchi**, the white marl cliff, **with the warning that access has been regulated and in certain periods forbidden** because of erosion: before going it's worth checking what the situation is.

Then the return toward Catania, with a stop at **Caltagirone** and its staircase of 142 ceramic steps, if time allows.

### Day 10 — Last Morning and Departure
A last granita with brioche, last purchases (Bronte pistachio, Modica chocolate, Etna wine) and a flight from Catania.

## What to see / what to do
- **Choose one half.** Doing all of Sicily in ten days means spending the vacation in the car.
- **Etna, checking the INGV bulletin**, with real layers.
- **Taormina before the tour buses**, or not at all.
- **The Cathedral of Syracuse**, where you touch Greek columns inside a Baroque church.
- **The Val di Noto without hurry**: there are twenty kilometers between one city and the next.
- **The interior: Piazza Armerina and Enna**, which almost everyone skips.
- **The Valley of the Temples at night or at dawn.**
- **The Kolymbethra Garden**, inside the Agrigento archaeological area, where almost no one goes.
- **An Etna winery by appointment**, for the ungrafted vines.

## Possible extensions
- **Western Sicily (+7 days):** Palermo, Monreale (six thousand square meters of gold mosaics, Arab craftsmanship and Norman structure), Segesta, Selinunte, the Trapani salt pans and the Egadi Islands. It's the second trip.
- **Aeolian Islands (+4-5 days):** from Milazzo, with Stromboli in permanent eruption and the smaller islands. To be done in June or September.
- **Pantelleria (+5 days):** the anti-island, with no sand beaches, with dammusi and vines grown in pits dug into the ground.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
- **Catania or the Etna side** for the first nights: the agriturismi on the slopes of Etna have better value than the city.
- **Ortigia** in Syracuse: it's the historic center, on an island, and everything is explored on foot.
- **Val di Noto** or back to Syracuse: distances are short.
- **Agrigento** only for the night tied to the Valley of the Temples.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
- **Catania:** pasta alla Norma, the Pescheria market, swordfish.
- **Syracuse:** sea urchins, fish and the Ortigia market in the morning.
- **Val di Noto:** Modica chocolate worked cold, which stays grainy because the sugar doesn't melt.
- **Everywhere:** cannoli **filled to order** (if they're already full in the window, the shell is soggy), cassata, arancini (masculine in Catania, feminine in Palermo: it's a real cultural fault line) and **granita with a brioche col tuppo** for breakfast.
- **To drink:** Etna Rosso, Nero d'Avola, Carricante.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't try to do the whole island.**
- **Increase by a third** the travel times estimated by the maps on the interior state roads.
- **Check the INGV bulletin** before going up Etna, and have a plan B.
- **Don't do Agrigento at noon in summer.**
- **Check the access status of the Scala dei Turchi**, which has been regulated and at times forbidden.
- **Don't skip the interior** to stay only on the coast.
- **Book the Etna wineries**, which are not shops.
- **Don't go in August**, the worst month on every parameter.

## Chiusura personale
What stays with you from eastern Sicily is not a monument but an overlap. You enter the Cathedral of Syracuse, which is a Baroque church with an eighteenth-century facade, and inside the walls are the Doric columns of a fifth-century BC temple: not reconstructed, not evoked; built in, and you can touch them with your hand. In between lie twenty-five hundred years in which that building has been a Greek temple, a Byzantine church, a mosque, a church again, and has never stopped being used. This is Sicily: not a place conquered by one culture after another, but a place that has kept them all on itself. And then there's Etna, which does the same thing in geological terms: it covers, it buries, and people come back to plant vines on top because that soil is the best there is.
