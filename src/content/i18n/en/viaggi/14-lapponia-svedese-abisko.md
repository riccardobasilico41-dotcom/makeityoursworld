# Swedish Lapland: New Year under the aurora between Kiruna and Abisko

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (itinerary, prices, overnight stays and anecdotes are ready; the closing is a draft to reread; photos still missing)
**Periodo del viaggio:** December 30 - January 5 (New Year)
**Durata:** 7 days
**Compagni di viaggio:** my own trip, later repeated almost identically by my friend Andrea, who did it again at a later time
**Categoria:** Europa · Svezia · Lapponia · aurora boreale · inverno estremo

---

## Apertura personale
I wanted the most different New Year possible from the ones I'd had up to then: no big dinner, no crowded cities, just darkness, real cold and a genuine chance of seeing the northern lights. Swedish Lapland, between Kiruna and Abisko, guarantees exactly that, at the price of a decidedly more expensive New Year than usual, something I only really understood later, when I started looking into how much it would cost to do it at a different time.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** I left at New Year (December 30 - January 5), the worst period for prices. The alternative is in the practical tips below.
- **Documents/visa:** none, Sweden is in the Schengen area.
- **Estimated budget:** about €1,500 per person for a basic budget, €1,800 for a more comfortable level, with flights, accommodation, local transport, excursions and meals included. These are the figures from the agency quote, not yet compared with actual spending on site.
- **How I got around:** Milan-Stockholm flight, domestic flight Stockholm-Kiruna, scenic train Kiruna-Abisko and back, same domestic and international flights on the way home.
- **Weather:** extreme cold, well below zero for the whole stay. The thermometer hit -25/-26 °C. It's a dry cold: you feel it, sure, but if you're very well wrapped up it doesn't really get into your bones. Your nose, though, is another story. With this cold some disruption should be expected: there is a tip about trains below.
- **Logistics base:** two nights in Stockholm (outbound and return, transit only), one night in Kiruna on the way out, two nights in Abisko, one night in Kiruna before heading home.
- **From the airport to the centre:** at **Stockholm Arlanda (ARN)** the **Arlanda Express** reaches Central Station in 18 minutes, about 340-350 SEK (online or in the app, cheaper if bought in advance) · **Flygbussarna** bus to the City Terminal in about 45 minutes, about 120-130 SEK · **taxi** at a fixed price to the centre, about 600-700 SEK: fares are unregulated in Sweden, so check the price on the yellow sticker on the window before getting in · at **Kiruna (KRN)** the bus timed to arrivals takes about 20 minutes to the centre, about 100-110 SEK; a taxi costs about 300-400 SEK.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Milan → Stockholm
I took the evening flight from Milan and landed in Stockholm late at night. I slept at the **Radisson Blu Royal Viking**, convenient for a single transit night before the flight to Kiruna the next day.

The Royal Viking is next to Stockholm Central Station, so from Arlanda airport the simplest option is the Arlanda Express, the fast train that reaches the centre in about twenty minutes; alternatively there are regional trains and Flygbussarna buses, slower but cheaper. The next day you go back to the airport on the same train: for domestic flights to the north, be at Arlanda an hour and a half before. *At the end of December flights to Kiruna are few and full: if the flight from Milan lands late, the night in Stockholm avoids missing the connection.*

**In practice:** Arlanda Express Arlanda-Stockholm Central about 20 minutes, ticket online or with a contactless card at the gates · in Sweden you pay by card almost everywhere, cash is rarely needed · snow clothing already in your hand luggage, you need it as soon as you land in Kiruna.

### Day 2 — Stockholm → Kiruna (New Year)
In the morning I flew to Kiruna. In the afternoon I took my first walk around the city: the famous **red wooden church** and the center, which in the coming years will be dismantled and rebuilt little by little a few kilometers away because of the expansion of the LKAB iron mine. It is one of the most curious things about Kiruna: a city that moves piece by piece. I spent New Year in town, at the pub, with the cold making itself felt right away. I slept at the **STF Malmfältens Folkhögskola**.

Visitors today will find the church somewhere else: in August 2025 it was moved in one piece, on trailers, about five kilometres east, between the cemetery and the new centre. The relocation of the rest of the town will go on for years, and a walk between the old and new centres shows well how it works. From the airport to the centre there is a bus timed to flight arrivals, about twenty minutes, or a taxi. *Around New Year the sun does not rise in Kiruna: for a few weeks there are only a few hours of blue light in the middle of the day, and the walk around town has to fit into that window.*

**In practice:** airport-centre bus with departures timed to flights, about 20 minutes and about 100-110 SEK, timetable and operator to double-check · Kiruna church free to enter, opening times vary · at New Year restaurants have reduced hours or close, book dinner.

### Day 3 — Kiruna → Abisko
I traveled on the scenic Kiruna-Abisko train: a good hour through a landscape that gets gradually barer and more Arctic. In Abisko I settled into the **STF Abisko Turiststation**, the historic mountain station inside the national park, where the **Aurora Sky Station** chairlift starts. The train is the one on the line to Narvik, run by SJ: Abisko has two stops, Abisko Östra for the village and Abisko Turiststation in front of the lodge, which is the right one. In daylight you can take a short walk to the canyon of the Abiskojåkka river, a few minutes from the station, with frozen Lake Torneträsk in the background.

I devoted the first evening to exactly that, and it was wow: an experience that's hard to put into words. With about 15-20 minutes of chairlift (open until 1:00 a.m. in winter) you climb Mount Nuolja (900 m), up to a station with a restaurant, a bar and an observation tower. It is probably the best place in the world to see the aurora, thanks to the dry microclimate of the area: the famous "blue hole" above Abisko, where the surrounding mountains deflect clouds more often than in the rest of the region. The chairlift alone costs 350-450 SEK (roughly €35-40) per person. There is also a package with an overnight stay up high, dinner, breakfast, thermal suit and sauna for about 2,990 SEK (€270-310), available only on Fridays by reservation. *Today the evening ascent is sold as the Night Visit, with a guide and warm clothing included, from 8:30 p.m. to 1:00 a.m. and with few places: it must be booked, and if wind stops the chairlift the ticket is not refunded.*

**In practice:** Kiruna-Abisko train with SJ, about an hour and a quarter, tickets on sj.se · Aurora Sky Station Night Visit 2026-27 from late October to late March, price on the STF website · up top the wind makes the cold harsher: layers, balaclava, double gloves.

### Day 4 — Abisko: snowmobile and huskies under the aurora
A full day, with the two most adrenaline-filled activities of the trip. Between morning and afternoon there is the **snowmobile on the frozen lake** of the national park. In the guided version you sit in a sled pulled by a snowmobile driven by a guide (about 2 hours, €100-110 per person); in self-drive you drive your own vehicle behind the guide at the head of the group (half a day, €150-250). Self-drive requires a driving licence. Operators provide overalls, boots, gloves and helmet: wear thermal layers underneath, because on the open lake the wind is strong and the chill multiplies with speed.

In the evening I did **husky sledding with aurora hunting**, with pickup directly at the STF Abisko Turiststation (it is best to arrive 45 minutes before departure). It is an hour or two of actual driving, in absolute silence and with the dogs' breath, with the sky that, weather permitting, lights up overhead. This evening version costs €240 per person. I slept again at the STF Abisko Turiststation. *The aurora is not guaranteed: check the forecasts and the sky, and if you have several evenings, put the most expensive outings on the night with the best weather.*

**In practice:** snowmobile and huskies to book before you leave, at New Year places run out · licence needed for self-drive · keep phone and camera batteries warm in a pocket, in the cold they drain in minutes.

### Day 5 — Abisko → Kiruna
During the day I took the return train to Kiruna. I slept at the **Elite Hotel Frost - Hotel & Spa**, a clear change of pace from the more basic nights at the mountain station: sauna and relaxation after two intense days in the cold.

Trains between Abisko and Kiruna are few each day, so check the timetable the evening before and, if there is time, use the morning for a last walk in the park in the Arctic midday light. *In extreme cold a train may be cancelled: in that case there is the local bus, on a different timetable, or a taxi, which costs more on those days.* In Kiruna, if you still have energy, the afternoon is free for the new centre or to plan the LKAB mine tour for another time.

**In practice:** Abisko-Kiruna train about an hour and a quarter, local bus as an alternative · LKAB mine tour only on some days of the week and with online booking · ICEHOTEL in Jukkasjärvi about twenty minutes from Kiruna for those with half a day free.

### Day 6 — Kiruna → Stockholm
The afternoon flight (4:40 p.m.) took me back to Stockholm. A free last evening in the city before heading home, again at the **Radisson Blu Royal Viking**.

With the flight in the afternoon, the morning in Kiruna is free: a walk in the new centre or a stop at the ICEHOTEL in Jukkasjärvi, with a day ticket for non-guests. From the hotel to the airport take the flight bus or a taxi, allowing a margin for snow. In Stockholm the evening can be spent in **Gamla Stan**, the old town, about twenty minutes' walk from Central Station. *In early January it gets dark early in Stockholm, in the early afternoon: the walk happens under the evening lights.*

**In practice:** ICEHOTEL day ticket about 349 SEK · from Arlanda airport to the centre again with the Arlanda Express · in Stockholm, subway and buses with a contactless card.

### Day 7 — Return
Afternoon flight (5:35 p.m.) from Stockholm to Milan, landing in the evening.

The free morning is for a museum or a last walk: the Vasa, the museum of the seventeenth-century warship recovered from the harbour, is on the island of Djurgården, reachable by tram or on foot from the centre. The visit takes about an hour and a half; if you prefer to stay near the station, the city hall, Stadshuset, on the shore of Lake Mälaren, is a few minutes away. For the flight, go back to Arlanda by train, at least two hours ahead. *If you bought heavy cold-weather gear, leave room in your suitcase, or wear it on the flight.*

**In practice:** Arlanda Express about 20 minutes from Central Station · Vasamuseet paid, shorter hours in winter · arrive at Arlanda about two hours before international flights.

## What to see / what to do
- **Aurora Sky Station:** chairlift up Mount Nuolja (15-20 min, open until 1:00 a.m.) and dinner up high, at the spot with the best chances of aurora in the whole region. Chairlift only €35-40, package with overnight stay and dinner about €270-310 (Fridays only).
- **Snowmobiling on the frozen lake:** the most adrenaline-filled activity of the stay in Abisko. Guided version (pulled sled, 2 hours) €100-110; self-drive (half a day, driving license required) €150-250.
- **Husky sledding with aurora hunting:** the best evening of the trip, between the dogs and the sky. Evening version with aurora hunting €240; shorter daytime versions ("panorama husky hike") from €70.
- **Kiruna's wooden church** and the town center, which will change face in the coming years because of the relocation caused by the mine. Free to visit.
- **Guided tour of the LKAB mine** (not done on this trip): 3 hours, up to 540 m underground in the largest underground iron deposit in the world, about 590 SEK (€52) per person. It is open only some days of the week (Tue/Thu/Fri/Sat) and online booking is mandatory.
- **Worth considering for a next trip (not done this time):** the ICEHOTEL in Jukkasjärvi, about twenty minutes from Kiruna, the most famous ice hotel in the world, rebuilt every winter. Day admission 349 SEK (€31), overnight in an ice room from 3,995 SEK (roughly €350) per night for 2 people, artist suite from 4,995 SEK (roughly €440); those who sleep there have free, unlimited access to the structure for the whole stay.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
**Stockholm — Radisson Blu Royal Viking** (outbound and return, transit only)
Two separate nights, one in each direction, just to break up the trip between the international flight and the domestic one to Kiruna. A reliable international chain, especially convenient for those arriving or leaving on evening flights.

**Kiruna — STF Malmfältens Folkhögskola** (outbound) and **Elite Hotel Frost - Hotel & Spa** (return)
On the way out, a simple, basic place, in the "STF" style (Svenska Turistföreningen, the Swedish hiking association that runs many properties in the area). On the way back, a clear step up in quality with the Elite Hotel Frost: an in-house spa, great for recovering after the coldest and most intense days in Abisko.

**Abisko — STF Abisko Turiststation**
The most iconic place of the trip: the historic mountain station inside Abisko National Park, where the Aurora Sky Station chairlift starts. Basic, but with an atmosphere all its own, in the middle of the park.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
For meals the estimated budget was €10-15 a day for breakfasts (often included at the STF properties) and €30-50 per meal for lunches and dinners.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't go at New Year if you can avoid it.** It is the most expensive time of year to fly and sleep in this area. I did it because I wanted that particular New Year, but if the goal is just to see the northern lights in Swedish Lapland, it is worth knowing beforehand.
- **February-March is the right time to save a lot**: the chances of aurora stay high and flights and accommodation cost much less than in late December.
- **In extreme cold, trains are sometimes canceled.** It doesn't happen often, but it can. When it does, the local taxis (the only alternative left) tend to raise prices a bit, because at that moment there aren't many other ways to get around. Better to keep an extra margin in the transport budget.
- Dressing in heavy layers goes without saying, but it is worth repeating: temperatures stay well below zero all week.

## Chiusura personale
I would recommend the New Year version only to those looking for exactly that (the charm of greeting the new year in absolute darkness and freezing cold), because on the wallet front the low-cost February-March version remains the most sensible choice, with better chances of aurora and less spending. Between the two nights, the one I take home as my best memory is the first at the STF Abisko Turiststation: simpler than the Elite Hotel Frost, but a short walk from the Aurora Sky Station, where I saw the aurora move across the sky for the first time.
