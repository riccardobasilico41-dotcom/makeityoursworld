# London and the Cotswolds in 8 Days: the City, Oxford, the Stone Villages and Bath

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (places Rick visited, put together into one practical route; practical information checked in October-November 2026, to recheck before departure; first-person memories only those given by Rick, none invented)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 8 days, 7 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** itinerary designed for a couple or a small group, in London by Tube and on foot, then by train to Oxford and by rental car through the Cotswolds and on to Bath
**Categoria:** Europa · Inghilterra · Londra · Cotswolds · Oxford · Bath · borghi · musei · treno

## Apertura personale
This itinerary is rooted in places Rick has personally visited, put in order for anyone who wants London and a piece of the English countryside in the same trip.

The first four days are in **London**, with no car: the Tube, buses and many hours on foot along the Thames. Then you take the train at Paddington and get off at **Oxford**, the city of colleges. There you pick up the car and drive into the **Cotswolds**, the hills of honey-coloured stone villages. The last stretch takes you to **Bath**, with its Roman baths, where the train takes you back to London.

You only need the car for three days. In the city it would be a burden, between traffic, parking and the Congestion Charge. In the countryside it's the easiest option: the villages are close together, but buses are few. You drive on the left, on narrow roads between hedges. If you don't feel up to it, you can do the Cotswolds by train to Moreton-in-Marsh and an organised tour of the villages.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** Late spring or early autumn, with long days and fewer crowds than in August
- **Documents:** the United Kingdom is not in the European Union. Since April 2, 2025, Italian citizens need an **ETA**, the electronic travel authorisation: you apply on gov.uk or with the official UK ETA app, it costs £20 (2026) and is valid for 2 years, or until your passport expires. You need a **passport**: the ID card is accepted only in special cases.
- **Currency and payments:** pound sterling. Cards and contactless work almost everywhere.
- **Getting around London:** Tube and buses, paying with a contactless card or an Oyster card, always the same card, which has a daily spending cap.
- **Trains:** trains leave London Paddington for Oxford, for the Cotswolds line to Moreton-in-Marsh and for Bath. Check times and prices on nationalrail.co.uk.
- **Driving:** you drive on the left. The Italian driving licence is valid with no international permit. In England the alcohol limit is 80 mg per 100 ml of blood, but Rick's advice is simple: whoever drives doesn't drink.
- **A car in London:** better not. In the central zone you pay the Congestion Charge, £18 a day (2026), plus the ULEZ for the most polluting vehicles.
- **Health:** the EHIC covers necessary treatment in the public NHS service; travel insurance is still recommended.
- **Sockets:** type G, with three rectangular pins. You need an adapter.
- **Weather:** it can rain in any month. Layers and a waterproof jacket.
- **From the airport to the centre:** **Heathrow (LHR)**. · **Piccadilly line** on the Underground: about £5.90 by contactless card, 50-60 minutes, the cheapest, and on Fridays and Saturdays it also runs through the night · **Elizabeth line**: about £15.50, about 30-40 minutes to Paddington and Tottenham Court Road · **Heathrow Express** to Paddington: about £25 bought on the day, about £10 well in advance, 15-20 minutes · metered **black cabs** about £70-110; Uber about £50-70 · the mistake to avoid is paying with two different cards or devices: the daily cap only works on one card.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in London: Westminster and the Thames
From the airport you reach the center by Underground or train. From Heathrow the Elizabeth line gets to Paddington in about half an hour and continues into the center; the Piccadilly line costs less but takes almost an hour. From Gatwick there is the Gatwick Express to Victoria. You pay with a contactless card, the same one for the whole trip. Drop the bags at the hotel and head straight out, with the afternoon free.

The afternoon is for **Westminster**: the Palace of Westminster with the **Big Ben** tower, **Westminster Abbey** and Whitehall. The Abbey is the church of coronations and royal burials, and is visited with a timed ticket. It closes early in the afternoon. You cross **St James's Park** to **Buckingham Palace**, past the lake and the squirrels. *The Changing of the Guard takes place in the morning and only on certain days: anyone who wants to see it should check the calendar and come back another day.* In the evening you go back to Westminster Bridge, the most famous view of Parliament, and have dinner on the South Bank.

**In practice:** Elizabeth line from Heathrow from about £14, Piccadilly line about £6 · Westminster Abbey about £31 online, closed to visitors on Sundays · to sleep, Paddington, the South Bank or Bloomsbury are convenient for the whole itinerary.

### Day 2 — British Museum, Covent Garden and the West End
The morning goes to the **British Museum**, with free entry. Arrive at opening, at ten, with a time slot booked online, also free. The museum is huge and can't be seen in a day. It's best to pick a few rooms: the Rosetta Stone, the Parthenon marbles, Egypt. The **Great Court**, the courtyard under the glass roof, is seen on the way in. Two or three hours are enough, then tiredness wins.

In the afternoon you walk down to **Covent Garden** and **Trafalgar Square**, with the National Gallery. Covent Garden has the old covered market and street performers in the piazza. The **National Gallery** is free like the British Museum: here too, focus on a few rooms, such as those with Van Gogh, Turner and the Italian Renaissance. From the steps you see Trafalgar Square with Nelson's Column. The evening may be the right one for a show in the **West End**. *The TKTS booth in Leicester Square has discounted tickets for the same evening, but the most popular musicals need booking weeks ahead.*

**In practice:** British Museum free, open from 10 am, on Fridays until 8:30 pm · National Gallery free · theatre tickets from the official sites, beware resellers with high fees.

### Day 3 — The Tower, Tower Bridge and the South Bank
At opening time you go into the **Tower of London**, with the Crown Jewels. Tickets are sold on the Historic Royal Palaces website. Head straight for the jewels, before the queue builds. Then the **White Tower**, the Norman keep with the armour, and one of the tours with the **Yeoman Warders**, the guards in red and black uniform, included in the ticket. You need at least three hours.

Then you walk across **Tower Bridge** and head back west along the South Bank: **Borough Market** for lunch, **Tate Modern** in the old power station, the **Millennium Bridge** with St Paul's ahead. The market is crowded at lunchtime, and the hot-food stalls have long queues. Tate Modern is free for the permanent collection, and from the tenth floor of the Blavatnik Building there is a viewing terrace over the city. You reach the London Eye for sunset. *A ride on the wheel lasts about half an hour: a pre-booked timed ticket avoids the longest queue.*

**In practice:** Tower of London about £37-38, opens at 9 (at 10 on Sundays and Mondays) · Borough Market open every day, with fewer stalls on Mondays · London Eye from about £29 online, more at the ticket office.

### Day 4 — Notting Hill, Hyde Park and the South Kensington museums
If it's a Saturday, the morning goes to the **Portobello Road** market, among the pastel houses of **Notting Hill**. Saturday is antiques day; on other days there are fruit, vegetables and a few shops. Arrive early, before ten, when the stalls open and the crowds have not yet arrived. The most photographed façades are in the side streets around the market.

Then you cross **Kensington Gardens** and **Hyde Park**. You pass near Kensington Palace and along the **Serpentine**, the lake in the middle of the park. The afternoon is for **South Kensington**: the **Natural History Museum**, with its free galleries, and the Victoria and Albert Museum next door. At the Natural History Museum the hall with the hanging blue whale and the dinosaur gallery are the busiest spots. The V&A, also free, has decorative arts, fashion and an inner courtyard for a break. At weekends it's best to book the free Natural History Museum ticket online. In the evening you sleep near Paddington.

**In practice:** Portobello Road in full swing on Saturdays · Natural History Museum and V&A free, temporary exhibitions paid · South Kensington Tube station, with the pedestrian tunnel leading to the museums.

### Day 5 — By train to Oxford, then the Cotswolds
From Paddington the train reaches **Oxford**. Fast trains take about an hour, and it is worth catching one of the first in the morning to have more time in the city. From the station to the center is a ten-to-fifteen-minute walk. Luggage can be left at a storage point or directly at the car rental office, if the opening times match.

In the morning you walk among the colleges: **Radcliffe Square** with the **Radcliffe Camera**, the **Bodleian Library**, the **Bridge of Sighs** and **Christ Church**. The Radcliffe Camera is seen from outside: only library readers and some Bodleian guided tours go inside. At Christ Church you visit the Great Hall and the college cathedral. College opening times change, and should be checked beforehand. In the afternoon you pick up the car and drive west. *Rental offices are often outside the center: check the address and closing time before booking.* Night in the Cotswolds, around **Stow-on-the-Wold**.

**In practice:** Paddington-Oxford about an hour on fast trains · Christ Church ticket around £20-25 depending on the day, sometimes closed for college events · Oxford-Stow-on-the-Wold about 45 minutes by car · in the Cotswolds you sleep in pubs with rooms or B&Bs, to be booked early.

### Day 6 — Bibury, Bourton-on-the-Water and the Slaughters
Set off early, to reach **Bibury** before the coaches. **Arlington Row** is the most photographed row of stone cottages in the area, and the houses are lived in. Look without going into the gardens or blocking the doors. Alongside flows the River Coln, with ducks and the Rack Isle water meadow. Half an hour is enough to see it all.

Then **Bourton-on-the-Water**, with its shallow river and little bridges. It is the most touristy village in the area, with tea rooms and shops along the river. In the afternoon you leave the car and walk between **Lower Slaughter** and **Upper Slaughter**, two tiny villages linked by a path through the fields. The path is about a mile long and takes around half an hour; in Lower Slaughter there is the old mill with its waterwheel on the stream. *The path crosses fields with sheep: close the gates behind you.* You finish at **Stow-on-the-Wold**, with its market square. On the north side of St Edward's church is the door framed by two ancient yew trees.

**In practice:** paid parking in Bibury and Bourton, few spaces · Lower Slaughter has very few parking spots, better to leave the car in Upper Slaughter or Bourton · waterproof shoes, the fields are often wet.

### Day 7 — Chipping Campden, Castle Combe and arrival in Bath
The morning goes to the northern Cotswolds: **Chipping Campden**, with its high street and old covered market, and **Broadway**. The market hall is seventeenth-century and belongs to the National Trust; Chipping Campden is also where the **Cotswold Way** begins, the long-distance path that runs down to Bath. Broadway is about twenty minutes away, with its wide high street and shops; on the hill above the village stands **Broadway Tower**, an eighteenth-century tower with views over several counties.

Then you head south. On the way is **Castle Combe**, in Wiltshire, with the bridge over the stream and stone houses around the market cross. You can't park in the village: there is a car park at the top, from which you walk down in ten minutes. It is the longest transfer of the Cotswolds stretch, so don't pile up too many stops. You reach **Bath** in the late afternoon and return the car. *In central Bath driving is slow and parking is expensive: return the car before going to the hotel.*

**In practice:** Chipping Campden-Castle Combe about an hour and a half by car · Castle Combe-Bath about half an hour · Broadway Tower with a ticket · to sleep, in Bath the Georgian center is close to everything.

### Day 8 — Bath and the return to London
The **Roman Baths** should be seen at opening time, with a ticket booked online. The audio guide is included and the route takes about two hours: the great pool with its warm green water, the underground baths and the temple of Sulis Minerva. Next door is **Bath Abbey**, with Jacob's ladders carved on the façade and tower tours at set times.

Then you walk up to the **Circus** and the **Royal Crescent**, the eighteenth-century Georgian terraces, and pass by **Pulteney Bridge**. The Royal Crescent is a half-moon of thirty houses facing a lawn; at No. 1 there is a museum with period interiors. Pulteney Bridge has shops built on it, like the Ponte Vecchio, and below it the stepped weir on the Avon. In the afternoon the train from Bath Spa takes you back to London Paddington, for the flight home. *If your flight from Heathrow is the same evening, count the train, the change at Paddington and the Elizabeth line: allow at least four hours between Bath and the gate.*

**In practice:** Roman Baths about £23-33 depending on day and season, £2 more at the ticket office · Bath Spa-Paddington about an hour and a half · from Paddington the Elizabeth line and the Heathrow Express go directly to Heathrow.

## How it changes with more or fewer days
- **6 days:** three in London, one in Oxford and two in the Cotswolds. Bath stays out, and you return to London by train from Moreton-in-Marsh.
- **Without a car:** train to Moreton-in-Marsh, one night there and an organised tour of the villages. Bath by train from London, as a day trip or with one night.
- **With Scotland:** from London to Edinburgh by train or plane, then the itinerary [*Scotland by car: Edinburgh, the Highlands and distilleries*](/viaggi/scozia-highlands-distillerie).

## What to see / what to do
- **London:** Westminster, the British Museum, the Tower and Tower Bridge, the South Bank with Tate Modern, Notting Hill, Hyde Park and South Kensington.
- **Oxford:** Radcliffe Square, the Bodleian Library and the colleges.
- **Cotswolds:** Bibury, Bourton-on-the-Water, Lower and Upper Slaughter, Stow-on-the-Wold, Chipping Campden, Broadway, Castle Combe.
- **Bath:** the Roman Baths, the Abbey, the Circus and the Royal Crescent.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Apply for the ETA before booking everything.** Do it only on gov.uk or with the official app: sites that sell it for more than £20 are not the government's.
- **Travel with your passport.** The ID card is no longer enough.
- **No car in London.** Pick it up in Oxford and return it in Bath.
- **Pay for the Tube with a contactless card.** It costs less than single tickets.
- **Reach Bibury and Bourton before 9.** The coaches arrive in the middle of the day.
- **Look right first when crossing.** Traffic comes from the opposite side compared with Italy.
- **Whoever drives doesn't drink.** Not even a pint at the village pub.

## Chiusura personale
Eight days are enough to see London calmly and to get a real taste of the English countryside, without spending the trip behind the wheel. If you still have a few days, you can head north, towards Scotland.
