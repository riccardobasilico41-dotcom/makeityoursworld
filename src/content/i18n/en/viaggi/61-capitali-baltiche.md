# The Baltic Capitals in 9 Days: Vilnius, Riga and Tallinn

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (trip taken by Rick; itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 9 days, 8 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, on foot in every city and by bus between one capital and the next
**Categoria:** Europa · Lituania · Lettonia · Estonia · città · storia · architettura

---

## Apertura personale
The three Baltic capitals have always been compared with each other, and the comparison holds up: Vilnius, Riga and Tallinn are all UNESCO sites, all emerged from half a century of Soviet occupation less than forty years ago, all use the euro; and yet they are three different countries, with three languages that don't understand each other (Lithuanian and Latvian are Baltic languages only distantly related; Estonian is Finno-Ugric, yet another language family, closer to Finnish than to its two neighbors) and three cities with a clearly distinct character. Vilnius is the most Baroque and the southernmost, with a neighborhood, Užupis, that declared itself an independent republic as a joke, but with a real constitution written on a wall in dozens of languages. Riga is the biggest, with a medieval center and, just outside its walls, one of the highest concentrations of Art Nouveau architecture in the world. Tallinn is the farthest north and the most "Viking" in look, an almost intact medieval center facing the Gulf of Finland, with an imperial Russian park on one side and a former Soviet industrial complex, now full of startups, on the other.

The right way to visit them is in a row, exactly as they sit on the map: from south to north, with the bus as the common thread. The Lux Express network (along with a few other companies on the same route) connects all of them, with frequent departures every day and no need for a rental car or a domestic flight: about four hours from Vilnius to Riga, another four to four and a half from Riga to Tallinn. Nine days are enough to do all three well, with one day trip each (the castle on the lake at Trakai from Vilnius, the beach and the Art Nouveau villas of Jūrmala from Riga) and without the forced pace of a long weekend per city.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** the recommended window is May to September. June to August is the warmest period, with the longest days of the year (up to 17-19 hours of daylight near the solstice), but also the busiest and most expensive, especially for lodging in the historic centers. May and September stay mild, with fewer tourists and lower prices, but the water is too cold for a real swim at Jūrmala. From November to March the cold is biting and often below freezing in all three cities, with frequent snow especially in Tallinn and in Estonia in general. The cities hold up well in winter too, with Christmas markets, but the days are very short.
- **Documents:** all three countries are in the European Union and the Schengen area. Italian citizens just need a valid ID card (or passport), with no formalities for tourist stays under ninety days, in any of the three.
- **Currency:** all three countries use the euro, unlike for example Poland, which the site covers elsewhere. You don't need to change anything between stops: compared with many other itineraries in Central and Eastern Europe, it's the main logistical simplification. Estonia adopted it first (2011), then Latvia (2014) and finally Lithuania (2015).
- **Payments:** all three countries are very advanced in digital payments, and Estonia in particular is one of the most cashless countries in Europe. Cards and contactless work almost everywhere, public transport included; cash is only needed for the smallest market stalls.
- **Connectivity:** with an Italian SIM, EU roaming is included in your regular plan in all three countries, and a local SIM isn't needed. 4G/5G coverage is very good in the three capitals and along the bus routes.
- **Languages, three different ones:** people who don't know the region often think "Baltic" means a single language or a single culture. It doesn't. Lithuanian and Latvian are both Baltic languages, but only distantly related and not mutually intelligible; Estonian is Finno-Ugric, of the same family as Finnish (with which there is partial mutual understanding) and entirely foreign to the language family of the other two. All three use the Latin alphabet, each with its own diacritics (ą, č, š, ž in Lithuanian; ā, č, ģ, ķ in Latvian; õ, ä, ö, ü in Estonian). English is widespread in the three capitals, especially among younger people and in tourism.
- **The bus between the three capitals:** Lux Express (with other companies on the same route, including Ecolines) is the standard way to move between Vilnius, Riga and Tallinn: several departures a day, comfortable seats, often wifi and outlets on board. **Vilnius-Riga:** about 4 hours, prices from a few euros if booked in advance up to €20-25 for the most in-demand runs. **Riga-Tallinn:** about 4-4h30, similar prices. For the best fares it's worth booking a few days ahead, especially in high season.
- **Safety and borders:** all three Baltic countries border Russia to the east, and Lithuania and Latvia also border Belarus. These are real frontier zones, with a reinforced military presence in recent years, but they remain entirely outside any standard tourist itinerary, this one included, which always moves along the coast and between the three capitals, never near a border. The capitals are safe by European standards, with the usual care about pickpockets in the most touristy areas.
- **Cost of living:** even paying in euros, all three capitals cost much less than the Nordic capitals covered elsewhere on this site (Oslo, Stockholm, Reykjavík). Vilnius and Riga are the cheapest of the three, Tallinn is a bit more expensive.
- **Getting around:** the historic centers of the three capitals can all be explored on foot. For the day trips (Trakai from Vilnius, Jūrmala from Riga) regional trains or buses are enough, cheap and frequent. No stop on this itinerary requires a rental car.
- **Electricity and time zone:** 230V, 50Hz, type C and F sockets in all three countries, so the same for the whole trip; from Italy you still need an adapter. The time zone is UTC+2 in winter and UTC+3 in summer (EET/EEST): one hour ahead of Italy all year round, in all three countries.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Vilnius and the Old Town at sunset
We landed at Vilnius International Airport (VNO), about 7 km from the center, from where a train or public bus gets you to the Old Town in 10-20 minutes. The train goes to the central station, from where you walk up towards the **Gate of Dawn**; the buses stop closer to the hotels in the center. It's best to sleep inside the Old Town or just outside it: from there the whole first day is on foot, and in the evening you get back without transport.

In the afternoon we took our first walk through the streets of the Baroque historic center, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1994. Pilies Street, the main pedestrian street, links Cathedral Square to the Gate of Dawn (Aušros Vartai), the only surviving gate of the medieval walls, with the chapel that houses the icon of the Black Madonna. You go up to the chapel quietly, because it's a busy place of prayer. Not far away is **Literatų gatvė**, the street of writers, with small artworks hung on the walls in tribute to writers connected with the city. The evening was quiet, in one of the restaurants of the old town, to settle in before the busiest day. *The dish to try at least once is **cepelinai**, big potato dumplings stuffed with meat: filling, better at lunch than at dinner.*

**In practice:** airport-central station train about 10 minutes, bus to the center 15-20 minutes · Lithuania uses the euro and cards are accepted almost everywhere · Gate of Dawn chapel free to enter, modest clothing.

### Day 2 — Vilnius: Cathedral, Gediminas Tower and the Palace of the Grand Dukes
We devoted the day to the heart of the Old Town. **Vilnius Cathedral**, neoclassical, stands in Cathedral Square, with the separate bell tower and, in the underground, the royal crypts of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania. Right behind it we climbed **Gediminas Hill**, on foot or by the short funicular (€2 one way, €3 round trip), up to **Gediminas Tower** (€8 entrance), the symbol of the city and the highest viewpoint over the historic center. At the foot of the hill is the **Palace of the Grand Dukes of Lithuania**, rebuilt on the archaeological remains of the medieval royal castle: it tells the story of the Grand Duchy, which for a time was the largest state in Europe.

In the afternoon we visited the **University Courtyard** of Vilnius University, founded in 1579 and one of the oldest in Central and Eastern Europe, with thirteen interconnected courtyards. We ended the day between St. Anne's Church, Gothic in red brick, and the nearby Church of St. Francis and St. Bernard, one of the most photographed Gothic views in the city.

**In practice:** Gediminas Tower about €8, funicular €2-3 · Palace of the Grand Dukes usually closed on Mondays · University courtyards with a ticket of a few euros · Cathedral free, crypts by guided tour only.

### Day 3 — Užupis and the island castle at Trakai
In the morning we were at **Užupis**, the neighborhood that since 1997 has proclaimed itself the "Independent Republic of Užupis", with a flag, an honorary president and a constitution of 41 articles, often surreal or poetic, engraved on plaques in dozens of languages along the **Constitution Wall**, on Paupio street. We walked among the art galleries and small workshops of the neighborhood, which in Soviet times was the most run-down area of Vilnius and today is the city's most bohemian district.

In the early afternoon we went to **Trakai**, reached by regional train (about 34 minutes) or bus (about 35 minutes), about 28 km west of Vilnius. It's the ancient capital of the Grand Duchy, before Vilnius itself, with the famous **Island Castle**, Gothic in red brick, built in the 14th-15th century by Grand Dukes Kęstutis and Vytautas and reachable by a wooden footbridge. We visited the museum inside the castle and the main tower, with views over Lake Galvė; if the weather allows, a ride in a rowboat or pedal boat shows you the castle from the water, the most photographed angle of all. Before heading back to Vilnius in the evening we stopped for **kibinai**, the stuffed pastries typical of the Karaim community, brought here by Grand Duke Vytautas in the 14th century.

**In practice:** train to Trakai about 35 minutes from Vilnius station, similar bus from the bus station next door · combined ticket for the Trakai castles about €17 in 2026 · in low season shorter hours and closing days: check first · kibinai in the places on Karaimų street.

### Day 4 — The bus to Riga and the first hours in the Old Town
We took the bus to **Riga**, the capital of Latvia: about 4 hours with Lux Express or a similar company, several departures a day, and the last hour of the journey follows the coast of the Gulf of Riga. It's best to buy the ticket online a few days ahead, when it's cheaper and you can choose your seat; the buses have power sockets and Wi-Fi, and the four hours pass easily. Riga bus station is next to the Central Market, a few minutes' walk from the Old Town.

We arrived in the early afternoon, with time to settle in and take a first walk around the **Old Town** (Vecrīga), a UNESCO site since 1997: **Cathedral Square** (Doma laukums) with Riga Cathedral, the largest church in the Baltics, and the **House of the Blackheads** (Melngalvju nams), the most photographed Renaissance façade on Town Hall Square, rebuilt after its destruction in the war. From there you walk to the **Swedish Gate**, the only surviving gate of the walls, and the **Cat House**, with black cats on its roof. Further east, beyond the canal, stands the **Freedom Monument**, the symbol of Latvian independence. In the evening, among the restaurants of the old town. *Riga Black Balsam, the local herbal liqueur, is better tasted in a cocktail or hot tea than neat.*

**In practice:** Vilnius-Riga about 4 hours by bus, cheap online tickets if bought ahead · bus station next to the Central Market · the Old Town is entirely walkable, and it's the handiest area to stay in.

### Day 5 — Riga: the Art Nouveau district and the Central Market
We spent the morning in the **Art Nouveau district**, concentrated along **Alberta iela**, Elizabetes iela and Strēlnieku iela: hundreds of facades decorated with masks, carved faces and floral motifs, many designed by the architect Mikhail Eisenstein, father of film director Sergei Eisenstein. It's estimated that about a third of the buildings in the center of Riga are in this style, one of the highest concentrations in the world. If you want to see the interiors too, at 12 Alberta iela you'll find the Art Nouveau Museum, which recreates a period bourgeois apartment.

In the afternoon we went to the **Central Market** (Centrāltirgus): five pavilions made from German zeppelin hangars of World War I. Opened in 1930, at the time it was the largest and most modern in Europe; today about 80,000 people still visit it every day and it's part of the UNESCO site along with the Old Town. We wandered among the stalls of smoked Baltic fish, cheeses and dark rye bread (rupjmaize), stopping to taste the local products. In the evening we climbed the tower of **St. Peter's Church** (72 meters, ticket €9, on sale until 5 p.m.) for the view over the Old Town and the Daugava river at sunset.

**In practice:** Art Nouveau Museum with a ticket of a few euros · Central Market open every day, best in the morning · St Peter's tower about €9, tickets until 5 pm.

### Day 6 — Jūrmala and a last walk around Riga
In the morning we went to **Jūrmala**, Latvia's historic seaside town, about 20 km from Riga: regional train from the central station, about 30 minutes, with departures at least every half hour. It's a strip of about 32 km of white sand along the Gulf of Riga, a holiday destination since the czarist era and then of the Soviet elite, with hundreds of wooden villas in Art Nouveau and Art Deco style on the residential streets of Majori and Dzintari. You stroll along the beach starting from Majori, with a swim if the season allows (June to August), and then through the inner streets, to look at the decorated villas from the outside.

In the late afternoon we went back to Riga for a last walk: the **Three Brothers** (Trīs brāļi) on Mazā Pils street, the trio of medieval and Baroque houses among the oldest stone buildings in the city, and then free time for last purchases or a stop at a rooftop bar in the Old Town.

**In practice:** Riga-Majori train about 30-35 minutes, tickets at the station or with the railway app · drivers entering Jūrmala pay an entry fee, the train avoids it · in summer the beaches fill up at weekends.

### Day 7 — The bus to Tallinn and the Old Town in the evening light
We took the bus to **Tallinn**, the capital of Estonia: about 4-4.5 hours with Lux Express or a similar company, several departures a day. As on the previous leg, tickets bought online in advance are cheaper. Tallinn bus station is a couple of kilometers from the Old Town, linked by tram; with light luggage you can also walk it in half an hour. It's best to stay inside the walls or just outside, near the **Viru Gate**.

We arrived in the late afternoon, with time to settle in and take a first walk around the **Old Town**, a UNESCO site since 1997 and one of the best-preserved medieval centers in northern Europe. The starting point is **Town Hall Square** (Raekoja plats), with Tallinn Town Hall from 1404, the oldest still in use in the Baltics. On one corner of the square is the **Town Hall Pharmacy** (Raeapteek), one of the oldest pharmacies in Europe still in business, with a small museum inside. With the evening light stretching over the red roofs, it's the right time to wander slowly, before the most intense day. *In the summer months it gets dark very late in Tallinn: the evening walk can last until ten.*

**In practice:** Riga-Tallinn about 4-4.5 hours by bus · from the bus station to the Old Town by tram or half an hour on foot · Estonia uses the euro, cards accepted almost everywhere · restaurants on Town Hall Square are pricier than those in the side streets.

### Day 8 — Tallinn: the Old Town and Toompea hill
In the morning we climbed **Toompea** hill, the upper part of the city, where power resided: **Toompea Castle** (now the seat of the Estonian parliament, the Riigikogu) and the Orthodox **Alexander Nevsky Cathedral**, with its golden onion domes. From the two viewing platforms of **Kohtuotsa** and **Patkuli**, a few steps from each other, you get the classic views over the lower town, the harbor and the Gulf of Finland.

In the afternoon we went down to the lower town and walked along the **medieval city walls**: about 1.85 km of them remain standing, with the surviving towers, including **Kiek in de Kök** (literally "peek into the kitchen"; the ticket for the four towers costs €12), one of the best-preserved defensive systems in Europe. Then there's **St. Olaf's Church** (Oleviste kirik), which in the 16th century was for a time the tallest building in the world. In the evening, among the restaurants of the Old Town, you should try kiluvõileib at least once, the open sandwich with marinated sprats typical of the Estonian snack.

**In practice:** Kiek in de Kök and towers about €12 · St Olaf's tower open only in the warm season · Alexander Nevsky Cathedral free, with modest clothing · the climb to Toompea is on cobblestones: comfortable shoes.

### Day 9 — Kadriorg, Telliskivi and departure
In the morning we went to **Kadriorg Park**, commissioned in 1718 by Peter the Great for his wife Catherine I and designed by the Italian architect Niccolò Michetti: the Baroque Palace, now a museum of foreign art, and the geometric gardens. A few steps away is **KUMU**, the Estonian art museum in a contemporary building, European Museum of the Year in 2008: a stop for anyone with time left before departure.

In the early afternoon we took a last walk around **Telliskivi Creative City**, the former Soviet industrial and railway complex converted since 2009 into a creative district, with independent shops, street art on the facades of the old brick sheds and craft breweries. It's the right counterpoint to close a trip that began in the Baroque center of Vilnius and ended in the most contemporary Tallinn, in the same country, it should be remembered, that gave birth to Skype and that in 2014 was the first in the world to offer e-Residency, a digital identity for setting up a European company online from anywhere in the world. Then the transfer to Tallinn Lennart Meri Airport (TLL), about 4 km from the center, one of the closest airports to a city center in Europe: by tram it takes 15-20 minutes.

**In practice:** Kadriorg Palace and KUMU usually closed on Mondays · Kadriorg is a few minutes by tram from the center · Telliskivi is behind Balti jaam railway station, walkable from the Old Town · tram to the airport 15-20 minutes.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
- **Vilnius:** the historic center (Senamiestis), with Pilies Street as the main axis, to have everything on foot; the part toward Užupis is a quieter alternative, with a bohemian atmosphere all its own.
- **Riga:** the Old Town (Vecrīga) for the nightlife and to have everything close by; the Art Nouveau district just outside the walls is an equally central alternative, where you can sleep inside an Art Nouveau building.
- **Tallinn:** the Old Town, with Toompea as a quieter and more scenic option compared with the livelier lower town; the Kalamaja district, between the historic center and Telliskivi, is a more residential alternative.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
- **Vilnius:** cepelinai, the big stuffed potato dumplings typical of Lithuanian cooking; in summer šaltibarščiai, the bright pink cold beetroot soup; Karaim kibinai, also worth trying in Trakai.
- **Riga:** the stalls of the Central Market for smoked fish, cheeses and dark rye bread; pelēkie zirņi ar speķi, gray peas with bacon, a signature winter dish.
- **Tallinn:** kiluvõileib, the open sandwich with marinated sprats; verivorst, blood sausage with cranberries, especially in winter; the younger food scene of Telliskivi, with street food and craft breweries.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Book Lux Express buses a few days ahead**, especially in high season, for the best fares on the two Vilnius-Riga and Riga-Tallinn legs.
- **Don't assume the three languages understand each other**: Lithuanian and Latvian are Baltic languages only distantly related, Estonian belongs to another language family (Finno-Ugric, close to Finnish).
- **Plan Jūrmala around the weather**, not the calendar: it's a beach trip that makes sense mostly from June to August.
- **Don't stick to the historic centers**: Riga's Art Nouveau district and the Kadriorg and Telliskivi districts in Tallinn show a side of each capital that the medieval center alone doesn't.
- **Count on half a day for Trakai**, not an hour: between transport, the castle visit and the boat ride, you need the time.
- **Check the weekly closing days** of museums and castles (Trakai in particular) before building the schedule.
- **Dress in layers even in summer**: the wind from the Baltic cools the evenings in all three cities, and even more on the lake at Trakai and on the beach at Jūrmala.
- **Stay away from the eastern border zones** of the three countries (with Russia and, for Lithuania and Latvia, with Belarus): this itinerary doesn't touch them at any point, and it's best to keep it that way.

## Chiusura personale
What stays with me from this trip is how much the three capitals manage to be both similar and sharply distinct: three UNESCO historic centers that came out of the same recent history of occupation and independence, and yet three languages that don't speak to each other, three different architectures (the Baroque of Vilnius, the Art Nouveau of Riga, the Hanseatic Gothic of Tallinn) and three different ways of telling what comes after the historic center: an imaginary republic in Vilnius, a market inside zeppelin hangars in Riga, a former Soviet complex full of startups in Tallinn. Traveling through them in a row, with the bus as the common thread, is the most honest way to see them for what they are: three small countries, close on the map, but anything but interchangeable.
