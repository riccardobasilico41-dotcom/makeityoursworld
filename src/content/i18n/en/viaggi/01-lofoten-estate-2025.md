# Lofoten in 7 Days: My Itinerary Through Fjords, Hikes and Polar Beaches

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress
**Periodo del viaggio:** July 6-13, 2025
**Durata:** 7 full days + return
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, a shared rental car for getting around the islands
**Categoria:** Europa · Norvegia · on the road · estate polare

---

## Apertura personale
What stayed with me most about the Lofoten was the light. In July the midnight sun doesn't set: it stays low on the horizon and the day never really ends. Then the roads, endless, running between fjords and mountains. At some point I realized the real trip was right there, behind the wheel, between one stop and the next. And then the bay of Reine: there I couldn't find any better words than "wow." This is the loop I did in seven days, with two fixed bases, a rental car and a few ferries, from arriving in Oslo to the last evening, back in the city.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** July, in the middle of the polar summer: very long daylight, but the climate is
  still cold and windy, to be factored in even in season.
- **Documents/visa:** none, Norway is in the Schengen area.
- **Budget:** €500-600 per person for meals alone; flights,
  car rental, ferries and lodging are extra.
- **How I got around:** international flight to Oslo, domestic flight Oslo–Bodø, rental car for
  everything else, local ferries for getting between islands and fjords.
- **Climate:** cold, even in July, with wind especially on beaches and exposed trails.
- **Logistics base:** two fixed stops (Svolvær and Nesland/Flakstad) from which to make day
  excursions.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 — Oslo
We landed at Gardermoen around lunchtime. The city is 19 minutes away by **Flytoget**: inside the terminal you follow the "Flytoget Airport Express" signs, trains leave every ten minutes and the ticket costs about 210 NOK. From Oslo S the Citybox Hotel is a five-minute walk.

The afternoon was one long walk: the Royal Palace gardens, the Aker Brygge waterfront, Renzo Piano's Astrup Fearnley museum and finally the Opera House, where we climbed onto the roof to look at the city and the sea. At the café inside, a hot tea hit the spot, even in July. We had dinner at **Rorbua**, also in Aker Brygge: traditional Norwegian cooking, generous portions, average prices, wood everywhere.

**In practice:** Flytoget about 210 NOK one way, pay by card at the gates or on the app · the Opera roof is free to access at any hour · Oslo trams, buses and metro with the Ruter app · at Aker Brygge it's worth booking dinner in summer

### Day 2 — Oslo → Bodø → Svolvær
We left before dawn, on the first Flytoget to the airport, and the flight to Bodø landed in the morning. With the car picked up, we walked around the center and the pier; for a view from above there's Nyholms Fort, ten minutes away. We got lunch to go from **Bryggerikaia**, which opens mid-morning and makes "mega-stuffed" sandwiches, and ate it at the city's viewpoint.

Then three and a quarter hours of driving to Bognes, with two stops that are worth it:
- **Kate Koisk Husflid**, a café with a view over a lake: cash only (NOK or euros) or a Norwegian payment app, no restroom.
- **Mørsvikbotn Handel**, a service station with a free restroom and another little lake in front.

At Bognes we took the ferry to Lødingen (best to be at the pier 40 minutes early), then an hour and forty minutes by car to Svolvær, where we arrived in the evening. Dinner at **Bacalo**, average.

**In practice:** from Bodø airport to the center is a few minutes by car · Bognes-Lødingen ferry about an hour, no booking: cars board in order of arrival and the fare is paid with AutoPASS or on board · in Norway you pay by card almost everywhere, cash is rarely needed

### Day 3 — Svolvær → Henningsvær
Breakfast at **Kringla**, an excellent bakery near the apartments. Then the first real thrill of the Lofoten: the **Lofoten Explorer** RIB boat to the **Trollfjord**, two hours with thermal suits provided by the crew, friendly guides and sea eagles a few meters from the boat. It's one of the best things about the trip. *Tip: gloves, glove liners and a hat.*

In late morning we went back to Svolvær and half an hour by car got us to **Henningsvær**. We had lunch at **Fiskekrogen**, on the village canal: excellent food. Best to book and ask for a window table, because the "little Venice" effect is real. After lunch, the famous soccer field, perfect for photos and drone, and a stroll among bakeries, crafts and vintage boutiques in a small but lively village.

The afternoon was for the **Festvågtind trail**, starting from Festvågtind Parking: 3 km and 500 meters of elevation gain, more technical than Reinebringen, across rock and sandy ground. In about forty minutes you reach an alpine pond halfway up; from there, to the right, there's an easier lookout, to the left the actual summit, more demanding. In the evening we had dinner at **Paleo Arctic**, the best restaurant of the whole trip: it costs a little more than average, but the quality is clearly superior.

**In practice:** RIB to the Trollfjord to book ahead, in summer places run out · in Henningsvær you park for a fee at the entrance to the village · Festvågtind about 3 hours up and down, shoes with soles that grip on rock · at Paleo Arctic it's worth booking

### Day 4 — Svolvær → Reine → Nesland
Breakfast at Kringla, a full tank of gas and 55 minutes by car to the **Borg Viking Museum**, which opens around mid-morning. It deserves an unhurried visit: an introductory film of about twenty minutes, a well-made exhibition and the reconstruction of the Viking longhouse. Outside you can shoot a bow, throw an axe or go out on horseback and by boat with a reservation; I skipped it, between the cold and the waits. We had lunch shortly after noon in Leknes, at **DIGG Restaurant**: simple, good, not memorable.

In the early afternoon we reached **Reine** (parking 200 NOK for three hours) for **Reinebringen**, the signature hike of the Lofoten: 1,978 stone steps built by Nepalese sherpas, 448 meters of elevation gain in 1.1 km. Technically it's easy, they're just steps, but you feel the elevation gain. The average time is between two and four hours; I took an hour and fifty in all: 45 minutes up, half an hour at the top and half an hour down. *The view repays the effort, but only with a clear sky.*

We had dinner at **Ramberg Resort**, a couple of steps from Ramberg beach, and a walk on the sand after dinner closed the day nicely.

**In practice:** Lofotr Viking Museum open every day in summer, a ticket of a few hundred kroner · Reinebringen: the steps are free, parking in Reine is paid; in rain the rock at the top is slippery · fill up in Leknes, further west there are few fuel stations

### Day 5 — Bunes Beach
Breakfast at the house, sandwiches bought at the Coop Prix in Reine and the morning ferry to **Vindstad**: forty minutes of sailing among rock walls, among the most scenic stretches of the trip. Even with a reserved spot it's best to be at the pier half an hour early.

At Vindstad the first stop was the "little yellow house," a former school turned community café run by volunteers, with excellent cinnamon rolls. Then twenty or thirty minutes of flat trail to a little white house, a quarter of an hour of gentle climbing past a small cemetery, a scenic hill and five minutes of descent. Below lies **Bunes beach**, huge, open to the ocean and enclosed by the mountains.

We ate our packed lunch at the yellow house, then hot tea and card games while waiting for the afternoon ferry. On the way back we shopped in Ramberg, better stocked and cheaper than the Coop in Reine, though it sells only beer, no wine or spirits. In the evening, a barbecue at the rorbu: grilled salmon, sautéed vegetables and a long table.

**In practice:** Reine-Vindstad ferry with few departures a day, worth booking a seat · in Vindstad there are no shops: bring water and lunch · camping is allowed at Bunes beach, but no fires on the sand and take your rubbish back with you

### Day 6 — Å and the Trail to Nusfjord
Fifty minutes by car and we arrived at **Å**, the village with the shortest name in the world. First the historic bakery, few seats and excellent pastries, then the guided tour in English of the **Fisherman's Museum**, in late morning: an hour that shows how the Lofoten fishing communities lived, interesting even in the rain. We had lunch in **Sakrisøy** at **Anita's Seafood**, with stuffed sandwiches, soups and sushi with a view of the fjord; you order at the counter.

In the afternoon, as soon as the sky opened up, we walked from **Nesland toward Nusfjord**: about 11 km round trip along the fjord, through woods and rocky stretches, with no technical difficulty. After the hikes of the previous days it was the right day for something lighter. Dinner at the rorbu with what was left from the barbecue: pasta with cream and salmon.

**In practice:** Å Fishing Village Museum with an entry ticket, guided tours in summer · Anita's Seafood in Sakrisøy, open in summer, you order at the counter · in Nusfjord in summer you pay a ticket to enter the village

### Day 7 — Bodø and Back to Oslo
Wake-up before dawn for the morning ferry from **Moskenes** to Bodø, three and a half hours of sailing (at the port 40 minutes early). In Bodø we had breakfast at Melkebaren, where the service is slow. Then **Mjelle** beach, pleasant but unremarkable after what we'd seen in the previous days, and the ten-minute ferry to **Misten and Kjerringøy**, staying in the car (at the pier half an hour early). Kjerringøy didn't thrill me, but it honestly fills a few hours. *Note: if Bodø/Glimt is playing at home, going to the stadium may be worth it.*

We came back on the early afternoon ferry, treated ourselves to a bit of beach between the Bobil Plats and Nils Fredrik campsites, then fuel, car return and the flight to Oslo, landing in the evening. The last dinner was at **Café Sara**, a simple place open late, perfect after a flight (on weekends it's best to book).

**In practice:** Moskenes-Bodø ferry about 3 hours 15 minutes, a car pays around 600 NOK plus the space reservation, passengers travel free · in summer the car space should be booked on the Torghatten Nord website · Bodø airport is a few minutes from the center, handy for returning the car

## What to see / what to do
- **Reinebringen:** the signature hike of the Lofoten, 1,978 steps, top view only
  with a clear sky.
- **Trollfjord by RIB** with Lofoten Explorer: sea eagles a few meters from the boat.
- **Festvågtind trail:** more technical and "wild" than Reinebringen.
- **Bunes beach:** reached only by ferry to Vindstad, one of the most
  beautiful.
- **Village of Å** + Fisherman's Museum: the "historic" and authentic Lofoten.
- **Henningsvær:** the most photographed soccer field in Norway and a
  bohemian atmosphere.
- **Off-the-beaten-path gem:** the Nesland–Nusfjord walk, scenic and without the
  crowds of the most famous hikes.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
**Oslo — Citybox Hotel** (outbound and return)
I chose an affordable design hotel a few minutes' walk from Oslo S: self
check-in, basic but clean rooms, comfortable beds, a coffee machine in the
room. That's what the reviews say too (Tripadvisor,
over 2,400 ratings, 4/5 score): excellent value for Oslo
standards and an unbeatable location near the station, against a deliberately
minimal style (no physical reception) and a few complaints about the
noisy rooms facing the tram. I recommend asking for a room on the
inner side.

#### Alternatives in Oslo
- **Budget:** Saga Poshtel (a few steps from Oslo S, among the cheapest
  options in the center) · Comfort Hotel Xpress Central Station (two minutes from
  Jernbanetorget, quick check-in)
- **Mid-range** (the Citybox chosen for this trip falls here too):
  Comfort Hotel Karl Johan (250 m from Oslo S, bright rooms)
- **Upscale, without going overboard:** Amerikalinjen (a historic boutique hotel, a
  couple of steps from the station) · The Thief (a design hotel on the Tjuvholmen
  waterfront, with an on-site spa: the highest rung among these)

**Svolvær — Nordis Apartment**
I slept in an apartment with a harbor view: big windows, a panoramic
terrace, a kitchenette. On Booking.com it scores 8.7/10 (417
reviews) and 9.7/10 for location, and the numbers confirm my
direct impression: spacious, clean rooms, a view that's worth the stay on its own, convenient for
anyone who wants to cook something in the evening instead of always going out.

#### Alternatives in Svolvær
- **Budget:** Lofoten Feriesenter (campsite and cabins, rates starting from
  about 200 NOK per person) · Lofoten Sommerhotell og Vandrerhjem (hostel in
  Kabelvåg, a few minutes from Svolvær, sauna and breakfast included)
- **Mid-range** (the Nordis Apartment chosen for this
  trip falls here): Thon Hotel Lofoten (central, harbor view)
- **Upscale, without going overboard:** Thon Hotel Svolvær (a large hotel on the
  waterfront, binoculars in every room) · Svinøya Rorbuer (historic rorbuer with
  their own kitchen, the top-rated in Svolvær on Tripadvisor)

**Nesland/Flakstad — Casa Flakstad** (rorbu)
Here I slept in a private rorbu overlooking the fjord, the most
isolated and quiet lodging of the trip. It's a small place, without enough
online reviews to quote honestly: so I'll just
say it's a simple, basic rorbu, with the atmosphere you'd
expect from this area. Few lights, lots of silence, the fjord a few steps away.
For a more solid verdict on a place in the area, the two
alternatives below have more feedback online.

#### Alternatives in Nesland/Flakstad
- **Budget:** Ramberg Gjestegård (simple cabins, 850-1050 NOK a night,
  discount in low season) · Lofoten Beach Camp (family-run
  campsite in Ramberg, sauna and picnic area)
- **Mid-range** (the rorbu chosen for this trip falls here): Lofoten
  Nesland Guesthouse (very high review score, quiet
  location with an ocean view)
- **Upscale, without going overboard:** Nusfjord Village & Resort (renovated historic rorbuer
  in the village of Nusfjord, sauna and hot tubs
  included; pricey, but with enthusiastic reviews about location and experience)

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- Book the Trollfjord excursion in good time: it's the top experience of the trip.
- Bring heavy layers even in July: the wind on beaches and ridges makes
  itself felt.
- Gloves and glove liners are useful for the RIB boat trip.
- Always arrive 30-40 minutes early for the ferries, even with a reserved
  spot.
- Reinebringen takes a minimum of fitness because of the elevation gain; if the
  sky is overcast, better to postpone it, because the view is everything.
- Bring NOK/EUR cash for the small places that don't accept cards (e.g. Kate
  Koisk Husflid).
- The supermarket in Ramberg is better stocked and cheaper than the one in Reine, but
  doesn't sell wine or spirits (only beer).

## Chiusura personale
One thing to know before you go: if the weather is bad, there's nothing to do in the Lofoten. Really nothing, apart from Svolvær and one or two slightly bigger towns. It's worth leaving some slack in the schedule and saving the hikes for clear days. If I were to go again I'd consider a camper or a van: the roads are beautiful and there are plenty of equipped sites. And if you like nature, the Lofoten are stunning.
