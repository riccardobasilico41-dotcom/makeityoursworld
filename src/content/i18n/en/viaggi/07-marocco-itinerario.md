# Morocco: from Marrakech to the Sahara

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (itinerary, practical info and anecdotes ready)
**Periodo del viaggio:** May 3-11
**Durata:** 9 days, 8 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, local guide Mustafa (+212 649431566), flawless organization, every detail in its place
**Categoria:** Africa · Marocco · deserto · kasbah · on the road

## Apertura personale
My first real impact with Morocco came at Jemaa El-Fna, the Marrakech square that turns at night into an orderly chaos of snake charmers, musicians and stalls of every kind: a taste of what awaited me in the following days. But the moment I carry with me most is still the night at Erg Chebbi, in the heart of the Sahara: a camel ride at sunset, a Bedouin dinner, and a starry sky that, far from any artificial light, seemed to have twice as many stars as I'm used to.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** early May. The weather is still manageable before the big summer heat, fine for both Marrakech and the desert (which stays cool at night anyway, even in spring).
- **Documents:** no visa needed for tourism, valid passport.
- **Money:** Moroccan dirham (MAD). Cash is essential outside the big cities: many kasbahs, hammams and small restaurants don't accept cards.
- **Guide:** Mustafa, +212 649431566. Flawless organization throughout the trip, from logistics to the restaurants chosen.
- **Getting around:** private van for all legs, some of them long (up to 4 hours), broken up by stops for typical purchases (saffron, turbans, pashminas) and visits along the way.
- **From the airport to the centre:** from **Marrakech Menara (RAK)**, about 6 km out, bus L19 goes to Jemaa el-Fnaa and Gueliz in about 30 minutes (about 30 MAD, ticket on board in cash, one bus roughly every half hour from 6am to 11.30pm) · official taxis at the rank outside arrivals, about 100-150 MAD by day and more after 8pm: the price is fixed before getting in, because in practice the meter isn't used · the easiest option is still the transfer from your accommodation, about 150-200 MAD · ride-hailing apps are not a reliable option here · cars can't enter the medina: whoever carries your bags to the riad expects a tip, and it's better to have someone from the riad meet you.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 — Marrakech
**Marrakech Menara** airport is a few kilometres from the city, fifteen or twenty minutes by car. Dirhams can't be bought outside Morocco: you withdraw from an ATM or change money right at the airport, where it's also worth getting a local SIM with a few gigabytes, useful for the whole trip. *With taxis, agree the price before getting in; easier still, have your accommodation send a transfer.* The rest of the day is for catching your breath and going over the week with the guide: departure times, stops, what to pack in the bag for the night in the desert.

The first day was free and gave us the chance to get to know Mustafa, the guide who stayed with me for the whole trip. We slept at **Villa Zahra**: comfortable, with more space and quiet than a hotel in the middle of the medina. The only compromise is the location, a bit outside the center, which you need to factor into evening trips. We had dinner at **Kui Zin**, then a drink at the Churchill Bar or La Pergola.

**In practice:** airport to city 15-20 minutes, taxi at a price agreed beforehand or the accommodation's transfer · dirhams only in Morocco: ATM or exchange at the airport, keeping the receipts to change back any left over · local SIM at the airport with your passport · Villa Zahra is outside the medina: allow for a taxi or transfer in the evening · book dinner at weekends.

### Day 2 — Marrakech-Aït Benhaddou
We set off early: 127 km, about two and a half hours, to the **Telouet Kasbah**, the former residence of the Glaoui family. The outside is partly collapsed rammed earth, the inside has richly decorated rooms, and that contrast is the reason to come.

Another hour on the road and we reached **Aït Benhaddou**, the UNESCO ksar that has served as the set for countless films and series, from *Gladiator* to *Game of Thrones*: a fortified mud village that looks like it came from another era. We had lunch at **Tawesna**, then headed back to the Ksar El Janoub hotel, with a pool and dinner there.

*Practical note for the next day: charge your power banks before the night in the desert and ask the guide where to stop for alcohol and snacks to bring to Merzouga, because supplies in the desert are scarce.*

**In practice:** Marrakech-Telouet about two and a half hours on the Tizi n'Tichka pass road, with many bends: if you get carsick, take something before leaving · Telouet has a small entrance fee, about 20-50 MAD in cash · you can walk into the ksar of Aït Benhaddou freely, some houses ask a few dirhams to go up to the terraces · the best light on the ksar is at sunset, seen from the opposite bank of the river.

### Day 3 — Merzouga, the gateway to the Sahara
On the road early: 200 km, three hours, to the **Todra Gorge**. Thirty minutes is enough to walk between rock walls that narrow to just a few meters, among the most beautiful in Morocco. We had a packed lunch along the way.

In the early afternoon we set off again toward **Erg Chebbi**, another 187 km. Halfway there, at a local market, we bought turbans and pashminas: they're needed against the sand and against the cold of the desert night. In the late afternoon we reached the tented camp **Camp La Source** and at sunset we climbed onto the camels for a ride among the dunes, included. Dinner is at the camp (allergies need to be flagged beforehand). *At night, a starry sky like no other, far from any light: one of the best moments of the whole trip.*

**In practice:** Todra Gorges free to enter, you walk on the canyon floor beside the river · a long day on the road: cash for coffee, toilets and fuel in the villages along the way · in the tented camp sockets and electricity are limited, so charged power banks · at night in the desert the temperature drops a lot, even in summer you need a fleece · turbans and pashminas are bought along the way, haggling.

### Day 4 — Merzouga-Agdz
The alarm went off before dawn for another camel ride, included, as the dunes changed color with the rising sun. Breakfast on our return, then the **quad in the desert**, 350 MAD per person, to be arranged with the guide. *If you have a license you can also ride a motorbike: it's best to book it a few days ahead, there are few vehicles.*

In late morning we left for **Agdz**, 300 km and about four hours. In the late afternoon we arrived at Kasbah Trane, with a bit of time by the pool. In the evening we visited the **Tamnougalt Kasbah**, one of the best preserved in the Draa Valley (20 MAD per person plus a 200 MAD tip for the local guide), and had dinner at the hotel.

**In practice:** quad about 350 MAD per person, to be agreed with the guide; motorbike to be booked a few days ahead · camera and phone in a bag during the quad ride: sand gets everywhere · Merzouga-Agdz about 300 km and four hours, with the last stretch in the Draa valley among the palm groves · Kasbah of Tamnougalt 20 MAD plus the tip for the local guide.

### Day 5 — Agdz-Agadir
We left early for **Taliouine**, 173 km, two and a half hours: it's Morocco's saffron capital, and that's where we bought it, in the old town. Better to have the guide point out the right place than stop at the first shop on the road. You can have lunch at **Taroudant**, and if so it's best to do it in the old town, to have time to see the ancient walls and the historic center.

Another three hours, 187 km, and we reached **Agadir**. Check-in at Hotel Oasis and free time, with the option of yoga at sunset with Khalid. We had dinner at **Les Blancs**, Spanish and Mediterranean cooking on a rooftop with a sea view, and finished the evening at LOFT, among hookahs, beers and an Arab-club atmosphere.

**In practice:** Agdz-Taliouine about two and a half hours, Taliouine-Agadir about three hours · real saffron has whole dark red threads and a high price: be wary of offers that look too cheap · in Taroudant the rammed-earth walls run around the whole old town and can be seen well from the road · in Agadir the seafront is handy for an evening walk before dinner.

### Day 6 — Agadir-Essaouira, with a surf stop at Taghazout
Half an hour on the road and we arrived at **Taghazout** for two hours of **surfing** with Chillout, 250 MAD per person (showers cost 10 MAD). We had lunch in the village and then drove two and a half hours, 152 km, to **Essaouira**.

The late-afternoon guided tour, included, lasts an hour and a half (tip of 150-200 MAD in total). Essaouira is the fortified port town on the Atlantic, with a white-and-blue medina among the most beautiful in Morocco. *Shopping here costs less than in Marrakech.* After days of van rides and sightseeing, the **hammam** at Kenza, an hour for 250 MAD, was a well-placed break. For dinner you can choose between **Triskala**, **La Table by Madada** and **Des Rêves**.

**In practice:** Agadir-Taghazout about half an hour · surfing with Chillout 250 MAD for two hours, showers 10 MAD, to be arranged the day before · Taghazout-Essaouira about two and a half hours for 152 km along the coast · hammam at Kenza 250 MAD for an hour · in Essaouira the wind blows hard most of the year: a jacket for the evening · book restaurants in high season.

### Day 7 — Essaouira-Marrakech
We spent the morning at the Ranch de Diabat: an hour of **quad**, included (it can get windy, so dress warmly), and an optional horseback ride (there are twelve horses at most). A picnic at noon, then three hours and 200 km to Marrakech. We had dinner at **Narwama**, with a dinner show, and went back to sleep at Villa Zahra.

The **Ranch de Diabat** is a few kilometres south of Essaouira, near the village of Diabat and the dunes that run down to the beach: it's best to go in the early hours, when the wind is usually lighter. The quad runs through sand and coastal scrub, the horse follows the beach, and for both you need long trousers and sunglasses against the sand.

The road to Marrakech crosses the plain of **argan trees**, which grow almost only in this part of Morocco, and it's the stretch where you see goats climbing in the branches. *They're often put there on purpose for photos, in exchange for a few dirhams: if you take a picture, you pay.* Along the way there are women's argan oil cooperatives, where you can see how the kernels are cracked and pressed; a short stop breaks up the three hours of driving. You reach Marrakech in the late afternoon, with time for a shower before dinner.

**In practice:** Essaouira-Marrakech about three hours for 200 km, good road · quad included, optional horse ride with twelve horses at most · long trousers, sunglasses and a windproof jacket · Narwama with an evening show: book ahead · night again at Villa Zahra.

### Day 8 — Marrakech
In the morning we took the guided tour of the city, included (tip of 200 MAD in total): the **Ali Ben Youssef Madrasa** (50 MAD), the **Bahia Palace** (70 MAD), the **Saadian Tombs** (70 MAD, open until late afternoon), the **Koutoubia Mosque**, from the outside only because non-Muslims can't enter, and finally **Jemaa El-Fna Square**, the heart of the medina, among snake charmers, musicians and stalls of every kind, especially after sunset.

We had lunch and wandered the souks. In the afternoon a taxi, 50 MAD per ride, took us to the **Jardin Majorelle**, the cobalt-blue garden created by Jacques Majorelle and later owned by Yves Saint Laurent: 150 MAD per person, capped entries, to be booked ahead (it opens early and the last entry is in the late afternoon). The rest was shopping and free time. We had our last dinner at **Safran by Koya**, good and beautiful, and spent the last night again at Villa Zahra.

**In practice:** Medersa Ali Ben Youssef 50 MAD, Bahia Palace 70 MAD, Saadian Tombs 70 MAD · taxi 50 MAD a ride, price agreed before getting in · Jardin Majorelle 150 MAD, timed entry to be booked online · in the medina, people who offer to show you the way usually ask for money afterwards: an offline map is better · in the souks you always haggle, starting well below the first price.

### Day 9 — Return
Check-out and goodbyes.

The last morning depends on your flight time. If you leave in the afternoon you still have a few hours for the souks or a coffee on a medina terrace, leaving your luggage with the accommodation. It's the moment for the purchases you put off: lamps, ceramics, argan oil, spices. *Liquids like argan oil go in the checked bag, tightly closed and wrapped in clothes.*

Before leaving, spend your remaining dirhams or change them back at the airport with your exchange receipts: they can't be changed outside Morocco. At Marrakech airport there's a first security check right at the terminal entrance, then passport control and security, and in peak months the queues can be long. Arrange the transfer the day before with the guide or the accommodation.

**In practice:** check-out usually by noon, luggage storage at the accommodation · from the city to the airport 15-30 minutes depending on the area · be at the airport at least two and a half hours before the flight · spend or change back dirhams before the gate.

## What to see / what to do
- **Aït Benhaddou:** the UNESCO ksar, the set of countless films.
- **Todra Gorge:** very narrow, sheer walls, worth it even for just a half-hour walk.
- **Night in the desert at Erg Chebbi:** camel rides at sunset and dawn, dinner and a starry sky at the tented camp.
- **Tamnougalt Kasbah:** one of the best preserved in the Draa Valley.
- **Essaouira:** the white-and-blue port town, with shopping that's better value than in Marrakech.
- **Jardin Majorelle:** Yves Saint Laurent's blue garden, an almost obligatory stop in Marrakech.
- **Jemaa El-Fna in the evening:** the square that transforms, among food stalls, music and street performances.

## Dove abbiamo dormito

I slept here three nights: a spacious, quiet villa, great for staying comfortable, with more room to breathe than a hotel in the heart of the medina. Reviews describe it as a very well-kept place, with a pool and helpful staff. The only real compromise is the location, a bit outside the center, which you need to factor into evening trips.
*Alternatives:* budget: Central House Marrakech Medina, Equity Point Marrakech · mid-range: Riad Nelia, Riad Yasmine · luxury (not over the top): IZZA, Riad Kaiss

One night before heading off to the desert. It's the place with the best reviews in the area: clean, well-kept rooms, a pool with a view of the landscape and service that many guests describe as attentive and friendly. Several reviews mention the owner, Khalid, as very hospitable.
*Alternatives:* budget: Riad Caravane, Dar Mouna · mid-range: Kasbah Tebi, Kasbah El Hajja · luxury (not over the top): Kasbah Rayane, Caravane Hotel (Ounila Valley)

**Merzouga (Day 3) — Camp La Source**
A night in the desert in this tented camp at the foot of the Erg Chebbi dunes: large, well-kept tents, family management described as very welcoming and a starry sky far from any artificial light. It's one of the best memories of the whole trip.
*Alternatives:* budget: Hostel La Liberte Merzouga, Merzouga Camp and Hostel · mid-range: Auberge La Khaima, Nomad Palace Camp · luxury (not over the top): Merzouga Luxury Desert Camp, Sahara Desert Luxury Camp

**Agdz (Day 4) — Kasbah Trane**
A night along the Draa Valley, with free time by the pool before dinner. A quiet area, a good base for visiting the nearby Tamnougalt Kasbah.
*Alternatives:* budget: Kasbah Maktob Tamnougalt, Guest House Jardin de Tamnougalt · mid-range: Maison d'Hôtes Kasbah Azul, Dar Jnane - La Maison Du Jardin · luxury (not over the top): Hotel and Restaurant Kasbah Draa, Kasbah Kissane

A comfortable night because of its proximity to the sea and restaurants, but online reviews are rather mixed: the location and the buffet breakfast are good, the upkeep of the rooms less so, which several guests find dated. Fine as a stopover, not the highlight of the trip.
*Alternatives:* budget: Hotel Sindibad Agadir, Résidence IGOUDAR · mid-range: Riad Dar Maktoub, Hôtel Riad Salam Agadir · luxury (not over the top): Sofitel Agadir Thalassa Sea & Spa, Hotel Riu Palace Tikida Agadir

A night right in the old town, a few steps from the souks and the port. Reviews speak of a house full of character, with handcrafted decor and very attentive staff; some mention small rooms and the calls to prayer you hear at night, normal in the heart of the medina.
*Alternatives:* budget: La Smala, Riad Al Madina · mid-range: Lyon Mogador, Riad Chakir Mogador · luxury (not over the top): Heure Bleue Palais, Riad Dar Maya

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Buy alcohol and snacks before entering the desert**: in Merzouga supplies are limited, so it's best to stock up with the guide's help along the way.
- **Charged power banks** before the night in the desert: the tented camps have no electrical outlets.
- **Saffron**: buy it only at the places the guide recommends in Taliouine, avoiding the roadside shops set up for passing tourists.
- **Motorbike in Merzouga**: if you have a license, it's the alternative to the quad. It must be booked a few days ahead because availability is limited.
- **Jardin Majorelle**: book in advance, entries are capped and it fills up fast during the middle of the day.
- **Always carry cash**, especially outside Marrakech and Agadir.

## Chiusura personale
Nine days from Marrakech to the Sahara and back, among mud kasbahs, gorges carved into the rock and the night in the desert: it's one of those trips that suits every taste, from culture to adventure. If I have to pick a single memory, it's still the tented camp at Erg Chebbi: the starry sky I saw there remains one of the most beautiful I've ever seen on a trip. Credit also goes to Mustafa, the guide who stayed with me: flawless organization that made everything simpler, from the first day to the last.
