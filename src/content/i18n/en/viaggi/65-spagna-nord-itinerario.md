# Northern Spain in 8 Days: San Sebastián, Bilbao and the Coast Toward Santiago

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (trip taken by Rick; itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 8 days, 7 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, by car for the whole coastal route
**Categoria:** Europa · Spagna · Paesi Baschi · Cantabria · Asturie · Galizia · gastronomia · cammino

---

## Apertura personale
There is a Spain that disproves every cliché about Spain: it isn't hot, it rains often, the landscape is green instead of ochre, and the language you hear in the street (Euskera, in the Basque Country) has no proven link to any other living language in the world. This is the North: San Sebastián and Bilbao in the Basque Country, the coast of Cantabria and Asturias, and finally Galicia, where all the Caminos de Santiago end. A coastal road trip of about 550 km, to be driven following more or less the route of the Camino del Norte, the coastal variant of the Camino de Santiago.

The common thread, besides the Atlantic coast, is food: the Basque pintxo in San Sebastián and Bilbao, Asturian fabada, Galician pulpo á feira. Eight days are not enough to walk the entire Camino del Norte (it is almost 900 km), but they are enough to see its most beautiful spots by car and maybe walk a stretch.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **June-September**, the driest window and the one with the highest temperatures of the year (still mild, rarely above 25-27°C). It is the opposite of Andalusia, where the same season is best avoided because of the heat: here it is the time when it rains least.
- **The Atlantic climate:** even in the best window it rains more often than in the rest of Spain, so a light rain jacket should always be packed, in every season.
- **Pintxos and txikiteo:** in San Sebastián and Bilbao the serious meal is eaten going from bar to bar, one pintxo and a glass of txakoli or cider per place. The unwritten rule: you eat standing at the bar, you pick up the pintxo with your hands, and you pay at the end by telling the bartender what you had (see the dedicated experience page).
- **Guggenheim Bilbao:** closed almost every Monday; in summer the opening is sometimes extended, so it's worth checking the calendar. Ticket €18 from mid-June to mid-September, €15 the rest of the year.
- **Camino del Norte:** about 825-865 km from Irún to Santiago de Compostela, through the Basque Country (~175 km), Cantabria (~170 km), Asturias (~280 km) and Galicia (~220 km). It is more demanding and less equipped than the Camino Francés, with exposed coastal stretches.
- **Santiago, the Compostela:** to get the official pilgrimage certificate you must have walked at least the last 100 km (200 by bike), with your credentials stamped along the way.
- **Car:** essential for this itinerary, which follows the coast for hundreds of km, touching small towns poorly connected by public transport.
- **Electricity and currency:** euro, type C/F sockets identical to Italy.
- **From the airport to the centre:** on the way out you usually land at **Bilbao (BIO)**: the direct Pesa bus to San Sebastián takes about 1h15 and costs about €14-17, roughly hourly, ticket from the machine or online; the Bizkaibus A3247 reaches central Bilbao in about 20 minutes, about €5 (less with the Barik card); a taxi to San Sebastián costs about €170-190 · closer to San Sebastián are the small airports of **Hondarribia (EAS)** and **Biarritz (BIQ)** · on the way back from **Santiago (SCQ)** the bus to Praza de Galicia and the station takes about 20 minutes, about €1, a taxi about €23 at a fixed fare · the typical mistake is a one-way Bilbao-Santiago rental without checking the drop-off fee.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in San Sebastián: the Parte Vieja
We landed in Bilbao (the airport with the best connections to Italy) and moved on to **San Sebastián** (about 1h20 by bus or car). The direct bus leaves from outside arrivals and drops you in the center of San Sebastián; anyone renting a car straight away should know that parking in the city is expensive and it is better to leave it in a garage for the first two days. The center is explored on foot, and the neighbourhoods are close together.

In the afternoon, a first walk around the **Parte Vieja**, the old town with the highest concentration of pintxos bars in the city, squeezed between the harbour, the Urumea river and **Monte Urgull**. You pass the **Basilica of Santa María**, its Baroque façade at the end of a narrow street, and the **Plaza de la Constitución**, whose numbered balconies recall the days when the square hosted bullfights. Those with energy left climb Urgull on the park paths, up to the statue of the Sacred Heart, with the bay below.

We dedicated the evening to a first **pintxos crawl**, following the local txikiteo rule: one bar, one specialty, one drink, then you move on (see the dedicated experience page). *Bars fill up between 8 and 10 pm, and many have a hot pintxo made to order: ask what the house specialty is.*

**In practice:** Bilbao airport-San Sebastián bus roughly every hour, ticket on board or online · Monte Urgull free, a 20-30 minute climb · to sleep, the Parte Vieja is noisy at night, Gros is quieter.

### Day 2 — San Sebastián: La Concha and Monte Igueldo
The morning at **Playa de la Concha**, the shell-shaped city beach, among the most beautiful in Europe. Walk it along the promenade with the white railing, from the city hall to **Ondarreta**, while the light is still low and the sand almost empty. At low tide the beach widens a lot, at high tide it shrinks to a strip: tide times are posted around town.

Then we went up **Monte Igueldo** on the historic funicular, for the view over the bay, and stopped at the **Peine del Viento**, the Eduardo Chillida sculpture set into the rocks at the end of La Concha. The three iron sculptures are fixed to the rocks where the waves break: in rough seas the water spouts from the holes in the paving.

The afternoon is free, or you can go to **Getaria** or **Zarautz**, the coastal towns just outside the city known for grilled fish and surfing. Getaria is the home of txakoli, the sparkling white wine of the coast, and of the street grills where whole turbot is cooked. In the evening, a second pintxos crawl, in different bars from the first.

**In practice:** Igueldo funicular return ticket of a few euros, shorter hours out of season · Getaria and Zarautz about half an hour away by car or bus · grilled fish is ordered by weight, ask the price first.

### Day 3 — From San Sebastián to Bilbao
By car to **Bilbao** (about 1h20), with a possible stop along the coast. The motorway is the quickest route; those with time follow the Basque coast instead as far as **Zumaia**, where Itzurun beach is closed in by layered cliffs, the flysch, one of the most photographed spots in the region. The detour adds a couple of hours but is the best part of the transfer. You reach Bilbao in the early afternoon.

We spent the afternoon in the **Casco Viejo**, the "Siete Calles" of the medieval old town, with the Cathedral of Santiago and the Mercado de la Ribera on the riverfront. The market is one of the largest covered markets in Europe, with fish stalls on the ground floor and a pintxos area to stop at. Then the **Plaza Nueva**, the nineteenth-century arcaded square full of bars, the right place for the evening. *On Sunday mornings there is a stamp and coin market under the arcades of the Plaza Nueva, and the surrounding bars fill up by aperitivo time.*

**In practice:** San Sebastián-Bilbao about 1h20 on the toll motorway, more than 2 hours along the coast · in the Casco Viejo you don't need the car, a guarded car park is better · Mercado de la Ribera stalls closed on Sundays.

### Day 4 — Bilbao: the Guggenheim and the Ensanche
The morning at the **Guggenheim Museum Bilbao**, Frank Gehry's titanium building on the Nervión estuary (opening hours and booking are on the dedicated experience page). Before going in, walk around the outside: **Puppy**, Jeff Koons's flower dog in front of the entrance, and **Maman**, Louise Bourgeois's spider on the river side. Inside, the large ground-floor gallery holds Richard Serra's steel sculptures; the rest of the exhibitions change several times a year. Two or three hours are enough.

The afternoon in the **Ensanche**, the bourgeois nineteenth-century Bilbao, and a walk along the Nervión. Its center is the **Gran Vía** with Plaza Moyúa; not far away is **Azkuna Zentroa**, the former wine warehouse turned cultural center, with every column in the atrium different.

In the afternoon you can also detour to the **Puente de Vizcaya** in Portugalete, the oldest transporter bridge in the world, a UNESCO World Heritage site. Since 1893 a suspended gondola has carried cars and people from one bank to the other; those who want can climb to the walkway at the top, above the estuary.

**In practice:** Guggenheim about €18 in summer and €15 the rest of the year, closed almost every Monday · Puente de Vizcaya about 20 minutes by metro from the center, gondola crossing cheap, walkway on a separate ticket · the Bilbao metro is the easiest way to get around.

### Day 5 — From Bilbao to Cantabria: Santander and Santillana del Mar
By car toward **Cantabria** (about 1h30-2h to Santander), with a stop at **Santillana del Mar**, one of the best-preserved medieval villages in Spain, near the famous Altamira caves: the originals are closed to the public for protection, but you can visit the Neocueva, the official replica. Go to Santillana first, in the morning, when the village's two cobbled streets are still quiet: at the end stands the Romanesque **Collegiate Church of Santa Juliana**, with its carved cloister capitals.

The Altamira museum is a couple of kilometres from the village. The Neocueva reproduces the ceiling of painted bison using the same techniques as in the Palaeolithic, and the museum explains who the people who painted them were. The afternoon in **Santander**, with the seafront and the Magdalena peninsula: the palace that was the kings' summer residence, the park around it and the beaches of **El Sardinero**. On the harbour stands the **Centro Botín**, Renzo Piano's art center suspended over the water. *The Altamira museum closes on Mondays: if day 5 falls on a Monday, swap the stops and see Santander first.*

**In practice:** Altamira museum and Neocueva closed on Mondays, entry about 3 euros, free for some categories · Santillana is car-free in the center, parking at the entrance to the village · in Santander, sleep near El Sardinero or in the center.

### Day 6 — Cantabria and Asturias: the Costa Verde
We continued along the Asturian **Costa Verde**, among cliffs, beaches and small fishing harbours. Leave Santander early and follow the Cantabrian motorway, turning off toward the sea when you like: **Comillas**, with Gaudí's Capricho, and **San Vicente de la Barquera** are short stops along the way. In Asturias the first stop is **Llanes**, with its medieval center on the sea.

In Llanes you walk the **Paseo de San Pedro**, the grassy clifftop path, and go down to the harbour to see the **Cubos de la Memoria**, the breakwater blocks painted by Agustín Ibarrola. Then on to **Gijón**, the largest city in Asturias: the fishermen's quarter of **Cimadevilla** on the headland, Chillida's sculpture *Elogio del Horizonte* at the top and the long San Lorenzo beach. The evening is for cider poured from a height in the sidrerías. Those who want to add a mountain detour can stop at the **Picos de Europa**. *The road to the Covadonga lakes, in the Picos, is closed to private cars at peak times and you go up by shuttle bus.*

**In practice:** Santander-Llanes about 1h15-1h30, Llanes-Gijón about 1h15 without stops · cider served by the bottle and poured by the waiter, drink it right away · in Gijón, sleep in the center or near San Lorenzo.

### Day 7 — From Asturias to Galicia: toward Santiago
The longest transfer (about 4-5 hours), through inland Galicia, to **Santiago de Compostela**. Leave early and break the journey: along the western Asturian coast, **Cudillero**, with its colourful houses stepped above the harbour, makes a one-hour stop; just past the Galician border is the **Playa de las Catedrales**, with rock arches visible only at low tide.

Anyone wanting to walk a stretch of the Camino del Norte can stop at one of the last coastal stages, continue on foot for a day, and then pick up the car again toward the final destination. *A single day is not enough for the Compostela, which requires the last 100 km on foot: it is a taste, and should be taken as one.* You reach Santiago in the evening, and the first thing to do is a walk through the lit-up old town, as far as the square in front of the Cathedral.

**In practice:** Playa de las Catedrales can only be visited at low tide, and in summer and at peak times with a free online booking · check the tide table the day before · in Santiago the car must be left outside the pedestrian old town.

### Day 8 — Santiago de Compostela and end of the Camino
The last day in **Santiago de Compostela**: the **Cathedral**, with the Romanesque Pórtico da Gloria and, on certain occasions, the botafumeiro swinging along the nave during the pilgrims' mass; the **Praza do Obradoiro**, the symbolic finishing point of all the Caminos; the UNESCO-listed old town. Start early at the Obradoiro, when the square is still empty and the first pilgrims arrive with their backpacks. The nave is free to visit; the **Pórtico da Gloria** is seen with a separate timed ticket, and the same goes for the guided tour of the roofs.

Then the **Mercado de Abastos**, where some stalls cook the fish you have just bought, and the **Alameda** park, with the best view of the Cathedral. *The botafumeiro does not swing at every mass, and at times it is out of use or masses move elsewhere because of works: check the calendar on the Cathedral website.* The trip ends with the return flight from Santiago-Rosalía de Castro airport.

**In practice:** Pórtico da Gloria and roofs with a ticket and time slot, book online · for pulpo á feira, try the bars around the center · the airport is about fifteen kilometres away, with a regular bus from the center.

## What to see / what to do
- **The txikiteo in the Parte Vieja** of San Sebastián, pintxo culture in its most concentrated form.
- **Playa de la Concha** and Monte Igueldo.
- **The Guggenheim Bilbao**, the building that reinvented an industrial city.
- **The Casco Viejo** of Bilbao, the medieval "Siete Calles".
- **Santillana del Mar**, one of the best-preserved villages in Spain.
- **The Asturian Costa Verde**, between Llanes and Gijón.
- **The Cathedral of Santiago de Compostela**, final destination of all the Caminos.

## Possible extensions
- **A longer stretch of the Camino del Norte (+3-5 days):** for those who want to really walk, as well as seeing the coast by car: the stretch between San Sebastián and Bilbao and the Asturian one around Llanes are among the most scenic.
- **Picos de Europa (+1-2 days):** the mountain massif between Cantabria, Asturias and Castile and León, with some of the most spectacular high-mountain landscapes in Spain.
- **Rías Baixas (+1-2 days):** the inlets of southwestern Galicia, for those who want to add sea and seafood after Santiago.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
In San Sebastián, the **Parte Vieja** to be in the heart of the pintxos life (noisy in the evening) or **Gros**, across the river, quieter and close to Zurriola beach. In Bilbao, the **Casco Viejo** for the historic atmosphere or the **Ensanche**, near the Guggenheim. Along the coast, pensiones and small hotels in the stopover towns; in Santiago, the old town around the Cathedral.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
In San Sebastián and Bilbao the **txikiteo** is an experience in itself: one bar, one specialty, a glass of txakoli or cider, then you change place. In Cantabria and Asturias, the catch of the day and **fabada asturiana** (stew of beans, chorizo and morcilla). In Galicia the gastronomic institution is **pulpo á feira** (boiled octopus with paprika, served on a wooden plate).

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't pile your plate with pintxos as at a buffet**: one or two pieces per bar, then you change place. That's how it's done here.
- **Always carry a light rain jacket**, in any season: in the North it rains more often than in the rest of Spain, even in midsummer.
- **Check the Guggenheim's hours before planning a Monday in Bilbao**: the museum is closed almost every Monday.
- **Don't underestimate coastal distances**: the roads follow the coast with many curves, and transfers take longer than they look on the map.
- **Book the albergues along the Camino in peak months** (June-September) if you decide to walk a stretch: in small towns the beds run out.
- **Arrive in Santiago with your pilgrim credentials stamped**, if you walked a stretch and want the Compostela.

## Chiusura personale
Northern Spain overturns the cliché: it isn't hot, it rains often, the landscape is green, and you eat standing at the bar, one pintxo at a time.
