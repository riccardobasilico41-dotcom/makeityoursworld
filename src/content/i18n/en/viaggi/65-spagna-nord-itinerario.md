# Northern Spain in 8 Days: San Sebastián, Bilbao and the Coast Toward Santiago

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 in progress (trip taken by Rick; itinerary and practical information verified)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 8 days, 7 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, independently, by car for the whole coastal route
**Categoria:** Europa · Spagna · Paesi Baschi · Cantabria · Asturie · Galizia · gastronomia · cammino

---

## Apertura personale
There is a Spain that disproves every cliché about Spain: it isn't hot, it rains often, the landscape is green instead of ochre, and the language you hear in the street (Euskera, in the Basque Country) has no proven link to any other living language in the world. This is the North: San Sebastián and Bilbao in the Basque Country, the coast of Cantabria and Asturias, and finally Galicia, where all the Caminos de Santiago end. A coastal road trip of about 550 km, to be driven following more or less the route of the Camino del Norte, the coastal variant of the Camino de Santiago.

The common thread, besides the Atlantic coast, is food: the Basque pintxo in San Sebastián and Bilbao, Asturian fabada, Galician pulpo á feira. Eight days are not enough to walk the entire Camino del Norte (it is almost 900 km), but they are enough to see its most beautiful spots by car and maybe walk a stretch.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **June-September**, the driest window and the one with the highest temperatures of the year (still mild, rarely above 25-27°C). It is the opposite of Andalusia, where the same season is best avoided because of the heat: here it is the time when it rains least.
- **The Atlantic climate:** even in the best window it rains more often than in the rest of Spain, so a light rain jacket should always be packed, in every season.
- **Pintxos and txikiteo:** in San Sebastián and Bilbao the serious meal is eaten going from bar to bar, one pintxo and a glass of txakoli or cider per place. The unwritten rule: you eat standing at the bar, you pick up the pintxo with your hands, and you pay at the end by telling the bartender what you had (see the dedicated experience page).
- **Guggenheim Bilbao:** closed almost every Monday; in summer the opening is sometimes extended, so it's worth checking the calendar. Ticket €18 from mid-June to mid-September, €15 the rest of the year.
- **Camino del Norte:** about 825-865 km from Irún to Santiago de Compostela, through the Basque Country (~175 km), Cantabria (~170 km), Asturias (~280 km) and Galicia (~220 km). It is more demanding and less equipped than the Camino Francés, with exposed coastal stretches.
- **Santiago, the Compostela:** to get the official pilgrimage certificate you must have walked at least the last 100 km (200 by bike), with your credentials stamped along the way.
- **Car:** essential for this itinerary, which follows the coast for hundreds of km, touching small towns poorly connected by public transport.
- **Electricity and currency:** euro, type C/F sockets identical to Italy.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in San Sebastián: the Parte Vieja
We landed in Bilbao (the airport best connected with Italy) and moved to **San Sebastián** (about 1h20 by bus or car). In the afternoon, the first walk through the **Parte Vieja**, the old town with the city's highest density of pintxos bars.

We devoted the evening to the first **pintxos crawl**, following the local rule of the txikiteo: one bar, one specialty, one glass, then you move on (see the dedicated experience page).

### Day 2 — San Sebastián: La Concha and Monte Igueldo
The morning at **Playa de la Concha**, the shell-shaped urban beach, among the most beautiful in Europe. Then we went up **Monte Igueldo** on the historic funicular, for the view over the bay, and stopped at the **Peine del Viento**, Eduardo Chillida's sculpture set into the rocks at the end of La Concha.

The afternoon is free, or you go to **Getaria** or **Zarautz**, the coastal towns just outside the city known for grilled fish and surfing. In the evening a second pintxos crawl, in different bars from the first.

### Day 3 — From San Sebastián to Bilbao
By car to **Bilbao** (about 1h20), with a possible stop along the coast. We spent the afternoon in the **Casco Viejo**, the "Siete Calles" of the medieval old town, with the Cathedral of Santiago and the Mercado de la Ribera on the riverfront.

### Day 4 — Bilbao: the Guggenheim and the Ensanche
The morning at the **Guggenheim Museum Bilbao**, Frank Gehry's titanium building on the Nervión estuary (hours and booking are on the dedicated experience page). The afternoon in the **Ensanche**, the bourgeois Bilbao of the 19th century, and a walk along the Nervión.

In the afternoon you can also detour to the **Puente de Vizcaya** in Portugalete, the oldest transporter bridge in the world, a UNESCO World Heritage site.

### Day 5 — From Bilbao to Cantabria: Santander and Santillana del Mar
By car toward **Cantabria** (about 1h30-2h to Santander), with a stop at **Santillana del Mar**, one of the best-preserved medieval villages in Spain, near the famous caves of Altamira: the originals are closed to the public for their protection, but you can visit the Neocueva, the official replica. The afternoon in **Santander**, with the seafront and the Magdalena peninsula.

### Day 6 — Cantabria and Asturias: the Costa Verde
We continued along the Asturian **Costa Verde**, among cliffs, beaches and small fishing ports: **Llanes**, with its medieval center by the sea, and **Gijón**, the largest city in Asturias. Anyone who wants to add a detour into the mountains can stop at the **Picos de Europa**.

### Day 7 — From Asturias to Galicia: toward Santiago
The longest transfer (about 4-5 hours), through inland Galicia, to **Santiago de Compostela**. Anyone who wants to walk a stretch of the Camino del Norte can stop at one of the last coastal stages, continue on foot for a day, and then take the car again toward the final destination.

### Day 8 — Santiago de Compostela and end of the Camino
The last day in **Santiago de Compostela**: the **Cathedral**, with the Romanesque Pórtico da Gloria and, on certain occasions, the botafumeiro swinging along the nave during the pilgrims' Mass; the **Praza do Obradoiro**, the symbolic arrival point of all the Caminos; the UNESCO World Heritage old town. The trip ends with the flight home from Santiago-Rosalía de Castro airport.

## What to see / what to do
- **The txikiteo in the Parte Vieja** of San Sebastián, pintxo culture in its most concentrated form.
- **Playa de la Concha** and Monte Igueldo.
- **The Guggenheim Bilbao**, the building that reinvented an industrial city.
- **The Casco Viejo** of Bilbao, the medieval "Siete Calles".
- **Santillana del Mar**, one of the best-preserved villages in Spain.
- **The Asturian Costa Verde**, between Llanes and Gijón.
- **The Cathedral of Santiago de Compostela**, final destination of all the Caminos.

## Possible extensions
- **A longer stretch of the Camino del Norte (+3-5 days):** for those who want to really walk, as well as seeing the coast by car: the stretch between San Sebastián and Bilbao and the Asturian one around Llanes are among the most scenic.
- **Picos de Europa (+1-2 days):** the mountain massif between Cantabria, Asturias and Castile and León, with some of the most spectacular high-mountain landscapes in Spain.
- **Rías Baixas (+1-2 days):** the inlets of southwestern Galicia, for those who want to add sea and seafood after Santiago.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
In San Sebastián, the **Parte Vieja** to be in the heart of the pintxos life (noisy in the evening) or **Gros**, across the river, quieter and close to Zurriola beach. In Bilbao, the **Casco Viejo** for the historic atmosphere or the **Ensanche**, near the Guggenheim. Along the coast, pensiones and small hotels in the stopover towns; in Santiago, the old town around the Cathedral.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
In San Sebastián and Bilbao the **txikiteo** is an experience in itself: one bar, one specialty, a glass of txakoli or cider, then you change place. In Cantabria and Asturias, the catch of the day and **fabada asturiana** (stew of beans, chorizo and morcilla). In Galicia the gastronomic institution is **pulpo á feira** (boiled octopus with paprika, served on a wooden plate).

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Don't pile your plate with pintxos as at a buffet**: one or two pieces per bar, then you change place. That's how it's done here.
- **Always carry a light rain jacket**, in any season: in the North it rains more often than in the rest of Spain, even in midsummer.
- **Check the Guggenheim's hours before planning a Monday in Bilbao**: the museum is closed almost every Monday.
- **Don't underestimate coastal distances**: the roads follow the coast with many curves, and transfers take longer than they look on the map.
- **Book the albergues along the Camino in peak months** (June-September) if you decide to walk a stretch: in small towns the beds run out.
- **Arrive in Santiago with your pilgrim credentials stamped**, if you walked a stretch and want the Compostela.

## Chiusura personale
What stays with me is the total contrast with the most common idea of "Spain": here it isn't hot, you eat standing at a bar counter instead of at a table, and the language you hear in the street in the Basque Country resembles no other. It is the Spain that corrects the stereotype, and perhaps that is why it is the most underrated of the four Spanish regions told here.
