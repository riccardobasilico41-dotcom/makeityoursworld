# Thailand: from Bangkok to the southern sea, with Chiang Mai, Phuket and the Similan Islands

## Apertura personale
I've seen Bangkok many times over the years, and each time I discovered something different: a line from seven in the morning for Jay Fai's Michelin-starred street food, a different rooftop bar every evening, a new neighborhood found almost by chance. More than a single trip, Thailand for me is the sum of several different trips over time (the main 11-day loop through jungle and southern islands, plus repeated visits to Bangkok and separate trips to Chiang Mai and the Similan Islands), each with its own different Thailand to tell.

Bangkok is incredible: a mix of contrasts.

## Scheda pratica
- **Documents:** no visa needed for short tourist stays (up to
  30 days by air), a valid passport is enough.
- **Currency:** Thai baht (THB). Cash is always useful outside Bangkok;
  7-Elevens (everywhere, open 24/7) are a reference point for everything:
  food, drinks, SIM cards, even bus tickets with a surcharge. After
  10:00 p.m. they don't sell alcohol (national law, applies everywhere).
- **Useful apps:** Line (more used than WhatsApp to communicate with locals),
  Grab (taxi/moto-taxi in all the main cities).
- **From the airport to the centre:** from **Suvarnabhumi (BKK)** the **Airport Rail Link** reaches Phaya Thai, on the BTS, in about 27 minutes, 15-45 baht, from 5:30 to midnight · metered taxis only from the kiosks on level 1, plus a 50 baht surcharge and the tolls: about 300-500 baht, 30-60 minutes · Grab from the point shown in the app. From **Don Mueang (DMK)**: SRT Red Line train to Krung Thep Aphiwat, about 20 baht, then the MRT; bus A1 to Mo Chit, about 30 baht; or a taxi or Grab. Anyone in arrivals offering a "fixed-price" taxi is almost always asking double: insist on the meter.
- **Luggage:** light backpack, long pants for Bangkok's rooftop bars
  (dress code) and for temples (in some, covering shoulders and
  knees is mandatory; sarongs and cloths can often be rented or bought
  at the entrance).
- **Organized groups:** by law, in Thailand an organized group must
  be accompanied by an authorized local guide. That is why tour
  operators ask you not to wear clothing/badges with their brand
  around temples and islands (risk of being stopped by the tourist
  police for "unlicensed guiding").

## Itinerario del giro principale (11 giorni)
### Day 1 — Bangkok
We spent the first evening on **Khao San Road**, for a first taste of Bangkok's nightlife. For the rest of the city there is a dedicated section below.

The first day is mostly for landing and settling in. From the airport you get into the city with Grab, a taxi from the official desk, or the Airport Rail Link if you land at Suvarnabhumi. Khao San Road comes alive after sunset: pad thai stalls, fruit shakes, bars with loud music and vendors selling everything. *It's the most touristy street in the city: take it as a taste, not as an idea of Bangkok.* Don't go to bed too late, because the next day pickup is at dawn.

**In practice:** Grab works well in the city · cash in baht for stalls and small purchases · Khao San at its best from the evening on · no alcohol sold at 7-Elevens after 22:00 · early alarm for the next day.

### Day 2 — Bangkok-Hua Hin
Hotel pickup was early in the morning. With a big group you split into two vans, and there's no way to have just one. We devoted the morning to markets: first the **Floating Market**, then the **Train Market**, the one that is dismantled when the train passes (there is a deeper look at both below).

Around noon we ate at a roadside rest stop, with lots of choice and good food, against all expectations. In the early afternoon, if there is time, you stop at the **Tham Khao Luang** cave, near Phetchaburi, a cave with Buddha statues lit from above; we reached **Hua Hin** in the late afternoon. For dinner there is one of the evening markets, Cicada, Tamarind or Chatchai, full of street food stalls. *At the train market, stay behind the line the vendors mark: the train really does pass a few centimetres away.*

**In practice:** leave at dawn to reach the markets before the crowds · prices at the floating market are inflated, bargain · Cicada open Friday to Sunday from about 4 pm to 11 pm, Tamarind next door on the same evenings · in Hua Hin stay near the beach or the centre.

### Day 3 — Hua Hin-Chumphon
We left early for **Sam Roi Yot National Park** (200 THB per person, usually collected by the coordinator in the shared kitty). The beach of **Laem Sala** is reached by boat, a quarter of an hour, or on foot in forty minutes, depending on the sea. From there a short hike in the forest leads to the **Phra Nakorn Cave**, with a royal pavilion inside: when the light filters in from above it is one of the most photographed views in Thailand. We stayed on the beach until lunch.

In the early afternoon we changed vehicle for the safari in **Kui Buri National Park** (1,050 THB per person), hunting for gaur and wild elephants. *Better to say it clearly to the group: it is not an African safari, the animals are often seen from far away and sometimes not seen at all.* If there is time, **Wat Thang Sai**; then three and a half hours of driving to **Chumphon**. For dinner, the Cube Bar or the Helio Restaurant.

**In practice:** Sam Roi Yot 200 THB per person · boat to Laem Sala depending on the sea, otherwise a forty-minute trail · closed shoes for the climb to the cave, the best light is mid-morning · Kui Buri 1,050 THB, binoculars help · three and a half hours of driving to Chumphon.

### Day 4 — Chumphon-Khao Sok
Early pickup and four and a half hours of driving to **Khao Sok**, with two stops at service stations; park admission costs 220 THB per person. On arrival the guide and driver say goodbye (the tip is optional, about 500 baht per car).

The optional activities, elephant safari, bamboo rafting, night safari and jungle trekking, are booked through the hotel, Smiley Bungalows, at least 24 hours ahead and never with unauthorized outside providers. Dinner at the Bamboo Bistro Khao Sok. Khao Sok is one of the oldest rainforests in the country, with limestone walls covered in vegetation and the river running between the bungalows: the afternoon suits a first walk or the rafting, which is gentle and fine for everyone. *If the elephant safari involves riding the elephants or bathing with them, skip it: the site's stance is the one explained in the trip [to northern Thailand](/viaggi/thailandia-nord).* The night safari is for spotting insects, frogs and snakes by torchlight.

**In practice:** four and a half hours of driving from Chumphon · park admission 220 THB · activities to book at the hotel at least 24 hours ahead · repellent, trekking shoes and a waterproof pouch for your phone · it can rain in the evening even in the dry season.

### Day 5 — Khao Sok, Cheow Lan Lake
Two days and one night on the lake, with breakfast, lunch and dinner included. The transfer took us to the pier, with a stop at a local shop, and from there an hour and a half by boat to the **floating resort**. The accommodation is very basic and expectations should be adjusted: the phone doesn't always get a signal and wifi is only sometimes available, at the reception. The rest of the day was free, to enjoy the lake.

In the late afternoon we did the sunset cruise, with an English-speaking local guide, among the limestone walls rising out of the water: one of the most beautiful sceneries in Thailand, often compared to a small Guilin. *Mind the resort's rules: no noise after dinner and no beer brought from outside.* **Cheow Lan** lake is an artificial reservoir created by a dam, and in the free hours you swim or kayak in front of the bungalows. A few things in your bag are enough; the rest can stay on land.

**In practice:** about an hour and a half by boat from the pier · meals included, drinks extra · small backpack for the night on the lake · torch, power bank and cash, cards aren't always accepted at the resort · swimsuit and water sandals.

### Day 6 — Khao Sok-Krabi
One hour by boat and two by road, and around noon we were in Krabi. In the afternoon we went to **Railay Beach**: a quarter of an hour by boat, 200 THB each way, then half an hour on foot to **Phra Nang Beach** and its cave. In the evening, on **Ao Nang Beach**, in front of the Boat Service Club, there is a free fire show.

Lunch at the Last Fisherman, dinner at the Sea Salt Krabi Restaurant or a local Thai restaurant. Railay has no roads and is reached only by boat, because of the rock walls that cut it off from the mainland: it's one of Thailand's most famous climbing spots. Many consider Phra Nang Beach the most beautiful beach in the area, with cliffs dropping straight into the water. *At low tide the long-tails moor far from the shore and you wade knee-deep: a dry bag for phone and documents.* Don't wait for dark to head back, when the boats thin out.

**In practice:** long-tail from Ao Nang to Railay 200 THB each way, a quarter of an hour · last boat before sunset, ask the time at the pier · reef shoes help · free fire show in the evening at Ao Nang.

### Day 7 — Krabi: Bond Island and canoe
We spent a whole day on a boat, from early morning to late afternoon, with an English-speaking guide. We passed **Koh Ta Poo**, the island made famous by *The Man with the Golden Gun*, and **Koh Ping Gun**, went by canoe into **Tham Lot** and had lunch at **Koh Panyee**, the fishing village on stilts; then the **Monkey Cave** and some rest at the waterfalls. Park admission, lunch and transfers are included.

For dinner, the Sea Salt Krabi Restaurant or the Reeve Beach Club Krabi; for the evening, Carlito's or, more of a club, Slinky. **Phang Nga** bay is full of limestone stacks rising from the sea, and the canoe slips under arches and into caves where the big boat can't go. *At the Monkey Cave the monkeys steal from bags and hands: no food in sight and no sunglasses perched on your head.* Koh Panyee is a Muslim village, so no alcohol is served at lunch and respectful clothing is a good idea.

**In practice:** full-day tour, back in the late afternoon · park admission, lunch and transfers included · sunscreen, a hat and a T-shirt for the canoe · cash for tips and drinks.

### Day 8 — Krabi-Koh Phi Phi
We took the ferry in the morning from Ao Nopparat Thara Pier (check-in half an hour before) and in two and a half hours we reached **Koh Phi Phi**, at Tonsai Pier. The afternoon offers a boat trip to **Bamboo Island** or half a day of diving with Blue View Divers.

For dinner, the Dow Restaurant or the ACQUA Restaurant. The evening is at the Backpacker Bar or the Reggae Bar, where the amateur Muay Thai fights are great fun to watch. Koh Phi Phi has no roads for cars: from the pier to the hotels you walk or use the porters' carts, so bring luggage that's easy to carry. On arrival at the pier you pay a small island entry fee. *If you have time, climb to the **viewpoint** above Tonsai, about half an hour of steps, to see the two bays almost touching: it's best at sunset, but bring water.* **Maya Bay** can only be visited with capped access and no swimming.

*My memory: on Phi Phi we were on the beach. Be careful with ice and local alcohol; and when they put on fire shows, if you stand too close you'll smell of kerosene the next day too, and now and then you risk stains on your clothes from the stuff they throw around.*

**In practice:** ferry from Ao Nopparat Thara, check-in half an hour before · entry fee at Tonsai pier of about 20 baht, in cash · viewpoint ticket of about 30-50 baht · book dives the day before · Tonsai is very noisy at night: if you want to sleep, pick a hotel away from the bars.

### Day 9 — Koh Phi Phi-Krabi
We took the return ferry in the early afternoon; it docks at Ao Nopparat Thara or Klong Jirat, depending on the operator. Dinner at the Reeve Beach Club Krabi, with the evening fire show.

The morning is free on the island: time for a swim at **Long Beach**, reachable on foot or by long-tail from Tonsai, or some snorkelling near the shore before the day-trip boats arrive. Check out before heading out, leaving your bags at reception. *Check your ticket for the arrival pier, because Ao Nopparat Thara and Klong Jirat are in different places and the transfer changes.* In Krabi you get back to your room in time for a shower before dinner.

**In practice:** ferry in the early afternoon, check-in at the pier half an hour before · long-tail to Long Beach, bargain at Tonsai · arrival at Ao Nopparat Thara or Klong Jirat depending on the operator · dinner in Ao Nang with the fire show.

### Day 10 — Krabi-Bangkok
We flew back to Bangkok on a domestic flight from Krabi, with about 15 kg of baggage included and check-in 80 minutes before. Watch out for the airport: you may land at BKK or Don Mueang (DMK), check your ticket. In the evening, a last drink on a rooftop and dinner at Sawasdee House or Sathorn is Charme Eatery.

Krabi airport is about half an hour by road from Ao Nang, and the flight to Bangkok takes just over an hour. Once in the city, the afternoon is the right time for souvenir shopping or a Thai massage before the evening. *Bangkok's rooftop bars require long trousers and closed shoes: in flip-flops and shorts you often won't get in.* If your bags got heavier at the seaside, remember the domestic flight's weight limit.

**In practice:** about 15 kg of baggage included, check-in 80 minutes before · Ao Nang to Krabi airport about half an hour · BKK or DMK, check your ticket · rooftop dress code.

### Day 11 — Bangkok
The last day is free, with no services included: the right moment to catch up on something from the Bangkok list below.

If your flight is in the evening, do the temples in the morning, starting early with the **Grand Palace** and **Wat Pho**, and a market or a river boat ride in the afternoon. If you've already seen the centre, pick the **Chatuchak Weekend Market**, if it's Saturday or Sunday, or a street food neighbourhood. *Leave your bags at the hotel after check-out and head to the airport at least three hours ahead: Bangkok's rush-hour traffic is unpredictable.* For Suvarnabhumi, the Airport Rail Link avoids the jams.

**In practice:** check-out with luggage storage at the hotel · temples with shoulders and knees covered · Chatuchak only at the weekend · Airport Rail Link for Suvarnabhumi, Grab or taxi for Don Mueang · leave for the airport at least three hours ahead.

## Bangkok: the rooftop bars
Bangkok is probably the world capital of rooftop bars: it is worth
choosing at least a couple for different evenings:
- **Vertigo & Moon Bar** (Banyan Tree): one of the most iconic, open-air,
  spectacular at sunset.
- **Lebua at State Tower**: where a scene from *The Hangover Part II*
  was shot, cinematic atmosphere.
- **Octave Rooftop** (Marriott Sukhumvit): on three levels, very
  panoramic.
- **MahaNakhon SkyWalk**: the tallest skyscraper in Thailand, recommended
  especially at sunset.
- **Tichuca Rooftop Bar**: less touristy, good value for money.
- **Scarlett Wine Bar** (Sathorn): for those looking for wine rather than
  cocktails.
Mind the dress code: almost all require long pants (no
flip-flops/tank tops) and close last orders early (often 12:30 a.m., with
closing at 1:00).

## Bangkok: temples and city tours
- **Grand Palace**: the royal palace, the most photographed monument in
  Bangkok (8:30-15:30, about 500 baht; strict dress code, shoulders and knees
  covered, cloths can be rented at the entrance). Also worth seeing is **Wat
  Phra Kaew**, the temple of the Emerald Buddha, inside the same
  complex.
- **Wat Pho**: a short walk from the Grand Palace (8:30-18:30, about 200 baht),
  famous for the huge gilded reclining Buddha 46 meters long; also home to
  one of the most renowned Thai massage schools in the country.
- **Wat Arun**, the "Temple of Dawn": reached by boat from the other
  bank of the Chao Phraya river (8:30-17:30, about 100 baht), spectacular at
  sunset when the central tower lights up with golden light.
- **Chatuchak Market**: the largest market in Southeast Asia, open
  only on weekends: about a thousand stalls of clothing, crafts,
  animals, food. Count on a good half day. It is the most
  "real" of Bangkok's markets, mostly frequented by locals.
- **Live Muay Thai**: the Rajadamnern or Lumpinee stadiums offer regular
  bouts, to be seen at least once.

## Floating market and train market: are they worth it?
The classic day trip from Bangkok to the **Floating Market** (e.g. Damnoen
Saduak or Amphawa) and the **Train Market** (Maeklong, where the stalls
pull back at the last second when the train passes) is one of the
most popular and "Instagrammable" in the country: colorful boats loaded with
fruit, vendors in straw hats, the train brushing the counters. It must
be said frankly: **it is very touristy**. The floating markets closest to
Bangkok are organized almost exclusively for visitors, prices are
inflated compared with any local market, and the "authentic" atmosphere
of the photos is largely staged for the occasion. That said,
**if it's your first time in Thailand and there is time, it is still worth
doing**: they are curious, photogenic glimpses, the
train passing a hand's breadth from the goods is really fun, and in half a day
you satisfy the curiosity without sacrificing too much time from the rest of the
itinerary. Those looking instead for a more authentic Thai market experience
would do better to aim for a local neighborhood market in
Bangkok itself (e.g. Khlong Toei) or the evening markets of Chiang Mai.

## Ayutthaya, the day trip
The ancient capital of the Kingdom of Siam, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is reached from
Bangkok in about 1h30 by train (the cheapest and most fun option: local
trains, often without air conditioning, but it is part of the trip) or in about
1h by car/van. Among the ruins not to miss: **Wat Mahathat** (the famous
Buddha head wrapped in tree roots), **Wat Chaiwatthanaram**
(on the riverbank, beautiful at sunset) and **Wat Phra Si Sanphet**.
It is easily done in a day from Bangkok.

## Bangkok: street food and Michelin street food
Bangkok is probably the world capital of street food, to the point that
the Michelin Guide has begun to reward street stalls just as it would
a restaurant:

- **Raan Jay Fai**: the only street stall in the world to have earned
  a **Michelin star**. The chef, now in her eighties, personally cooks
  every dish wearing her iconic welding goggles to protect herself
  from the charcoal flame. Her crab omelette (kai jeaw poo) costs
  over 1,000 THB: expensive for street food, but an experience in itself.
  *You have to arrive early (before 8:00) and put your name on a list on a
  sheet of paper, or book by email well in advance.* *My memory:
  an hour and a half in line, and I got lucky.*
- **Thipsamai**: the most famous pad thai in Bangkok, often cited among the
  Bib Gourmands (the "good value for money" category of the Michelin
  Guide), in the Old City near Jay Fai.
- **Nai Ek Roll Noodle** (Yaowarat/Chinatown): Bib Gourmand, soup of
  rolled rice noodles with toppings of your choice (offal, sliced pork,
  roast pork).
- **Jeh O Chula**: famous for its very spicy tom yum with instant
  noodles, another recurring name among the Bib Gourmands.
- **Charoen Saeng**: an institution for almost sixty years for khao kha
  moo (braised pork knuckle on rice): it opens early and often runs out of
  ingredients by lunch, so it should be treated as an early breakfast/lunch,
  not dinner.
- **Polo Fried Chicken** (Soi Polo): legendary fried chicken among the
  Bib Gourmands.
- **Pad Thai Fai Ta Lu**: the "savory" alternative to Thipsamai's sweet pad thai,
  with crispy pork.

**The areas to hit for street food, summed up:**
- **Yaowarat (Chinatown)**: the most concentrated area, especially after
  sunset, for Sino-Thai style fish and seafood.
- **Old City (Phra Nakhon)**: where you find Jay Fai, Thipsamai, Pad
  Thai Fai Ta Lu and Krua Apsorn, all close to each other and to the main temples.
- **Ari**: the reference neighborhood for northern cuisine, excellent khao
  soi.
- **Victory Monument**: the realm of cheap street food, with the Boat
  Noodle Alley.
- **Silom/Soi Convent**: lunchtime carts for office workers,
  genuine street food at lunch.

## Bangkok: nightlife
Bangkok at night has very different faces: it is worth knowing what
to expect from each before choosing where to go:
- **Khao San Road**: the historic headquarters of backpackers: open-air
  bars, music everywhere, street food, vendors of everything. Chaotic,
  touristy, but an almost obligatory rite of passage at least for one evening.
- **Soi Cowboy** and **Nana Plaza**: the two best-known streets/complexes of
  Bangkok's "red light district", full of neon go-go bars. They are part of the
  city's nightlife landscape and you may end up there even just
  strolling around Sukhumvit; there are also the famous **ping pong shows**,
  which remain a rather so-so experience: certainly not the
  best way to spend an evening in Bangkok, more an anecdote to tell than
  something to live.
- **RCA (Royal City Avenue)**: the area of big clubs for a
  younger and more local crowd.
- **Thonglor/Ekkamai**: more refined venues and high-level cocktail bars, the
  "international" and slightly pricier Bangkok.
- **Rot Fai Night Market (Train Night Market)** and **Asiatique The
  Riverfront**: night markets designed for evening shopping and
  street food in a more orderly and photogenic setting than the daytime
  markets; Asiatique in particular is on the river, with a Ferris wheel and
  an elegant amusement park atmosphere.
- **Chatuchak Night Market/JJ Green**: an evening and more chill version of the
  daytime weekend market, with more bars and fewer crowds.

## The cultural north: Chiang Mai
The north of Thailand has a completely different atmosphere from Bangkok: slower,
greener, more "mountain". *(We didn't visit Chiang Rai; if
there is a next trip, the White Temple/Wat Rong Khun and the Blue Temple
deserve a separate stop, about 3h from Chiang Mai.)*

- **Old City**: surrounded by a square moat, it holds the main temples:
  **Wat Phra Singh**, **Wat Chedi Luang** (with the large partly ruined
  stupa) and the small but sparkling **Wat Sri Suphan**, the
  silver temple.
- **Wat Phra That Doi Suthep**: the symbol temple of the city, perched
  on the mountain of the same name with a view over all of Chiang Mai: you climb a staircase
  of about 300 steps lined with nagas (the mythological serpents), or
  you can get to the golden pagoda by tram. Reachable by songthaew,
  motorbike or organized tour.
- **Wat Pha Lat**: the "hidden" temple in the jungle along the path that
  descends from Doi Suthep, much less crowded and as beautiful as the other.
- **Night markets**: the Night Bazaar (every evening) and the Sunday Walking
  Street Market (on Sundays, along Ratchadamnoen Road), not to be missed for
  street food, crafts and atmosphere.
- **Nimman**: the contemporary neighborhood, full of specialty
  coffee shops and trendy venues, a good counterpoint to the temples.
- **Ethical elephant sanctuary**: in Chiang Mai the choice is among
  very many centers, not all equally ethical. The serious ones (like
  **Elephant Nature Park**, the best known, run by a nonprofit
  association that rescues elephants from exploitation and abuse) don't involve
  riding the animals, only observing them, feeding them and bathing
  with them in the river: a half-day or full-day experience,
  often combinable with a waterfall or a bamboo
  rafting trip.
- **Doi Inthanon National Park**: the highest mountain in Thailand,
  waterfalls, hill tribe villages, two twin pagodas dedicated to the king and
  queen.
- **Thai cooking class**: Chiang Mai is probably the best place
  in the country to take one, with many schools that also include a
  visit to the local market to choose the ingredients.

## The southern sea

Southern Thailand has **two coasts with completely out-of-sync climates**
and it is the most important thing to know before choosing where to go
and when:

| Coast | Islands/destinations | Dry season (ideal) | Rainy season |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Andaman Sea** (west) | Phuket, Krabi, Koh Phi Phi, Similan, Khao Lak | November-April | May-October (southwest monsoon) |
| **Gulf of Thailand** (east) | Koh Samui, Koh Phangan, Koh Tao | January-April and July-September | October-December (northeast monsoon) |

In practice: **when it rains on the Andaman, the Gulf is often sunny, and
vice versa**. If you travel in the height of the European summer (July-August), the
Gulf islands (Samui, Phangan, Tao) remain a safer bet;
if you travel between December and March, you can choose freely because
both coasts are in dry season, and it is also the high
tourist season on all the islands.

## The Andaman side: Phuket, Krabi, Koh Phi Phi, Similan
- **Phuket**: the big hub of the southwest, with beaches for all tastes
  (Patong for nightlife, Kata/Karon for a quieter mix) and
  a good international airport: the most convenient base from which to leave on
  boat trips to Phi Phi, the Similan Islands or the smaller islands.
- **Krabi**: more authentic than Phuket, starting point for Railay Beach,
  Bond Island and the canoe tours in Phang Nga Bay (see Day 7
  of the itinerary above).
- **Koh Phi Phi**: the most photographed island on this coast, made
  famous by the film *The Beach*: spectacular bays (Maya Bay, reopened to the
  public with limited access to protect the coral reef),
  lively nightlife concentrated in Tonsai Village, great as a stop of
  2-3 days during a wider trip. **It is an inhabited island**, with hotels,
  bars, restaurants and a permanent population: mass tourism is felt here,
  especially in high season.
- **Similan Islands**: something else entirely compared with Phi Phi: it is an **uninhabited
  national marine park**, reachable only with tours organized from
  Khao Lak or Phuket, closed from mid-May to mid-October. No hotels,
  no nightlife, no crowd comparable to Phi Phi: just crystal
  clear water, white granite and the best coral reef on the Andaman
  coast. The difference in one sentence: **Phi Phi is visited to experience
  the island (hotels, venues, atmosphere), the Similan Islands are visited for the sea
  itself** (day snorkeling/diving, then you go back to the mainland). If you
  have to choose just one of the two and the goal is the most beautiful sea,
  the Similan Islands win; if the goal is a mix of scenery, island life and
  evenings out, Phi Phi wins.

## The Gulf side: Koh Samui, Koh Phangan, Koh Tao
- **Koh Samui**: the most "comfortable" of the three: its own airport on the island,
  good tourist infrastructure, resorts in every price range, also suited
  to those looking for relaxation and comfort more than backpacking. A good base also
  for getting to the other two islands for the day or for a few nights.
- **Koh Phangan**: the island of the **Full Moon Party**, but not only: outside
  full moon days it is a young and relaxed island, with
  quieter beaches on the north side (Thong Nai Pan) very different from the chaos of
  Haad Rin.
- **Koh Tao**: the smallest and least worldly of the three, a paradise for
  divers: probably the cheapest place in the world to get the
  PADI certification, thanks to the concentration of diving schools and the good
  visibility in the water.

### The Full Moon Party, explained
It is held every month on the beach of **Haad Rin**, on Koh Phangan, on the night
of the full moon (with some date changes if it coincides with Buddhist
holidays, during which alcohol can't be sold in Thailand). It was born
semi-spontaneously in the mid-'80s among a handful of backpackers, and
became over the years one of the largest beach parties in the world: between
10,000 and 30,000 people in a single night, more at the New Year parties.

- **What to expect:** the entire Haad Rin beach turns into one single
  open-air dance floor, with multiple sound systems playing different genres
  at once (mostly electronic, but also reggae,
  drum'n'bass, pop), fire shows (including the classic jump rope of
  flames), fluorescent paint everywhere, food stalls and the
  famous "buckets", pails with liquor, energy drink and ice, the
  signature drink of the evening (to be handled with judgment: they are stronger
  than they look, and mixing with energy drink doesn't help).
- **Ticket:** 200 baht, paid in cash directly at the gates of
  the beach on the night itself. Be wary of those selling "tickets" online
  in advance, there are no official presale channels.
- **Getting there:** if you stay elsewhere on the island, songthaews (shared
  taxis) connect all of Koh Phangan with Haad Rin all night long; if
  you come from Koh Samui, the last useful ferry leaves in the late
  afternoon and the first one the next morning is around 6:00: in practice,
  **it is best to sleep on Koh Phangan on the night of the party**, not go
  back and forth in a day from Samui.
- **When to go:** dates are published a year ahead on the
  official Full Moon Party website; it is best to book accommodation on Koh
  Phangan well in advance, prices rise a lot and the islands
  fill up in the days around the party.

## What to see / what to do: the highlights
- **Cheow Lan Lake in Khao Sok**: the sunset cruise among the limestone
  towers, among the most beautiful experiences in all of Thailand.
- **Bond Island and canoe from Krabi**: movie-set views, in the literal sense.
- **The Similan Islands**: probably the best snorkeling/diving reachable
  in a day from the mainland in the whole country.
- **Koh Phi Phi**: despite mass tourism, it remains a crazy
  scenery, especially seen from above from the viewpoint.
- **Full Moon Party in Koh Phangan**: if it falls on the right dates, an
  experience to have at least once, knowing what to expect.
- **Jay Fai in Bangkok**: the only Michelin-starred street stall in the
  world, an experience to have at least once in a lifetime.
- **Doi Suthep and an ethical elephant sanctuary in Chiang Mai**: the more
  "mountain" and spiritual side of Thailand.
- **A rooftop bar in Bangkok at sunset**: the best way to say goodbye
  to the city.

## Practical tips
- **Choose the sea based on the season, not the other way around**: first you
  decide the period, then you choose between the Andaman coast and the Gulf of Thailand
  looking at the table above.
- **Book activities in Khao Sok 24h in advance**, through the hotel:
  unofficial providers in the area often don't have a license.
- **Always check the arrival airport for domestic flights** (Bangkok has
  two airports, BKK and Don Mueang) to arrange the right transfer.
- **At the Similan Islands the weather decides everything**: the park closes for the season (mid-May
  to mid-October), so it must be planned with a calendar in hand.
- **Muay Thai, elephants, sanctuaries**: always find out about the ethics of the
  facility before booking: the difference between a serious center and one that
  exploits animals is huge, and in Chiang Mai as elsewhere not all
  "sanctuaries" really are.
- **Most sought-after street food restaurants** (Jay Fai at the top): expect
  long lines or advance booking emails.
- **Full Moon Party**: stay on Koh Phangan on the night of the party, don't go
  back and forth from Samui.

## Chiusura personale
It's hard to close the story of a country visited so many times and in such different ways, but if I have to choose the moments I take home they are the sunset cruise on Cheow Lan Lake in Khao Sok, among limestone towers that look like a small Guilin, and Bangkok's street food, always different and always living up to its fame. Between the Similan Islands and Koh Phi Phi the sea of the former clearly wins, but if I'm after island life and evenings out I always choose Phi Phi. I always happily return to Thailand, and it is perhaps the only country of which I can say with certainty that I will go back again.
