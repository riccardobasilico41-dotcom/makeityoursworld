# Bulgaria on the Snow: Skiing in Bansko and Rila Monastery

**Stato bozza:** 🟢 ready for the site
**Periodo del viaggio:** ski season
**Durata:** 5-6 days
**Compagni di viaggio:** with friends, rental car from Sofia, driven to Bansko
**Categoria:** Europa · Bulgaria · sci · montagna · Balcani

---

## Apertura personale
I didn't expect much from skiing in Bulgaria, and that's exactly what I found: nice, well-kept slopes, nothing over the top compared to the Alps. But that's exactly the charm: you don't go so much for the skiing itself as for the exotic experience of skiing in a corner of Europe that rarely makes it into winter holiday brochures. A different weekend, inexpensive, with a surprise ending among the frescoes of Rila Monastery.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** the Bulgarian ski season runs from December 1 to mid-April, with artificial snow on most of the slopes (snow-gun coverage over 90%): snow is guaranteed even in the driest periods.
- **Documents/visa:** Bulgaria is in the EU, no visa for Italian citizens; an ID card valid for travel abroad or a passport is enough.
- **Currency:** **euro.** At the time of this trip the Bulgarian lev (BGN) was in use, with a
  fixed rate of €1 = 1.95583 BGN, but on January 1, 2026 Bulgaria joined the
  eurozone and from February 1, 2026 the euro is the only legal currency: lev prices
  in older travel accounts need to be converted.
- **Getting there:** you fly into **Sofia**, the only convenient international airport for Bansko (about 160 km, 2-2.5 hours by car).
- **From the airport to the centre:** from **Sofia airport (SOF)** metro line M4, the yellow line, leaves from Terminal 2 and reaches Serdika, in the centre, in about 20 minutes: ticket about €0.80 from the machines or with a contactless card at the gates, running from about 5:30 to midnight; from Terminal 1 there is a free shuttle to T2 · taxis only from the official OK Supertrans desk in arrivals, metered, about €10-15 to the centre, more at night · there is no Uber in Bulgaria · the typical mistake is following someone offering a taxi inside the terminal. For Bansko, see the alternatives below.
- **From Sofia to Bansko, alternatives to the rental car:** I picked up the car directly at the airport and drove to Bansko, but it's not the only option:
  - **Rental car** (my choice): the most flexible solution, especially handy for stopping at Rila Monastery along the way. Mountain road, better with winter tires in winter.
  - **Private or shared shuttle/transfer**: the most popular choice for people who go just to ski. Companies like Traventuria, Bansko Shuttle or Welcome Pickups offer direct transfers from the airport to the hotel or the gondola station. Shared generally costs €15-30 per person, private €80-90 for the whole car (4 people), 2.5-3 hours of travel. It must be booked in advance, because on high-season weekends the shared seats run out.
  - **Public bus**: the cheapest option (about €20-22), but first you need a transfer from the airport to Sofia Central Station, then the bus itself (3-5 hours in all). Not recommended with ski gear or with little time.
  - **Train + minibus**: from Sofia a train to Blagoevgrad (2-3 hours) and from there a minibus to Bansko. Picturesque, but slow.
- **Indicative budget:** Bulgaria remains one of the cheapest ski destinations in Europe: day ski pass around €35-40, a local beer at the mountain hut about €1.50-2, dinner in a mehana (traditional tavern) €15-20 per person.
- **Luggage:** technical ski/snowboard clothing, thermal layers, sun protection, a dressier change of clothes for the evenings in Bansko, covering clothes (shoulders and knees) for the visit to the monastery.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Sofia and transfer to Bansko
We landed in Sofia, picked up the rental car at the airport and set off toward **Bansko** (about 2-2.5 hours of driving, 160 km). The road climbs gently toward the Pirin Mountains, and the landscape quickly goes from hills to mountains. The route follows the Struma motorway south for most of the way and then the river valley up to Razlog, the last town before Bansko. *In winter it's best to drive in daylight: the last stretch can be snowy, and winter tires are essential.*

We reached the hotel in the late afternoon and took a first walk through Bansko's old town: a mix of the stone and wood houses typical of the "Bansko school" and the more modern part, which grew fast over the last twenty years around ski tourism. The old center takes an hour to walk around, between the **Holy Trinity** church with its bell tower and the house-museum of the poet **Nikola Vaptsarov**. Welcome dinner in a traditional **mehana**: **kapama** (a stew of meat and vegetables cooked slowly in a clay pot), **banitsa** (cheese-filled pastry), **shopska salata** (tomatoes, cucumbers, onion and grated cheese, the national salad) and **rakia**, the fruit spirit that is never missing from any meal.

**In practice:** Sofia-Bansko about 160 km, 2-2.5 hours of driving · roads require the electronic vignette, usually already included with the rental: ask at pickup · chains in the car and winter tires from December to March · for easy skiing, sleep near the gondola base.

### Day 2-4 — Skiing on the Bansko slopes
We spent three full days on the slopes of **Bansko Ski Zone**: about 70-75 km of slopes spread over three areas (Banderishka Polyana, Chalin Valog and Shiligarnika), connected by an 8-seat gondola that climbs from 990 to 1,660 meters, up to a maximum altitude of 2,560 meters on Todorka Peak.

There are runs for all levels: blues for beginners (ski school area at Banderishka Polyana), several reds and two blacks, including the Todorka run, the most challenging in the ski area, with a view of the Pirin. The long run down to the valley (about 7.5 km) that goes all the way to the center of Bansko, lit once a week for night skiing, remained the best moment of the days on the snow.

The level of the ski area is honest but unpretentious. It can't stand up to the big Alpine resorts, but it's perfect for a few relaxed days, with modern lifts and almost no lines compared to the Alps in high season. We bought the ski pass online in advance, at a discount compared to the ticket office on site. We rented the gear in one of the many shops in the center, at prices much lower than in the Alpine ski capitals.

On the slopes I remember the Batman statue: a fun touch. Eating in the mountain huts, on the other hand, isn't cheap: prices are very high by local standards, I'd say Italian, European level.

**Après-ski**: at the base of the gondola, Sopranos and Happy End Bar are the classic meeting point at the end of the day; in the center, the old quarter comes alive every evening with mehanas, pubs and live music, at very reasonable prices.

**In practice:** adult day pass about €33 in the 2025/26 season, check the new season's prices on the resort's official website · in high season it pays to be at the gondola early in the morning · rent gear in the shops in town, and try it on the evening before.

### Day 5 — Excursion to Rila Monastery
The day was for **Rila Monastery**, the largest and most important Orthodox monastery in Bulgaria, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1983, founded in the 10th century by the hermit Ivan of Rila. We stopped on the way back to Sofia, detouring from the main road.

The complex is enclosed by 24-meter-high stone walls, with over 300 rooms and 110 monastic cells. What strikes you right away is the five-domed **Church of the Nativity of the Virgin**, entirely covered in brightly colored frescoes. Entrance to the complex and the church is free; you pay (about €4, in euros since 2026) for the Museum of Ecclesiastical History, with ancient manuscripts, icons and the famous Rafail Cross, carved in wood with over 1,500 tiny biblical figures.

*Practical rules for the visit: cover shoulders and knees to enter the church, no photos/video inside the religious buildings.*

After the days on the snow, the monastery was the real twist of the trip: a total contrast, and probably the strongest memory of the whole tour.

**In practice:** from Bansko to the monastery about 2 hours by road, from the monastery to Sofia about 2 more hours · complex and church free, museum about €4 · outside the walls there are restaurants and stalls for lunch · in winter the road up the Rila valley can be icy: leave early.

### Day 6 — Return
Last stretch to Sofia and drop-off of the car at the airport. If your flight is in the afternoon, the morning is enough for a walk around central **Sofia**: the **Alexander Nevsky** cathedral with its gilded domes, the small early-Christian rotunda of **St George**, tucked into a courtyard between government buildings, and the Roman remains of **Serdica**, visible in the underpass of Serdika metro station. The center is walkable, and the car can be left in a paid parking lot.

Coming from the south, you can stop first at the **Boyana Church**, at the foot of Mount Vitosha, a UNESCO site for its frescoes from 1259, among the most important in medieval Eastern European art. Visitors go in small groups for a few minutes to protect the paintings, so on busy days there's a short wait. *Before returning the car, fill the tank and photograph it, as you did at pickup.*

**In practice:** Sofia airport is about 10 km from the center, also reachable by metro to Terminal 2 · Boyana Church about half an hour from the center, inexpensive ticket · be at the airport at least two hours before the flight.

## What to see / what to do
- **The Bansko slopes**, in particular the 7.5 km lit run down to the town center for night skiing.
- **Rila Monastery**, the real highlight of the trip.
- **Bansko's old town**, with the stone houses of the local painting school.
- **Off-the-beaten-path gem:** the mehanas outside the tourist center, where prices drop and the food is more genuine.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
I chose a hotel in Bansko, a few minutes' walk from the gondola station: convenient for reaching the slopes in the morning without moving the car, and a couple of steps from the old town for evenings in the mehana. It wasn't a branded or particularly refined place, just a practical base for the week on the snow.

**Alternatives near the gondola:**
*Budget:* Hotel Dumanov (about 150 meters from the lower gondola station), La Vien Guest House (5 minutes on foot, family-run)
*Mid-range:* Hotel Ida (500 meters from the gondola, indoor pool and hot tub), MPM Hotel Sport (about 100 meters away, almost direct access to the slopes)
*More luxurious:* Kempinski Hotel Grand Arena Bansko (right in front of the gondola station, the only true ski-in/ski-out in town), Premier Luxury Mountain Resort (a few minutes from the gondola with a dedicated shuttle, large spa)

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
Mehanas in Bansko's old town, with kapama, banitsa and shopska salata, always accompanied by rakia.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- If you choose the car instead of the transfer/shuttle, check the mountain road conditions in case of fresh snow; winter tires are always recommended.
- Book ski passes online a few days ahead: discounts compared to the ticket office on site can reach 25%.
- Bring cash: many mehanas outside the center and small shops don't always accept cards (since 2026 you pay in euros, no longer in lev).
- Don't expect Alpine slopes: you go for the experience and the price, not for the level of the ski area.
- Rila Monastery fits nicely on the way back to Sofia: there's no need to dedicate a separate day to it.

## Chiusura personale
A trip that doesn't bet everything on skiing (the slopes are pleasant but honest, without the charm of the big Alpine resorts) and that instead works very well as an exotic, inexpensive interlude off the usual circuit. Rila Monastery, with its frescoes and the silence of the mountain around it, remains the most vivid memory of the whole trip.
