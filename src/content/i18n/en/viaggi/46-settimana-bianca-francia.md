## Apertura personale
France has the largest linked ski areas in the world, built from scratch starting in the 1960s with a single criterion: altitude. This itinerary follows a classic week on a big high-altitude ski area, with the option of swapping it for Chamonix for anyone looking for a different experience.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **January-March, checking the French school holiday calendar**
- **The real constraint is the school holiday calendar**, staggered across three zones: check it before booking.
- **Piste rescue insurance**: in France rescue is paid for, so buy it with the ski pass.
- **Lift pass online in advance**: it costs less than at the ticket office.
- **Saturday in high season**: long queues in the valley, so it's better to leave on Sunday or very early.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 — Arrival
We arrived at the resort, picked up the apartment and the equipment and, if the lift pass allows it, did the first runs that afternoon. On Saturdays in high season the queues in the valley are long: better to arrive on Sunday or very early. A lift pass bought online costs less than at the ticket office, and the piste rescue insurance, which is paid for in France, is bought along with it.

### Day 2 — Skiing the main ski area
The first full day is for exploring the chosen area: Three Valleys, Paradiski or Tignes-Val d'Isère. These are linked ski areas built since the 1960s with a single criterion, altitude, and so big that you never repeat a run.

### Day 3 — Skiing the main ski area
On the second day we pushed out toward the sectors farthest from the base.

### Day 4 — A day in a side valley or in Chamonix
If you're in a nearby ski area you can spend the day in **Chamonix**: the ride up the **Aiguille du Midi** is worth it even if you don't ski, and for expert skiers there's the **Vallée Blanche**, with a guide only.

### Day 5 — Free skiing
We went back to the ski area. For anyone who wants to leave the groomed runs, there's half a day of ski touring or snowshoeing in a side valley.

### Day 6 — Free skiing
We dedicated the last full day to our favorite sectors, discovered over the previous days. In the evening, raclette, fondue savoyarde or tartiflette, with a white wine from Savoie.

### Day 7 — Last morning and departure
The last morning on skis, lunch, and departure in the early afternoon.

## What to see / what to do
- The Aiguille du Midi, even if you don't ski.
- The Vallée Blanche, with a guide only, for expert skiers.
- Half a day of ski touring in a side valley.

## Possible extensions
- **Chamonix for the whole week (alternative):** instead of the big ski area, a valley with a life of its own and terrain for experts.
- **Côte d'Azur (+3-4 days):** from the Southern Alps, skiing in the morning and the sea in the afternoon.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
A residence apartment at the foot of the slopes for those who ski all day; a traditional village like Megève or Chamonix for those who want more life off the skis.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
Raclette, fondue savoyarde, tartiflette, Beaufort and Reblochon cheeses, the white wines of Savoie.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Check the school holiday calendar** before booking.
- **Buy the piste rescue insurance.**
- **Don't arrive on a Saturday in high season without allowing for the queues.**

## Chiusura personale
The difference between an integrated resort and a traditional village shows on a bad-weather day: in the first there's nothing to do if the lifts close, in the second village life goes on anyway.
