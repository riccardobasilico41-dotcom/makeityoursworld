# Ski Week in France: Which Ski Area to Choose

## Apertura personale
France has the largest linked ski areas in the world, built from scratch starting in the 1960s with a single criterion: altitude. This itinerary follows a classic week on a big high-altitude ski area, with the option of swapping it for Chamonix for anyone looking for a different experience.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** **January-March, checking the French school holiday calendar**
- **The real constraint is the school holiday calendar**, staggered across three zones: check it before booking.
- **Piste rescue insurance**: in France rescue is paid for, so buy it with the ski pass.
- **Lift pass online in advance**: it costs less than at the ticket office.
- **Saturday in high season**: long queues in the valley, so it's better to leave on Sunday or very early.
- **From the airport to the centre:** the most used airport is Geneva (GVA), which also has a French sector, handy for renting a car already fitted for snow · for Chamonix, about 1h15: shared shuttles from about €30-50 per person, private transfer about €200-270 per vehicle · for Les 3 Vallées, Paradiski and Tignes-Val d'Isère, 2h30-3h30 depending on traffic: Altibus coaches to Moûtiers, Aime and Bourg-Saint-Maurice only in the winter season, then a local bus, or a shared transfer from about €40-70 per person · alternatives: Lyon (LYS) and Turin (TRN) · the classic mistake is not booking: on Saturdays the shuttles are full.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno
### Day 1 — Arrival
We arrived at the resort, picked up the apartment and the equipment and, if the lift pass allows it, did the first runs that afternoon. On Saturdays in high season the queues in the valley are long: better to arrive on Sunday or very early. A lift pass bought online costs less than at the ticket office, and the piste rescue insurance, which is paid for in France, is bought along with it.

The order of things matters more than usual. First the rental, booked online in the days before, so skis and boots are ready and the fitting takes a few minutes; then the keys to the apartment, usually handed over from the afternoon; then shopping at the resort supermarket before it closes. If there is daylight left, a couple of easy runs near the apartment help you get your ski legs back. *On the road up, the Loi Montagne rules apply: from November to March you need winter tyres or chains on board, and there are checks.*

**In practice:** Carré Neige insurance costs about 3.50 euros a day on top of the lift pass · in many resorts a pass bought online is loaded onto your card or collected at automatic machines · do a big shop on the first day: shops up in the resort cost more than in the valley.

### Day 2 — Skiing the main ski area
The first full day is for exploring the chosen area: Three Valleys, Paradiski or Tignes-Val d'Isère. These are linked ski areas built since the 1960s with a single criterion, altitude, and so big that you never repeat a run. The size should be taken seriously: the **Three Valleys** advertise about 600 kilometers of runs, **Paradiski** about 425, **Tignes-Val d'Isère** about 300. On the first day stay in your own valley and the nearby sectors, to understand how the lifts connect and which runs lead back to base.

Go up with the first lifts of the morning, when the snow is freshly groomed and the runs are empty; take lunch early or late to avoid the crowds in the mountain restaurants between noon and two. A paper piste map in your pocket, as well as the app, helps when your phone dies in the cold. *The most common mistake is being on the far side of the area when the lifts back close: a taxi between valleys is expensive.*

**In practice:** lifts open around nine and close in the late afternoon, earlier in January and a little later in March · the area's official app shows open lifts and last-lift times · many resorts have picnic rooms, useful for cutting costs.

### Day 3 — Skiing the main ski area
On the second day we pushed out toward the sectors farthest from the base. This is the day you see the scale of the area. In the Three Valleys it means reaching Val Thorens and the highest runs; in Paradiski crossing between Les Arcs and La Plagne on the **Vanoise Express**, the cable car linking the two areas high above the valley; in Tignes-Val d'Isère heading up to the **Grande Motte** glacier, above 3,400 meters.

Start with the first lift and plan the return backward: first decide what time you need to be on the last lift home, then how far you can go. East-facing runs are best skied in the morning, west-facing ones in the afternoon, following the sun on the snow. In fog or flat light the tree runs are the easiest to read, and it pays to stay lower down.

**In practice:** the pass must cover the whole linked area, not just your base resort · adult 6-day pass for the 2026/27 season: about €421 in the Three Valleys, about €412 in Paradiski, about €468 in Tignes-Val d'Isère (where it is valid for 7 days) · in strong wind the highest lifts and the links between valleys can close: check the app before setting off · the weather changes fast at altitude, so always carry a jacket and goggles.

### Day 4 — A day in a side valley or in Chamonix
If you're in a nearby ski area you can spend the day in **Chamonix**: the ride up the **Aiguille du Midi** is worth it even if you don't ski, and for expert skiers there's the **Vallée Blanche**, with a guide only. From many resorts in the Tarentaise or Haute-Savoie, Chamonix is a couple of hours or more by car, so leave early. The **Aiguille du Midi** cable car climbs to 3,842 meters: from the terrace you see Mont Blanc up close, and you feel the altitude straight away.

The **Vallée Blanche** starts here: an off-piste descent of about twenty kilometers across glaciers, for skiers confident on black runs and to be done with a mountain guide, who brings rope and gear and knows the crevasses. You descend toward the Mer de Glace and return on the Montenvers train, or on skis all the way to town if the snow allows.

**In practice:** the Aiguille du Midi costs about €83 return: book your ascent time online · book the Vallée Blanche with a Chamonix guiding company, with spare days in case of bad weather · non-skiers can go down to the Mer de Glace on the Montenvers train.

### Day 5 — Free skiing
We went back to the ski area. For anyone who wants to leave the groomed runs, there's half a day of ski touring or snowshoeing in a side valley. The morning is for skiing on piste, the afternoon for the outing: ski schools and guides' offices run half days of **ski touring** with rental gear, and snowshoe walks in the woods and pastures near the resorts, suitable for non-skiers too.

Off piste there is one simple rule: go only with someone who knows the terrain and with the full avalanche kit, meaning transceiver, shovel and probe. *Météo-France publishes an avalanche bulletin every day on a risk scale from 1 to 5: read it before every outing, even with a guide.* In the evening, the apartment complex's pool or spa, good for the legs halfway through the week.

**In practice:** half a day with a guide costs a few tens of euros per person in a group, more for a private outing · book the outing one or two days ahead, depending on the weather · avalanche kits can be rented in resort shops if not included.

### Day 6 — Free skiing
We dedicated the last full day to our favorite sectors, discovered over the previous days. In the evening, raclette, fondue savoyarde or tartiflette, with a white wine from Savoie. After five days you know where the snow holds best and which runs deserve a second go. Build the day around them: the steepest in the morning, when your legs are fresh and the snow is firm, and the long panoramic runs in the afternoon, with a stop at a mountain restaurant with a view.

Anyone who has taken lessons with the ski school does a last session with the instructor. Back down, return the skis to the rental shop if the contract ends that evening, or leave them in the locker for the morning. Dinner is the most Savoyard of the week, with **Beaufort** and **Reblochon** on the table and, in the glass, an Apremont or a Chignin.

**In practice:** resort restaurants fill up on Friday evenings: book at lunchtime · raclette and fondue usually cost a few tens of euros per person, wine excluded · check return times with the rental shop, often the evening before the last day or the morning of departure.

### Day 7 — Last morning and departure
The last morning on skis, lunch, and departure in the early afternoon. First the check-out, which in apartment residences is usually in the morning: leave the bags in the car or the luggage room and head up with the last day of your lift pass, if it covers it.

Choose a sector close to the apartment, without risking being caught far away, and come down in time to return the equipment and have lunch. If you have a car, load it before going up the lifts, so you can leave straight after lunch. *On high-season Saturdays the valley road toward Albertville jams for a long time at peak hours: leaving early or after dinner can make all the difference.*

**In practice:** Bison Futé, the French traffic website, publishes forecasts for holiday weekends with the days to avoid · the French school holiday calendar, staggered across three zones between February and early March, decides how many people are on the roads · fill up in the valley, where fuel is cheaper than in the resort.

## What to see / what to do
- The Aiguille du Midi, even if you don't ski.
- The Vallée Blanche, with a guide only, for expert skiers.
- Half a day of ski touring in a side valley.

## Possible extensions
- **Chamonix for the whole week (alternative):** instead of the big ski area, a valley with a life of its own and terrain for experts.
- **Côte d'Azur (+3-4 days):** from the Southern Alps, skiing in the morning and the sea in the afternoon.

## Dove abbiamo dormito
A residence apartment at the foot of the slopes for those who ski all day; a traditional village like Megève or Chamonix for those who want more life off the skis.

## Dove abbiamo mangiato
Raclette, fondue savoyarde, tartiflette, Beaufort and Reblochon cheeses, the white wines of Savoie.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Check the school holiday calendar** before booking.
- **Buy the piste rescue insurance.**
- **Don't arrive on a Saturday in high season without allowing for the queues.**
- **I ski here the way I ski everywhere**, and it's what I always recommend: go up as soon as the lifts open, to try to be among the first, grab a quick snack mid-morning and then keep going until two, two-thirty. At that point you unplug at the mountain hut and call it a day. That way you have lunch when there are fewer people around, and in the afternoon, when the slopes are more "chewed up," you're already done.

## Chiusura personale
The difference between an integrated resort and a traditional village shows on a bad-weather day: in the first there's nothing to do if the lifts close, in the second village life goes on anyway.
