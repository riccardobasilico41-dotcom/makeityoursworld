# Sumatra and the Mentawai in Two Weeks: Bukittinggi, the Siberut Jungle and the Sea of Sipora

**Stato bozza:** 🟡 work in progress (stay on Siberut with Mentawai Tribe done by Rick; the rest of the itinerary built on research from November 4, 2026. Ferry times and prices and the amount of the surf tax are not confirmed and need to be rechecked)
**Periodo del viaggio:**
**Durata:** 14 days, 13 nights
**Compagni di viaggio:** independently in Sumatra, with a Mentawai operator and its guides on Siberut, then independently again on Sipora; car with driver, fast ferry, motorized canoe, trekking through mud and boat
**Categoria:** Asia · Indonesia · Sumatra · Mentawai · giungla · cultura · surf · spiagge

---

## Apertura personale
I went to the Mentawai with **Mentawai Tribe**, an operator based in Padang that works with Mentawai guides from Siberut. We lived for a few days in the jungle with a family, in their home. These places are unreal.

I should say right away that this isn't a trip for everyone. There's mud everywhere, there are insects, there's no electricity and you sleep on a mat. And there's an ethical side you can't skip: you're entering a family's life, and how you do it matters. I talk about it properly on day 7.

After the jungle, the sea. The beaches of the Mentawai are beautiful, and Sipora is one of the most famous places in the world for surfing. This itinerary puts the two together with the **Bukittinggi** highlands as a first stop in Sumatra. It's the most sensible combination for two weeks: short transfers and ferry days that fit together.

## Scheda pratica
- **When to go:** May to September. On Siberut the driest months are May to early September; from November to January it rains a lot. In the dry season the river can be too low for the canoe, and then you walk more. June-August is also high season.
- **Getting to Padang:** flights from Jakarta, Bali or Kuala Lumpur. From Jakarta or Bali a round trip costs about 130-200 USD.
- **Documents:** passport valid for at least 6 months, **e-VOA** online at evisa.imigrasi.go.id and the **All Indonesia Arrival Card** before entry.
- **Ferry to the Mentawai:** the **Mentawai Fast** leaves from the port at the mouth of the Batang Arau river, in Padang. It takes about 3.5 hours to **Tuapejat** (Sipora) and about 4 to **Siberut**. Sources on days and prices don't agree: they range from 300,000 to 400,000 IDR per person, with a surcharge for surfboards. It's best to buy the ticket the day before at the port. The most reliable source for schedules is the official Mentawai Fast Facebook page.
- **Sea and weather:** boats only go out with the green light from the Syahbandar, the port authority. With a red flag nobody leaves, and ferries can be canceled because of weather or poor visibility. That's why the itinerary keeps a buffer day in Padang before the flight.
- **Flights to the Mentawai:** the Mentawai airport (RKI) replaced Rokot in 2023, but no confirmed regular flights show up. Better not to count on them.
- **Surf tax:** anyone surfing in the Mentawai pays a local tax. The current amount isn't confirmed: sources range from 75 to 130 USD, and in 2025 the local administration announced a range between 500,000 and 2,000,000 IDR depending on length of stay and spot. Ask your lodging or operator for the up-to-date figure.
- **Health:** for malaria, the CDC lists a risk in rural areas of Sumatra. Before leaving you need to stop by a travel medicine clinic, which will decide on prophylaxis. Repellent, long sleeves and long pants.
- **Insurance:** mandatory for the stay with Mentawai Tribe, and it must cover evacuation. If the return ferry is canceled once the trip has started, the operator doesn't refund.
- **Cash:** outside the cities of Sumatra cards are of little use. It's best to leave Padang with cash for the islands.
- **Time zone:** Sumatra and the Mentawai are on WIB (UTC+7).
- **From the airport to the centre:** you land at Minangkabau International (PDG), about 23 km north of Padang · the Minangkabau Ekspres train runs to Padang and to Pulau Air station, near the port for the Mentawai ferries, in about 40 minutes for about IDR 10,000, but with few runs a day: check the timetable in the KAI Access app · DAMRI bus to Padang, about IDR 25,000-30,000 · Grab and Gojek from the pick-up area, about IDR 100,000-150,000 to the centre · to Bukittinggi, a private car for about IDR 500,000 · avoid unlicensed drivers touting in arrivals: fix the price first.

## Itinerario giorno per giorno

### Day 1 — Arrival in Padang and the road to Bukittinggi
You land in Padang and head straight to **Bukittinggi**, in the Minangkabau highlands. From the airport it's about 3 hours by road, but it can turn into 6 if you take the mountain road. A private car costs about 500,000 IDR. The DAMRI bus plus a local bus costs about 65,000 IDR, with more time and more changes. Bukittinggi is your base for the next three days.

It's better not to stop in Padang on the first night: the city is easier to see at the end of the trip, when you come back from the islands. The main road climbs through the **Lembah Anai** gorge, with the waterfall right by the roadside, and passes Padang Panjang before reaching the highlands. Bukittinggi sits at around 900 metres, and the evenings are cool: you'll want a sweater, which comes as a surprise after the humid heat of the coast. *If your flight lands in the afternoon, set off straight away rather than tackling the bends in the dark.* For a place to sleep, the area around the **Jam Gadang**, the clock tower in the centre, works well and lets you walk to dinner.

**In practice:** Minangkabau International Airport to Bukittinggi about 3 hours by car, longer at weekends · private car about 500,000 IDR, agree the price first · get cash and a local SIM at the airport · accommodation in central Bukittinggi, near the Jam Gadang.

### Day 2 — The Harau Valley
A day trip to the **Harau Valley**, within car range of Bukittinggi. For the trips on these days the simplest solution is a car with a driver: Mentawai Tribe, for example, offers one at 75 € a day for up to 4 people and 90 € for up to 9. You're back in Bukittinggi for the evening.

The valley lies beyond Payakumbuh, about 47 km away, and takes an hour and a half to two hours to reach. It's a basin of rice fields closed in by vertical rock walls, with small waterfalls running down the cliffs: the **sarasah**. The best known are Sarasah Bunta and **Sarasah Murai**, about ten minutes' walk into the forest. Leave early, because the morning light on the walls is the best and clouds and rain often roll in during the afternoon. The rest of the day is spent walking through the rice fields along the valley's lanes. *At weekends and on Indonesian holidays the valley fills up with local day-trippers: a weekday is better.* If you want more quiet, you can also sleep in one of the homestays inside the valley.

**In practice:** Bukittinggi to Harau about 1.5-2 hours by car · entry about 10,000-20,000 IDR per person, figures vary from area to area · shoes that can get wet for the waterfalls · simple lunch at the valley's warungs.

### Day 3 — Lake Maninjau
A second day trip, toward **Lake Maninjau**. This one also starts from Bukittinggi, with the same driver or a car arranged on the spot. You eat dinner in town, where **nasi padang** is at home: display cases with lots of ready-made dishes, you point to what you want and pay only for what you touch.

Maninjau is a volcanic lake inside a caldera, and the best way to see it is from above. First you go up to the **Puncak Lawang** viewpoint, about 45 minutes from Bukittinggi, ideally in the morning, when the lake is still free of clouds. Then you head down to the shore along the **Kelok 44**, the road with 44 tight hairpin bends, each one numbered. *If you get carsick, take something beforehand: the bends are many and close together.* By the lake you have lunch with lake fish and wander around the villages before driving back up. You get back to Bukittinggi comfortably the same day.

**In practice:** Bukittinggi to Puncak Lawang about 45 minutes, the lake shore about an hour and a half to two with the descent · Puncak Lawang about 10,000-25,000 IDR per person, the lake shore is free · narrow road, better with a local driver · nasi padang for dinner: you only pay for what you eat.

### Day 4 — Bukittinggi, buffer day
A day without a fixed plan is useful. Roads in West Sumatra can close: in June 2024 the main road to Padang was shut. If everything goes smoothly, it's the day to rest before the jungle. If you love photography, ask about the **Pacu Jawi**, the bull race in the mud typical of West Sumatra: some operators, Mentawai Tribe included, organize dedicated photo outings.

If you stay in town, you can explore it on foot without hurrying. Start from the **Jam Gadang**, where you can no longer climb to the top, and go through the Pasar Atas market. Then the Panorama park on the edge of the **Ngarai Sianok** canyon, with walls dropping toward the river, and under the same park the **Lobang Jepang**, the tunnels dug during the Japanese occupation in the Second World War. The Pacu Jawi, on the other hand, is held in the Tanah Datar area only after the rice harvest, on dates and in villages that change: ask a few days in advance. *During Ramadan the races stop.*

**In practice:** Ngarai Sianok about 15,000 IDR · Lobang Jepang open roughly from 8 am to late afternoon, cheap ticket · Pacu Jawi only at certain times after the harvest, date to confirm with the operator · if the roads are closed, leave for Padang a day early.

### Day 5 — Back to Padang
You return to **Padang** by car, about 3 hours. The afternoon is for getting ready for the jungle: ferry ticket bought at the port, cash, repellent, dry bag, power bank, rain jacket, shoes that can end up in the mud. Jewelry and expensive watches stay at the hotel. You go to bed early.

It's best to leave Bukittinggi in the morning, so you reach Padang with time to sort everything out before offices close. The first stop is the port at the mouth of the Batang Arau, where you buy the Mentawai Fast ticket and ask for the exact boarding time. Then an ATM, because on the islands cash is the only safe way to pay. *Keep the jungle backpack light: the trail to the uma is done on foot, in the mud, carrying everything yourself.* The rest of your luggage can stay in storage at the hotel until you return. For the night, a hotel close to the port is the practical choice.

**In practice:** Bukittinggi to Padang about 3 hours by car · Mentawai Fast ticket about 300,000-400,000 IDR, buy it the day before at the port · cash for all your days on the islands · ask about hotel luggage storage when you book.

### Day 6 — Ferry to Siberut and the trip up the river
The departure day has to be chosen based on the public ferry to Siberut, which according to the operator leaves on Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday. Mentawai Tribe picks you up at the hotel at 6. The ferry leaves at 7 and reaches Siberut in about 4 hours. From there you continue by minivan or pickup to a village, then 1.5-2 hours by **motorized canoe** on the river and about 3.5 km on foot to the **uma**, the family's longhouse. The trail is mud, roots and bridges made of logs over swamps and rivers. You arrive filthy up to the knees.

The open-sea crossing can be rough, even in the good season. *If you get seasick, take a tablet before boarding and sit in the middle of the boat.* Phone, documents and money go straight into the dry bag, because you'll definitely get wet in the canoe and on the logs. It's a long day, almost all of it on the move, and you reach the uma in the afternoon, with little time to rest before dinner. From the port onwards there's no reliable phone signal: if you need to let someone know, do it before you leave.

**In practice:** hotel pickup at 6, ferry at 7 · Padang to Siberut about 4 hours, then road, 1.5-2 hours by canoe and about 3.5 km on foot · everything that mustn't get wet goes in the dry bag · no signal in the jungle, tell people beforehand.

### Day 7 — In the uma with the family
The day follows the family's rhythm: you gather and prepare food, and talk about their culture with the guide translating. This is where the ethical side comes out, and it has to be faced honestly. It's not a human zoo. You're a guest in someone's home, and for many families tradition has long been a risk. Since 1954 the state has kept unrecognized beliefs under control, and **Arat Sabulungan**, the animist religion of the Mentawai, was classified as a mere belief. In the 1970s and 80s, under Suharto's New Order, security forces raided the uma of Siberut. They burned ritual objects and traditional clothing, arrested the **sikerei**, the shaman healers, and forcibly cut men's long hair. Many families were moved from the forest to villages on the coast. Today only a small share still lives by tradition, mostly in southern Siberut.

From this come a few simple rules. Always ask permission before taking a photo, and don't pay for poses or costumes. Don't ask for tattoos or rituals as souvenirs: if the sikerei offers a ritual, it's up to him. The guide arranges gifts (the Mentawai Tribe package includes a gift for the clan). Don't judge their customs and don't ask for made-to-order activities. Take your trash away with you.

**In practice:** the Mentawai guide translates and acts as go-between with the family: questions go through them · photos only with permission · gifts arranged with the guide · your trash goes back to Padang with you.

### Day 8 — The Siberut jungle
Another day with the family and in the forest around the uma. It's hot, between 27 and 33 degrees Celsius (about 80-91°F), with very high humidity and lots of mosquitoes. You eat what the family cooks, simple food. In the evening there's no electricity, and you sleep on a mat under a mosquito net. This is where you find out whether the trip is for you.

What you do depends on the family and the season, and the guide decides it with them. Often you walk in the forest to gather food or work the **sago**, the palm that gives the Mentawai their staple food. A good part of the Siberut jungle is a national park, home to primates found only on these islands, like the Kloss's gibbon: you're more likely to hear them than see them. *The middle of the day is the hardest: it's best to go out early in the morning and leave the afternoon for resting in the uma.* Drink only boiled or filtered water, and check your feet and legs every evening for scratches and bites, which get infected quickly in the humidity.

**In practice:** boiled or filtered water, never from the river · repellent, long sleeves and long trousers from sunset · disinfectant and plasters for scratches · fully charged power bank: nothing gets recharged here.

### Day 9 — From the jungle to the sea, toward Sipora
You set off again on foot and by canoe toward the coast, then by boat toward **Sipora**. Mentawai Tribe has a 6-day package that combines Siberut and a seaside stop on Sipora (from 480 € per person): it's the easiest way to arrange the passage between the islands. If you do it yourself, count on a private boat, roughly 1,500,000-3,000,000 IDR a day. On Sipora there's **Tuapejat**, the capital of the Mentawai, and the ferries to Padang leave from there.

Leave early, because the way back retraces the way in: muddy trail, canoe on the river and road to the coast, before you even get to the boat. The crossing to Sipora depends on the sea, and boats only go out with the port authority's go-ahead. *If the sea is big, wait: losing half a day is better than crossing high waves in a small boat.* You reach Sipora in the afternoon, and the first thing to do is have a real shower. Accommodation is spread along the coast, between Tuapejat and the south of the island: choose one that includes the transfer from the boat.

**In practice:** Mentawai Tribe Siberut + Sipora package from 480 € per person · private boat about 1,500,000-3,000,000 IDR a day · departures only with the Syahbandar's go-ahead · book your Sipora accommodation ahead, with transfer.

### Day 10 — Sipora: beaches and waves
After the jungle, the first day by the sea. The beaches of the Mentawai are beautiful. If you don't surf, you still have the beach and no plans. If you do, you've found one of the most famous places in the world, with a serious warning: the best-known reefs are for intermediate or expert surfers, not beginners. For beginners there are beach breaks, with lessons and rental at about 500,000-1,000,000 IDR a day. Homestays cost roughly 700,000-1,500,000 IDR a night, and resorts start at about 4,000,000 IDR.

Take the day slowly. In the morning the sea is usually smoother and the wind lighter, and it's the best time both for getting in the water and for snorkelling on the reef in front of the beaches. In the middle of the day the sun is fierce, even under a hazy sky, and it's better to stay in the shade. *Coral cuts: even a small scratch should be cleaned and disinfected at once, because in the tropics it gets infected easily.* If you don't surf, you can get a boat to one of the beaches near Tuapejat or simply stay where you're sleeping. In the evening you'll almost always eat at your accommodation, because there are few restaurants outside the main town.

**In practice:** surf lessons and rental about 500,000-1,000,000 IDR a day · homestays about 700,000-1,500,000 IDR a night, resorts from about 4,000,000 IDR · water-resistant sunscreen and a rash vest · first-aid kit for coral cuts.

### Day 11 — Sipora: the southern breaks
A day by boat toward the south of Sipora. In Katiet Bay there's **Lance's Left**, the most accessible left in the area, which still gets serious in big swells. Nearby is **HTs**, or Lance's Right: a right that's hard to read, with a sharp reef on the inside, for experts. In the north of the island there's **Telescopes**, a long left with several barrel sections. Remember to pay the surf tax before getting in the water.

The boat leaves early, because the first hours have the least wind and the cleanest waves, and you also get there before the other boats. The spot is chosen that morning with the boatman or your accommodation, based on swell direction and tide: at low tide the reef is just centimetres below. *On these reefs you need booties, or at least a lot of care when getting out, and you never go in alone.* Non-surfers can come too: snorkel in calm water or stay on the boat and watch. You head back in the early afternoon, when the wind picks up.

**In practice:** day boat to arrange the evening before with your accommodation, price to agree · surf tax to pay beforehand, usually about IDR 1,000,000 per surfer for up to 15 days (some resorts quote 2,000,000), confirm the amount locally · reef booties and a kit for cuts · water and food on board.

### Day 12 — Sipora, slow or buffer day
A day kept free on purpose. If the sea is good you surf again or look for another beach. If the weather knocked out a boat in the previous days, this is where you catch up. On Sipora "The Sound of Love Mentawai" is under construction, chef Max Mariola's project with a restaurant on the beach: construction announced from November 2026.

It's also the day to prepare the return. The ferry ticket to Padang needs confirming now: according to March 2026 reports, the Padang-Tuapejat route can also be booked online, but it's still worth asking your accommodation to check the time of the next day's sailing. *If tomorrow's sailing turns out to be cancelled, the buffer day in Padang becomes ferry day: that's why it should never be cut.* Otherwise it's a slow day: a swim in the morning, a hammock, the cash counted out to pay for your stay before leaving. If you still have energy, take one last boat trip.

**In practice:** reconfirm tomorrow's ferry time today · Tuapejat-Padang ticket also online, according to March 2026 reports · pay for accommodation in cash · dry bag ready for the journey.

### Day 13 — Fast ferry from Tuapejat to Padang
The Mentawai Fast from **Tuapejat** to Padang takes about 3.5 hours, leaving in the early afternoon. The exact day needs to be checked beforehand: runs change often, and in October 2026 a Monday run was suspended for maintenance. You arrive in Padang in the evening.

The morning is free for a last swim, but don't push it: get to the port well ahead of time, because boarding can be chaotic and surfboards pay a separate surcharge. *During Indonesian holiday periods, such as Lebaran, sailings between Padang and the islands fill up: book well ahead.* It's cold on board because of the air conditioning, so a sweater helps. Once in Padang, go straight to the hotel: it's best to sleep near the old town, handy both for the port and for the next day's evening.

**In practice:** Tuapejat to Padang about 3.5 hours, leaving in the early afternoon · ticket about 300,000-400,000 IDR, surcharge for boards · arrive at the port early · hotel in Padang near the old town.

### Day 14 — Buffer day in Padang and departure
If the ferry left on time, this is a quiet day in Padang before the flight. If it was canceled, it's the day that saves the trip. Don't cut it: with the sea around the Mentawai you never know.

With a free day, Padang can be seen in half a day. The **old town** along the Batang Arau river has old Dutch-era warehouses, and the **Siti Nurbaya** bridge is the best spot at sunset. South of the city is **Air Manis** beach, with the rock that legend says is Malin Kundang, the ungrateful son turned to stone. It's also your last chance for a proper nasi padang and to buy packaged **rendang** to take home. *Leave for the airport with plenty of time: traffic out of the city is unpredictable.*

**In practice:** Padang to Minangkabau airport by car, time varies with traffic: allow plenty · old town and Siti Nurbaya bridge on foot, at sunset · Air Manis south of the centre, by car or Grab · packaged rendang for the journey.

## What to see / what to do
- **Bukittinggi and the Minangkabau highlands**, with day trips to the Harau Valley and Lake Maninjau.
- **The Pacu Jawi**, the bull race in the mud of West Sumatra, for photography lovers.
- **A few days in an uma on Siberut** with a Mentawai family, accompanied by Mentawai guides.
- **The beaches of Sipora**, after the mud of the jungle.
- **Surfing on Sipora:** Lance's Left, HTs and Telescopes, only for those who already have the level.

## Practical tips / mistakes not to repeat
- **Choose an operator with Mentawai guides** and ask how much reaches the host family, and in what form. Mentawai Tribe states groups of at most 8 people and 25-40% of revenue going to the community: these are the operator's figures, not verified by anyone else.
- **Always ask before photographing**, and don't pay for poses or costumes.
- **Leave jewelry and expensive watches at home:** they attract requests for gifts.
- **No tattoos or rituals requested as souvenirs.**
- **Go prepared for mud:** shoes you can afford to ruin, dry bag, dry changes of clothes.
- **See a travel doctor** about malaria before leaving.
- **Insurance with evacuation**, mandatory with Mentawai Tribe.
- **Count backward from the ferry:** the departure day for Siberut decides the whole rest of the itinerary.
- **Keep a buffer day** between the return ferry and the flight.
- **Don't trust a single schedule found online** for the Mentawai Fast: check the official page.
- **Ask for the up-to-date surf tax amount** before leaving.

## Chiusura personale
The Mentawai are unreal places. The Siberut jungle is hard, though, and I wouldn't recommend it to just anyone. I ask one thing of anyone who goes: enter that house with respect, knowing what the Mentawai went through to keep their traditions alive. Then there's the sea of Sipora, which after those days looks even more beautiful.
